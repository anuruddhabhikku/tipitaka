Māyākatame taṃ, bhikkhave, bālalāpanaṃ. Ye ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmā, ye ca samparāyikā kāmā; yā ca diṭṭhadhammikā <pb ed="P" n="2.0262"/> kāmasaññā, yā ca samparāyikā kāmasaññā – ubhayametaṃ māradheyyaṃ, mārassesa <note>mārasseva (ka.)</note> visayo, mārassesa nivāpo, mārassesa gocaro. Etthete <pb ed="M" n="3.0049"/> pāpakā akusalā mānasā abhijjhāpi byāpādāpi sārambhāpi saṃvattanti, teva ariyasāvakassa idhamanusikkhato antarāyāya sambhavanti. Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, paṭhamā āneñjasappāyā paṭipadā akkhāyati’’.

66. Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One was staying among the Kurus at a town of the Kurus named Kammāsadhamma.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: "Monks."

"Venerable sir," those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: "Impermanent, monks, are sensual pleasures; hollow, false, deceptive.

They are illusory, monks, the prattle of fools.

Sensual pleasures here and now and sensual pleasures in lives to come, sensual perceptions here and now and sensual perceptions in lives to come - both alike are Māra’s realm, Māra’s domain, Māra’s bait, Māra’s hunting ground.

On account of them, these evil unwholesome mental states such as covetousness (abhijjhā), ill will (byāpāda), and presumption (sārambhā) arise. And they constitute an obstruction to a noble disciple in training here.

Therein, monks, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘Sensual pleasures here and now and sensual pleasures in lives to come, sensual perceptions here and now and sensual perceptions in lives to come - both alike are Māra’s realm, Māra’s domain, Māra’s bait, Māra’s hunting ground.’

On account of them, these evil unwholesome mental states such as covetousness (abhijjhā), ill will (byāpāda), and presumption (sārambhā) arise. And they constitute an obstruction to a noble disciple in training here.

Suppose I were to abide with a mind abundant and exalted, having transcended the world and made a firm resolution with the mind.

When I do so, there will be no more evil unwholesome mental states such as covetousness (abhijjhā), ill will (byāpāda), and presumption (sārambhā) in me.

and with the abandoning of them my mind will be unlimited, immeasurable, and well developed.’

When he practises in this way and frequently abides thus, his mind acquires confidence in this base.

Once there is full confidence, he either attains to the imperturbable (āneñjaṃ) now or else he decides upon perfecting wisdom. On the dissolution of the body, after death,

it is possible that this consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) of his, leading to rebirth, may pass on to rebirth in the imperturbable (āneñjūpagaṃ).

This, monks, is declared to be the first way directed to the imperturbable (āneñjasappāyā) path."

67. Again, monks, a noble disciple considers thus: 'Those sensual pleasures here and now, and those sensual pleasures in future lives; that perception of sensual pleasures here and now, and that perception of sensual pleasures in future lives; whatever form there is (all form)—the four great elements and form derived from the four great elements.'

When he practices in this way and frequently abides thus, his mind finds peace in that base.

When there is complete confidence, he either attains to the imperturbable (āneñjaṃ) in this very life, or he inclines through wisdom at the breaking up of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā).

It is possible that this consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) of his, which leads to rebirth, may go to the imperturbable (āneñjūpagaṃ).

This, monks, is declared to be the second path leading to the imperturbable (āneñjasappāyā paṭipadā akkhāyati).

Again, monks, a noble disciple considers thus: 'Those sensual pleasures here and now, and those sensual pleasures in future lives; that perception of sensual pleasures here and now, and that perception of sensual pleasures in future lives; those forms here and now, and those forms in future lives; that perception of forms here and now, and that perception of forms in future lives—both are impermanent (aniccaṃ).'

What is impermanent (yadaniccaṃ) is not worth delighting in, not worth welcoming, not worth clinging to.'

When he practices in this way and frequently abides thus, his mind finds peace in that base.

When there is complete confidence, he either attains to the imperturbable (āneñjaṃ) in this very life, or he inclines through wisdom at the breaking up of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā).

It is possible that this consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) of his, which leads to rebirth, may go to the imperturbable (āneñjūpagaṃ).

This, monks, is declared to be the third path leading to the imperturbable (āneñjasappāyā paṭipadā akkhāyati).

Yatthetā aparisesā nirujjhanti etaṃ santaṃ etaṃ paṇītaṃ – yadidaṃ ākiñcaññāyatana’nti. Tassa evaṃpaṭipannassa tabbahulavihārino āyatane cittaṃ pasīdati. Again, bhikkhus, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘Sensual pleasures here and now and sensual pleasures in lives to come, sensual perceptions here and now and sensual perceptions in lives to come, material forms here and now and material forms in lives to come, perceptions of forms here and now and perceptions of forms in lives to come, and perceptions of the imperturbable - all are perceptions (saññā).

Where these perceptions cease without remainder, that is the peaceful, that is the sublime, namely, the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana).’

When he practises in this way and frequently abides thus, his mind acquires confidence in this base (āyatana).

Once there is full confidence (sampasāde sati), he either attains to the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ samāpajjati) now or else he decides upon perfecting wisdom (paññāya). On the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (maraṇā),

it is possible that this consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) of his, leading to rebirth, may pass on to rebirth in the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanūpagaṃ).

This, bhikkhus, is called the first practice conducive to the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanasappāyā paṭipadā).

Tassa evaṃpaṭipannassa tabbahulavihārino āyatane cittaṃ pasīdati. Sampasāde sati etarahi vā ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ samāpajjati paññāya vā adhimuccati kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā. “Again, monks, a noble disciple, gone to the forest, or to the root of a tree, or to an empty dwelling, reflects thus: ‘This is empty of self or of what belongs to a self.’

When he practices in this way and frequently abides thus, his mind gains confidence in that base (āyatana).

Once there is full confidence (sampasāde sati), he either attains to the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ) now, or he decides upon perfecting wisdom (paññāya). On the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (maraṇā),

it is possible that this consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) of his, leading to rebirth (taṃsaṃvattanikaṃ), may pass on to rebirth in the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanūpagaṃ).

This, monks, is declared to be the second way (dutiyā paṭipadā) directed to the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanasappāyā)."

71. When this was said, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: “Here, venerable sir, a bhikkhu is practising thus: ‘If it were not, it would not be mine; it will not be and it will not be mine. What exists, what has come to be, that I am abandoning.’
Thus he obtains equanimity (upekkhā).
Venerable sir, does such a bhikkhu attain Nibbāna?”
“Here, Ānanda, a bhikkhu attains Nibbāna, while another bhikkhu does not attain Nibbāna.”
“What is the cause and reason, venerable sir, why one bhikkhu here might attain Nibbāna, while another bhikkhu here might not attain Nibbāna?”
“Here, Ānanda, a bhikkhu is practising thus: ‘If it were not, it would not be mine; it will not be and it will not be mine. What exists, what has come to be, that I am abandoning.’
Thus he obtains equanimity (upekkhā).
He delights in that equanimity (upekkhā), welcomes it, and remains holding to it.
As he delights in that equanimity (upekkhā), welcomes it, and remains holding to it, his consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) becomes dependent on it and clings to it (upādānaṃ).
That bhikkhu clings, Ānanda, and does not attain parinibbāna.”
“But, venerable sir, when that bhikkhu clings, what does he cling to?”
“The base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ), Ānanda.”
“When that bhikkhu clings, venerable sir, it seems he clings to the best object of clinging (upādāna) .”
“When that bhikkhu clings, Ānanda, he clings to the best object of clinging (upādāna).
for this is the best object of clinging (upādāna), namely, the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ).”

<p n="72" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">72</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Idhānanda, bhikkhu evaṃ paṭipanno hoti – ‘no cassa, no ca me siyā; na bhavissati, na me bhavissati; yadatthi, yaṃ bhūtaṃ – taṃ pajahāmī’ti. Evaṃ upekkhaṃ paṭilabhati. So taṃ upekkhaṃ nābhinandati, nābhivadati, na ajjhosāya tiṭṭhati. Tassa taṃ upekkhaṃ anabhinandato anabhivadato anajjhosāya <pb ed="M" n="3.0052"/> tiṭṭhato na tannissitaṃ hoti viññāṇaṃ na tadupādānaṃ. Anupādāno, ānanda, bhikkhu parinibbāyatī’’ti.

72. Here, Ānanda, a bhikṣu (monk) is practicing thus: 'If it were not, it would not be mine; it will not be and it will not be mine. What exists, what has come to be, that I am abandoning.'
Thus he obtains upekṣā (equanimity).
He does not delight in that upekṣā (equanimity), welcome it, or remain holding to it.
Since he does not do so, his vijñāna (consciousness) does not become dependent on it and does not cling to it.
A bhikṣu (monk), Ānanda, who is without clinging attains parinirvāṇa (complete nirvāṇa)."

<p n="73" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">73</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Acchariyaṃ, bhante, abbhutaṃ, bhante! Nissāya nissāya kira no, bhante, bhagavatā oghassa nittharaṇā akkhātā. Katamo pana, bhante, ariyo vimokkho’’ti? ‘‘Idhānanda, bhikkhu ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ye ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmā, ye ca samparāyikā kāmā; yā ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmasaññā, yā ca samparāyikā kāmasaññā; ye ca diṭṭhadhammikā rūpā, ye ca samparāyikā rūpā; yā ca diṭṭhadhammikā rūpasaññā, yā ca samparāyikā rūpasaññā; yā ca āneñjasaññā, yā ca ākiñcaññāyatanasaññā <pb ed="T" n="3.0080"/>, yā ca nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññā – esa sakkāyo yāvatā sakkāyo. Etaṃ amataṃ yadidaṃ anupādā cittassa vimokkho. Iti, kho, ānanda, desitā mayā āneñjasappāyā paṭipadā, desitā ākiñcaññāyatanasappāyā paṭipadā, desitā nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasappāyā paṭipadā, desitā nissāya nissāya oghassa nittharaṇā, desito ariyo vimokkho. Yaṃ kho, ānanda, satthārā karaṇīyaṃ sāvakānaṃ hitesinā anukampakena anukampaṃ upādāya, kataṃ <pb ed="P" n="2.0266"/> vo taṃ mayā. Etāni, ānanda, rukkhamūlāni, etāni suññāgārāni. Jhāyathānanda, mā pamādattha, mā pacchā vippaṭisārino ahuvattha. Ayaṃ vo amhākaṃ anusāsanī’’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā ānando bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti.

<p rend="centre">Āneñjasappāyasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ.

73. "It is wonderful, venerable sir, it is marvelous, venerable sir! Indeed, venerable sir, by the Blessed One, the crossing of the flood has been explained in dependence upon one support or another.

But what, venerable sir, is noble liberation vimokṣa?"

"Here, Ānanda, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘Sensual pleasures here and now and sensual pleasures in lives to come; sensual perceptions here and now and sensual perceptions in lives to come; material forms here and now and material forms in lives to come; perceptions of forms here and now and perceptions of forms in lives to come; perceptions of the imperturbable āneñjasaññā, perceptions of the base of nothingness ākiñcaññāyatanasaññā, and perceptions of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññā - this is the aggregation, as far as there is aggregation ( satkāya ).

This is the Deathless (amata), namely, the liberation of the mind (citta) through not clinging ( anupādā vimokṣa ).’

Thus, Ānanda, I have taught the way directed to the imperturbable (āneñjasappāyā paṭipadā), I have taught the way directed to the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanasappāyā paṭipadā), I have taught the way directed to the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasappāyā paṭipadā), I have taught the crossing of the flood (ogha) in dependence upon one support or another ( nissāya nissāya ), I have taught noble liberation (ariyo vimokkho).

What should be done for his disciples out of compassion by a teacher who seeks their welfare and has compassion for them, that I have done for you, Ānanda.

There are these roots of trees (rukkhamūlāni), these empty huts (sunyagarani).

Meditate, Ānanda, do not be negligent, lest you regret it later.

This is our instruction to you."

The Blessed One said this.

The venerable Ānanda was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Āneñjasappāyasuttaṃ is finished, the sixth.
