Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jetavana in Anāthapiṇḍika’s park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: “Bhikkhus.”

“Yes, lord (bhadante),” those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: “Bhikkhus, I shall teach you the character of a true man (satpuruṣadharma) and the character of an untrue man (asatpuruṣadharma).

Listen & pay close attention. I will speak.”

“As you say, lord,” the monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Bhikkhus, what is the character of an untrue man (asatpuruṣadharma)? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man has gone forth from an aristocratic family (uccākulā pabbajito hoti).

He considers thus: ‘I have gone forth from an aristocratic family; but these other bhikkhus have not gone forth from aristocratic families.’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his aristocratic family.

This, bhikkhus, is the character of an untrue man (asatpuruṣadharma).

But a true man (satpuruṣa) considers thus: ‘It is not because of one’s aristocratic family that states of greed (lobhadhammā), hatred (dosadhammā), or delusion (mohadhammā) are destroyed.

Even though someone may not be of aristocratic family (uccākulā pabbajito hoti); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dhamma (dhammānudhammappaṭipanno), entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the Dhamma (anudhammacārī), he should be honoured for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his aristocratic family.

This, bhikkhus, is the character of a true man (satpuruṣadharma).

“Furthermore, bhikkhus, an untrue man (asatpuruṣa) has gone forth from a wealthy family (mahākulā pabbajito hoti)…pe… A note explains that what follows should be elaborated upon in the same way as the section on aristocratic families. He has gone forth from a family of great possessions (mahābhogakulā)…pe…He has gone forth from an influential family of great possessions (uḷārabhogakulā).

He considers thus: ‘I have gone forth from an influential family of great possessions (uḷārabhogakulā); but these other bhikkhus have not gone forth from influential families of great possessions.’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his influential family of great possessions.

This, bhikkhus, is the character of an untrue man (asatpuruṣadharma).

But a true man (satpuruṣa) considers thus: ‘It is not because of one’s influential family of great possessions that states of greed (lobhadhammā), hatred (dosadhammā), or delusion (mohadhammā) are destroyed.

Even though someone may not have gone forth from an influential family of great possessions (uḷārabhogakulā pabbajito hoti); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dhamma (dhammānudhammappaṭipanno), entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the Dhamma (anudhammacārī), he should be honoured for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his influential family of great possessions.

This, bhikkhus, is the character of a true man (satpuruṣadharma).

106. Furthermore, bhikṣus, an untrue man is known and famous (ñāto yasassī).

He considers thus: ‘I am known and famous (ñāto yasassī); but these other bhikṣus are unknown and of no account.’

On account of that renown, he lauds himself and disparages others.

This too, bhikṣus, is the characteristic of an untrue man (asappurisadhamma).

But a true man considers thus: ‘It is not because of renown that states of greed (lobhadhamma), hatred (dosadhamma), or delusion (mohadhamma) are destroyed.

Even though someone may not be known and famous (ñāto yasassī); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dharma (dhammānudhammappaṭipanno), entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the Dharma (anudhammacārī), he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of that renown.

This too, bhikṣus, is the characteristic of a true man (sappurisadhamma).

Furthermore, bhikṣus, an untrue man gains robes (cīvara), almsfood (piṇḍapāta), resting places (senāsana), and requisites of medicine (gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāra).

He considers thus: ‘I gain robes (cīvara), almsfood (piṇḍapāta), resting places (senāsana), and requisites of medicine (gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāra); but these other bhikṣus do not gain these things.’

So, on account of that gain, he lauds himself and disparages others.

This too, bhikṣus, is the characteristic of an untrue man (asappurisadhamma).

But a true man considers thus: ‘It is not because of gain that states of greed (lobhadhamma), hatred (dosadhamma), or delusion (mohadhamma) are destroyed.

Even though someone may not gain robes (cīvara), almsfood (piṇḍapāta), resting places (senāsana), and requisites of medicine (gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāra); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dharma (dhammānudhammappaṭipanno), entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the Dharma (anudhammacārī), he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of that gain.

This too, bhikṣus, is the characteristic of a true man (sappurisadhamma).

Furthermore, bhikṣus, an untrue man is learned (bahussuto).

He considers thus: ‘I am learned (bahussuto), but these other bhikṣus are not learned.’

So, on account of that great learning (bāhusacca), he lauds himself and disparages others.

This too, bhikṣus, is the characteristic of an untrue man (asappurisadhamma).

But a true man considers thus: ‘It is not because of great learning (bāhusacca) that states of greed (lobhadhamma), hatred (dosadhamma), or delusion (mohadhamma) are destroyed.

Even though someone may not be learned (bahussuto); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dharma (dhammānudhammappaṭipanno), entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the Dharma (anudhammacārī), he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of that great learning (bāhusacca).

This too, bhikṣus, is the characteristic of a true man (sappurisadhamma).

Furthermore, bhikṣus, an untrue man is a master of the monastic discipline (vinayadhara).
He considers thus: ‘I am an expert in the Discipline (vinayadhara); but these other bhikkhus are not experts in the Discipline.’
So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his expertise in the Discipline.
This too, monks, is the characteristic of an untrue man.
But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘Not because of expertise in the Discipline do the qualities of greed (lobhadhamma) go to their destruction, nor do the qualities of hatred (dosadhamma), nor do the qualities of delusion (mohadhamma).
Even though someone may not be an expert in the Discipline, yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dhamma, entered upon the proper way, and conducts himself according to the Dhamma, he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’
So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his expertise in the Discipline.
This too, monks, is the characteristic of a true man.

Moreover, monks, an untrue man is a preacher of the Dhamma (dhammakathika).
He considers thus: ‘I am a preacher of the Dhamma; but these other bhikkhus are not preachers of the Dhamma.’
So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his being a preacher of the Dhamma.
This too, monks, is the characteristic of an untrue man.
But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘Not because of being a preacher of the Dhamma do the qualities of greed go to their destruction, nor do the qualities of hatred, nor do the qualities of delusion.
Even though someone may not be a preacher of the Dhamma, yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dhamma, entered upon the proper way, and conducts himself according to the Dhamma, he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’
So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his being a preacher of the Dhamma.
This too, monks, is the characteristic of a true man.

108. “Furthermore, monks, an untrue man, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters upon and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion.

He considers thus: ‘I have gained the attainment of the first jhāna; but these other monks have not gained the attainment of the first jhāna.’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his attainment of the first jhāna.

This too, monks, is the character of an untrue man.

But a true man considers thus: ‘Non-identification even with the attainment of the first jhāna has been declared by the Blessed One;

for in whatever way they conceive, the fact is ever other than that.’

So, putting non-identification first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his attainment of the first jhāna.

This too, monks, is the character of a true man.

“Furthermore, monks, an untrue man, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, with internal assurance, unification of awareness, free from applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of concentration, enters upon and abides in the second jhāna…With the fading away as well of rapture…he enters upon and abides in the third jhāna…With the abandoning of pleasure and pain…he enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna.

He considers thus: ‘I have gained the attainment of the fourth jhāna; but these other monks have not gained the attainment of the fourth jhāna.’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his attainment of the fourth jhāna.

This too, monks, is the character of an untrue man.

But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘Non-identification even with the attainment of the fourth jhāna has been declared by the Blessed One.

For in whatever way they conceive, it turns out otherwise.

So, putting non-identification first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his attainment of the fourth jhāna.

This too, monks, is the character of a true man.

“Furthermore, monks, an untrue man, with the complete surmounting of perceptions of form, with the disappearance of perceptions of sensory impact, with non-attention to perceptions of diversity, aware that ‘space is infinite,’ enters upon and abides in the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatana).

He considers thus: ‘I have gained the attainment of the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatana-samāpatti); but these other monks have not gained the attainment of the base of infinite space.’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his attainment of the base of infinite space.

This too, monks, is the character of an untrue man.

But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘Non-identification even with the attainment of the base of infinite space has been declared by the Blessed One.

For however it conceives it, it turns out otherwise.

So, putting non-identification first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his attainment of the base of infinite space.

This too, monks, is the character of a true man.

“Furthermore, monks, an untrue man, by completely surmounting the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatana), aware that ‘consciousness is infinite,’ enters upon and abides in the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatana).

He considers thus: ‘I have gained the attainment of the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatana-samāpatti); but these other monks have not gained the attainment of the base of infinite consciousness.’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his attainment of the base of infinite consciousness.

This too, monks, is the character of an untrue man.
“But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘Non-identification even with the attainment of the base of the infinity of consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatana-samāpatti) has been declared by the Blessed One (bhagavatā)’.

For however it conceives it, it turns out otherwise.

So, putting non-identification first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his attainment of the base of the infinity of consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatana-samāpatti).

This too, monks, is the character of a true man.

“Again, monks, an untrue man, by completely surmounting the base of the infinity of consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatana), enters upon and abides in the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana), [thinking] ‘there is nothing’.

He considers thus: ‘I have gained the attainment of the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana-samāpatti); but these other monks have not gained the attainment of the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana-samāpatti)’.

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his attainment of the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana-samāpatti).

This too, monks, is the character of an untrue man.

But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘Non-identification even with the attainment of the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana-samāpatti) has been declared by the Blessed One (bhagavatā)’.

For however it conceives it, it turns out otherwise.

This too, monks, is the character of a true man.

“Again, monks, an untrue man, by completely surmounting the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana), enters upon and abides in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana).

He considers thus: ‘I have gained the attainment of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana-samāpatti); but these other monks have not gained the attainment of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana-samāpatti)’.

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his attainment of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana-samāpatti).

This too, monks, is the character of an untrue man.

But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘Non-identification even with the attainment of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana-samāpatti) has been declared by the Blessed One (bhagavatā)’.

For however it conceives it, it turns out otherwise.

So, putting non-identification first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his attainment of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana-samāpatti).

This too, monks, is the character of a true man.

“Again, monks, a true man, by completely surmounting the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana), enters upon and abides in the cessation of perception and feeling (saññāvedayitanirodha).

And through wisdom, having seen [them], his taints are destroyed.

This monk, monks, does not conceive anything, does not conceive in regard to anything, does not conceive in any way.”
