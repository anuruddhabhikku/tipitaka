207. Thus have I heard.

Once the Blessed One was staying in the Kuru country at a town of the Kurus named Kammasadhamma, on a spread of grass in the fire chamber of a brāhmaṇa belonging to the Bhāradvāja clan.

Then, when it was morning, the Blessed One dressed, and taking his bowl and outer robe, went into Kammasadhamma for alms.

When he had wandered for alms in Kammasadhamma and had returned from his almsround, after his meal he went to a certain grove for the day’s abiding.

Having entered the grove, he sat down at the root of a tree for the day’s abiding.

Then the wanderer Māgaṇḍiya, while walking and wandering for exercise, went to the fire chamber of the brāhmaṇa belonging to the Bhāradvāja clan.

The wanderer Māgaṇḍiya saw that a spread of grass had been prepared in the fire chamber of the brāhmaṇa belonging to the Bhāradvāja clan.

Having seen this, he said to the brāhmaṇa of the Bhāradvāja clan: “For whom has this spread of grass been prepared in Master Bhāradvāja’s fire chamber? It seems like a recluse’s bed.”

“Master Māgaṇḍiya, there is the recluse Gotama, the son of the Śākyans, who went forth from a Śākyan clan.

And concerning that Blessed Gotama, such a good report has spread about: ‘Indeed, the Blessed One is an arahaṃ, perfectly enlightened, accomplished in true knowledge and conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), well-gone (sugato), knower of the world (lokavidū), unsurpassed trainer of persons to be tamed (purisadammasārathi), teacher of gods and humans (devamanussānaṃ), the Awakened One (buddho), the Blessed One (bhagavā)’.

This bed has been prepared for that Master Gotama.”

“It is ill-seen, Master Bhāradvāja, what we have seen; it is ill-seen, Master Bhāradvāja, what we have seen! That we have seen the bed of that Master Gotama, a destroyer of growth (bhūnahuno)!”

“Be careful what you say, Māgaṇḍiya, be careful what you say!

Many learned nobles (khattiyapaṇḍitā), learned brāhmaṇas (brāhmaṇapaṇḍitā), learned householders (gahapatipaṇḍitā), and learned recluses (samaṇapaṇḍitā) have full confidence in that Master Gotama, and have been disciplined by him in the noble true way (ariye ñāye), in the Dhamma (dhamme) that is wholesome (kusale)".

“Even face to face, Master Bhāradvāja, we would see that Master Gotama, even face to face we would say to him: ‘The recluse Gotama is a destroyer of growth (bhūnahu)!’

Why is this? Because that is how it occurs in our teachings (sutte).”

“If Master Māgaṇḍiya has no objection, may I tell this to the recluse Gotama?”

“Let Master Bhāradvāja be at ease. Tell him just what I have said.”

207. Thus have I heard: the Māgaṇḍiya Sūtra.

Here, agyāgāre means in the fire-hall for burnt offerings.

tiṇasanthārake means two Māgaṇḍiyas, an uncle and a nephew.

Of these, the uncle, having gone forth, attained arhatship; the nephew, with the right support, not long after going forth, will attain arhatship.

Then, the Blessed One, having seen his support, and having abandoned the beautiful, deva-chamber-like Perfume Chamber, laid out a straw mat (tiṇasanthāraka) in the fire-hall (agyāgāre) with grass, chaff, rubbish, and so forth, and dwelt there for a few days for the purpose of helping others.

This was said regarding this.

tenupasaṅkamī means not only on that day, but because that fire-hall (agyāgāra) was crowded with boys and girls in the vicinity of the village, and was not secluded, the Blessed One always spent the day in that forest grove, and in the evening went there to stay.

addasā kho…pe… tiṇasanthārakaṃ paññatta means on other days the Blessed One would sweep together the straw mat (tiṇasanthārakam) and leave, making a sign, but on that day he went only after laying it out.

Why? Because then, at dawn, having looked upon the world, he saw – “Today, Māgaṇḍiya, having come here and seen this straw mat (tiṇasanthārakam), will engage in conversation with Bhāradvāja about the straw mat (tiṇasanthārakam), then I will come and teach the Dharma, and having heard the Dharma, he will go forth in my presence and attain arhatship.

For I have fulfilled the perfections only for the purpose of helping others,"—having laid out the straw mat (tiṇasanthārakam), he left.

addasā kho…pe… tiṇasanthārakaṃ paññatta

"samaṇaseyyānurūpaṃ maññe" means I think that this straw mat (tiṇasanthārakaṃ) is a suitable bed for a renunciant (samaṇa).

And this is not a place for an unrestrained renunciant (samaṇa) to live.

For here, a place pulled by hand, or a place pulled by foot, or a place struck by the head is not evident; it is undisturbed, uncrowded, unbroken, as if laid out by a clever painter, having marked it off with a brush.

It is a place for a restrained renunciant (saññatasamaṇa) to live; whose dwelling place is it, sir, he asks.

"bhūnahuno" means of the destroyer of growth, the maker of boundaries.

Why does he say this? Because he has gained the doctrine of the establishment of growth in the six sense-spheres.

For this is his doctrine – the eye should be strengthened and increased; what has not been seen should be seen; what has been seen should be transcended.

The ear should be strengthened and increased; what has not been heard should be heard; what has been heard should be transcended.

The nose should be strengthened and increased; what has not been smelled should be smelled; what has been smelled should be transcended.

The tongue should be strengthened and increased; what has not been tasted should be tasted; what has been tasted should be transcended.

The body should be strengthened and increased; what has not been touched should be touched; what has been touched should be transcended.

The mind should be strengthened and increased; what has not been cognized should be cognized; what has been cognized should be transcended.

Thus, he establishes growth in the six sense-spheres.

But the Blessed One –

"samaṇaseyyānurūpaṃ maññe bhūnahuno"

“Restraint of the eye is good, good is restraint of the ear;

Restraint of the nose is good, good is restraint of the tongue.

Restraint of the body is good, good is restraint of speech;

Restraint of the mind is good, good is restraint everywhere;

The renunciant restrained everywhere is freed from all suffering.”

(Dhp. 360-361) –

establishes restraint in the six sense-spheres.

Therefore, thinking, "The renunciant Gotama is one whose growth has been destroyed, a maker of boundaries," he said, "bhūnahuno."
"ariye ñāye dhamme kusale" means in pure causal states, faultless.

What does this indicate by this? It indicates that when speaking about someone renowned, learned, and famous, one should speak after having reflected, considered, and kept watch over one's mouth. Therefore, it shows: "Don't speak hastily, keep watch over your mouth."

"evañhi no sutte ocaratī" Because it comes thus in our sūtra, we don't speak merely out of what we have picked up by mouth. And since we speak according to what has come in the sūtra, why should we fear anyone? Therefore, [it means] we can speak even face to face.

"appossukko" means not being eager, not being distracted, for the sake of protecting me.

"vuttova naṃ vadeyyā" means Bhava Bharadvaja should speak according to the rule spoken by me, as if not asking but setting forth, not filling up like taking mangoes, jambu fruits, etc.; he should speak, let him speak, according to what I have said.

Then, in the evening, the Blessed One arose from seclusion and went to the fire chamber of the brahmin of the Bharadvaja clan; having arrived, the Blessed One sat down on the spread of grass made ready.

Tassa te saha cakkhuppādā yo pañcasupādānakkhandhesu chandarāgo so pahīyetha; api ca te evamassa – ‘dīgharattaṃ vata, bho, ahaṃ iminā cittena nikato vañcito paluddho paladdho (sī. pī.). Ahañhi rūpaṃyeva upādiyamāno upādiyiṃ, vedanaṃyeva upādiyamāno upādiyiṃ, saññaṃyeva upādiyamāno upādiyiṃ, saṅkhāreyeva upādiyamāno upādiyiṃ, viññāṇaṃyeva upādiyamāno upādiyiṃ. Tassa me upādānapaccayā bhavo, bhavapaccayā jāti, jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṃ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā sambhavanti; evametassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa samudayo hotī’’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ pasanno ahaṃ bhoto gotamassa! Pahoti me bhavaṃ gotamo tathā dhammaṃ desetuṃ yathāhaṃ imamhā āsanā anandho vuṭṭhaheyya’’nti.
220. “Suppose, Māgaṇḍiya, there was a man born blind who could not see dark and light forms, who could not see blue, yellow, red, or pink forms, who could not see what was even and uneven, who could not see the stars or the sun and moon.
He might hear a man with good eyesight saying: ‘Good indeed, sirs, is a white cloth ( odātaṃ vatthaṃ), beautiful, spotless, and clean!’ He would seek after a white cloth.
Then a man would cheat him with a dirty soiled garment ( telamalikatena sāhuḷicīrena) thus: ‘Good man, here is a white cloth ( odātaṃ vatthaṃ )for you, beautiful, spotless, and clean.’
And he would accept it and put it on.
Then his friends and companions, his kinsmen and relatives, would bring a surgeon.
The physician would make medicine - emetics (uddhaṃvirecanaṃ) and purgatives (adhovirecanaṃ), ointments (añjanaṃ) and counter-ointments (paccañjanaṃ) and nasal treatment (natthukammaṃ).
And by means of that medicine the man’s vision would arise and be purified.
Together with the arising of his vision, his desire and liking for that dirty soiled garment (telamalikate sāhuḷicīre chandarāgo) would be abandoned;
then he might burn with indignation and enmity towards that man and might think that he ought to be killed thus: ‘Indeed, I have long been tricked, cheated, and defrauded by this man with this dirty soiled garment (telamalikatena sāhuḷicīrena) when he told me: “Good man, here is a white cloth (odātaṃ vatthaṃ )for you, beautiful, spotless, and clean.’“
So too, Māgaṇḍiya, if I were to teach you the Dharma ( dhammaṃ) thus: ‘This is that health ( ārogyaṃ), this is that Nirvāṇa ( nibbānaṃ)’
you might know health ( ārogyaṃ) and see Nirvāṇa ( nibbānaṃ).
Together with the arising of your vision, your desire and lust for the five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcasupādānakkhandhesu chandarāgo) might be abandoned. Then perhaps you might think: ‘Indeed, I have long been tricked, cheated, and defrauded by this mind.
For when clinging, I have been clinging just to material form (rūpaṃ), I have been clinging just to feeling (vedanaṃ), I have been clinging just to perception (saññaṃ), I have been clinging just to formations (saṅkhāraṃ), I have been clinging just to consciousness (viññāṇaṃ).
With my clinging (upādāna) as condition, being (bhava) comes to be; with being (bhava) as condition, birth (jāti); with birth (jāti) as condition, ageing and death (jarāmaraṇaṃ), sorrow (soka), lamentation (parideva), pain (dukkha), grief (domanassa), and despair (upāyāsā) come to be. Such is the origin of this whole mass of suffering (dukkhakkhandhassa samudayo).’“
“Thus, I am pleased with the Blessed Gotama! May the Blessed Gotama teach me the Dhamma ( dhammaṃ) in such a way that I might rise up from this seat without blindness."
220. Dutiyavāre jaccandhoti jātakālato paṭṭhāya pittādipaliveṭhena andho. Amusminti tasmiṃ pubbe vutte. Amittatopi daheyyāti amitto me ayanti evaṃ amittato ṭhapeyya. Dutiyapadepi eseva nayo. Iminā cittenāti vaṭṭe anugatacittena. Tassa me upādānapaccayāti ekasandhi dvisaṅkhepo paccayākāro kathito, vaṭṭaṃ vibhāvitaṃ.
220.
"Born blind" (jaccandho) means blind from birth, enveloped by bile and so forth (pittādipaliveṭhena andho).
"amusminti" means in that previously stated.
"amittatopi daheyyāti" means "May [he] burn even from enmity," thus he establishes [it] from enmity.
And the same rule (nayo) applies in the second phrase (dutiyapadepi eseva nayo).
"iminā cittenāti" means with a mind that follows (anugatacittena) in the round (vaṭṭe).
"tassa me upādānapaccayāti" means a single connection (ekasandhi), a double summary (dvisaṅkhepo), the condition-form (paccayākāro) has been spoken, the round (vaṭṭaṃ) has been made manifest (vibhāvitaṃ).

221. ‘‘Tena hi tvaṃ, māgaṇḍiya, sappurise bhajeyyāsi. Yato kho tvaṃ, māgaṇḍiya, sappurise bhajissasi tato tvaṃ, māgaṇḍiya, saddhammaṃ sossasi; yato kho tvaṃ, māgaṇḍiya, saddhammaṃ sossasi tato tvaṃ, māgaṇḍiya, dhammānudhammaṃ paṭipajjissasi; yato kho tvaṃ, māgaṇḍiya, dhammānudhammaṃ paṭipajjissasi tato tvaṃ, māgaṇḍiya, sāmaṃyeva ñassasi, sāmaṃ dakkhissasi – ime rogā gaṇḍā sallā; idha rogā gaṇḍā sallā aparisesā nirujjhanti. Tassa me upādānanirodhā bhavanirodho, bhavanirodhā jātinirodho, jātinirodhā jarāmaraṇaṃ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā nirujjhanti; evametassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa nirodho hotī’’ti.
221. “Then, Māgaṇḍiya, associate with true men ( sappurise bhajeyyāsi).

When you, Māgaṇḍiya, associate with true men ( sappurise bhajissasi), you will hear the true Dharma ( saddhammaṃ sossasi); when you, Māgaṇḍiya, hear the true Dharma ( saddhammaṃ sossasi), you will practice in accordance with the true Dharma ( dhammānudhammaṃ paṭipajjissasi); when you, Māgaṇḍiya, practice in accordance with the true Dharma ( dhammānudhammaṃ paṭipajjissasi), then you, Māgaṇḍiya, will know for yourself, you will see for yourself: ‘These are diseases, tumors, and darts; here these diseases, tumors, and darts cease without remainder.’

With the cessation of my clinging ( upādānanirodhā) comes cessation of being ( bhavanirodho); with the cessation of being ( bhavanirodhā), cessation of birth ( jātinirodho); with the cessation of birth ( jātinirodhā), aging and death ( jarāmaraṇaṃ), sorrow ( soka), lamentation ( parideva), pain ( dukkha), grief ( domanassa), and despair ( upāyāsā ) cease. Such is the cessation of this whole mass of suffering ( dukkhakkhandhassa nirodho hotī).”
221.Dhammānudhammanti dhammassa anudhammaṃ anucchavikaṃ paṭipadaṃ. Ime rogā gaṇḍā sallāti pañcakkhandhe dasseti. Upādānanirodhāti vivaṭṭaṃ dassento āha. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Māgaṇḍiyasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
" Dhammānudhamma means conduct in accordance with the Dhamma, conduct befitting the Dhamma.
Ime rogā gaṇḍā sallā shows the five aggregates.
Upādānanirodhā he says, showing the ending of becoming.
The rest is clear throughout.

The Māgaṇḍiyasuttavaṇṇanā, commentary on the Majjhimanikāya, in the Papañcasūdanī, is finished.

222. Evaṃ vutte, māgaṇḍiyo paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama, abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama! Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya – cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantīti; evamevaṃ bhotā gotamena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Esāhaṃ bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca . Labheyyāhaṃ bhoto gotamassa santike pabbajjaṃ, labheyyaṃ upasampada’’nti. ‘‘Yo kho, māgaṇḍiya, aññatitthiyapubbo imasmiṃ dhammavinaye ākaṅkhati pabbajjaṃ, ākaṅkhati upasampadaṃ, so cattāro māse parivasati; catunnaṃ māsānaṃ accayena āraddhacittā bhikkhū pabbājenti , upasampādenti bhikkhubhāvāya. Api ca mettha puggalavemattatā viditā’’ti. ‘‘Sace, bhante, aññatitthiyapubbā imasmiṃ dhammavinaye ākaṅkhantā pabbajjaṃ, ākaṅkhantā upasampadaṃ cattāro māse parivasanti, catunnaṃ māsānaṃ accayena āraddhacittā bhikkhū pabbājenti upasampādenti bhikkhubhāvāya; ahaṃ cattāri vassāni parivasissāmi, catunnaṃ vassānaṃ accayena āraddhacittā bhikkhū pabbājentu, upasampādentu bhikkhubhāvāyā’’ti . Alattha kho māgaṇḍiyo paribbājako bhagavato santike pabbajjaṃ, alattha upasampadaṃ. Acirūpasampanno kho panāyasmā māgaṇḍiyo eko vūpakaṭṭho appamatto ātāpī pahitatto viharanto nacirasseva – yassatthāya kulaputtā sammadeva agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajanti tadanuttaraṃ – brahmacariyapariyosānaṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja vihāsi. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti abbhaññāsi. Aññataro kho panāyasmā māgaṇḍiyo arahataṃ ahosīti. Māgaṇḍiyasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ pañcamaṃ.
222. When this was said, the wanderer Māgaṇḍiya said this to the Blessed One: “Excellent, Master Gotama, excellent! Master Gotama, it is as if you were setting upright what had been overthrown, revealing what was hidden, showing the way to one who was lost, or holding up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes could see. In the same way the Dhamma has been made clear in many ways by Master Gotama. I go to Master Gotama for refuge, to the Dhamma, and to the Community of monks. May I receive the going forth in the presence of reverend Gotama, may I receive the full admission.”

“Māgaṇḍiya, one who formerly belonged to another sect and desires the going forth and the full admission in this Dhamma and Discipline lives on probation for four months. At the end of four months, if the bhikkhus are satisfied with him, they give him the going forth and the full admission to the bhikkhus’ state. But there can be a distinction of persons in this.”

“Venerable sir, if those who formerly belonged to another sect and desire the going forth and the full admission in this Dhamma and Discipline live on probation for four months, and if at the end of the four months the bhikkhus being satisfied with them give them the going forth and the full admission to the bhikkhus’ state, then I will live on probation for four years. At the end of the four years if the bhikkhus are satisfied with me, let them give me the going forth and the full admission to the bhikkhus’ state.”

Then the wanderer Māgaṇḍiya received the going forth under the Blessed One, and he received the full admission.

And soon, not long after his full admission, dwelling alone, withdrawn, diligent, ardent, and resolute, the venerable Māgaṇḍiya, by realising for himself with direct knowledge, here and now entered upon and abided in that supreme goal of the holy life for the sake of which clansmen rightly go forth from the home life into homelessness.

He directly knew: “Birth is destroyed, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of being.”

And the venerable Māgaṇḍiya became one of the arahants (arhat).

The Māgaṇḍiya Sutta, the fifth, is finished.
