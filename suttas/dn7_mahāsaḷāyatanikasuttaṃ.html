<p n="428" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">428</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0521"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0347"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘mahāsaḷāyatanikaṃ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Anathapindika’s monastery (anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme), in the Jeta Grove (jetavane), near Savatthi (sāvatthiyaṃ).

There the Fortunate One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: “Monks (bhikkhavo)!”

“Venerable sir (bhadante),” they replied.

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this: “Bhikkhus (bhikkhave), I shall teach you a discourse on the great sixfold base (mahāsaḷāyatanikaṃ).

Listen & pay close attention (taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha). I will speak (bhāsissāmīti).”

“As you say, lord (evaṃ, bhante),” the monks (bhikkhū) responded.

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said,

429. “Bhikkhus, when one does not know and see the eye (cakkhu) as it actually is, when one does not know and see forms (rūpa) as they actually are, when one does not know and see eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇa) as it actually is, when one does not know and see eye-contact (cakkhusamphassa) as it actually is, when one does not know and see as it actually is the feeling (vedayitaṃ) felt as pleasant (sukha) or painful (dukkha) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukha) that arises with eye-contact as condition, then one is inflamed by lust for the eye, for forms, for eye-consciousness, for eye-contact, and for whatever feeling felt as pleasant or painful or neither-painful-nor-pleasant that arises with eye-contact as condition.

“When one abides inflamed by lust (sārattassa), fettered (saṃyuttassa), infatuated (sammūḷhassa), contemplating gratification (assādānupassino), then the five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhā) are built up for oneself in the future; and one’s craving (taṇhā) - which brings renewal of being (ponobbhavikā), is accompanied by delight and lust (nandīrāgasahagatā), and delights in this and that (tatratatrābhinandinī) - increases (pavaḍḍhati).
One’s bodily troubles (kāyikāpi darathā) increase (pavaḍḍhanti), and one’s mental troubles (cetasikāpi darathā) increase; one’s bodily torments (kāyikāpi santāpā) increase, and one’s mental torments (cetasikāpi santāpā) increase; one’s bodily fevers (kāyikāpi pariḷāhā) increase, and one’s mental fevers (cetasikāpi pariḷāhā) increase.
one experiences bodily suffering (kāyadukkhampi) and mental suffering (cetodukkhampi paṭisaṃvedeti).

430. “Bhikkhus, when one knows and sees the eye (cakkhu) as it actually is, when one knows and sees forms (rūpe) as they actually are, when one knows and sees eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) as it actually is, when one knows and sees eye-contact (cakkhusamphassaṃ) as it actually is, when one knows and sees as it actually is the feeling felt as pleasant (sukhaṃ) or painful (dukkhaṃ) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukhaṃ) that arises with eye-contact as condition, then one is not inflamed by lust for the eye, for forms, for eye-consciousness, for eye-contact, for the feeling felt as pleasant or painful or neither-painful-nor-pleasant that arises with eye-contact as condition.

“When one abides uninflamed by lust (asārattassa), unfettered (asaṃyuttassa), uninfatuated (asammūḷhassa), contemplating danger (ādīnavānupassino), then the five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhā) are diminished (apacayaṃ gacchanti) for oneself in the future; and one’s craving (taṇhā) - which brings renewal of being (ponobbhavikā), is accompanied by delight and lust (nandīrāgasahagatā), and delights in this or that (tatratatrābhinandinī) - is abandoned (pahīyati).

One’s bodily (kāyikāpi) troubles (darathā) are abandoned (pahīyanti), one’s mental (cetasikāpi) troubles are abandoned; one’s bodily torments (santāpā) are abandoned, one’s mental torments are abandoned; one’s bodily fevers (pariḷāhā) are abandoned, one’s mental fevers are abandoned.

And one experiences bodily (kāyasukhaṃpi) and mental pleasure (cetosukhaṃpi).

"When his view is of such a nature, that is his right view (sammādiṭṭhi); when his thought is of such a nature, that is his right thought (sammāsaṅkappo); when his effort is of such a nature, that is his right effort (sammāvāyāmo); when his mindfulness is of such a nature, that is his right mindfulness (sammāsati); when his concentration is of such a nature, that is his right concentration (sammāsamādhi). But his bodily action, his verbal action, and his livelihood have already been well purified earlier. Thus this Noble Eightfold Path comes to fulfillment in him by development.

"When he develops this Noble Eightfold Path in this way, the four foundations of mindfulness also come to fulfillment in him by development; the four right kinds of striving also come to fulfillment in him by development; the four bases for spiritual power also come to fulfillment in him by development; the five faculties also come to fulfillment in him by development; the five powers also come to fulfillment in him by development; the seven enlightenment factors also come to fulfillment in him by development.

"These two things - serenity (samatha) and insight (vipassanā) - occur in him yoked evenly together. He fully understands by direct knowledge (abhiññā) those things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge. He abandons by direct knowledge those things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge. He develops by direct knowledge those things that should be developed by direct knowledge. He realizes by direct knowledge those things that should be realized by direct knowledge.

"And what, monks, are the things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge? It should be said, 'the five aggregates subject to grasping (pañcupādānakkhandhā)', that is to say: the aggregate of form subject to grasping (rūpupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of feeling subject to grasping (vedanupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of perception subject to grasping (saññupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of mental formations subject to grasping (saṅkhārupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of consciousness subject to grasping (viññāṇupādānakkhandho). These are the things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge.

"And what, monks, are the things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge? Ignorance (avijjā) and craving for existence (bhavataṇhā) - these are the things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge.

"And what, monks, are the things that should be developed by direct knowledge? Serenity (samatha) and insight (vipassanā) - these are the things that should be developed by direct knowledge.

"And what, monks, are the things that should be realized by direct knowledge? True knowledge (vijjā) and deliverance (vimutti) - these are the things that should be realized by direct knowledge."

<pb ed="M" n="3.0339"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katame ca, bhikkhave, dhammā abhiññā pariññeyyā? ‘Pañcupādānakkhandhā’ tissa vacanīyaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – rūpupādānakkhandho, vedanupādānakkhandho, saññupādānakkhandho, saṅkhārupādānakkhandho, viññāṇupādānakkhandho. Ime dhammā abhiññā pariññeyyā.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katame ca, bhikkhave, dhammā abhiññā pahātabbā? Avijjā ca bhavataṇhā ca – ime dhammā abhiññā pahātabbā.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katame ca, bhikkhave, dhammā abhiññā bhāvetabbā? Samatho ca vipassanā ca – ime dhammā abhiññā bhāvetabbā.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katame <pb ed="V" n="3.0352"/> ca, bhikkhave, dhammā abhiññā sacchikātabbā? Vijjā ca vimutti ca – ime dhammā abhiññā sacchikātabbā’’ti.

<pb ed="V" n="3.0352"/> <p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca <pb ed="T" n="3.0527"/> bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0527"/> <p rend="centre">Mahāsaḷāyatanikasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ sattamaṃ.

433. "The view of a person such as this is right view (sammādiṭṭhi). His intention is right intention (sammāsaṅkappo), his effort is right effort (sammāvāyāmo), his mindfulness is right mindfulness (sammāsati), his concentration is right concentration (sammāsamādhi).

But his bodily action (kāyakammaṃ), his verbal action (vacīkammaṃ), and his livelihood (ājīvo) have already been well purified earlier.

Thus, this noble eightfold path (ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo) comes to fulfillment in him by development (bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchati).

“When he develops this Noble Eightfold Path (ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ), the four foundations of mindfulness (satipaṭṭhānā) also come to fulfilment in him by development (bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti); the four right kinds of striving (sammappadhānā) also come to fulfilment in him by development; the four bases for spiritual power (iddhipādā) also come to fulfilment in him by development; the five faculties (indriyāni) also come to fulfilment in him by development; the five powers (balāni) also come to fulfilment in him by development; the seven enlightenment factors (bojjhaṅgā) also come to fulfilment in him by development.

“These two things - serenity (samatho) and insight (vipassanā) - occur in him yoked evenly together.

He fully understands by direct knowledge (abhiññā parijānāti) those things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge (abhiññā pariññeyyā).

He abandons by direct knowledge (abhiññā pajahati) those things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge (abhiññā pahātabbā).

He develops by direct knowledge (abhiññā bhāveti) those things that should be developed by direct knowledge (abhiññā bhāvetabbā).

He realizes by direct knowledge (abhiññā sacchikaroti) those things that should be realized by direct knowledge (abhiññā sacchikātabbā).

“And what, bhikkhus, are the things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā pariññeyyā)? They should be spoken of as ‘the five aggregates subject to grasping (pañcupādānakkhandhā),’ that is to say: the aggregate of form subject to grasping (rūpupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of feeling subject to grasping (vedanupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of perception subject to grasping (saññupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of mental formations subject to grasping (saṅkhārupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of consciousness subject to grasping (viññāṇupādānakkhandho). These are the things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge.

“And what, bhikkhus, are the things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā pahātabbā)? Ignorance (avijjā) and craving for existence (bhavataṇhā). These are the things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge.

“And what, bhikkhus, are the things that should be developed by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā bhāvetabbā)? Serenity (samatho) and insight (vipassanā). These are the things that should be developed by direct knowledge.

“And what, bhikkhus, are the things that should be realized by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā sacchikātabbā)? True knowledge (vijjā) and deliverance (vimutti). These are the things that should be realized by direct knowledge."

This is what the Blessed One said. Gratified, the monks delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Mahāsaḷāyatanika Sutta, the seventh, is finished.
