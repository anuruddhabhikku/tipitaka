193. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe. Atha kho vacchagotto paribbājako yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho vacchagotto paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘dīgharattāhaṃ bhotā gotamena sahakathī. Sādhu me bhavaṃ gotamo saṃkhittena kusalākusalaṃ desetū’’ti. ‘‘Saṃkhittenapi kho te ahaṃ, vaccha, kusalākusalaṃ deseyyaṃ, vitthārenapi kho te ahaṃ, vaccha, kusalākusalaṃ deseyyaṃ; api ca te ahaṃ, vaccha, saṃkhittena kusalākusalaṃ desessāmi. Taṃ suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi, bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bho’’ti kho vacchagotto paribbājako bhagavato paccassosi. Bhagavā etadavoca –
193. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was living at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary (veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe).

Then the wanderer Vacchagotta approached the Blessed One (bhagavā) and exchanged greetings with him. When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

Seated to one side, the wanderer Vacchagotta said to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ): "I have had conversations with Master Gotama for a long time. It would be good if Master Gotama would teach me in brief the wholesome (kusala) and the unwholesome (akusala)."

"I can teach you the wholesome (kusala) and the unwholesome (akusala) in brief, Vaccha, and I can teach you the wholesome (kusala) and the unwholesome (akusala) at length. Still I will teach you the wholesome (kusala) and the unwholesome (akusala) in brief. Listen to that and attend closely, I will speak."

"As you say, lord," (evaṃ, bho) the wanderer Vacchagotta replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this:
193.Evaṃme sutanti mahāvacchasuttaṃ. Tattha sahakathīti saddhiṃvādo, bahuṃ mayā tumhehi saddhiṃ kathitapubbanti kathaṃ sāreti mettiṃ ghaṭeti. Purimāni hi dve suttāni etasseva kathitāni, saṃyuttake abyākatasaṃyuttaṃ (saṃ. ni. 4.416 ādayo) nāma etasseva kathitaṃ – ‘‘kiṃ nu kho, bho gotama, sassato loko idameva saccaṃ moghamaññanti abyākatameta’’nti evaṃ ekuttaranikāyepi iminā saddhiṃ kathitaṃ atthiyeva. Tasmā evamāha. Sammāsambuddhopi tassa āgatāgatassa saṅgahaṃ katvā okāsamakāsiyeva. Kasmā? Ayañhi sassatadiṭṭhiko, sassatadiṭṭhikā ca sīghaṃ laddhiṃ na vissajjenti, vasātelamakkhitapilotikā viya cirena sujjhanti. Passati ca bhagavā – ‘‘ayaṃ paribbājako kāle gacchante gacchante laddhiṃ vissajjetvā mama santike pabbajitvā cha abhiññāyo sacchikatvā abhiññātasāvako bhavissatī’’ti. Tasmā tassa āgatāgatassa saṅgahaṃ katvā okāsamakāsiyeva. Idaṃ panassa pacchimagamanaṃ. So hi imasmiṃ sutte taraṇaṃ vā hotu ataraṇaṃ vā, yaṭṭhiṃ otaritvā udake patamāno viya samaṇassa gotamassa santikaṃ gantvā pabbajissāmīti sanniṭṭhānaṃ katvā āgato. Tasmā dhammadesanaṃ yācanto sādhu me bhavaṃ gotamotiādimāha. Tassa bhagavā mūlavasena saṃkhittadesanaṃ, kammapathavasena vitthāradesanaṃ desesi. Mūlavasena cettha atisaṃkhittā desanā, kammapathavasena saṃkhittā vitthārasadisā. Buddhānaṃ pana nippariyāyena vitthāradesanā nāma natthi. Catuvīsatisamantapaṭṭhānampi hi sattapakaraṇe abhidhammapiṭake ca sabbaṃ saṃkhittameva. Tasmā mūlavasenāpi kammapathavasenāpi saṃkhittameva desesīti veditabbo.
193. It is like this.

"Thus I have heard" is the Mahāvacchasutta.

There, sahakathī means friendly discourse. How does he remember the affection, saying, "I have spoken much with you before"?

Indeed, the previous two sūtras were spoken to him. In the Saṃyutta, the abyākatasaṃyutta (Saṃ. Ni. 4.416 and so on) is said to him - "What now, venerable Gotama, is the world eternal, is this alone true, is the rest worthless?" Aabyākatameta’ thus, in the Ekuttaranikāya* also it is said together with this that there is meaning.

Therefore, he spoke thus.

The Sammāsambuddha also, having taken up the coming and going, made an opportunity.

Why? Because he is a sassatadiṭṭhiko, and sassatadiṭṭhikas do not quickly abandon their views, they are cleansed slowly like a wick smeared with sesame oil.

The Blessed One sees: "This wanderer, as time goes on, having abandoned his views, having gone forth near me, having realized the six higher knowledges, will become a well-known disciple."

Therefore, having taken up his coming and going, he made an opportunity.

This, however, was his last journey.

He, indeed, in this sūtra, whether there be a crossing or no crossing, having descended from the staff, falling into the water, as it were, having gone to the presence of the Samaṇa Gotama, having made the determination, "I will go forth," has come.

Therefore, asking for a teaching of the Dharma, he said, "Good for me, venerable Gotama," and so on.

To him, the Blessed One taught a concise teaching based on the root, and a detailed teaching based on the path of action (karmapatha).

Here, the teaching based on the root is extremely concise, the teaching based on the path of action is concise and similar to detail.

For the Buddhas, there is no detailed teaching by an indirect method.

Indeed, in the twenty-four conditioned relations (catuvīsatisamantapaṭṭhāna) also, in the seven treatises (sattapakaraṇa) and in the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, everything is concise.

Therefore, it should be understood that he taught concisely based on the root and also based on the path of action.

Iti kho, vaccha, ime dasa dhammā akusalā, dasa dhammā kusalā. ‘‘Yato kho, vaccha, bhikkhuno taṇhā pahīnā hoti ucchinnamūlā tālāvatthukatā anabhāvaṃkatā āyatiṃ anuppādadhammā, so hoti bhikkhu arahaṃ khīṇāsavo vusitavā katakaraṇīyo ohitabhāro anuppattasadattho parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojano sammadaññā vimutto’’ti.
194. "Vaccha, greed (lobha) is unwholesome (akuśala), non-greed (alobha) is wholesome (kuśala); hate (dosa) is unwholesome, non-hate (adosa) is wholesome; delusion (moha) is unwholesome, non-delusion (amoha) is wholesome. In this way, Vaccha, these three things are unwholesome, and these three things are wholesome.

“Killing living beings (pāṇātipāta) is unwholesome, abstaining from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā veramaṇī) is wholesome; taking what is not given (adinnādāna) is unwholesome, abstaining from taking what is not given (adinnādānā veramaṇī) is wholesome; misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācāra) is unwholesome, abstaining from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇī) is wholesome; false speech (musāvāda) is unwholesome, abstaining from false speech (musāvādā veramaṇī) is wholesome; malicious speech (pisuṇā vācā) is unwholesome, abstaining from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya veramaṇī) is wholesome; harsh speech (pharusā vācā) is unwholesome, abstaining from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya veramaṇī) is wholesome; idle chatter (samphappalāpa) is unwholesome, abstaining from idle chatter (samphappalāpā veramaṇī) is wholesome; covetousness (abhijjhā) is unwholesome, non-covetousness (anabhijjhā) is wholesome; ill will (byāpāda) is unwholesome, non-ill will (abyāpāda) is wholesome; wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi) is unwholesome, right view (sammādiṭṭhi) is wholesome. In this way, Vaccha, these ten things are unwholesome, and these ten things are wholesome.

“When, Vaccha, a bhikkhu has abandoned craving (taṇhā), cut it off at the root, made it like a palm stump, done away with it so that it is no longer subject to future arising, then that bhikkhu is an arahaṃ, one with taints destroyed (khīṇāsava), one who has lived the holy life (vusitavā), done what had to be done (katakaraṇīyo), laid down the burden (ohitabhāro), reached the true goal (anuppattasadattho), destroyed the fetters of being (parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojano), and is completely liberated through final knowledge (sammadāññā vimutto).”

Yatokho, vaccha, bhikkhunoti kiñcāpi aniyametvā vuttaṃ, yathā pana jīvakasutte ca caṅkīsutte ca, evaṃ imasmiṃ sutte ca attānameva sandhāyetaṃ bhagavatā vuttanti veditabbaṃ. 194. Furthermore, refraining from killing living beings is wholesome, etc. The seven dharmas pertaining to the desire realm (kāmāvacara), and the three (trayo) such as non-covetousness (anabhijjhādayo) belonging to the four planes of existence (catubhūmikāpi) are valid.

Since, O Vatsa (vaccha), it is said "of a bhikkhu," although it is said without restriction, just as in the Jīvaka Sutta and the Caṅkī Sutta, so in this Sutta too, it should be understood that the Blessed One (bhagavatā) spoke with reference to himself (attānameva sandhāya).

Since, O Vatsa (vaccha), [it is said] "of a bhikkhu."

197. "What is to be attained by the training knowledge (sekhāya vijjāya pattabba)?" means the three lower fruits are to be attained. He says, "All that I have attained."

But the quibbler says: "Which things are in training (sekkhā)? The four paths not included, and the three lower fruits of the life of a recluse (sāmaññaphalānī)" (Dhs. 1023). According to that statement, the path of Arahantship is also attained by him. But the fruit is not attained, for he is making an additional effort for the attainment of that.

He should be made to understand thus:

"That man is said to be established who, having abandoned the five defilements, is fully trained, not subject to decline, has attained mastery of mind, and whose senses are composed." (A.N. 4.5)

The non-returner (anāgāmi-puggalo) is indeed perfectly trained. Referring to that, he said, "What is to be attained by the training knowledge." But since the path lasts only for a single moment of consciousness, there is no question of one standing on it. If it is asked, "Should the path also be of many moments of consciousness, according to this sutta?" That is not the word of the Buddha, and the meaning contradicts the verse quoted. Therefore, it should be understood that, standing in the fruit of non-return, he causes insight (vipassanā) into the path of Arahantship.

Since for him there is a basis (upanissayo) not only for pure Arahantship, but also a basis for the six superknowledges (abhiññā), therefore the Blessed One, without mentioning only insight, taught both serenity and insight, saying: "Thus, having done work in serenity, he will produce the five superknowledges, having done work in insight, he will attain Arahantship. Thus, he will be a great disciple with the six superknowledges."

May I understand a mind (cittaṃ) with lust (sarāgaṃ) as a mind (cittaṃ) with lust (sarāgaṃ), and a mind (cittaṃ) without lust (vītarāgaṃ) as a mind (cittaṃ) without lust (vītarāgaṃ); a mind (cittaṃ) with hatred (sadosaṃ) as a mind (cittaṃ) with hatred (sadosaṃ), and a mind (cittaṃ) without hatred (vītadosaṃ) as a mind (cittaṃ) without hatred (vītadosaṃ); a mind (cittaṃ) with delusion (samohaṃ) as a mind (cittaṃ) with delusion (samohaṃ), and a mind (cittaṃ) without delusion (vītamohaṃ) as a mind (cittaṃ) without delusion (vītamohaṃ); a contracted mind (saṃkhittaṃ) as contracted (saṃkhittaṃ), and a distracted mind (vikkhittaṃ) as distracted (vikkhittaṃ); an exalted mind (mahaggataṃ) as exalted (mahaggataṃ), and an unexalted mind (amahaggataṃ) as unexalted (amahaggataṃ); a surpassed mind (sauttaraṃ) as surpassed (sauttaraṃ), and an unsurpassed mind (anuttaraṃ) as unsurpassed (anuttaraṃ); a concentrated mind (samāhitaṃ) as concentrated (samāhitaṃ), and an unconcentrated mind (asamāhitaṃ) as unconcentrated (asamāhitaṃ); a liberated mind (vimuttaṃ) as liberated (vimuttaṃ), and an unliberated mind (avimuttaṃ) as unliberated (avimuttaṃ)’—you will attain the ability to witness any aspect therein, there being a suitable basis.

“To the extent that you may wish: ‘May I recollect my manifold past lives (anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussareyyaṃ), that is, one birth (ekaṃ jātiṃ), two births (dve jātiyo)...... Thus with their aspects and particulars may I recollect my manifold past lives (anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussareyyaṃ)’—you will attain the ability to witness any aspect therein, there being a suitable basis.

“To the extent that you may wish: ‘May I, with the divine eye (dibbena cakkhunā), which is purified (visuddhena) and surpasses the human (atikkantamānusakena), see beings (satte) passing away (cavamāne) and reappearing (upapajjamāne), inferior (hīne) and superior (paṇīte), fair (suvaṇṇe) and ugly (dubbaṇṇe), fortunate (sugate) and unfortunate (duggate), and may I understand how beings fare in accordance with their kamma (yathākammūpage satte pajāneyyaṃ) thus: “These worthy beings (bhonto sattā) who were ill-conducted in body (kāyaduccaritena), speech (vacīduccaritena), and mind (manoduccaritena), revilers of noble ones (ariyānaṃ upavādakā), wrong in their views (micchādiṭṭhikā), giving effect to wrong view in their actions (micchādiṭṭhikammasamādānā), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), have reappeared in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in a bad destination (duggatiṃ), in the lower realms (vinipātaṃ), in hell (nirayaṃ upapannā). But these worthy beings (bhonto sattā) who were well-conducted in body (kāyasucaritena), speech (vacīsucaritena), and mind (manosucaritena), non-revilers of noble ones (ariyānaṃ anupavādakā), right in their views (sammādiṭṭhikā), giving effect to right view in their actions (sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), have reappeared in a good destination (sugatiṃ), in a heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannāti)”’—you will attain the ability to witness any aspect therein, there being a suitable basis.

“To the extent that you may wish: ‘May I, by realising for myself with direct knowledge (sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā), here and now enter upon and abide in the deliverance of mind (cetovimuttiṃ) and deliverance by wisdom (paññāvimuttiṃ) that are taintless (anāsavaṃ) with the destruction of the taints (āsavānaṃ khayā)’—you will attain the ability to witness any aspect therein, there being a suitable basis.”
198.Satisatiāyataneti sati satikāraṇe. Kiñcettha kāraṇaṃ? Abhiññā vā abhiññāpādakajjhānaṃ vā avasāne pana arahattaṃ vā kāraṇaṃ arahattassa vipassanā vāti veditabbaṃ.
198. smṛti (sati), because of mindfulness (smṛti, sati), there is the cause of mindfulness (satikāraṇe).

Furthermore, what is the cause here? Is it direct knowledge (abhijñā) or the dhyāna that is the basis of direct knowledge (abhijñāpādakajjhānaṃ)? Or, in the end, is it arhattva? It should be understood that the cause of arhattva is vipaśyanā.

199. Atha kho āyasmā vacchagotto bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā pakkāmi. Atha kho āyasmā vacchagotto eko vūpakaṭṭho appamatto ātāpī pahitatto viharanto nacirasseva – yassatthāya kulaputtā sammadeva agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajanti tadanuttaraṃ – brahmacariyapariyosānaṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja vihāsi. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti abbhaññāsi. Aññataro kho panāyasmā vacchagotto arahataṃ ahosi.
Then the venerable Vacchagotta, having delighted and rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words (bhāsitaṃ), rose from his seat, and after paying homage to the Blessed One, keeping him on his right (padakkhiṇaṃ katvā), he departed.

Then the venerable Vacchagotta, dwelling alone, withdrawn (vūpakaṭṭho), diligent (appamatto), ardent (ātāpī), and resolute (pahitatto), before long – by realising for himself with direct knowledge (sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā) here and now entered upon and abided in that supreme goal (anuttaraṃ) of the holy life (brahmacariyapariyosānaṃ) for the sake of which clansmen rightly go forth from the home life (agārasmā) into homelessness (anagāriyaṃ pabbajanti).

He directly knew: “Birth is destroyed (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti abbhaññāsi).”

And the venerable Vacchagotta became one of the arhats (arahataṃ ahosi).

200. Tena kho pana samayena sambahulā bhikkhū bhagavantaṃ dassanāya gacchanti. Addasā kho āyasmā vacchagotto te bhikkhū dūratova āgacchante. Disvāna yena te bhikkhū tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā te bhikkhū etadavoca – ‘‘handa! Kahaṃ pana tumhe āyasmanto gacchathā’’ti? ‘‘Bhagavantaṃ kho mayaṃ, āvuso, dassanāya gacchāmā’’ti . ‘‘Tenahāyasmanto mama vacanena bhagavato pāde sirasā vandatha, evañca vadetha – ‘vacchagotto, bhante, bhikkhu bhagavato pāde sirasā vandati, evañca vadeti – pariciṇṇo me bhagavā, pariciṇṇo me sugato’’’ti. ‘‘Evamāvuso’’ti kho te bhikkhū āyasmato vacchagottassa paccassosuṃ. Atha kho te bhikkhū yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘āyasmā, bhante, vacchagotto bhagavato pāde sirasā vandati, evañca vadeti – ‘pariciṇṇo me bhagavā, pariciṇṇo me sugato’’’ti. ‘‘Pubbeva me, bhikkhave, vacchagotto bhikkhu cetasā ceto paricca vidito – ‘tevijjo vacchagotto bhikkhu mahiddhiko mahānubhāvo’ti. Devatāpi me etamatthaṃ ārocesuṃ – ‘tevijjo, bhante, vacchagotto bhikkhu mahiddhiko mahānubhāvo’’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Mahāvacchasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ tatiyaṃ.
Now at that time a number of monks were going to see the Blessed One.

The venerable Vacchagotta saw them coming in the distance.

He approached those monks and said to them, "Well, where are you venerable ones going?"

"We are going to see the Blessed One, friend."

"In that case, venerable ones, pay homage with your head at the Blessed One’s feet in my name, saying: ‘Venerable sir, the monk Vacchagotta pays homage with his head at the Blessed One’s feet.’ And say: ‘The Blessed One has been worshipped by me, the Well-Gone One has been worshipped by me.’"

"Yes, friend," those monks replied to the venerable Vacchagotta.

Then those monks went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, sat down at one side.

Sitting to one side, those monks said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, the venerable Vacchagotta pays homage with his head at the Blessed One’s feet, and he says: ‘The Blessed One has been worshipped by me, the Well-Gone One has been worshipped by me.’"

"Monks, even before this, Vacchagotta the monk was known to me, having encompassed his mind with my own mind: ‘The monk Vacchagotta has the threefold knowledge, is powerful and mighty.’

Deities also told me this: ‘Venerable sir, the monk Vacchagotta has the threefold knowledge, is powerful and mighty.’"

This is what the Buddha said.

Elated, those monks delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Great Vacchagotta Discourse is finished, the third.
200.Pariciṇṇo me bhagavāti satta hi sekhā bhagavantaṃ paricaranti nāma, khīṇāsavena bhagavā pariciṇṇo hoti. Iti saṅkhepena arahattaṃ byākaronto thero evamāha. Te pana bhikkhū tamatthaṃ na jāniṃsu, ajānantāva tassa vacanaṃ sampaṭicchitvā. Bhagavato ārocesuṃ. Devatāti tesaṃ guṇānaṃ lābhī devatā. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Mahāvacchasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
200. "The Blessed One attends to me," because seven trainees attend to the Blessed One, and the Blessed One is attended to by one whose outflows are ended.

Thus, in brief, the elder spoke, explaining Arhatship in this way.

Those monks did not understand that meaning; not understanding, they accepted his word.

They told the Blessed One.

Devatā means that the devatā is the gainer of those qualities (guṇas).

The rest is clear throughout.
