312. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho piṅgalakoccho brāhmaṇo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho piṅgalakoccho brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘yeme, bho gotama, samaṇabrāhmaṇā saṅghino gaṇino gaṇācariyā ñātā yasassino titthakarā sādhusammatā, bahujanassa, seyyathidaṃ – pūraṇo kassapo, makkhali gosālo, ajito kesakambalo, pakudho kaccāyano, sañcayo sañjayo (sī. syā. pī. ka.) belaṭṭhaputto, nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto, sabbete sakāya paṭiññāya abbhaññaṃsu sabbeva nābbhaññaṃsu, udāhu ekacce abbhaññaṃsu ekacce nābbhaññaṃsū’’ti? ‘‘Alaṃ, brāhmaṇa, tiṭṭhatetaṃ – sabbete sakāya paṭiññāya abbhaññaṃsu sabbeva nābbhaññaṃsu, udāhu ekacce abbhaññaṃsu ekacce nābbhaññaṃsūti. Dhammaṃ te, brāhmaṇa, desessāmi, taṃ suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi, bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bho’’ti kho piṅgalakoccho brāhmaṇo bhagavato paccassosi. Bhagavā etadavoca –
Thus have I heard:

On one occasion the Blessed One (Bhagavā) was staying at Sāvatthī (Sāvatthiyaṃ), in the Jeta Grove (Jetavane), at Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery (Anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

Then Piṅgalakoccha (Piṅgalakoccho) the Brahmin approached the Blessed One (Bhagavā), and after exchanging greetings with the Blessed One (Bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi), he sat down at one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdi).

Seated at one side (ekamantaṃ nisinno), Piṅgalakoccha (Piṅgalakoccho) the Brahmin said this to the Blessed One (Bhagavantaṃ etadavoca): “Venerable Gotama (bho Gotama), those ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā) who are leaders of groups (saṅghino gaṇino), teachers of groups (gaṇācariyā), well-known (ñātā), famous (yasassino), founders of sects (titthakarā), and highly esteemed (sādhusammatā) by many people (bahujanassa), such as Pūraṇa Kassapa, Makkhali Gosāla, Ajita Kesakambalo, Pakudha Kaccāyana, Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta, and Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta – have all of them directly known the truth according to their claims (sabbete sakāya paṭiññāya abbhaññaṃsu), or have none of them directly known it (sabbeva nābbhaññaṃsu), or have some of them directly known it while others have not (udāhu ekacce abbhaññaṃsu ekacce nābbhaññaṃsū)?”

“[Enough], Brahmin (brāhmaṇa), let that be! [It is sufficient that] all of them according to their own assertions have claimed direct knowledge, or none of them has claimed direct knowledge, or some have claimed direct knowledge and others have not.”

“I will teach you the dharma (dhammaṃ), Brahmin (brāhmaṇa). Listen (suṇāhi) and pay close attention (sādhukaṃ manasi karohi), I will speak (bhāsissāmīti).”

“Yes, sir (evaṃ bho),” Piṅgalakoccha (Piṅgalakoccho) the Brahmin replied to the Blessed One (Bhagavato paccassosi).

The Blessed One (Bhagavā) said this (etadavoca):
312.Evaṃme sutanti cūḷasāropamasuttaṃ. Tattha piṅgalakocchoti so brāhmaṇo piṅgaladhātuko. Kocchoti panassa nāmaṃ, tasmā ‘‘piṅgalakoccho’’ti vuccati. Saṅghinotiādīsu pabbajitasamūhasaṅkhāto saṅgho etesaṃ atthīti saṅghino. Sveva gaṇo etesaṃ atthīti gaṇino. Ācārasikkhāpanavasena tassa gaṇassa ācariyāti gaṇācariyā. Ñātāti paññātā pākaṭā. ‘‘Appicchā santuṭṭhā, appicchatāya vatthampi na nivāsentī’’tiādinā nayena samuggato yaso etesaṃ atthīti yasassino. Titthakarāti laddhikarā. Sādhusammatāti ime sādhu sundarā sappurisāti evaṃ sammatā. Bahujanassāti assutavato andhabālaputhujjanassa. Idāni te dassento seyyathidaṃ pūraṇotiādimāha. Tattha pūraṇoti tassa satthupaṭiññassa nāmaṃ. Kassapoti gottaṃ. So kira aññatarassa kulassa ekūnadāsasataṃ pūrayamāno jāto, tenassa ‘‘pūraṇo’’ti nāmaṃ akaṃsu. Maṅgaladāsattā cassa ‘‘dukkaṭa’’nti vattā natthi, akataṃ vā na katanti. ‘‘So kimahamettha vasāmī’’ti palāyi. Athassa corā vatthāni acchindiṃsu. So paṇṇena vā tiṇena vā paṭicchādetumpi ajānanto jātarūpeneva ekaṃ gāmaṃ pāvisi. Manussā taṃ disvā, ‘‘ayaṃ samaṇo arahā appiccho, natthi iminā sadiso’’ti pūvabhattādīni gahetvā upasaṅkamanti. So ‘‘mayhaṃ sāṭakaṃ anivatthabhāvena idaṃ uppanna’’nti tato paṭṭhāya sāṭakaṃ labhitvāpi na nivāsesi, tadeva pabbajjaṃ aggahesi. Tassa santike aññepi pañcasatā manussā pabbajiṃsu, taṃ sandhāyāha ‘‘pūraṇo kassapo’’ti. Makkhalīti tassa nāmaṃ. Gosālāya jātattā gosāloti dutiyaṃ nāmaṃ. Taṃ kira sakaddamāya bhūmiyā telaghaṭaṃ gahetvā gacchantaṃ, ‘‘tāta, mā khalī’’ti sāmiko āha. So pamādena khalitvā patitvā sāmikassa bhayena palāyituṃ āraddho. Sāmiko upadhāvitvā sāṭakakaṇṇe aggahesi. Sopi sāṭakaṃ chaḍḍetvā acelako hutvā palāyi, sesaṃ pūraṇasadisameva. Makkhalī gosālo Ajitoti tassa nāmaṃ. Kesakambalaṃ dhāretīti kesakambalo. Iti nāmadvayaṃ saṃsanditvā ‘‘ajito kesakambalo’’ti vuccati. Tattha kesakambalo nāma manussakesehi katakambalo, tato paṭikiṭṭhataraṃ vatthaṃ nāma natthi. Yathāha – ‘‘seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, yāni kānici tantāvutānaṃ vatthānaṃ, kesakambalo tesaṃ paṭikiṭṭho akkhāyati, kesakambalo, bhikkhave, sīte sīto uṇhe uṇho dubbaṇṇo duggandho dukkhasamphasso’’ti (a. ni. 3.138). Ajito kesakambalo kesakambalo Pakudhoti tassa nāmaṃ. Kaccāyanoti gottaṃ. Iti nāmagottaṃ saṃsanditvā, ‘‘pakudho kaccāyano’’ti vuccati. Sītudakapaṭikkhittako esa, vaccaṃ katvāpi udakakiccaṃ na karoti, uṇhodakaṃ vā kañjiyaṃ vā labhitvā karoti, nadiṃ vā maggodakaṃ vā atikkamma, ‘‘sīlaṃ me bhinna’’nti vālikathūpaṃ katvā sīlaṃ adhiṭṭhāya gacchati, evarūpo nissirikaladdhiko esa. Pakudho Kaccāyano Sañjayoti tassa nāmaṃ. Belaṭṭhassa puttoti belaṭṭhaputto. Amhākaṃ gaṇṭhanakileso palibujjhanakileso natthi, kilesagaṇṭharahitā mayanti evaṃ vāditāya laddhanāmavasena nigaṇṭho. Nāṭassa puttoti nāṭaputto. Abbhaññaṃsūti yathā tesaṃ paṭiññā, tatheva jāniṃsu. Idaṃ vuttaṃ hoti – sace nesaṃ sā paṭiññā niyyānikā sabbe abbhaññaṃsu. No ce, na abbhaññaṃsu. Tasmā kiṃ tesaṃ paṭiññā niyyānikā na niyyānikāti, ayametassa pañhassa attho. Atha bhagavā nesaṃ aniyyānikabhāvakathanena atthābhāvato alanti paṭikkhipitvā upamāya atthaṃ pavedento dhammameva desetuṃ, dhammaṃ, te brāhmaṇa, desessāmīti āha. Sañjayo belaṭṭhaputto nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto. Abbhaññaṃsū dhammaṃ, te brāhmaṇa, desessāmī
312. Thus have I heard: the Cūḷasāropamasutta.

There, piṅgalakoccho means that brahmin was of a yellowish-brown nature.

koccho means his name, therefore he is called "Piṅgalakoccho."

saṅghino in "etc." means the saṅgha, reckoned as an assembly of renunciates, exists for those, therefore, they are saṅghino.

sveva gaṇo means a group exists for those, therefore, they are gaṇino.

ācārasikkhāpanavasena tassa gaṇassa ācariyāti means a teacher of that group by way of teaching conduct is a gaṇācāriya.

ñātā means known, celebrated, manifest.

"Having few desires, content, due to having few desires, they do not even wear clothes," in this way, the glory that has arisen for those exists, therefore, they are yasassino.

titthakarā means those who make gains.

sādhusammatā means these are considered good, beautiful, and true persons, thus, they are esteemed.

bahujanassā means for the uneducated, blind, foolish common person.

Now, showing them, he said, "For example, Pūraṇa" and so forth.

There, pūraṇo means that was the name of the teacher.

kassapo means the clan.

It is said that he was born completing one hundred minus ten slaves of a certain family, therefore, they named him "Pūraṇa."

Because he was an auspicious slave, there was no one to say to him, "This is badly done," or "This has not been done."

"So, why should I live here?" he fled.

Then thieves stole his clothes.

Not even knowing how to cover himself with a leaf or grass, he entered a certain village naked.

People saw him and approached, taking cakes, rice, etc., thinking, "This renunciate is an arhat, with few desires; there is no one like him."

He [thought], "This has arisen for me because of not wearing a cloak," and from then on, even having obtained a cloak, he did not wear it; he accepted that very going forth.

Near him, other five hundred men went forth; referring to that, he said, "Pūraṇa Kassapa."

makkhalī means that was his name.

Because he was born in a cowshed, gosālo means that was his second name.

It is said that as he was going carrying a pot of oil across muddy ground, his master said, "Dear, do not stumble."

Due to carelessness, having stumbled and fallen, he began to flee out of fear of his master.

The master, having run up, grabbed the edge of the cloak.

He, having thrown off the cloak, became naked and fled; the rest is just like Pūraṇa.

makkhalī gosālo ajito means that was his name.

Because he wears a hair blanket, he is kesakambalo.

Thus, combining the two names, he is called "Ajita Kesakambalo."

There, kesakambalo means a blanket made of human hair; there is no clothing more inferior than that.

As he said, "Just as, monks, of whatever woven clothes there are, a hair blanket is said to be the most inferior of those; a hair blanket, monks, is cold in the cold, hot in the heat, discolored, foul-smelling, and of painful touch" (A.N.
3.
138).

Ajita Kesakambala. Ajita was his name.

Pakudha. Tassa nāmaṃ.

Kaccāyana was his gotra.

Having combined the name and gotra, he is called "Pakudha Kaccāyana".

He refused to use cool water. Even after moving his bowels, he did not wash with cool water, but only with warm water or oil. If he passed through a river or a puddle of water on the path, he thought: “My moral discipline has been broken.” He then re-established his moral discipline by building a mound of sand and continued on his way. He was such a person who had obtained a livelihood without any dirt.

Pakudha.

Kaccāyana.

Sañjaya was his name.

He was the son of Belaṭṭha, thus Belaṭṭhaputta.

"We have no knot of defilement, no defilement of entanglement, we are without knots of defilement," thus by reason of the name received through this kind of doctrine, [he is called] Nigaṇṭha.

He was the son of Nāta, thus Nātaputta.

Just as their promise, so they knew.

This is what was said – if that promise of theirs is a means of escape, all understood.

If not, they did not understand.

Therefore, is their promise a means of escape or not a means of escape? This is the meaning of this question.

Then the Bhagavān, having rejected [their view] as insufficient because of the statement of the non-means of escape, while making known the meaning by means of a simile, in order to teach the Dharma, said, “I will teach you the Dharma, O Brahman.”

Sañjaya, Belaṭṭhaputta, Nigaṇṭha, Nātaputta.

abbhaññaṃsū, I will teach you the Dharma, O Brahmin.

313. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, brāhmaṇa, puriso sāratthiko sāragavesī sārapariyesanaṃ caramāno mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato atikkammeva sāraṃ atikkamma phegguṃ atikkamma tacaṃ atikkamma papaṭikaṃ, sākhāpalāsaṃ chetvā ādāya pakkameyya ‘sāra’nti maññamāno. Tamenaṃ cakkhumā puriso disvā evaṃ vadeyya – ‘na vatāyaṃ bhavaṃ puriso aññāsi sāraṃ, na aññāsi phegguṃ, na aññāsi tacaṃ, na aññāsi papaṭikaṃ, na aññāsi sākhāpalāsaṃ. Tathā hayaṃ bhavaṃ puriso sāratthiko sāragavesī sārapariyesanaṃ caramāno mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato atikkammeva sāraṃ atikkamma phegguṃ atikkamma tacaṃ atikkamma papaṭikaṃ, sākhāpalāsaṃ chetvā ādāya pakkanto ‘sāra’nti maññamāno. Yañcassa sārena sārakaraṇīyaṃ tañcassa atthaṃ nānubhavissatī’ti.
“Suppose, brahmin, a man needing heartwood, seeking heartwood, wandering in search of heartwood, came to a great tree standing possessed of heartwood. Passing over its heartwood (sāraṃ), its sapwood (phegguṃ), and its inner bark (tacaṃ), he would cut off its outer bark (papaṭikaṃ) and its twigs and leaves (sākhāpalāsaṃ) and take them away thinking they were heartwood (sāra).

Then a man with good sight, seeing him, might say: ‘This good man did not know the heartwood (sāraṃ), the sapwood (phegguṃ), the inner bark (tacaṃ), the outer bark (papaṭikaṃ), or the twigs and leaves (sākhāpalāsaṃ).

Thus, while needing heartwood, seeking heartwood, wandering in search of heartwood, he came to a great tree standing possessed of heartwood, and passing over its heartwood (sāraṃ), its sapwood (phegguṃ), its inner bark (tacaṃ), its outer bark (papaṭikaṃ), he cut off its twigs and leaves (sākhāpalāsaṃ) and took them away thinking they were heartwood (sāra).

Whatever it was this good man had to make with heartwood (sārena), his purpose (atthaṃ) will not be served.’

Whatever it was this good man had to make with heartwood (sārakaraṇīyaṃ), his purpose will not be served (atthaṃ nānubhavissati)’.

317. ‘‘Seyyathāpi vā pana, brāhmaṇa, puriso sāratthiko sāragavesī sārapariyesanaṃ caramāno mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato sāraññeva chetvā ādāya pakkameyya ‘sāra’nti jānamāno. Tamenaṃ cakkhumā puriso disvā evaṃ vadeyya – ‘aññāsi vatāyaṃ bhavaṃ puriso sāraṃ, aññāsi phegguṃ, aññāsi tacaṃ, aññāsi papaṭikaṃ, aññāsi sākhāpalāsaṃ. Tathā hayaṃ bhavaṃ puriso sāratthiko sāragavesī sārapariyesanaṃ caramāno mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato sāraññeva chetvā ādāya pakkanto ‘sāra’nti jānamāno. Yañcassa sārena sārakaraṇīyaṃ tañcassa atthaṃ anubhavissatī’ti.
“Suppose, brahmin, a man needing heartwood (sāratthiko), seeking heartwood (sāragavesī), wandering in search of heartwood (sārapariyesanaṃ caramāno) came to a great tree standing possessed of heartwood (sāravato), and cutting off only its heartwood (sāraññeva), he took it away thinking it was heartwood (sāra).

Then a man with good sight (cakkhumā puriso), seeing him, might say: “This good man knew the heartwood (sāraṃ), knew the sapwood (phegguṃ), knew the inner bark (tacaṃ), knew the outer bark (papaṭikaṃ), and knew the twigs and leaves (sākhāpalāsaṃ).

Thus, while needing heartwood (sāratthiko), seeking heartwood (sāragavesī), wandering in search of heartwood (sārapariyesanaṃ caramāno), he came to a great tree standing possessed of heartwood (sāravato), and cutting off only its heartwood (sāraññeva), he took it away knowing it was heartwood (sāra).

And so whatever it was he had to make with heartwood (sārakaraṇīyaṃ), his purpose will be served (atthaṃ anubhavissati)".

318. ‘‘Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco puggalo saddhā agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti – ‘otiṇṇomhi jātiyā jarāya maraṇena sokehi paridevehi dukkhehi domanassehi upāyāsehi, dukkhotiṇṇo dukkhapareto, appeva nāma imassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa antakiriyā paññāyethā’ti. So evaṃ pabbajito samāno lābhasakkārasilokaṃ abhinibbatteti. So tena lābhasakkārasilokena attamano hoti paripuṇṇasaṅkappo. 323. “And what, brahmin, are the states that are higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision? Here, brahmin, a bhikkhu, quite secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion. This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

“Again, brahmin, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, a bhikkhu enters and abides in the second jhāna, which has self-confidence and singleness of mind without applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of concentration. This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

“Again, brahmin, with the fading away of rapture, a bhikkhu abides in equanimity, mindful and fully aware, still feeling pleasure with the body, he enters upon and abides in the third jhāna, on account of which noble ones announce: ‘He has a pleasant abiding who has equanimity and is mindful.’ This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

“Again, brahmin, with the abandoning of pleasure and pain, and with the previous disappearance of joy and grief, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna, which has neither-pain-nor-pleasure and purity of mindfulness due to equanimity. This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

“Again, brahmin, with the complete surmounting of perceptions of form (rūpa-saññā), with the disappearance of perceptions of sensory impact (paṭigha-saññā), with non-attention to perceptions of diversity (nānattasaññā), aware that ‘space is infinite’ (ananto ākāso), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ). This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

“Again, brahmin, by completely surmounting the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ), aware that ‘consciousness is infinite’ (anantaṃ viññāṇaṃ), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ). This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

“Again, brahmin, by completely surmounting the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ), aware that ‘there is nothing’ (natthi kiñci), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ). This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

“Again, brahmin, by completely surmounting the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ). This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

“Again, brahmin, by completely surmounting the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the cessation of perception and feeling (saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ), and his taints (āsavā) are destroyed by seeing with wisdom (paññāya). This is a state higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.

These are the states that are higher and more sublime than knowledge and vision.
323.Idha, brāhmaṇa bhikkhu, vivicceva kāmehīti kathaṃ ime paṭhamajjhānādidhammā ñāṇadassanena uttaritarā jātāti? Nirodhapādakattā. Heṭṭhā paṭhamajjhānādidhammā hi vipassanāpādakā , idha nirodhapādakā, tasmā uttaritarā jātāti veditabbā. Iti bhagavā idampi suttaṃ yathānusandhināva niṭṭhapesi. Desanāvasāne brāhmaṇo saraṇesu patiṭṭhitoti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Cūḷasāropamasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā. Tatiyavaggavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
Here: "A brāhmaṇa monk, truly secluded from sensual pleasures." How are these first jhāna and so forth more excellent through the knowledge and vision? Because of being the foundation of cessation (nirodha).

The first jhāna and so forth are the foundation of vipaśyanā below, here they are the foundation of cessation; therefore, it should be understood that they are more excellent.

Thus, the Blessed One concluded even this sūtra according to the connection.

”I say that this is like, brahmin, a man needing heartwood, seeking heartwood, wandering in search of heartwood, who came to a great tree standing possessed of heartwood, and cutting off only its heartwood, took it away knowing it was heartwood.
And so whatever it was he had to make with heartwood, his purpose will have been served.
This person, I say, is just like that, brahmin.

“So this holy life, brahmin, does not have gain, honor, and renown for its benefit, or the attainment of virtue for its benefit, or the attainment of concentration for its benefit, or knowledge and vision for its benefit.
But rather, brahmin, the unshakeable liberation of the mind—this is the aim, brahmin, of the spiritual life; this is the heartwood, this is the final goal.”
