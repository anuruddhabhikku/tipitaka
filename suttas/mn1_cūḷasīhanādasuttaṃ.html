139. Thus have I heard: the Cūḷasīhanāda Sutta.

Since there is a presentation of its contextual origin, we will explain that and then give a word-by-word commentary in the proper order.

How is this placed in terms of its contextual origin? Because of the gains and honors [received by the Buddha], the Tīrthikas lamented.

It seems that a great gain and honor arose for the Blessed One, according to the method stated in the Dhammadāyāda Sutta.

This world-system is based on four standards, namely, those who are pleased by form, those who are pleased by sound, those who are pleased by austerity, and those who are pleased by the Dhamma.

Among them, what sort of person is pleased by form? Here, a certain person, having seen stature, or having seen circumference, or having seen shape, or having seen completeness, taking that as a standard, generates faith. This is called a person who is pleased by form.

What sort of person is pleased by sound? Here, a certain person, by another's praise, another's eulogy, another's commendation, another's eloquence, taking that as a standard, generates faith. This is called a person who is pleased by sound.

What sort of person is pleased by austerity? Here, a certain person, having seen the coarseness of robes, or having seen the coarseness of a bowl, or having seen the coarseness of lodgings, or having seen various difficult practices, taking that as a standard, generates faith. This is called a person who is pleased by austerity.

What sort of person is pleased by the Dhamma? Here, a certain person, having seen virtue, or having seen concentration, or having seen wisdom, taking that as a standard, generates faith. This is called a person who is pleased by the Dhamma.

Among these four types of individuals, even one who is pleased by form, seeing the Blessed One's perfection of stature, circumference, shape, completeness, beauty, adorned with eighty minor marks, like a great cloth of gold variegated with various jewels, crowded with thirty-two marks of a great man, like the surface of the sky radiant with a multitude of stars, like a Pāricchatta tree a hundred leagues high completely covered with blossoms, surrounded by a fathom-long aura eighteen times the size of a jewel, with a beautiful and incomparable body, is pleased only with the Sammāsambuddha.

Even one who is pleased by sound, hearing the Blessed One's voice, which arose because the ten perfections, the ten secondary perfections, and the ten highest perfections were fulfilled for four incalculable aeons plus one hundred thousand aeons, [and because] the sacrifice of limbs, the sacrifice of children and spouses, the sacrifice of kingdoms, the sacrifice of self, and the sacrifice of eyes, etc., were made, is pleased only with the Sammāsambuddha.

Even one who is pleased by austerity, seeing the Blessed One's coarse robes, [thinks,] "If the Blessed One were to live at home, he would wear only Kāsika cloth. Having gone forth, however, he is content with a robe made of hemp or discarded rags, and he does something extraordinary," and is pleased only with the Sammāsambuddha.

Also, having seen the coarseness of the bowl, [he thinks,] "This man, while living at home, partook of fragrant Sāli rice worthy of a universal monarch in golden dishes adorned with red gems. Having gone forth, however, taking a bowl made of stone, wandering for alms from house to house in villages high and low, he is content with a mouthful of food obtained by alms, and he does something extraordinary," and is pleased only with the Sammāsambuddha.

Also, having seen the coarseness of the lodgings, [he thinks,] "This man, while living at home, having enjoyed the prosperity of kingship like divine wealth in three palaces suitable for the three seasons, surrounded by a variety of dancers, now, having gone forth, is content with lodgings at the root of a tree, etc., with wooden planks, stone slabs, benches, and beds, and he does something extraordinary," and is pleased only with the Sammāsambuddha.

Also, having seen his difficult practices, [he thinks,] "For six years, he will sustain himself with only a handful of mung bean soup, horsebean soup, vetch soup, and wild grain soup, he will meditate on mindless meditation, and he will live without concern for his body or his life. How difficult is the Blessed One's practice!" and is pleased only with the Sammāsambuddha.

Even one who is pleased by the Dhamma, seeing the Blessed One's virtues of morality, virtues of concentration, virtues of wisdom, the accomplishment of the attainment of jhāna, liberation, concentration, and samāpatti, the perfection of the higher knowledges, the twin miracle, the descent from the divine realm, the taming of Pāthikaputta, etc., and many other wonders, is pleased only with the Sammāsambuddha. Being thus pleased, they bring great gain and honor to the Blessed One.

However, the gains and honors of the Tīrthikas declined, like the crow in the Bāveru Jātaka.

As it was said:

In the Bāveru Jātaka:

"When the peacock, with its crest and sweet voice, was not seen,
There they worshiped the crow with meat and fruit.

But when the peacock, full of charm, came to Bāveru,
Then the gain and honor of the crow vanished.

As long as the Buddha, the Dhamma-king, the radiant one, had not arisen,
So long did others worship various ascetics and brahmins.

But when the Buddha, full of charm, taught the Dhamma,
Then the gain and honor of the Tīrthikas vanished."
(Jā. 1.
4.153-156);

Having thus lost their gains and honor, they, even though shining a finger or two's breadth during the night, became devoid of luster like fireflies at sunrise.

As fireflies, indeed, on a dark night, show a light; that is their domain.

And when the radiant, light-making sun rises, then the light of the firefly assembly disappears.

Even so, the sectarians, though diverse here, display their qualities in a world comparable to a dark night.

And when the Buddha, of limitless radiance, arises in the world, the sectarians become lusterless, like fireflies at sunrise.

143. Now, showing the cutting off of views, he says, "These four, monks, are grasping ( cattārimāni, bhikkhave, upādānāni)," and so on.
Their detailed discussion is indeed stated in the Visuddhimagga.

"Those who profess the doctrine of the complete knowledge of all grasping (sabbupādānapariññāvādā paṭijānamānā)" means those who profess, "We declare the transcendence of the knowledge of all grasping," thus professing.
"There is no correct complete knowledge of all grasping (na sammā sabbupādānapariñña)" means they do not correctly teach the transcendence of the knowledge of all grasping.
Some teach the knowledge of only grasping at sensual pleasure (kāmupādānamattassa pariññaṃ paññapenti).
Some teach the knowledge of only grasping at views, and some [teach] even grasping at ethical practices and vows.
But there is no one who teaches the knowledge of grasping at the doctrine of self.
Now, showing the distinction among them, he says, "Those who teach the knowledge of grasping at sensual pleasure (kāmupādānassa pariññaṃ paññapentī)," and so on.
There, all of them indeed teach the knowledge of grasping at sensual pleasure. For even the ninety-six heretics do not teach that the proper practice is the rejection of the basis, saying, "Sensual pleasures should not be cultivated by one who has gone forth," but they teach that it is indeed improper.
But those who cultivate them, cultivate them by stealth.
Therefore, it was said, "Those who teach the knowledge of grasping at sensual pleasure (kāmupādānassa pariññaṃ paññapentī)."

Those who profess the doctrine of the complete knowledge of all grasping (sabbupādānapariññāvādā paṭijānamānā), [there is] no correct complete knowledge of all grasping (na sammā sabbupādānapariñña), those who teach the knowledge of grasping at sensual pleasure (kāmupādānassa pariññaṃ paññapentī), because they conduct themselves by taking up, "There is no giving," and so on.
They grasp, "Purification is by ethical conduct, purification is by vows, purification is by meditation," they do not abandon the attainment of self, therefore, they do not teach the knowledge of grasping at views, nor grasping at ethical practices and vows, nor the knowledge of grasping at the doctrine of self.
"What is the reason for that? (taṃ kissa hetū)" means what is the reason, what is the cause, for that non-teaching of these? "These, monks (imāni hi te bhonto)," means because those monks do not know these three causes as they really are, is the meaning.
Those who here know as they really are the cause of the teaching of the two kinds of knowledge, both views and ethical practices and vows, [saying], "This should be abandoned."
With reference to them, two further statements have been made.
There, those who grasp, "There is giving," and so on, teach the knowledge of grasping at views.
But those who grasp, "There is no purification by ethical conduct, no purification by vows, no purification by meditation," teach the knowledge of grasping at ethical practices and vows.
But not even one is able to teach the knowledge of grasping at the doctrine of self.
Even those who have gained the eight attainments, the heretics who go about caressing the moon and sun with their hands, also teach three kinds of knowledge.
They are not able to relinquish the doctrine of self.
Therefore, again and again, they fall into the cycle itself.
For these are like a rabbit that is disgusted with the earth.

What is the reason for that? (taṃ kissa hetū) These, monks (imāni hi te bhonto)

There, this is an illustrative simile that accords with the meaning: the earth, it seems, said to a rabbit, "Hey, rabbit."
The rabbit said, "Who is that?"
"[It is] because you, making all bodily activities on top of me, making excrement and urine, do not know me."
"I have been seen well by you. The place trampled by me is like a place touched by the tips of the fingers, with scattered water, very little, with dung the size of a kataka fruit.
But even the place trampled by elephants and horses is great, and their urine is a potful, and even the excrement is a cartload. Enough of you for me!" Having flown up, he fell in another place.
Then the earth said to him, "Hey, even having gone far away, you have indeed fallen on top of me!"
That one, again, disgusted with it, having flown up, fell elsewhere. In this way, even for a thousand years, the rabbit, flying up and falling, is not able to relinquish the earth.
Just so, even though the heretics teach the knowledge of all grasping, they teach the transcendence of only three, beginning with grasping at sensual pleasure.
But they are not able to relinquish the doctrine of self. Not being able, they fall again and again into the cycle itself.

Thus, having spoken of the doctrine of the cutting off of views, in dependence on which the heretics are not able to transcend, now, showing the doctrine of the cutting off of confidence, he says, "In such a doctrine and discipline as this, monks (evarūpe kho, bhikkhave, dhammavinaye)," and so on.

Or the teacher is a cat, and the disciples are chickens or mice. Then he eats them without compassion in just the way that has been said. "In such a Dharma and Vinaya, O monks,"

"The faith in the Dharma" indeed, in the teaching that does not lead out, the faith in the Dharma, by name, is the faith in the Dharma of the texts, merely [in] the texts that have been learned, recited, and kept in mind, there is no escape from the round [of rebirths] here. Therefore, the faith that is here, again and again only makes the round deep, thus it is said to be not well-gone and not substantial in the teaching.

"The perfection in virtue" indeed, that which is the perfection in virtue in the teaching that does not lead out by way of goat-virtue etc., since that does not bring about escape from the round [of rebirths] and release from existence, but bringing about rebirth in the animal womb, ripening in hell, therefore it is said to be not well-gone.

[It is] "not proclaimed by a Fully Awakened One," [that is,] not proclaimed by the All-Knowing Fully Awakened One. Tattha kiṃnidānātiādīsu nidānādīni sabbāneva kāraṇavevacanāni. Kāraṇañhi yasmā phalaṃ nideti handa, naṃ gaṇhathāti appeti viya, tasmā nidānanti vuccati. Yasmā taṃ tato jāyati samudeti pabhavati, tasmā samudayo, jāti, pabhavoti vuccati. Ayaṃ panettha padattho – kiṃ nidānaṃ etesanti kiṃnidānā. Ko samudayo etesanti kiṃsamudayā. Kā jāti etesanti kiṃjātikā. Ko pabhavo etesanti kiṃpabhavā. Yasmā pana tesaṃ taṇhā yathāvuttena atthena nidānañceva samudayo ca jāti ca pabhavo ca, tasmā ‘‘taṇhānidānā’’tiādimāha. Evaṃ sabbapadesu attho veditabbo. Yasmā pana bhagavā na kevalaṃ upādānasseva paccayaṃ jānāti, upādānassa paccayabhūtāya taṇhāyapi, taṇhādipaccayānaṃ vedanādīnampi paccayaṃ jānātiyeva, tasmā taṇhā cāyaṃ, bhikkhavetiādimāha. Yatoca khoti yasmiṃ kāle. Avijjā pahīnā hotīti vaṭṭamūlikā avijjā anuppādanirodhena pahīnā hoti. Vijjā uppannāti arahattamaggavijjā uppannā. So avijjāvirāgā vijjuppādāti. So bhikkhu avijjāya ca pahīnattā vijjāya ca uppannattā. Neva kāmupādānaṃ upādiyatīti neva kāmupādānaṃ gaṇhāti na upeti, na sesāni upādānāni. Anupādiyaṃ na paritassatīti evaṃ kiñci upādānaṃ aggaṇhanto taṇhāparitassanāya na paritassati. Aparitassanti aparitassanto taṇhaṃ anuppādento. Paccattaṃyeva parinibbāyatīti sayameva kilesaparinibbānena parinibbāyati. Evamassa āsavakkhayaṃ dassetvā idāni khīṇāsavassa bhikkhuno paccavekkhaṇaṃ dassento khīṇā jātītiādimāha. Taṃ vuttatthamevāti. 145. Now, to show the condition of those graspings about which the sectarians do not explain the correct understanding, but the Tathāgata explains, he said, "These, monks," etc.

There, in "What is the cause?" etc., all the words beginning with cause are synonyms for kāraṇa.

For, because a kāraṇa gives a fruit, as if saying, "Here, take it," it is called nidāna (cause).

Because that is born, arises, and originates from that, it is called samudaya (arising), jāti (birth), and pabhava (origin).

Here, the meaning of the words is this: "What is the cause of these?" is kiṃnidānā.

"What is the arising of these?" is kiṃsamudayā.

"What is the birth of these?" is kiṃjātikā.

"What is the origin of these?" is kiṃpabhavā.

But because for them, craving is the cause, the arising, the birth, and the origin, in the sense previously stated, therefore he said, "craving is the cause," etc.

In this way, the meaning should be understood in all the words.

But because the Blessed One knows not only the condition of grasping, but also the condition of craving, which is the condition for grasping, and he knows the condition of feeling, etc., which are the conditions for craving, etc., therefore he said, "This craving, monks," etc.

"When," that is, at what time.

"Ignorance is abandoned," that is, ignorance, the root of the round [of rebirth], is abandoned by the cessation of non-arising.

"Knowledge arises," that is, knowledge of the path of arhattva arises.

"He, through the fading away of ignorance and the arising of knowledge."

That monk, because of the abandoning of ignorance and because of the arising of knowledge.

"He does not grasp sensual grasping," that is, he does not grasp sensual grasping, he does not approach it, nor the remaining graspings.

"Not grasping, he is not agitated," thus, not grasping any grasping, he is not agitated by the agitation of craving.

"Not agitated," not agitating, not producing craving.

"He attains parinirvāṇa for himself," that is, he himself attains parinirvāṇa by the parinirvāṇa of defilements.

Having thus shown the destruction of the influxes for him, now, showing the reviewing of a monk whose influxes are destroyed, he said, "Destroyed is birth," etc.

That is just what has been said.

When ignorance is abandoned, knowledge arises, he, through the fading away of ignorance and the arising of knowledge, does not grasp sensual grasping, not grasping, he is not agitated, not agitated, he attains parinirvāṇa for himself, destroyed is birth.
