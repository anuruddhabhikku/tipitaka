17. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ āyasmā ānando vesāliyaṃ viharati beluvagāmake veḷuvagāmake (syā. kaṃ. ka.). Tena kho pana samayena dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro pāṭaliputtaṃ anuppatto hoti kenacideva karaṇīyena. Atha kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro yena kukkuṭārāmo yena aññataro bhikkhu tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā taṃ bhikkhuṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro taṃ bhikkhuṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kahaṃ nu kho, bhante, āyasmā ānando etarahi viharati? Dassanakāmā hi mayaṃ taṃ āyasmantaṃ ānanda’’nti. ‘‘Eso, gahapati, āyasmā ānando vesāliyaṃ viharati beluvagāmake’’ti. Atha kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro pāṭaliputte taṃ karaṇīyaṃ tīretvā yena vesālī yena beluvagāmako yenāyasmā ānando tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Venerable Ānanda was living at Beluvagāmaka near Vesālī.

Now on that occasion, the householder Dasama of Aṭṭhakanāgara had arrived at Pāṭaliputta for some business or other.

Then the householder Dasama of Aṭṭhakanāgara went to a certain bhikṣu in Kukkuta's Park; having gone there, he paid homage to that bhikṣu and sat down at one side.

Seated at one side, the householder Dasama of Aṭṭhakanāgara said to that bhikṣu: "Where, venerable sir, does the venerable Ānanda live now? For we desire to see the venerable Ānanda."

"The venerable Ānanda is living at Beluvagāmaka near Vesālī, householder."

Then the householder Dasama of Aṭṭhakanāgara, having completed that business at Pāṭaliputta, went to the venerable Ānanda at Beluvagāmaka near Vesālī; having gone there, he paid homage to the venerable Ānanda and sat down at one side.
17.Evaṃme sutanti aṭṭhakanāgarasuttaṃ. Tattha beluvagāmaketi vesāliyā dakkhiṇapasse avidūre beluvagāmako nāma atthi, taṃ gocaragāmaṃ katvāti attho. Dasamoti so hi jātigottavasena ceva sārappattakulagaṇanāya ca dasame ṭhāne gaṇīyati, tenassa dasamotveva nāmaṃ jātaṃ. Aṭṭhakanāgaroti aṭṭhakanagaravāsī. Kukkuṭārāmoti kukkuṭaseṭṭhinā kārito ārāmo.
17.
Thus, "I have heard" refers to the Aṭṭhakanāgarasutta.

There, "Beluvagāmaka" means that near Vesālī, not far on the southern side, there is a place called Beluvagāmaka, making it a pasture village.

"Dasamo" means since he is counted in the tenth place in the calculation of reaching the essence of the family lineage, therefore his name became Dasamotveva.

"Aṭṭhakanāgaro" means a resident of Aṭṭhakanagara.

"Kukkuṭārāmo" means a park caused to be made by Kukkuṭaseṭṭhi.

18. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘atthi nu kho, bhante ānanda, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo akkhāto yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttañceva cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā ca āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti , ananuppattañca anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇātī’’ti? ‘‘Atthi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo akkhāto yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttañceva cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā ca āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti, ananuppattañca anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇātī’’ti. ‘‘Katamo pana, bhante ānanda, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo akkhāto yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttañceva cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā ca āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti, ananuppattañca anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇātī’’ti?
After paying homage to him, the venerable Ānanda was thus addressed by Dasama Gahapati Aṭṭhakanāgara, who was sitting to one side: "Venerable Ānanda, has any one thing been proclaimed by the Blessed One (bhagavatā), who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened (sammāsambuddhena), wherein if a bhikkhu abides diligent (appamattassa), ardent (ātāpino), and resolute (pahitattassa), his unliberated mind (avimuttañceva cittaṃ) comes to be liberated (vimuccati), his undestroyed taints (āsavā) come to be destroyed (parikkhayaṃ gacchanti), and he attains the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ) that he had not attained before (ananuppattañca anupāpuṇāti)?"

"This is one thing proclaimed by the Blessed One (bhagavatā), who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened (sammāsambuddhena), wherein if a bhikkhu abides diligent (appamattassa), ardent (ātāpino), and resolute (pahitattassa), his unliberated mind (avimuttañceva cittaṃ) comes to be liberated (vimuccati), his undestroyed taints (āsavā) come to be destroyed (parikkhayaṃ gacchanti), and he attains the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ) that he had not attained before (ananuppattañca anupāpuṇāti)."

"But what is that one thing, venerable Ānanda, proclaimed by the Blessed One (bhagavatā), who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened (sammāsambuddhena), wherein if a bhikkhu abides diligent (appamattassa), ardent (ātāpino), and resolute (pahitattassa), his unliberated mind (avimuttañceva cittaṃ) comes to be liberated (vimuccati), his undestroyed taints (āsavā) come to be destroyed (parikkhayaṃ gacchanti), and he attains the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ) that he had not attained before (ananuppattañca anupāpuṇāti)?"
18.Tena bhagavatā…pe… akkhātoti ettha ayaṃ saṅkhepattho, yo so bhagavā samatiṃsa pāramiyo pūretvā sabbakilese bhañjitvā anuttaraṃ sammāsambodhiṃ abhisambuddho, tena bhagavatā, tesaṃ tesaṃ sattānaṃ āsayānusayaṃ jānatā, hatthatale ṭhapitaāmalakaṃ viya sabbaṃ ñeyyadhammaṃ passatā. Apica pubbenivāsādīhi jānatā, dibbena cakkhunā passatā, tīhi vijjāhi chahi vā pana abhiññāhi jānatā, sabbattha appaṭihatena samantacakkhunā passatā, sabbadhammajānanasamatthāya paññāya jānatā, sabbasattānaṃ cakkhuvisayātītāni tirokuṭṭādigatānipi rūpāni ativisuddhena maṃsacakkhunā passatā, attahitasādhikāya samādhipadaṭṭhānāya paṭivedhapaññāya jānatā, parahitasādhikāya karuṇāpadaṭṭhānāya desanāpaññāya passatā, arīnaṃ hatattā paccayādīnañca arahattā arahatā, sammā sāmañca saccānaṃ buddhattā sammāsambuddhena. Antarāyikadhamme vā jānatā, niyyānikadhamme passatā, kilesārīnaṃ hatattā arahatā, sāmaṃ sabbadhammānaṃ buddhattā sammāsambuddhenāti evaṃ catuvesārajjavasena catūhi kāraṇehi thomitena. Atthi nu kho eko dhammo akkhātoti.
18. "tena bhagavatā…pe… akkhāto"ti: Here is the summarized meaning: that Blessed One (bhagavān), having fulfilled the thirty perfections (pāramī), having shattered all defilements (kilesa), having awakened to unsurpassed perfect enlightenment (anuttaraṃ sammāsambodhiṃ abhisambuddho), that Blessed One, knowing the inclinations and latent tendencies (āsayānusayaṃ jānatā) of those beings, seeing all knowable things (ñeyyadhammaṃ passatā) as if an amalaka fruit placed on the palm of the hand;

moreover, knowing by means of recollection of former lives (pubbenivāsādīhi jānatā), seeing with the divine eye (dibbena cakkhunā passatā), knowing with the three knowledges (tīhi vijjāhi) or even the six higher knowledges (chahi abhiññāhi), seeing with the unobstructed all-around eye (appaṭihatena samantacakkhunā passatā), knowing with the wisdom capable of knowing all things (sabbadhammajānanasamatthāya paññāya jānatā), seeing even forms beyond the range of the eye of all beings, such as those behind walls (tirokuṭṭādigatānipi rūpāni), with the exceedingly pure fleshly eye (ativisuddhena maṃsacakkhunā passatā), knowing with the wisdom of penetration (paṭivedhapaññāya jānatā) based on concentration (samādhipadaṭṭhānāya) for the accomplishment of one's own benefit (attahitasādhikāya), seeing with the wisdom of teaching (desanāpaññāya passatā) based on compassion (karuṇāpadaṭṭhānāya) for the accomplishment of the benefit of others (parahitasādhikāya), being an Arhat (arahatā), because of having slain the enemies (arīnaṃ hatattā) and being worthy of offerings (paccayādīnañca arahattā), by the Sammāsambuddha (sammāsambuddhena), because of having rightly awakened to all the truths (sammā sāmañca saccānaṃ buddhattā);

knowing things that cause obstruction (antarāyikadhamme vā jānatā), seeing things that lead to deliverance (niyyānikadhamme passatā), being an Arhat (arahatā), because of having slain the enemies of defilements (kilesārīnaṃ hatattā), by the Sammāsambuddha (sammāsambuddhena), because of having awakened to all things (sabbadhammānaṃ buddhattā) by himself – thus, by him who is praised with these four reasons in terms of the four kinds of self-confidence (catuvesārajjavasena catūhi kāraṇehi thomitena).

"Is there one thing that has been declared (atthi nu kho eko dhammo akkhāto)?"

19. ‘‘Idha, gahapati, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘idampi paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ. Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti. No ce āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā. Ayampi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo akkhāto yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttañceva cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā ca āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti, ananuppattañca anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti.
19. Here, householder, quite secluded from sensual pleasures ( kāmehi), secluded from unwholesome states ( akusalehi dhammehi), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought ( savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ), with rapture and pleasure ( pītisukhaṃ) born of seclusion ( vivekajaṃ).

He considers this and understands it thus: This first jhāna is conditioned ( abhisaṅkhataṃ) and volitionally produced ( abhisañcetayitaṃ).

But whatever is conditioned ( abhisaṅkhataṃ) and volitionally produced ( abhisañcetayitaṃ) is impermanent ( aniccaṃ), subject to cessation ( nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti).

Standing upon that, he attains the destruction of the taints ( āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti).

But if he does not attain the destruction of the taints ( āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti), then because of that desire for the Dhamma (dhammarāgena), that delight in the Dhamma (dhammanandiyā), with the destruction of the five lower fetters ( pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā), he becomes one due to reappear spontaneously ( opapātiko hoti) in the Pure Abodes and there attain final Nibbāna without ever returning from that world.

“This is one thing proclaimed by the Blessed One ( bhagavatā ) who knows ( jānatā ) and sees ( passatā), accomplished ( arahatā) and fully enlightened ( sammāsambuddhena), wherein if a bhikkhu abides diligent ( appamattassa), ardent ( ātāpino), and resolute ( pahitattassa), his unliberated mind comes to be liberated, his undestroyed taints come to be destroyed, and he attains the supreme security from bondage ( anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ) that he had not attained before.
19.Abhisaṅkhatanti kataṃ uppāditaṃ. Abhisañcetayitanti cetayitaṃ pakappitaṃ. So tattha ṭhitoti so tasmiṃ samathavipassanādhamme ṭhito. Dhammarāgena dhammanandiyāti padadvayehi samathavipassanāsu chandarāgo vutto. Samathavipassanāsu hi sabbena sabbaṃ chandarāgaṃ pariyādiyituṃ sakkonto arahā hoti, asakkonto anāgāmī hoti. So samathavipassanāsu chandarāgassa appahīnattā catutthajjhānacetanāya suddhāvāse nibbattati, ayaṃ ācariyānaṃ samānakathā. Vitaṇḍavādī panāha ‘‘teneva dhammarāgenāti vacanato akusalena suddhāvāse nibbattatī’’ti so ‘‘suttaṃ āharā’’ti vattabbo, addhā aññaṃ apassanto idameva āharissati, tato vattabbo ‘‘kiṃ panidaṃ suttaṃ neyyatthaṃ nītattha’’nti, addhā nītatthanti vakkhati. Tato vattabbo – evaṃ sante anāgāmiphalatthikena samathavipassanāsu chandarāgo kattabbo bhavissati, chandarāge uppādite anāgāmiphalaṃ paṭividdhaṃ bhavissati ‘‘mā suttaṃ me laddha’’nti yaṃ vā taṃ vā dīpehi. Pañhaṃ kathentena hi ācariyassa santike uggahetvā attharasaṃ paṭivijjhitvā kathetuṃ vaṭṭati, akusalena hi sagge, kusalena vā apāye paṭisandhi nāma natthi. Vuttañhetaṃ bhagavatā – ‘‘Na, bhikkhave, lobhajena kammena dosajena kammena mohajena kammena devā paññāyanti, manussā paññāyanti, yā vā panaññāpi kāci sugatiyo, atha kho, bhikkhave, lobhajena kammena dosajena kammena mohajena kammena nirayo paññāyati, tiracchānayoni paññāyati, pettivisayo paññāyati, yā vā panaññāpi kāci duggatiyo’’ti – Evaṃ paññāpetabbo. Sace sañjānāti sañjānātu, no ce sañjānāti, ‘‘gaccha pātova vihāraṃ pavisitvā yāguṃ pivāhī’’ti uyyojetabbo. Yathā ca pana imasmiṃ sutte, evaṃ mahāmālukyovādepi mahāsatipaṭṭhānepi kāyagatāsatisuttepi samathavipassanā kathitā. Tattha imasmiṃ sutte samathavasena gacchatopi vipassanāvasena gacchatopi samathadhurameva dhuraṃ, mahāmālukyovāde vipassanāva dhuraṃ, mahāsatipaṭṭhānaṃ pana vipassanuttaraṃ nāma kathitaṃ, kāyagatāsatisuttaṃ samathuttaranti. Ayaṃ kho gahapati…pe… ekadhammo akkhātoti ekadhammaṃ pucchitena ayampi ekadhammoti evaṃ pucchāvasena kathitattā ekādasapi dhammā ekadhammo nāma jāto. Mahāsakuludāyisuttasmiñhi ekūnavīsati pabbāni paṭipadāvasena ekadhammo nāma jātāni, idha ekādasapucchāvasena ekadhammoti āgatāni. Amatuppattiyatthena vā sabbānipi ekadhammoti vattuṃ vaṭṭati. abhisaṅkhata means done, produced.
abhisañcetayita means intended, purposed.
so tattha ṭhito means he is established in that samatha-vipassanā-dhamma.
dhammarāgena dhammanandiyā means, through these two terms, desire and passion for samatha-vipassanā is stated.
In samatha-vipassanā, because one is able to completely consume all desire and passion, he is an araha; if not able, he is an anāgāmī.
He, in samatha-vipassanā, because of not abandoning desire and passion, is reborn in suddhāvāsa by the consciousness of the fourth jhāna; this is a consistent teaching of the teachers.
But the debater says, "Because of the statement 'through that very desire and passion', he is reborn in suddhāvāsa through unwholesome [desire and passion]". He should be told, "Bring forth the sutta." Surely, not seeing another, he will bring forth this very one. Then he should be told, "Is this sutta of interpretable meaning (neyyattha) or of definitive meaning (nītattha)?" Surely, he will say it is of definitive meaning.
Then he should be told: "Thus being the case, one who desires the fruit of non-returning (anāgāmiphala) will have to create desire and passion in samatha-vipassanā. With desire and passion created, the fruit of non-returning (anāgāmiphala) will be attained." Display whatever sutta you have obtained for me.
Indeed, one speaking a question, having learned it in the presence of a teacher, having penetrated the flavor of the meaning, is worthy to speak. Indeed, through unwholesome [action], there is no rebirth in heaven (sagga), or through wholesome [action], in hell (apāya).
Thus, it was said by the Blessed One (bhagavatā)— "Monks (bhikkhave), through action born of greed (lobhajena kammena), action born of hatred (dosajena kammena), action born of delusion (mohajena kammena), gods (devā) are not discerned, humans (manussā) are not discerned, nor are any good destinations (sugatiyo). But, monks, through action born of greed (lobhajena kammena), action born of hatred (dosajena kammena), action born of delusion (mohajena kammena), hell (nirayo) is discerned, the animal realm (tiracchānayoni) is discerned, the realm of ghosts (pettivisayo) is discerned, nor are any bad destinations (duggatiyo)." Thus should it be made known.
If he understands, let him understand; if he does not understand, he should be encouraged, "Go early in the morning, enter the monastery (vihāraṃ), and drink rice gruel (yāguṃ)!"
And just as in this sutta, so also in the Mahāmālukya discourse (mahāmālukyovāde), in the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna (mahāsatipaṭṭhāne), and in the Kāyagatāsati sutta, samatha-vipassanā is spoken of.
There, in this sutta, whether going by way of samatha or going by way of vipassanā, the yoke is indeed samatha; in the Mahāmālukya discourse, the yoke is indeed vipassanā; the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna, however, is said to be vipassanā-uttara, the Kāyagatāsati sutta is samatha-uttara.
ayaṃ kho gahapati…pe… ekadhammo akkhāto means that, by one asking about a single dhamma (ekadhammaṃ), this too is a single dhamma (ekadhammoti). Thus, because it is spoken of from the perspective of a question (pucchāvasena kathitattā), eleven dhammas become known as a single dhamma.
Indeed, in the Mahāsakuludāyi sutta, nineteen sections (pabbāni) become known as a single dhamma by way of practice (paṭipadāvasena); here, eleven questions become known as a single dhamma by way of a question (ekādasapucchāvasena).
Or, by the meaning of the attainment of the Deathless (amatuppattiyatthena), it is fitting to say that all are a single dhamma (ekadhammoti vattuṃ vaṭṭati).

20. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ…pe… dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘idampi kho dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ… anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu pītiyā ca virāgā…pe… tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘idampi kho tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ…pe… anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu sukhassa ca pahānā …pe… catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘idampi kho catutthaṃ jhānaṃ abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ… anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ, tathā tatiyaṃ, tathā catutthaṃ catutthiṃ (sī. pī.). Iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ upekkhāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ayampi kho upekkhācetovimutti abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā. Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito… anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu sabbaso rūpasaññānaṃ samatikkamā paṭighasaññānaṃ atthaṅgamā nānattasaññānaṃ amanasikārā ‘ananto ākāso’ti ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ayampi kho ākāsānañcāyatanasamāpatti abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā. Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito…pe… anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma ‘anantaṃ viññāṇa’nti viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ayampi kho viññāṇañcāyatanasamāpatti abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā. Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito…pe… anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu sabbaso viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma ‘natthi kiñcī’ti ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ayampi kho ākiñcaññāyatanasamāpatti abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā. Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti. No ce āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā. Ayampi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo akkhāto yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttañceva cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā ca āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti, ananuppattañca anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇātī’’ti.
“Again, householder, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, a bhikkhu enters and abides in the second jhāna…He considers this and understands it thus: ‘This second jhāna is conditioned and volitionally produced…[and does not lead to] the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti).

“Again, householder, with the fading away as well of rapture…a bhikkhu enters and abides in the third jhāna. He considers this and understands it thus: ‘This third jhāna is conditioned and volitionally produced…[and does not lead to] the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti).

“Again, householder, with the abandoning of pleasure… a bhikkhu enters and abides in the fourth jhāna. He considers this and understands it thus: ‘This fourth jhāna is conditioned and volitionally produced…[and does not lead to] the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti).

“Again, householder, a bhikkhu abides pervading one quarter with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettāsahagatena cetasā), likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth; so above, below, around, and everywhere, and to all as to himself, he abides pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettāsahagatena cetasā), abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility (averena), and without ill will (abyābajjhena). He considers this and understands it thus: ‘This deliverance of mind through loving-kindness (mettācetovimutti) is conditioned and volitionally produced. But whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent, subject to cessation.’ Standing upon that…[he does not attain] the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti).

“Again, householder, a bhikkhu abides pervading one quarter with a mind imbued with compassion (karuṇāsahagatena cetasā)…with a mind imbued with appreciative joy (muditāsahagatena cetasā)…with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhāsahagatena cetasā), likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth; so above, below, around, and everywhere, and to all as to himself, he abides pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhāsahagatena cetasā), abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility (averena), and without ill will (abyābajjhena). He considers this and understands it thus: ‘This deliverance of mind through equanimity (upekkhācetovimutti) is conditioned and volitionally produced. But whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent, subject to cessation.’
Standing upon that, he attains the unsurpassed security from bondage ( anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti).

Furthermore, householder, a bhikkhu, with the complete surmounting of perceptions of form (rūpasaññānaṃ samatikkamā), with the disappearance of perceptions of sensory impact (paṭighasaññānaṃ atthaṅgamā), with non-attention to perceptions of diversity (nānattasaññānaṃ amanasikārā), aware that ‘space is infinite’ (ananto ākāso), enters upon and abides in the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati).

He considers this and understands it thus: ‘This attainment of the base of infinite space is conditioned and volitionally produced (abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā).

But whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced (abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ) is impermanent, subject to cessation (aniccaṃ nirodhadhamma).’

Standing upon that, he attains the unsurpassed security from bondage.

Furthermore, householder, a bhikkhu, with the complete surmounting of the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma), [thinking,] “Infinite consciousness” (anantaṃ viññāṇa), enters upon and abides in the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati).

He considers this and understands it thus: “This attainment of the base of infinite consciousness is conditioned and volitionally produced (abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā).

But whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced (abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ) is impermanent, subject to cessation (aniccaṃ nirodhadhamma).’

Standing upon that, he attains the unsurpassed security from bondage.

Furthermore, householder, a bhikkhu, with the complete surmounting of the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma), [thinking,] “There is nothing” (natthi kiñcī), enters upon and abides in the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati).

He considers this and understands it thus: “This attainment of the base of nothingness is conditioned and volitionally produced (abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā).

But whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced (abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ) is impermanent, subject to cessation (aniccaṃ nirodhadhamma).’

Standing upon that, he attains the destruction of the taints (āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti).

But if he does not attain the destruction of the taints, then because of that desire for the Dhamma, that delight in the Dhamma, with the destruction of the five lower fetters (pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā) he becomes one due to reappear spontaneously (opapātiko hoti) in the Pure Abodes and there attain final Nibbāna (parinibbāyī) without ever returning from that world (anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā).

“This, householder, is one thing proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened (arahatā sammāsambuddhena), wherein if a bhikkhu abides diligent, ardent, and resolute (appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato), his unliberated mind comes to be liberated (avimuttañceva cittaṃ vimuccati), his undestroyed taints come to be destroyed (aparikkhīṇā ca āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti), and he attains the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti) that he had not attained before.

21. Evaṃ vutte, dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘seyyathāpi, bhante ānanda, puriso ekaṃva nidhimukhaṃ gavesanto sakideva ekādasa nidhimukhāni adhigaccheyya; evameva kho ahaṃ, bhante, ekaṃ amatadvāraṃ gavesanto sakideva sakiṃ deva (ka.) ekādasa amatadvārāni alatthaṃ bhāvanāya. Seyyathāpi, bhante, purisassa agāraṃ ekādasadvāraṃ, so tasmiṃ agāre āditte ekamekenapi dvārena sakkuṇeyya attānaṃ sotthiṃ kātuṃ; evameva kho ahaṃ, bhante, imesaṃ ekādasannaṃ amatadvārānaṃ ekamekenapi amatadvārena sakkuṇissāmi attānaṃ sotthiṃ kātuṃ. Imehi nāma, bhante, aññatitthiyā ācariyassa ācariyadhanaṃ pariyesissanti, kimaṅgaṃ kiṃ (sī. pī.) panāhaṃ āyasmato ānandassa pūjaṃ na karissāmī’’ti ! Atha kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro pāṭaliputtakañca vesālikañca bhikkhusaṅghaṃ sannipātetvā paṇītena khādanīyena bhojanīyena sahatthā santappesi sampavāresi, ekamekañca bhikkhuṃ paccekaṃ dussayugena acchādesi, āyasmantañca ānandaṃ ticīvarena acchādesi, āyasmato ca ānandassa pañcasatavihāraṃ kārāpesīti. Aṭṭhakanāgarasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ dutiyaṃ.
21. When this had been said, the householder Dasama of Āṭṭhakanāgara said to the venerable Ānanda: “Just as, venerable Ānanda, if a man seeking a single entrance to a treasure should happen to find eleven entrances to the treasure, so too, venerable sir, while I was seeking one door to the deathless ( amṛtadvāra), I have attained eleven doors to the deathless through cultivation ( bhāvanā). Just as, venerable sir, if a man had a house with eleven doors, and when that house was on fire, he would be able to save himself by any one of the doors; so too, venerable sir, I shall be able to save myself by any one of these eleven doors to the deathless. Surely, venerable sir, other sectarians seek a teacher’s fee for their teachers; why then should I not make an offering (pūjā) to the venerable Ānanda!”

Then the householder Dasama of Āṭṭhakanāgara assembled the saṃgha of bhikkhus from Pāṭaliputta and Vesālī, and with his own hands satisfied and served them with delicious food both hard and soft. He gave each bhikkhu a pair of cloths, and he gave the venerable Ānanda a triple robe (ticīvara), and he had a dwelling worth five hundred (coins) built for the venerable Ānanda.

The Āṭṭhakanāgarasutta, the second (sutta), is finished.
21.Nidhimukhaṃ gavesantoti nidhiṃ pariyesanto. Sakidevāti ekapayogena. Kathaṃ pana ekapayogeneva ekādasannaṃ nidhīnaṃ adhigamo hotīti. Idhekacco araññe nidhiṃ gavesamāno carati, tamenaṃ aññataro atthacarako disvā ‘‘kiṃ bho carasī’’ti pucchati. So ‘‘jīvitavuttiṃ pariyesāmī’’ti āha. Itaro ‘‘tena hi samma āgaccha, etaṃ pāsāṇaṃ pavattehī’’ti āha. So taṃ pavattetvā uparūpari ṭhapitā vā kucchiyā kucchiṃ āhacca ṭhitā vā ekādasa kumbhiyo passeyya, evaṃ ekapayogena ekādasannaṃ nidhīnaṃ adhigamo hoti. Ācariyadhanaṃ pariyesissantīti aññatitthiyā hi yassa santike sippaṃ uggaṇhanti, tassa sippuggahaṇato pure vā pacchā vā antarantare vā gehato nīharitvā dhanaṃ denti. Yesaṃ gehe natthi, te ñātisabhāgato pariyesanti, tathā alabhamānā bhikkhampi caritvā dentiyeva. Taṃ sandhāyetaṃ vuttaṃ. Ācariyadhanaṃ pariyesissantī Kimaṅgaṃ panāhanti bāhirakā tāva aniyyānikepi sāsane sippamattadāyakassa dhanaṃ pariyesanti; ahaṃ pana evaṃvidhe niyyānikasāsane ekādasavidhaṃ amatuppattipaṭipadaṃ desentassa ācariyassa pūjaṃ kiṃ na karissāmi, karissāmiyevāti vadati. Paccekadussayugenaacchādesīti ekamekassa bhikkhuno ekekaṃ dussayugamadāsīti attho. Samudācāravacanaṃ panettha evarūpaṃ hoti, tasmā acchādesīti vuttaṃ. Pañcasatavihāranti pañcasatagghanikaṃ paṇṇasālaṃ kāresīti attho. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Kimaṅgaṃ panāha Paccekadussayugena acchādesī Pañcasatavihāra Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Aṭṭhakanāgarasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
21. "Searching for the entrance to the treasure" means searching for treasure.
"Just once" means with a single effort.
How then does the attainment of eleven treasures occur with just a single effort?
Here, a certain man wanders in the forest searching for treasure. Another man who is seeking his own gain sees him and asks, “What are you doing, sir?”
He says, "I am seeking a livelihood."
The other says, "Then come here, friend, roll this stone."
Having rolled it, if he should see eleven pots placed one on top of the other, or standing touching each other, in this way the attainment of eleven treasures occurs with a single effort.

"They will seek teacher's fee" means that other sectarians, when they learn a craft from someone, give wealth after taking it out of their house, either before or after or in between.
Those who do not have [wealth] in their house seek [it] from their relatives. If they do not obtain [it] in that way, they even go begging and give [it].
This was said with reference to that.

"What need is there to say 'kimaṅgaṃ panāha'?" Even outsiders seek wealth for a teacher who gives only a small craft in a doctrine that does not lead out [of suffering]. But I, in such a doctrine that leads out [of suffering], why would I not do honor to a teacher who teaches the practice of attaining the undying in eleven ways? Surely I will do [it]!" he says.
"With a pair of cloths for each" means that he gave a pair of cloths to each and every monk; this is the meaning.
The customary usage here is of such a form; therefore it is said "he gave a pair of cloths."
"Five hundred monasteries" means that he had a leaf hut built that was worth five hundred.
The rest is clear throughout.

What need is there to say 'kimaṅgaṃ panāha'? With a pair of cloths for each. He gave a pair of cloths. Five hundred monasteries. The commentary on the Aṭṭhakanāgara Sutta in the Papañcasūdanī, commentary to the Middle Length Discourses, is finished.
