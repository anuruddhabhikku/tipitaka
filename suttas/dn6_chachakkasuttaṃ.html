<p n="420" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">420</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0509"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0337"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘dhammaṃ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāsessāmi, yadidaṃ – cha chakkāni. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Cha ajjhattikāni āyatanāni veditabbāni, cha bāhirāni āyatanāni veditabbāni, cha viññāṇakāyā veditabbā, cha phassakāyā veditabbā, cha vedanākāyā veditabbā, cha taṇhākāyā veditabbā.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying near Sāvatthi (Śrāvastī) in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's Park (ārāma).

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: "Monks!"

"Venerable sir (bhadante)," those monks replied to the Fortunate One.

The Blessed One said this: "Monks (bhikkhave), I will teach you the Dhamma (dharma) admirable in the beginning (ādikalyāṇaṃ), admirable in the middle (majjhekalyāṇaṃ), admirable in the end (pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ), with its meaning (sātthaṃ) and with its expression (sabyañjanaṃ), entirely complete (kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ), surpassingly pure (parisuddhaṃ): the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ). In other words, the six sextets (cha chakkāni).

Listen to that (taṃ suṇātha) and attend closely (sādhukaṃ manasi karotha); I will speak (bhāsissāmītti)."

"As you say, lord (evaṃ bhante)," those monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

"The six internal sense bases (cha ajjhattikāni āyatanāni) should be known. The six external sense bases (cha bāhirāni āyatanāni) should be known. The six aggregates of consciousness (cha viññāṇakāyā) should be known. The six aggregates of contact (cha phassakāyā) should be known. The six aggregates of feeling (cha vedanākāyā) should be known. The six aggregates of craving (cha taṇhākāyā) should be known."

421. “‘The six internal sense-spheres should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to what was it said? The eye sense-sphere (cakṣāyatana), the ear sense-sphere (sotāyatana), the nose sense-sphere (ghānāyatana), the tongue sense-sphere (jivhāyatana), the body sense-sphere (kāyāyatana), the mind sense-sphere (manāyatana). ‘The six internal sense-spheres should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said. This is the first sextet.

‘‘‘The six external sense-spheres should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to what was it said? The form sense-sphere (rūpāyatana), the sound sense-sphere (saddāyatana), the smell sense-sphere (gandhāyatana), the taste sense-sphere (rasāyatana), the touch sense-sphere (phoṭṭhabbāyatana), the mental object sense-sphere (dhammāyatana). ‘The six external sense-spheres should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said. This is the second sextet.

‘‘‘The six aggregates of consciousness (vijñāṇakāyā) should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to what was it said? Dependent on the eye (cakṣu) and forms (rūpe), eye-consciousness (cakṣuviññāṇa) arises; dependent on the ear (sota) and sounds (sadde), ear-consciousness (sotaviññāṇa) arises; dependent on the nose (ghāna) and smells (gandhe), nose-consciousness (ghānaviññāṇa) arises; dependent on the tongue (jivha) and tastes (rase), tongue-consciousness (jivhāviññāṇa) arises; dependent on the body (kāya) and tactile objects (phoṭṭhabbe), body-consciousness (kāyaviññāṇa) arises; dependent on the mind (mana) and mental objects (dhamme), mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇa) arises. ‘The six aggregates of consciousness should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said. This is the third sextet.

‘‘‘The six aggregates of contact (phassakāyā) should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to what was it said? Dependent on the eye and forms, eye-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact (phasso); dependent on the ear and sounds, ear-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; dependent on the nose and smells, nose-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; dependent on the tongue and tastes, tongue-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; dependent on the body and tactile objects, body-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; dependent on the mind and mental objects, mind-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact. ‘The six aggregates of contact should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said. This is the fourth sextet.

‘‘‘The six aggregates of feeling (vedanākāyā) should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to what was it said? Dependent on the eye and forms, eye-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition, feeling (vedanā) arises; dependent on the ear and sounds, ear-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition, feeling arises; dependent on the nose and smells, nose-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition, feeling arises; dependent on the tongue and tastes, tongue-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition, feeling arises; dependent on the body and tactile objects, body-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition, feeling arises; dependent on the mind and mental objects, mind-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition, feeling arises. ‘The six aggregates of feeling should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said. This is the fifth sextet.

‘‘‘The six aggregates of craving (taṇhākāyā) should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to what was it said? Dependent on the eye and forms, eye-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition, feeling arises; with feeling as condition, craving (taṇhā) arises; dependent on the ear and sounds, ear-consciousness arises…pe… dependent on the nose and smells, nose-consciousness arises… dependent on the tongue and tastes, tongue-consciousness arises… dependent on the body and tactile objects, body-consciousness arises… dependent on the mind and mental objects, mind-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition, feeling arises; with feeling as condition, craving arises. ‘The six aggregates of craving should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said.
This is the sixth sextet.

422. “If anyone were to say, ‘The eye (cakṣu) is the self (ātman),’ that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of the eye (cakṣus) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (ātman) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘The eye (cakṣu) is the self (ātman)’ So the eye (cakṣu) is not-self (anātman).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Forms (rūpa) are the self (ātman),’ that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of forms (rūpa) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (ātman) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Forms (rūpa) are the self (ātman)’ Thus the eye (cakṣu) is not-self (anātman) and forms (rūpa) are not-self (anātman).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Consciousness at the eye (cakṣurvijñāna) is the self (ātman),’ that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of consciousness at the eye (cakṣurvijñāna) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (ātman) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Consciousness at the eye (cakṣurvijñāna) is the self (ātman).’ Thus the eye (cakṣu) is not-self (anātman), forms (rūpa) are not-self (anātman), consciousness at the eye (cakṣurvijñāna) is not-self (anātman).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Contact at the eye (cakṣu-samsparśa) is the self (ātman),’ that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of contact at the eye (cakṣu-samsparśa) are discerned.
And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (ātman) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Contact at the eye (cakṣu-samsparśa) is the self (ātman)’ Thus the eye (cakṣu) is not-self (anātman), forms (rūpa) are not-self (anātman), consciousness at the eye (cakṣurvijñāna) is not-self (anātman), contact at the eye (cakṣu-samsparśa) is not-self (anātman).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Feeling (vedanā) is the self (ātman),’ that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of feeling (vedanā) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (ātman) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Feeling (vedanā) is the self (ātman)’ Thus the eye (cakṣu) is not-self (anātman), forms (rūpa) are not-self (anātman), consciousness at the eye (cakṣurvijñāna) is not-self (anātman), contact at the eye (cakṣu-samsparśa) is not-self (anātman), feeling (vedanā) is not-self (anātman).

423. “If anyone were to say, ‘The ear is the self’…and so on… ‘The nose is the self’… ‘The tongue is the self’… ‘The body is the self’… ‘The intellect is the self,’ that wouldn’t be tenable.
The arising and falling away of the intellect are discerned.
And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self arises and falls away.’

Disenchanted, he becomes dispassionate ( nibbindaṃ virajjati ); through dispassion, he is liberated ( virāgā vimuccati ). In the liberated one, there is knowledge that he is liberated. He understands: ‘Destroyed is birth, the spiritual life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming back to any state of being.’"

The Blessed One said this. Gratified, those monks delighted in the Blessed One’s words ( attamana te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti ). And while this explanation was being given, the minds of sixty monks, through non-clinging, were fully released from defilements.

The Sixth Sixes Discourse is finished, the sixth.
