Atha kho anāthapiṇḍiko gahapati aññataraṃ purisaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘ehi tvaṃ, ambho purisa, yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkama; upasaṅkamitvā mama vacanena bhagavato pāde sirasā vandāhi vandāhi evañca vadehi (sabbattha) aññasuttesu pana natthi – ‘anāthapiṇḍiko, bhante, gahapati ābādhiko dukkhito bāḷhagilāno. 383. Thus have I heard: At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthī, in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

At that time, Anāthapiṇḍika the householder was ill, suffering, and seriously sick.

Then Anāthapiṇḍika the householder addressed a certain man: "Come, good man, go to the Blessed One; having gone there, pay homage to the Blessed One’s feet with your head in my name and say — ‘Venerable sir, Anāthapiṇḍika the householder is ill, suffering, and seriously sick.

He pays homage to the Blessed One’s feet with his head.’

Then go to the venerable Sāriputta; having gone there, pay homage to the venerable Sāriputta’s feet with your head in my name and say — ‘Venerable sir, Anāthapiṇḍika the householder is ill, suffering, and seriously sick.

He pays homage to the venerable Sāriputta’s feet with his head.’

And speak thus: ‘Venerable sir, it would be good if the venerable Sāriputta would visit Anāthapiṇḍika’s house out of compassion.’"

"So be it, venerable sir," that man replied to Anāthapiṇḍika the householder. Then he went to the Blessed One; having gone there, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down on one side.

Sitting on one side, that man said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, Anāthapiṇḍika the householder is ill, suffering, and seriously sick.

He pays homage to the Blessed One’s feet with his head."

Then he went to the venerable Sāriputta; having gone there, he paid homage to the venerable Sāriputta and sat down on one side.

Sitting on one side, that man said to the venerable Sāriputta: "Venerable sir, Anāthapiṇḍika the householder is ill, suffering, and seriously sick.

He pays homage to the venerable Sāriputta’s feet with his head; and he speaks thus: ‘Venerable sir, it would be good if the venerable Sāriputta would visit Anāthapiṇḍika’s house out of compassion.’"

The venerable Sāriputta consented in silence.
383.Evaṃme sutanti anāthapiṇḍikovādasuttaṃ. Tattha bāḷhagilānoti adhimattagilāno maraṇaseyyaṃ upagato. Āmantesīti gahapatissa kira yāva pādā vahiṃsu, tāva divase sakiṃ vā dvikkhattuṃ vā tikkhattuṃ vā buddhupaṭṭhānaṃ akhaṇḍaṃ akāsi. Yattakañcassa satthu upaṭṭhānaṃ ahosi, tattakaṃyeva mahātherānaṃ. So ajja gamanapādassa pacchinnattā anuṭṭhānaseyyaṃ upagato sāsanaṃ pesetukāmo aññataraṃ purisaṃ āmantesi. Tenupasaṅkamīti bhagavantaṃ āpucchitvā sūriyatthaṅgamanavelāya upasaṅkami.
Thus have I heard: the Anāthapiṇḍikovāda-sutta.

There, bāḷhagilāno means extremely ill, having approached the deathbed.

Āmantesī: It seems that as long as the householder's feet were working, he uninterruptedly attended upon the Buddha once, twice, or three times a day. As much as he attended upon the Teacher, so much he attended upon the great elders. Today, with his ability to walk cut off, having approached a bed where he cannot get up, wishing to send a message, he addressed a certain man.

Tenupasaṅkamī: Having taken leave of the Blessed One, he approached at sunset.

Then the venerable Sāriputta, having dressed, taken his bowl and robe, went to the dwelling of Anāthapindika, the householder, with the venerable Ānanda as his attendant monk; having arrived, he sat down on the prepared seat.

Having sat down, the venerable Sāriputta said to Anāthapindika, the householder: "I hope, householder, you are comfortable; I hope you are well. I hope your painful feelings are subsiding and not increasing; that their subsiding is apparent, and not their increasing?"

384.Paṭikkamantīti osakkanti, tanukā bhavanti. Abhikkamantīti abhivaḍḍhanti ottharanti, balavatiyo honti. Abhikkamosānaṃ paññāyati no paṭikkamoti yasmiñhi samaye māraṇantikā vedanā uppajjati, uparivāte jalitaggi viya hoti, yāva usmā na pariyādiyati, tāva mahatāpi upakkamena na sakkā vūpasametuṃ, usmāya pana pariyādinnāya vūpasammati. Abhikkamosānaṃ paññāyati no paṭikkamo
Paṭikkamantī means recede, become thin. Abhikkamantī means increase, spread, become strong.

Abhikkamosānaṃ paññāyati no paṭikkamo means when a deadly pain arises, it is like a fire burning in the wind; until the heat is exhausted, it cannot be calmed even with great effort, but when the heat is exhausted, it subsides.

385. Athāyasmā sāriputto cintesi – ‘‘ayaṃ mahāseṭṭhissa vedanā māraṇantikā, na sakkā paṭibāhituṃ, avasesā kathā niratthakā, dhammakathamassa kathessāmī’’ti. Atha naṃ taṃ kathento tasmātihātiādimāha. Tattha tasmāti yasmā cakkhuṃ tīhi gāhehi gaṇhanto uppannaṃ māraṇantikaṃ vedanaṃ paṭibāhituṃ samattho nāma natthi, tasmā. Na cakkhuṃ upādiyissāmīti cakkhuṃ tīhi gāhehi na gaṇhissāmi. Na ca me cakkhunissitaṃ viññāṇanti viññāṇañcāpi me cakkhunissitaṃ na bhavissati. Na rūpanti heṭṭhā āyatanarūpaṃ kathitaṃ, imasmiṃ ṭhāne sabbampi kāmabhavarūpaṃ kathento evamāha.
Then venerable Śāriputta thought: "This great merchant's feeling is deadly, it is not possible to ward it off. The remaining talk is meaningless. I will speak the dharma-talk to him."

Then, while telling that to him, he spoke beginning with "tasmātihā".

There, "tasmā": Because while seizing the eye with three grasps, there is no one who is able to ward off the deadly feeling that has arisen, therefore.

"Na cakkhuṃ upādiyissāmī": I will not take up the eye. I will not seize the eye with three grasps.

"Na ca me cakkhunissitaṃ viññāṇa": And there will not be consciousness dependent on the eye for me.

"Na rūpa": Form. Below, the sense-sphere form has been spoken of. In this place, while speaking of all form of the world of desire and existence, he said thus.

387. "Because of being attached," [means:] seeing one’s own prosperity, you are bound and cling to sense objects.

Thus spoke the venerable Ānanda: “This householder, though faithful and devoted, is afraid of death. Who else would not be afraid?” Thinking this, he gave him firm encouragement and spoke as follows.

"Never have I heard such a dharma talk!" This lay follower [says:] "Never have I heard such a dharma talk even from the Teacher himself!" Why doesn't the Teacher give such a subtle, profound talk?" No, he does give it.

Thus, these six internal sense bases, six external ones, six aggregates of consciousness, six aggregates of contact, six aggregates of feeling, six elements, five aggregates, four immaterial [spheres], this world and the next, having shown [all these] and having placed [them] in the state of arhat through insight, learning, experience and knowledge, the words spoken by this [Ānanda] have never been heard before. Therefore, he speaks thus.

Moreover, this lay follower, being intent on giving and delighting in giving, when going to the presence of the Buddhas, never went empty-handed.

When going for the forenoon meal, he would have rice gruel, cakes, and so on, brought along; and for the afternoon meal, ghee, honey, molasses, and so on.

If those were not available, he would have sand brought and spread around the monastery's perfumed chamber, and having given dāna, kept the precepts, and gone home.

It seems this lay follower was destined to become a bodhisattva. Therefore, the Blessed One for twenty-four years mainly talked to the lay follower only about dāna, saying, “Lay follower, this giving is called the path gone by the bodhisattvas, the path gone by me as well. I have given dāna for four incalculable asaṃkhyeyyas, plus hundreds of thousands of kappas. You are indeed following the same path gone by me.”

Dharma-senapati and other great disciples also, when they came to his presence, would talk to him only about dāna.

Therefore he said: "It is not that such a dharma talk occurs to householders wearing white clothes."

This means: Householders have intense attachment and clinging to fields, property, gold, silver, male and female servants, sons, wives, and so on. For them, talk about, "Attachment should not be made here, clinging should not be made," neither occurs nor pleases them.

Why did he approach the Blessed One, [as in the phrase,] "Where the Blessed One was, there he approached"? While in the Tuṣita heaven, as soon as he was born, seeing his own essence, shining like a mountain of gold measuring three gāvutas, and the abundance of gardens, celestial mansions, and so on, [he thought,] “Great is this prosperity of mine. What good karma did I do on the human plane?” Looking about, seeing [his] authority over the three jewels, he thought, “This state of being a god is a place of negligence. For even while I am enjoying this prosperity, there could be a loss of mindfulness. Come now, I will go, and having spoken the praises of my Jetavana and of the community of monks and of the tathāgata and of the noble path (āryamārga) and of the elder Śāriputta, I will return from there and experience prosperity."

He did so.

To show that, [the words,] "Then, Anāthapiṇḍika," and so on, were spoken.

"Haunted by the ṛṣi community" means haunted by the monk community.

Thus, having spoken the praises of Jetavana in the first verse, now, while speaking the praises of the noble path, he said, "Action and knowledge," and so on.

There, "Karma" means the intention of the path.

"Knowledge" means the wisdom of the path.

"Dharma" means the dharma that is on the side of samādhi.

"Moral conduct is the highest life" means it shows that life is highest for one established in moral conduct.

Or else, "Knowledge" means right thought.

"Dharma" means effort, mindfulness, and samādhi.

"Moral conduct" means speech, actions, and livelihood.

"The highest life" means life is indeed highest for one established in that moral conduct.

"By this, mortals are purified" means by this eightfold path, beings are purified.

"Therefore," because they are purified by the path, not by lineage or wealth, therefore.

"He should investigate the dharma wisely" means he should investigate the dharma on the side of samādhi by means of skillful devices (upāya).

"Thus he is purified there" means thus he is purified in that noble path (āryamārga).

Or else, "He should investigate the dharma wisely" means he should investigate the dharma of the five aggregates by means of skillful devices (upāya).

"Thus he is purified there" means thus he is purified in those four noble truths (satya).
Therefore, carefully examine the dhamma; thus, one is purified there. Carefully examine the dhamma; thus, one is purified there.

Now, while describing the virtues of the Elder Sāriputta, he said, beginning with "Sāriputto vā." There, "Sāriputto vā" is a term of emphasis. By these [qualities] such as wisdom, he says that Sāriputta is the best. "Upasamenā" means by the pacification of defilements (kilesaupasamena). "Pāraṅgato" means gone to Nibbāna (nibbānaṃ gato). Whatever bhikkhu has attained Nibbāna, he would be supreme to this extent; there is no one superior to the Elder, so he says. The rest is clear throughout.

Sāriputto vā, Sāriputto vā, upasamenā, pāraṅgato.
