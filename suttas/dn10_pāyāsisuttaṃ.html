Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the venerable Kumārakassapa, while wandering in Kosala, arrived together with a large Saṅgha of monks, about five hundred monks, at a town of the Kosalas named Setabyā.

There the venerable Kumārakassapa was staying near Setabyā in the śiṃśapā (rosewood) forest to the north of Setabyā.

At that time, Prince Payāsi was residing at Setabyā, a populous place, full of grass, timber, water, and grain, a royal possession given by King Pasenadi of Kosala as a royal gift, a brahmadeyya (brahmin grant).

The subject of Prince Payāsi.

406. Thus have I heard. This is from the Pāyāsi-rājañña-sutta.

Here is an explanation of the words: Āyasmā is a term of endearment. Kumārakassapa was his name. Because he went forth in his youth, when the Blessed One said, “Call Kassapa, give this fruit or food to Kassapa,” [people asked,] “Which Kassapa?” Because he was known as “Kumārakassapa,” from then on, even in old age, he was called “Kumārakassapa.” Moreover, because he was the king’s adopted son, they recognized him as Kumārakassapa.

This is the story of his manifestation from a previous endeavor: It seems that in the time of the Blessed One Padumuttara, he was the son of a wealthy man. One day, seeing the Blessed One placing a certain disciple of his, a skilled speaker, at the forefront, he gave alms to the Blessed One for seven days, and made the aspiration: “May I also, in the future, become a disciple of a Buddha, a skilled speaker like this elder.” Making merit, he was not able to bring about a distinction, having gone forth in the dispensation of the Blessed One Kassapa.

It seems that at that time, when the dispensation of the Blessed One was declining after his complete extinction, five monks tied a ladder and climbed a mountain, and performed the ascetic life. The Saṅghathera attained arahantship on the third day, the Anuthera became a non-returner on the fourth day, and the other three, being unable to bring about a distinction, were reborn in the world of the gods.

Of these, after experiencing prosperity among gods and humans for an era of Buddhas, one was reborn in a royal family in Takkasilā and, becoming a king named Pukkusāti, went forth dedicating himself to the Blessed One, and, coming towards Rājagaha, hearing the Blessed One's teaching of the Dhamma in a potter's hall, attained the fruit of non-returning.

One was reborn in a family in a coastal city, went aboard a ship, had his ship wrecked, put on bark clothes, and attained gain and prosperity, generated the thought, “I am an Arhat,” was urged by a deity who desired his welfare, “You are not an Arhat, go, approach the Teacher and ask a question,” did so, and attained the fruit of arahantship.

One was born in Rājagaha in the womb of a certain woman from a good family. She, first asking her parents [for permission] to go forth, not obtaining it, went to a family home and conceived a child. Not knowing that she was pregnant, she pleased her husband and, being permitted by him, went forth among the nuns. Seeing the sign of the pregnancy, the nuns asked Devadatta. He said, "She is not an ascetic." They asked the One with Ten Powers. The Teacher had Upālitthera investigate. The elder, summoning the families living in Sāvatthinagara and the female lay follower Visākha, investigating, said, "The pregnancy was conceived before, the going forth is healthy." The Teacher gave his approval to the elder, saying, "The matter is well decided." That nun gave birth to a son resembling a golden statue. Taking him, King Pasenadi Kosala raised him. Naming him "Kassapa," later, having him adorned, he took him to the Teacher and had him go forth. Thus, because he was the adopted son of the king, they recognized him as "Kumārakassapa."

One day, while he was practicing the ascetic life in the Andhavana, a deity desiring his welfare had him learn some questions and said, “Ask the Blessed One these questions.” The elder, asking the questions, at the end of the answers to the questions, attained arahantship. The Blessed One also placed him at the forefront of the monks who were skilled speakers.

Setabyā is the name of that city. Uttarena Setabyā means to the north of Setabyā. Rājañño means an unanointed king.
Diṭṭhigata means simply diṭṭhi (view).

Just as, when it is said "gone to excrement" (gūthagataṃ), "gone to urine" (muttagataṃ), there is nothing other than excrement, so diṭṭhigata is simply diṭṭhi.

"Itipi natthi" means having pointed out that particular cause, he says, "Even in this way, it does not exist."

"Purā...pe...saññāpetī" means as long as he does not make [it] known.

Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ kumārakassapaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘paṇḍito byatto medhāvī bahussuto cittakathī kalyāṇapaṭibhāno vuddho buddho (syā. ka.) ceva arahā ca. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’’ti. Atha kho setabyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā setabyāya nikkhamitvā saṅghasaṅghī gaṇībhūtā uttarenamukhā gacchanti yena siṃsapāvanaṃ yena siṃsapāvanaṃ, tenupasaṅkamanti (sī. pī.).
At that time, Prince Pāyāsi had arisen with the following evil view: "Indeed, there is no other world ( paro loko), there are no beings who are spontaneously born (sattā opapātikā), there is no fruit (phalaṃ) or result (vipāko) of deeds well done or ill done (sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ)."

The Brahmins and householders of Setabya heard: "The śramaṇa Kumārakassapa, a disciple (sāvako) of the śramaṇa Gotama, while wandering in Kosala with a great bhikkhu-saṅgha, with about five hundred bhikkhus, has arrived at Setabya and is staying north of Setabya in the Siṃsapāvana (Simsapa Forest)."

Now, concerning the venerable Kumārakassapa, such a good report (kalyāṇo kittisaddo) has spread about: "He is learned (paṇḍito), experienced (byatto), wise (medhāvī), well-informed (bahussuto), a fine speaker (cittakathī), able to give good replies (kalyāṇapaṭibhāno), venerable (vuddho), and an arhat (arahā)."

Then the Brahmins and householders of Setabya, having left Setabya, gathered in groups and crowds and went northwards to the Siṃsapāvana (Simsapa Forest).

Then Prince Payasi (Pāyāsi rājañño), accompanied by the Brahmins (brāhmaṇagahapatikehi) and householders of Setavya, went to the Simsapa Forest (Siṃsapāvanaṃ) where the Venerable Kumāra-Kassapa was. Then Prince Pāyāsi, having sat down to one side, said this to the Venerable Kumāra-Kassapa: “Reverend Kassapa, I hold to this tenet and this view: There is no other world ( paro loko), there are no spontaneously born beings (sattā opapātikā), there is no fruit or result (phalaṃ vipāko) of good or evil deeds (sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ).”

“Well, Prince, I have never seen or heard of such a tenet or view as you declare. How could anyone say such a thing: ‘There is no other world, there are no spontaneously born beings, there is no fruit or result of good or evil deeds’?”

411. ‘‘Tena hi, rājañña, taññevettha paṭipucchissāmi, yathā te khameyya, tathā naṃ byākareyyāsi. Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, rājañña, ime candimasūriyā imasmiṃ vā loke parasmiṃ vā, devā vā te manussā vā’’ti? ‘‘Ime, bho kassapa, candimasūriyā parasmiṃ loke, na imasmiṃ; devā te na manussā’’ti. ‘‘Imināpi kho te, rājañña, pariyāyena evaṃ hotu – itipi atthi paro loko, atthi sattā opapātikā, atthi sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko’’ti.
‘Then, Prince, I will question you about that very thing here. Answer as you see fit. What do you think, Prince? Are these suns and moons in this world or in another? Are they gods or humans?’ ‘These suns and moons, Master Kassapa, are in another world, not in this one; they are gods, not humans.’ ‘In the same way, Prince, you should consider: “There is another world, there are beings who arise spontaneously (opapātikā), there is a result (vipāka) and fruit (phala) to actions (kamma) that are well-done (sukata) and badly-done (dukkaṭānaṃ)".’
411.Ime bho, kassapa, candimasūriyāti so kira therena pucchito cintesi ‘‘ayaṃ samaṇo paṭhamaṃ candimasūriye upamaṃ āhari, candimasūriyasadiso bhavissati paññāya, anabhibhavanīyo aññena, sace panāhaṃ ‘candimasūriyā imasmiṃ loke’ti bhaṇissāmi, ‘kiṃ nissitā ete, kittakapamāṇā, kittakaṃ uccā’tiādīhi paliveṭhessati. Ahaṃ kho panetaṃ nibbeṭhetuṃ na sakkhissāmi, ‘parasmiṃ loke’ iccevassa kathessāmī’’ti. Tasmā evamāha. Bhagavā pana tato pubbe na cirasseva sudhābhojanīyajātakaṃ kathesi. 411. “These, O Kashyapa, are the candimasūriyā.” When asked by that elder, he thought, "This samaṇa first brought the simile of the candimasūriye, he will be like the candimasūriya in wisdom, unexcelled by another. If I should say, ‘The candimasūriyā are in this world,’ he will entangle me with questions like, ‘What are they based on? What is their measure? How high are they?’

I will not be able to disentangle this. I will just tell him, ‘[They are] in another world.’”

Therefore, he spoke in this way.

The Blessed One then, before that, not long before, told the sudhābhojanīyajātakaṃ.

There it says, “The moon is a devaputta in the moon, the sun is a devaputta in the sun.”

The jātaka or suttanta told by the Blessed One is widespread in all of sakalajambudīpa. Therefore, thinking, “It is not possible to say that there are no devaputtas living here,” he said, “They are gods, not humans.”

Then, Payasi (Pāyāsi) the prince, having given a charity grudgingly (asakkaccaṃ dānaṃ datvā), not with his own hands (asahatthā dānaṃ datvā), without proper concern (acittīkataṃ dānaṃ datvā), like something casually tossed aside (apaviddhaṃ dānaṃ datvā), after death, at the breaking-up of the body (kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā), was reborn in the company of the Four Great Kings (cātumahārājikānaṃ devānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajji) in the empty Serisaka mansion (suññaṃ serīsakaṃ vimānaṃ).

But Uttara (uttaro nāma māṇavo), a young Brahmin, was put in charge of that distribution (yo pana tassa dāne vāvaṭo ahosi).

He, having given charity ungrudgingly (sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ datvā), with his own hands (sahatthā dānaṃ datvā), with proper concern (cittīkataṃ dānaṃ datvā), not as something carelessly cast aside (anapaviddhaṃ dānaṃ datvā), after death, at the breaking-up of the body (kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā), was reborn in a good place (sugatiṃ), a heavenly realm (saggaṃ lokaṃ), in the company of the Thirty-Three Gods (devānaṃ tāvatiṃsānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajji).

Acittīkata means devoid of consideration; without even establishing consideration, he gave without making his mind pure.

It is said that at its door was a large sirīsaka tree; therefore, it is called "Serīsaka."

441. At that time, the Venerable Gavampati was accustomed to go to the empty Serisaka mansion for his midday rest.

Then, the deva Pāyāsi approached the Venerable Gavampati; having approached, after respectfully greeting the Venerable Gavampati, he stood to one side.

As Pāyāsi the deva was standing to one side, the Venerable Gavampati said this to him: "Who are you, friend?" "I am Pāyāsi, your majesty," he said.

"Surely, friend, you held such a view as this: 'There is no other world, there are no beings reborn spontaneously, there is no fruit or result of good and bad actions'?" "Indeed, venerable sir, I held such a view: 'There is no other world, there are no beings reborn spontaneously, there is no fruit or result of good and bad actions.'

But I was converted from that evil view by the Noble Kumārakassapa."

"Where is Uttara, the young man who managed your almsgiving, friend?" "Uttara, the young man who managed my almsgiving, venerable sir, by carefully giving alms, giving alms with his own hands, giving alms with proper concern, not giving alms carelessly, at the breaking up of the body, after death, has been reborn in a good destination, a heavenly world, in the company of the Thirty-Three Gods.

But I, venerable sir, by giving alms carelessly, not giving alms with my own hands, giving alms without proper concern, giving alms carelessly, at the breaking up of the body, after death, have been reborn in the company of the Four Great Kings in the empty Serisaka mansion.

Therefore, venerable Gavampati, go to the human world and announce this: ‘Give alms carefully, give alms with your own hands, give alms with proper concern, give alms unreservedly.

Pāyāsi, your majesty, by giving alms carelessly, not giving alms with your own hands, giving alms without proper concern, giving alms carelessly, at the breaking up of the body, after death, has been reborn in the company of the Four Great Kings in the empty Serisaka mansion.

Uttara, the young man who managed his almsgiving, by carefully giving alms, giving alms with his own hands, giving alms with proper concern, not giving alms carelessly, at the breaking up of the body, after death, has been reborn in a good destination, a heavenly world, in the company of the Thirty-Three Gods.’"

Then the Venerable Gavampati went to the human world and announced this: "Give alms carefully, give alms with your own hands, give alms with proper concern, give alms unreservedly.

Pāyāsi, your majesty, by giving alms carelessly, not giving alms with your own hands, giving alms without proper concern, giving alms carelessly, at the breaking up of the body, after death, has been reborn in the company of the Four Great Kings in the empty Serisaka mansion.

The venerable Gavampati: It seems that in a former life, that elder, as the eldest of the cowherd boys, having cleared the base of a great sirīsa tree, sprinkled sand, seated an alms-mendicant (piṇḍapātika) elder at the foot of the tree, and given the food (āhāra) he had obtained himself, after death, through the power of that [deed], was reborn in that silver mansion (rajatavimāna).

A sirīsa tree stood at the gate of the mansion.

It bears fruit for fifty years, and the god (devaputta) experiences agitation when fifty years have passed.

At another time, having been reborn among humans at the time of our Blessed One, having heard the Blessed One’s teaching (dhamma-katha), he attained Arhatship (arahattaṃ patto).

Due to his past habit, he frequently goes to that same mansion for the sake of the day's dwelling (divāvihāratthāya); it is his seasonal bliss (utusukhaṃ hoti).

With reference to that, it was said: "Then, on that occasion, the venerable Gavampati," and so forth.

He so, having given alms respectfully (sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ datvā), that is, he, even with property belonging to another, gave alms respectfully (sakkaccaṃ datvā).

He announced thus (evamārocesī), that is, he announced in a manner beginning with "Give alms respectfully (sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ dethā)."

And the great multitude, having heard that announcement of the elder, having given alms respectfully (sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ datvā), was reborn in the world of the gods (devaloke nibbatto).

The attendants of Pāyāsi the prince (rājañña), even having given alms respectfully (sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ datvāpi), having gone by way of desire (nikantivasena), were reborn in the presence of that same one.

That wandering mansion (disācārikavimāna) was in Vaṭṭaniaṭavi.

And Pāyāsidevaputta, one day, having shown it to the merchants, told of his past deed, it is said.

He so, having given alms respectfully (sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ datvā), he announced thus (evamārocesī).
