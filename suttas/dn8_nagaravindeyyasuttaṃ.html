<p n="434" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">434</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0528"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0353"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā kosalesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ yena nagaravindaṃ nāma kosalānaṃ brāhmaṇānaṃ gāmo tadavasari. Assosuṃ kho nagaravindeyyakā <note>nagaravindeyyā (ka.)</note> brāhmaṇagahapatikā – ‘‘samaṇo khalu, bho, gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito kosalesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0291"/> nagaravindaṃ anuppatto. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. So <pb ed="M" n="3.0340"/> imaṃ lokaṃ sadevakaṃ samārakaṃ sabrahmakaṃ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ pajaṃ sadevamanussaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti. So dhammaṃ deseti ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Atha kho nagaravindeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā appekacce bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu; sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce yena bhagavā tenañjaliṃ paṇāmetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce bhagavato santike nāmagottaṃ sāvetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce tuṇhībhūtā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0529"/> nisinne kho nagaravindeyyake brāhmaṇagahapatike bhagavā etadavoca –

<pb ed="T" n="3.0529"/>
434. Thus have I heard.

On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the Kosalan country with a large saṃgha (community) of bhikkhus (monks), and eventually arrived at a Kosalan village named Nagaravinda, inhabited by brāhmaṇas (Brahmins).

The brāhmaṇa (Brahmin) householders of Nagaravinda heard: "The samaṇa (ascetic) Gotama, the son of the Śākyans, who went forth from the Śākyan clan, is wandering in the Kosalas with a large bhikkhu-saṃgha (community of monks), and has arrived at Nagaravinda."

Now a good report of Master Gotama has been spread to this effect: 'That Blessed One is an arhat (worthy one), a sammāsambuddha (fully enlightened one), perfect in true knowledge and conduct (vijjā-caraṇa-sampanno), a sugata (sublime one), a lokavidū (knower of the worlds), an anuttara (incomparable one), a purisadammasārathi (leader of persons to be tamed), a satthā devamanussānaṃ (teacher of gods and humans), a buddha (enlightened one), a bhagavā (blessed one).'

He, having realized it by his own super-knowledge (abhiññā), proclaims this world with its devas (gods), Māras, and Brahmās, this generation with its samaṇas (ascetics) and brāhmaṇas (Brahmins), its princes and its people, which he has himself realised by direct knowledge.

He teaches the dhamma (teaching) that is good at the beginning, good in the middle, and good at the end, with the spirit and in the letter, and displays the fully-perfected and purified holy life (brahmacariya).

“It is good to see such arahats (worthy ones)."

Then the brāhmaṇa (Brahmin) householders of Nagaravinda went to the Blessed One. Some paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side; some exchanged greetings with him, and when this courteous and amiable talk was finished, sat down at one side; some extended their hands in reverential salutation towards the Blessed One and sat down at one side; some pronounced their name and clan in the Blessed One’s presence and sat down at one side; some kept silent and sat down at one side.

When the brāhmaṇa (Brahmin) householders of Nagaravinda were seated to one side, the Blessed One said this to them:

<p n="435" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">435</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Sace vo, gahapatayo, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘kathaṃbhūtā, gahapatayo, samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā’ti? Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyātha – ‘ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā, ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ caranti kāyena vācāya manasā, evarūpā samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā. Taṃ kissa hetu? Mayampi hi cakkhuviññeyyesu <pb ed="V" n="3.0354"/> rūpesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā, ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ carāma kāyena vācāya manasā, tesaṃ no samacariyampi hetaṃ uttari apassataṃ. Tasmā te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā. Ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā sotaviññeyyesu saddesu… ghānaviññeyyesu gandhesu… jivhāviññeyyesu rasesu… kāyaviññeyyesu phoṭṭhabbesu… manoviññeyyesu dhammesu avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā, ajjhattaṃ avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ caranti kāyena vācāya manasā, evarūpā samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā. Taṃ kissa hetu? Mayampi hi manoviññeyyesu dhammesu <pb ed="T" n="3.0530"/> avītarāgā avītadosā avītamohā <pb ed="M" n="3.0341"/>, ajjhattaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0292"/> avūpasantacittā, samavisamaṃ carāma kāyena vācāya manasā, tesaṃ no samacariyampi hetaṃ uttari apassataṃ. Tasmā te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā na sakkātabbā na garukātabbā na mānetabbā na pūjetabbā’ti. Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyātha.

“435. If, householders, wanderers of other sects should ask you thus: ‘What sort of recluses and Brahmins should not be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated?’ being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects thus: ‘Those recluses and Brahmins who are not rid of lust ( rāga ), hate ( dveṣa ), and delusion ( moha ) regarding forms cognizable by the eye ( cakṣurvijñeya), whose minds are not inwardly peaceful, and who conduct themselves now righteously, now unrighteously in body ( kāya ), speech ( vācā ), and mind ( manasā ) - such recluses and Brahmins should not be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

What is the reason for that? Because we ourselves are not rid of lust, hate, and delusion regarding forms cognizable by the eye, our minds are not inwardly peaceful, and we conduct ourselves now righteously, now unrighteously in body, speech, and mind. Since we do not see any higher righteous conduct on the part of those recluses and Brahmins, they should not be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

Those recluses and Brahmins who are not rid of lust, hate, and delusion regarding sounds cognizable by the ear…regarding odours cognizable by the nose…regarding flavours cognizable by the tongue…regarding tangibles cognizable by the body…regarding mind-objects cognizable by the mind, whose minds are not inwardly peaceful, and who conduct themselves now righteously, now unrighteously in body, speech, and mindshould not be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

What is the reason for that? Because we ourselves are not rid of lust, hate, and delusion regarding mind-objects cognizable by the mind, our minds are not inwardly peaceful, and we conduct ourselves now righteously, now unrighteously in body, speech, and mind. Since we do not see any higher righteous conduct on the part of those recluses and Brahmins, they should not be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.’

Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way."

436.
“If, householders, wanderers of other sects should ask you thus: ‘What sort of recluses and Brahmins, householders, should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated?’ Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way: ‘Those recluses and Brahmins who are rid of lust ( vītarāga), hate (vītadosā), and delusion (vītamohā) regarding forms cognizable by the eye (cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu), whose minds are inwardly peaceful (ajjhattaṃ vūpasantacittā), and who conduct themselves righteously (samacariyaṃ caranti) in body (kāyena), speech (vācāya), and mind (manasā) - such recluses and Brahmins should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

What is the reason for that? Because we ourselves are not rid of lust, hate, and delusion regarding forms cognizable by the eye, our minds are not inwardly peaceful, and we conduct ourselves now righteously, now unrighteously (samavisamaṃ carāma), in body, speech, and mind. We see higher righteous conduct on the part of those good recluses and Brahmins.

Therefore, those recluses and Brahmins should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

“Those recluses and Brahmins who are rid of lust, hate, and delusion regarding sounds cognizable by the ear (sotaviññeyyesu saddesu)…regarding odours cognizable by the nose (ghānaviññeyyesu gandhesu)…regarding flavours cognizable by the tongue (jivhāviññeyyesu rasesu)…regarding tangibles cognizable by the body (kāyaviññeyyesu phoṭṭhabbesu)…regarding mind-objects cognizable by the mind (manoviññeyyesu dhammesu), whose minds are inwardly peaceful, and who conduct themselves righteously in body, speech, and mind…should be honoured…

What is the reason for that? Because we ourselves are not rid of lust, hate, and delusion regarding mind-objects cognizable by the mind, our minds are not inwardly peaceful, and we conduct ourselves now righteously, now unrighteously, in body, speech, and mind. We see higher righteous conduct on the part of those good recluses and Brahmins.

Therefore, those recluses and Brahmins should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.’

Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way."

<p n="437" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">437</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Sace pana vo <note>sace te (syā. kaṃ. pī. ka.)</note>, gahapatayo, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘ke panāyasmantānaṃ ākārā, ke anvayā, yena tumhe āyasmanto <pb ed="M" n="3.0342"/> evaṃ vadetha? Addhā te āyasmanto vītarāgā <pb ed="P" n="3.0293"/> vā rāgavinayāya vā paṭipannā, vītadosā vā dosavinayāya vā paṭipannā, vītamohā vā mohavinayāya vā paṭipannā’ti? Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyātha – ‘tathā hi te āyasmanto araññavanapatthāni pantāni senāsanāni paṭisevanti. Natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā ye disvā disvā abhirameyyuṃ, natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā sotaviññeyyā saddā ye sutvā sutvā abhirameyyuṃ, natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā ghānaviññeyyā gandhā ye ghāyitvā ghāyitvā abhirameyyuṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0532"/>, natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā jivhāviññeyyā rasā ye sāyitvā sāyitvā abhirameyyuṃ, natthi kho pana tattha tathārūpā kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā ye phusitvā phusitvā abhirameyyuṃ. Ime kho no, āvuso, ākārā, ime anvayā, yena mayaṃ <note>yena mayaṃ āyasmanto (sī. pī.), yena mayaṃ āyasmante (syā. kaṃ.)</note> evaṃ vadema – addhā te āyasmanto vītarāgā vā rāgavinayāya vā paṭipannā, vītadosā vā dosavinayāya vā paṭipannā, vītamohā vā mohavinayāya vā paṭipannā’ti. Evaṃ puṭṭhā tumhe, gahapatayo, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ evaṃ byākareyyāthā’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Evaṃ vutte, nagaravindeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama, abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama! Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya – ‘cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantī’ti; evamevaṃ bhotā gotamena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Ete mayaṃ bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāma dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsake no bhavaṃ gotamo dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupete saraṇaṃ gate’’ti.

<p rend="centre">Nagaravindeyyasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.

437. “But if wanderers of other sects should question you thus, householders: ‘What are the reasons, what is the evidence whereby you say about those venerable ones: “Surely these venerable ones are either rid of lust, or are practising for the removal of lust, either rid of ill will, or are practising for the removal of ill will, either rid of delusion, or are practising for the removal of delusion”?’ Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way: ‘The venerable ones frequent remote wilderness and woodland resting places. For there are no forms cognizable by the eye there of a kind that they could look at and delight in. There are no sounds cognizable by the ear there of a kind that they could listen to and delight in. There are no odours cognizable by the nose there of a kind that they could smell and delight in. There are no flavours cognizable by the tongue there of a kind that they could taste and delight in. There are no tangibles cognizable by the body there of a kind that they could touch and delight in. These are our reasons, friends, this is our evidence whereby we say: “Surely these venerable ones are either rid of lust, or are practising for the removal of lust, either rid of ill will, or are practising for the removal of ill will, either rid of delusion, or are practising for the removal of delusion.”’ Being thus asked, householders, you should answer those wanderers of other sects in this way.”

When this was said, the brahmin householders of Nagaravindeyya said to the Blessed One: “Magnificent, Master Gotama! Magnificent, Master Gotama! Master Gotama has made the Dhamma clear in many ways, as though he were turning upright what had been overthrown, revealing what was hidden, showing the way to one who was lost, or holding up a lamp in the dark for those with eyesight to see forms. We go to Master Gotama for refuge, to the dharma and to the bhikkhu-saṅgha. May Master Gotama accept us as lay-followers who have gone to him for refuge from this day forth as long as life shall last!”

The Nagaravindeyya-sutta, the eighth, is finished.
