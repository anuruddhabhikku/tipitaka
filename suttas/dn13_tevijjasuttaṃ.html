518. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā kosalesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi yena manasākaṭaṃ nāma kosalānaṃ brāhmaṇagāmo tadavasari. Tatra sudaṃ bhagavā manasākaṭe viharati uttarena manasākaṭassa aciravatiyā nadiyā tīre ambavane.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the Blessed One was wandering in Kosala with a large Saṅgha of approximately five hundred monks, and he arrived at a Kosalan Brahmin village called Manasākaṭa.

There, it seems, the Blessed One was staying near Manasākaṭa, in a mango grove on the bank of the Aciravatī river, north of Manasākaṭa.
518.Evaṃme sutaṃ…pe… kosalesūti tevijjasuttaṃ. Tatrāyaṃ anuttānapadavaṇṇanā. Manasākaṭanti tassa gāmassa nāmaṃ. Uttarena manasākaṭassāti manasākaṭato avidūre uttarapasse. Ambavaneti taruṇaambarukkhasaṇḍe, ramaṇīyo kira so bhūmibhāgo, heṭṭhā rajatapaṭṭasadisā vālikā vippakiṇṇā, upari maṇivitānaṃ viya ghanasākhāpattaṃ ambavanaṃ. Tasmiṃ buddhānaṃ anucchavike pavivekasukhe ambavane viharatīti attho.
Thus have I heard... in Kosala, namely the Tevijja Sutta.

Herein is an explanation of the terms that are not obvious.

"Manasākaṭa" is the name of that village.

"To the north of Manasākaṭa" means not far from Manasākaṭa, on the northern side.

"Ambavana" means in a grove of young mango trees; that place is delightful. Below, sand is scattered like silver plates; above, the mango grove is like a jeweled canopy with dense branches and leaves.

The meaning is that he dwelt in that mango grove, which was suitable for Buddhas, secluded, and pleasant in its solitude.

519. Tena kho pana samayena sambahulā abhiññātā abhiññātā brāhmaṇamahāsālā manasākaṭe paṭivasanti, seyyathidaṃ – caṅkī brāhmaṇo tārukkho brāhmaṇo pokkharasāti brāhmaṇo jāṇusoṇi brāhmaṇo todeyyo brāhmaṇo aññe ca abhiññātā abhiññātā brāhmaṇamahāsālā.
And at that time, many well-known and wealthy Brahmins were living in Manasakata, namely the Brahmin Caṅkī, the Brahmin Tārukkha, the Brahmin Pokkharasāti, the Brahmin Jāṇusoṇi, the Brahmin Todeyya, and other well-known and wealthy Brahmins.
519.Abhiññātā abhiññātāti kulacārittādisampattiyā tattha tattha paññātā. Caṅkītiādīni tesaṃ nāmāni. Tattha caṅkī opāsādavāsiko. Tārukkho icchānaṅgalavāsiko. Pokkharasātī ukkaṭṭhavāsiko. Jāṇusoṇī sāvatthivāsiko. Todeyyo tudigāmavāsiko. Aññe cāti aññe ca bahujanā. Attano attano nivāsaṭṭhānehi āgantvā mantasajjhāyakaraṇatthaṃ tattha paṭivasanti. Manasākaṭassa kira ramaṇīyatāya te brāhmaṇā tattha nadītīre gehāni kāretvā parikkhipāpetvā aññesaṃ bahūnaṃ pavesanaṃ nivāretvā antarantarā tattha gantvā vasanti.
519. Abhiññātā abhiññāta means well-known, recognized in various places due to their accomplishments in lineage, conduct, etc.

Caṅkī and the others are their names.

Among them, Caṅkī resides in Opasāda.

Tārukkha resides in Ichānaṅgala.

Pokkharasātī resides in Ukkaṭṭha.

Jāṇusoṇī resides in Sāvatthi.

Todeyya resides in Tudigāma.

Aññe ca means and also other many people.

Having come from their own respective places of residence, they dwell there to recite mantras.

It seems that due to the beauty of Manasākaṭa, those Brahmins, having built and enclosed houses on the riverbank there, prevent the entry of many others, and occasionally go and live there.

520. Atha kho vāseṭṭhabhāradvājānaṃ māṇavānaṃ jaṅghavihāraṃ anucaṅkamantānaṃ anuvicarantānaṃ maggāmagge kathā udapādi. Atha kho vāseṭṭho māṇavo evamāha – ‘‘ayameva ujumaggo, ayamañjasāyano niyyāniko niyyāti takkarassa brahmasahabyatāya, yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena pokkharasātinā’’ti. Bhāradvājopi māṇavo evamāha – ‘‘ayameva ujumaggo, ayamañjasāyano niyyāniko, niyyāti takkarassa brahmasahabyatāya, yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena tārukkhenā’’ti. Neva kho asakkhi vāseṭṭho māṇavo bhāradvājaṃ māṇavaṃ saññāpetuṃ, na pana asakkhi bhāradvājo māṇavopi vāseṭṭhaṃ māṇavaṃ saññāpetuṃ.
520. Then, while the young Brahmins Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja were strolling and wandering about, a discussion arose between them about the right and wrong paths.

Then the young Brahmin Vāseṭṭha said: "This is the only straight path (ujumagga), this is the direct path (añjasāyana), the path of salvation (niyyānika) that leads one who follows it to union with brahman, as is taught by the brāhmaṇa Pokkharasāti!"

And the young Brahmin Bhāradvāja said: "This is the only straight path (ujumagga), this is the direct path (añjasāyana), the path of salvation (niyyānika) that leads one who follows it to union with brahman, as is taught by the brāhmaṇa Tārukkha!"

And Vāseṭṭha could not convince Bhāradvāja, nor could Bhāradvāja convince Vāseṭṭha.
520-521.Vāseṭṭhabhāradvājānanti vāseṭṭhassa ca pokkharasātino antevāsikassa, bhāradvājassa ca tārukkhantevāsikassa. Ete kira dve jātisampannā tiṇṇaṃ vedānaṃ pāragū ahesuṃ. Jaṅghavihāranti aticiranisajjapaccayā kilamathavinodanatthāya jaṅghacāraṃ. Te kira divasaṃ sajjhāyaṃ katvā sāyanhe vuṭṭhāya nhānīyasambhāragandhamālateladhotavatthāni gāhāpetvā attano parijanaparivutā nhāyitukāmā nadītīraṃ gantvā rajatapaṭṭavaṇṇe vālikāsaṇḍe aparāparaṃ caṅkamiṃsu. Ekaṃ caṅkamantaṃ itaro anucaṅkami, puna itaraṃ itaroti. Tena vuttaṃ ‘‘anucaṅkamantānaṃ anuvicarantāna’’nti. Maggāmaggeti magge ca amagge ca. Katamaṃ nu kho paṭipadaṃ pūretvā katamena maggena sakkā sukhaṃ brahmalokaṃ gantunti evaṃ maggāmaggaṃ ārabbha kathaṃ samuṭṭhāpesunti attho. Añjasāyanoti ujumaggassetaṃ vevacanaṃ, añjasā vā ujukameva etena āyanti āgacchantīti añjasāyano niyyāniko niyyātīti niyyāyanto niyyāti, gacchanto gacchatīti attho. Takkarassa brahmasahabyatāyāti yo taṃ maggaṃ karoti paṭipajjati, tassa brahmunā saddhiṃ sahabhāvāya, ekaṭṭhāne pātubhāvāya gacchatīti attho. Yvāyanti yo ayaṃ. Akkhātoti kathito dīpito. Brāhmaṇena pokkharasātināti attano ācariyaṃ apadisati. Iti vāseṭṭho sakameva ācariyavādaṃ thometvā paggaṇhitvā vicarati. Bhāradvājopi sakamevāti. Tena vuttaṃ ‘‘neva kho asakkhi vāseṭṭho’’tiādi. Tato vāseṭṭho ‘‘ubhinnampi amhākaṃ kathā aniyyānikāva, imasmiñca loke maggakusalo nāma bhotā gotamena sadiso natthi, bhavañca gotamo avidūre vasati, so no tulaṃ gahetvā nisinnavāṇijo viya kaṅkhaṃ chindissatī’’ti cintetvā tamatthaṃ bhāradvājassa ārocetvā ubhopi gantvā attano kathaṃ bhagavato ārocesuṃ. Tena vuttaṃ ‘‘atha kho vāseṭṭho…pe… yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena tārukkhenā’’ti.
520-521.
Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja know Vāseṭṭha as the resident disciple of Pokkharasāti, and Bhāradvāja as the resident disciple of Tārukkha.

It is said that these two were accomplished in birth and had mastered the three Vedas.

Jaṅghavihāra means walking meditation, for the purpose of alleviating fatigue due to prolonged sitting.

It is said that in the evening, having finished their recitation during the day, they would rise, take bathing requisites, fragrances, garlands, oil, and washed clothes, and surrounded by their own attendants, desiring to bathe, they would go to the riverbank and stroll back and forth on the sandy shore the color of a silver plate.

When one was strolling, the other would follow; then the other would [stroll] and the one would follow.

Therefore it was said, "while those strolling back and forth, wandering about..."

Maggāmaggeti means on the path and off the path.

What practice, when completed, and by which path is it possible to easily go to the world of Brahmā? Thus, what is the meaning of initiating a discussion concerning the path and the non-path?

Añjasāyano is a synonym for the straight path; by this, people come and approach directly, therefore añjasāyano is a means of departure; one who departs, therefore one who is departing departs; one who goes, goes, is the meaning.

Takkarassa brahmasahabyatāyāti: whoever does and practices that path, for him, to be together with Brahmā, to go to the manifestation in one place, is the meaning.

Yvāyanti yo ayaṃ: That which is such.

Akkhāto ti kathito dīpito: Akkhāto means spoken and revealed.

Brāhmaṇena pokkharasātināti: [He] attributes his teacher to the brāhmaṇa Pokkharasāti.

Thus, Vāseṭṭha praises and upholds only his own teacher's doctrine and wanders about.

Bhāradvāja [does] likewise.

Therefore it was said, "Vāseṭṭha was not able..." etc.

Then Vāseṭṭha, thinking, "The talk of both of us is truly without issue, and in this world, there is no one named skilled in the path like the venerable Gotama, and the venerable Gotama dwells not far away; he, having taken a scale, like a merchant sitting [in his shop], will cut off our doubt," having announced that matter to Bhāradvāja, both of them went and announced their talk to the Blessed One.

Therefore it was said, "Then, indeed, Vāseṭṭha... yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena tārukkhenā" etc.

Atha kho vāseṭṭho māṇavo bhāradvājaṃ māṇavaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘ayaṃ kho, bhāradvāja, samaṇo gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito manasākaṭe viharati uttarena manasākaṭassa aciravatiyā nadiyā tīre ambavane. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’’ti . Āyāma, bho bhāradvāja, yena samaṇo gotamo tenupasaṅkamissāma; upasaṅkamitvā etamatthaṃ samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ pucchissāma. Yathā no samaṇo gotamo byākarissati, tathā naṃ dhāressāmā’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bho’’ti kho bhāradvājo māṇavo vāseṭṭhassa māṇavassa paccassosi.
Then Vāseṭṭha, the student, addressed Bhāradvāja, the student, saying: "This ascetic Gotama (Gautama), the son of the Śākya (Sākya) clan, who went forth from the Śākya (Sākya) family, is dwelling in Manasākaṭa (Manasakata), in the mango grove on the bank of the Aciravatī (Aciravati) River, north of Manasākaṭa (Manasakata).

Now, concerning the esteemed Gotama (Gautama), such a fine reputation has spread: "Indeed, that Blessed One is an Arhat (Arahant), a Fully Enlightened One (sammāsambuddha), endowed with knowledge and conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), a Well-Farer (sugata), Knower of the World (lokavidū), unsurpassed trainer of beings (anuttaro purisadammasārathi), teacher of gods and humans (satthā devamanussānaṃ), the Awakened One (buddha), the Blessed One (bhagavā)."

Come, Bhāradvāja (Bharadvaja), let us go to the ascetic Gotama (Gautama); having gone, let us ask the ascetic Gotama (Gautama) about this matter. As the ascetic Gotama (Gautama) explains to us, so shall we remember it."

"So it is, sir (evaṃ bho)," replied Bhāradvāja (Bharadvaja), the student, to Vāseṭṭha (Vasettha), the student.

520-521.

Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja knew of Vāseṭṭha's resident disciple Pokkharasāti, and of Bhāradvāja's resident disciple Tārukkha.

It is said that these two were accomplished in birth and had mastered the three Vedas.

Jaṅghavihāra means jaṅghacāraṃ, walking back and forth to dispel weariness due to sitting for a very long time.

It is said that during the day, having made a recitation (sajjhāyaṃ katvā), in the evening, having risen up, having had bathing preparations, perfumes, garlands, oil, and washed cloths taken, surrounded by their own attendants, wanting to bathe, having gone to the bank of the river, on a sandbank the color of a silver plate, they walked back and forth, one after another.

When one is walking back and forth, the other walks along, then the other [walks along with] the other.

Therefore, it was said, "Of those walking back and forth, of those walking through."

Maggāmaggeti means on the path and on the non-path.

What practice should be fulfilled, by which path is it possible to happily go to the Brahmā world? Thus, what is the meaning of initiating a discussion concerning the path and the non-path (maggāmaggaṃ ārabbha kathaṃ samuṭṭhāpesunti attho)?

Añjasāyanoti is a synonym for the straight path (ujumaggassetaṃ vevacanaṃ); by this they come and go straight, therefore, añjasāyano is a means of egress (niyyāniko); he goes out (niyyātīti), one going out, goes out (niyyāyanto niyyāti), one going, goes (gacchanto gacchati), this is the meaning (attho).

Takkarassa brahmasahabyatāyāti means he who does and undertakes that path (yo taṃ maggaṃ karoti paṭipajjati), for him there is companionship with Brahmā (tassa brahmunā saddhiṃ sahabhāvāya), he goes to manifestation in one place (ekaṭṭhāne pātubhāvāya gacchati), this is the meaning (attho).

Yvāyanti yo ayaṃ—whoever goes.

Akkhātoti means told, illuminated (kathito dīpito).

Brāhmaṇena pokkharasātināti means he indicates his teacher (attano ācariyaṃ apadisati) as the Brāhmin Pokkharasāti.

Thus, Vāseṭṭha, praising and approving only his own teacher's doctrine (sakameva ācariyavādaṃ thometvā paggaṇhitvā), wanders about (vicarati).

Bhāradvāja [does] likewise (bhāradvājopi sakamevāti).

Therefore, it was said, "Indeed, Vāseṭṭha did not manage," etc. (‘‘neva kho asakkhi vāseṭṭho’’tiādi).

Then Vāseṭṭha [thought], "Indeed, the talk of both of us is not a means of egress (aniyyānikāva); and in this world, there is no one skilled in the path (maggakusalo) like the honored Gotama (bhotā gotamena sadiso natthi), and the honored Gotama lives not far away (avidūre vasati), he, like a merchant who has taken a scale, sitting, will cut off our doubt (kaṅkhaṃ chindissati)"; having thought this, having announced that matter to Bhāradvāja, both having gone, they announced their talk to the Blessed One (bhagavato ārocesuṃ).

Therefore it was said, "Then indeed, Vāseṭṭha…pe…he is declared by the Brahmin Tārukkha (‘‘atha kho vāseṭṭho…pe… yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena tārukkhenā’’ti)."

522. Atha kho vāseṭṭhabhāradvājā māṇavā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho vāseṭṭho māṇavo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘idha, bho gotama, amhākaṃ jaṅghavihāraṃ anucaṅkamantānaṃ anuvicarantānaṃ maggāmagge kathā udapādi. Ahaṃ evaṃ vadāmi – ‘ayameva ujumaggo, ayamañjasāyano niyyāniko niyyāti takkarassa brahmasahabyatāya, yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena pokkharasātinā’ti. Bhāradvājo māṇavo evamāha – ‘ayameva ujumaggo ayamañjasāyano niyyāniko niyyāti takkarassa brahmasahabyatāya, yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena tārukkhenā’ti. Ettha, bho gotama, attheva viggaho, atthi vivādo, atthi nānāvādo’’ti.
Then the two brahmin students, Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja, went to see the Lord.

After exchanging polite and courteous greetings, they sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, Vāseṭṭha said to the Blessed One: “Here, Master Gotama, as we were strolling and wandering about, a discussion arose between us about the path and the non-path.

I say: ‘This is the only straight path, this is the direct path, the path of salvation that leads one who follows it to union with Brahmā, as is taught by the Brahmin Pokkharasāti!’

And Bhāradvāja says: ‘This is the only straight path, this is the direct path, the path of salvation that leads one who follows it to union with Brahmā, as is taught by the Brahmin Tārukkha!’

Here, Master Gotama, there is indeed disagreement, there is dispute, there is divergence of views."
522.Ettha bho gotamāti etasmiṃ maggāmagge. Viggaho vivādotiādīsu pubbuppattiko viggaho. Aparabhāge vivādo. Duvidhopi eso nānāācariyānaṃ vādato nānāvādo.

Here, sir Gotama, in this path of the path.
Dispute is a quarrel, and in the beginning, a dispute.
In the other part, a dispute.
Although twofold, this is a diverse doctrine from the speech of various teachers.

523. ‘‘Iti kira , vāseṭṭha, tvaṃ evaṃ vadesi – ‘‘ayameva ujumaggo, ayamañjasāyano niyyāniko niyyāti takkarassa brahmasahabyatāya, yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena pokkharasātinā’’ti. Bhāradvājo māṇavo evamāha – ‘‘ayameva ujumaggo ayamañjasāyano niyyāniko niyyāti takkarassa brahmasahabyatāya, yvāyaṃ akkhāto brāhmaṇena tārukkhenā’’ti. Atha kismiṃ pana vo, vāseṭṭha, viggaho, kismiṃ vivādo, kismiṃ nānāvādo’’ti?
‘So, Vāseṭṭha, you say: "This is the only straight path, this is the direct path, the path of salvation that leads one who follows it to union with Brahmā (brahmasahabyatāya), as is taught by the Brahmin Pokkharasāti."’

Bharadvāja the youth says: "This is the only straight path, this is the direct path, the path of salvation that leads one who follows it to union with Brahmā (brahmasahabyatāya), as is taught by the Brahmin Tārukkha."’

Then what is the dispute, Vāseṭṭha, what is the quarrel, what is the difference all about?
523.Atha kismiṃ pana voti tvampi ayameva maggoti attano ācariyavādameva paggayha tiṭṭhasi, bhāradvājopi attano ācariyavādameva, ekassāpi ekasmiṃ saṃsayo natthi. Evaṃ sati kismiṃ vo viggahoti pucchati.
Furthermore, upon what then do you also, thinking "This alone is the path," firmly rely on your own teacher's doctrine, and Bhāradvāja also on his own teacher's doctrine, when not even one of you has doubt about a single thing?
If this is so, upon what do you have a disagreement? Thus he asks.

524. ‘‘Maggāmagge, bho gotama. Evameva kho, vāseṭṭha, evaṃ bhāvitāya upekkhāya cetovimuttiyā yaṃ pamāṇakataṃ kammaṃ na taṃ tatrāvasissati, na taṃ tatrāvatiṭṭhati. Ayaṃ kho, vāseṭṭha, brahmānaṃ sahabyatāya maggo.
556. The Blessed One said this: "Here, Vāseṭṭha, a Tathāgata arises in the world, an Arhat, a perfectly enlightened Buddha…pe… (as it should be explained in sections 190-212 in this way, in detail).

In this way, Vāseṭṭha, a bhikkhu is accomplished in virtue…pe… Seeing these five hindrances abandoned within himself, gladness (pāmojja) arises; for one who is gladdened, rapture (pīti) arises; when the mind is filled with rapture (pīti), the body grows tranquil; the body being tranquil, he experiences happiness (sukha); being happy, the mind becomes concentrated (samādhiyati).

“He dwells, pervading one direction with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettāsahagatena cetasā).

Likewise the second direction.

Likewise the third direction.

Likewise the fourth direction.

Thus, above, below, across, everywhere, identifying himself with all, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettāsahagatena cetasā), abundant, exalted, measureless, free from enmity, and free from affliction.

"Just as, Vāseṭṭha, a mighty trumpeter could make himself heard without difficulty in the four directions; so too, Vāseṭṭha, by this development, by this liberation of the heart through loving-kindness (mettā), no limiting action remains there, none persists there.

This, too, Vāseṭṭha, is the path to companionship with Brahmā.

"Furthermore, Vāseṭṭha, a bhikkhu dwells pervading one direction with a mind imbued with compassion (karuṇāsahagatena cetasā)…pe… with a mind imbued with sympathetic joy (muditāsahagatena cetasā)…pe… with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhāsahagatena cetasā).

Likewise the second direction.

Likewise the third direction.

Likewise the fourth direction.

Thus, above, below, across, everywhere, identifying himself with all, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhāsahagatena cetasā), abundant, exalted, measureless, free from enmity, and free from affliction.

"Just as, Vāseṭṭha, a mighty trumpeter could make himself heard without difficulty in the four directions.

So too, Vāseṭṭha, by this development, by this liberation of the heart through equanimity (upekkhā), no limiting action remains there, none persists there.

This, too, Vāseṭṭha, is the path to companionship with Brahmā.

557. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, vāseṭṭha, evaṃvihārī bhikkhu sapariggaho vā apariggaho vā’’ti? ‘‘Apariggaho, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Saveracitto vā averacitto vā’’ti? ‘‘Averacitto, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Sabyāpajjacitto vā abyāpajjacitto vā’’ti? ‘‘Abyāpajjacitto, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Saṃkiliṭṭhacitto vā asaṃkiliṭṭhacitto vā’’ti? ‘‘Asaṃkiliṭṭhacitto, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Vasavattī vā avasavattī vā’’ti? ‘‘Vasavattī, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Iti kira, vāseṭṭha, apariggaho bhikkhu, apariggaho brahmā. Api nu kho apariggahassa bhikkhuno apariggahena brahmunā saddhiṃ saṃsandati sametī’’ti? ‘‘Evaṃ, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Sādhu, vāseṭṭha, so vata vāseṭṭha apariggaho bhikkhu kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apariggahassa brahmuno sahabyūpago bhavissatī’’ti, ṭhānametaṃ vijjati.
‘What do you think, Vāseṭṭha? Is a monk dwelling thus encumbered with possessions (sapariggaha) or unencumbered with possessions (apariggaha)?’ ‘Unencumbered, Venerable Gotama.’

‘Is he with hatred (saveracitto) or without hatred (averacitto)?’ ‘Without hatred, Venerable Gotama.’

‘Is he full of ill-will (sabyāpajjacitto) or without ill-will (abyāpajjacitto)?’ ‘Without ill-will, Venerable Gotama.’

‘Is he with defiled mind (saṃkiliṭṭhacitto) or without defiled mind (asaṃkiliṭṭhacitto)?’ ‘Without defiled mind, Venerable Gotama.’

‘Is he disciplined (vasavattī) or undisciplined (avasavattī)?’ ‘Disciplined, Venerable Gotama.’

‘Then, Vāseṭṭha, the monk is unencumbered with possessions (apariggaha), and Brahmā is unencumbered with possessions (apariggaha).

Has that unencumbered monk anything in common with the unencumbered Brahmā?’ ‘Yes, Venerable Gotama.’

‘That is right, Vāseṭṭha. Then that an unencumbered monk, after death, at the breaking-up of the body, should attain to union with the unencumbered Brahmā - that is possible.’

558. ‘‘Iti kira, vāseṭṭha, averacitto bhikkhu, averacitto brahmā…pe… abyāpajjacitto bhikkhu, abyāpajjacitto brahmā… asaṃkiliṭṭhacitto bhikkhu, asaṃkiliṭṭhacitto brahmā… vasavattī bhikkhu, vasavattī brahmā, api nu kho vasavattissa bhikkhuno vasavattinā brahmunā saddhiṃ saṃsandati sametī’’ti ? ‘‘Evaṃ, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Sādhu, vāseṭṭha, so vata, vāseṭṭha, vasavattī bhikkhu kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā vasavattissa brahmuno sahabyūpago bhavissatīti, ṭhānametaṃ vijjatī’’ti.
"Thus, Vāseṭṭha, a monk with a mind free from enmity, Brahmā with a mind free from enmity... a monk with a mind free from ill-will, Brahmā with a mind free from ill-will... a monk with a mind free from defilement, Brahmā with a mind free from defilement... a monk who has mastered his mind, Brahmā who has mastered his mind, could it be possible that a monk who has mastered his mind communes and unites with Brahmā who has mastered his mind?" "Yes, Venerable Gotama."

"Excellent, Vāseṭṭha, that a monk who has mastered his mind, after death, at the breaking up of the body, should attain union with Brahmā who has mastered his mind – that is indeed possible."

Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya ‘cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantī’ti. Evamevaṃ bhotā gotamena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito . Ete mayaṃ bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāma, dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsake no bhavaṃ gotamo dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupete saraṇaṃ gate’’ti. Tassuddānaṃ –

When this was said, the youths Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja said this to the Blessed One: "Excellent, Venerable Gotama, excellent! It is as if, Venerable Gotama, one were to set upright what had been overturned, or reveal what had been hidden, or show the way to one who was lost, or hold up an oil lamp in the dark, so that those with eyes might see forms."

Just so, the Dharma has been made clear by Venerable Gotama in many ways (anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito).

We go to the esteemed Gotama for refuge (saraṇaṃ gacchāma), and to the Dharma, and to the Saṃgha of monks.

May the esteemed Gotama remember us as lay followers (upāsake) who have gone for refuge from this day forth, as long as life lasts."

This is the summary (tassuddānaṃ).
559.Etemayaṃ bhavantaṃ gotamanti idaṃ tesaṃ dutiyaṃ saraṇagamanaṃ. Paṭhamameva hete majjhimapaṇṇāsake vāseṭṭhasuttaṃ sutvā saraṇaṃ gatā, imaṃ pana tevijjasuttaṃ sutvā dutiyampi saraṇaṃ gatā. Katipāhaccayena pabbajitvā aggaññasutte upasampadañceva arahattañca alatthuṃ. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti.
559. This is their second refuge-going, saying, "We go to you, Master Gotama."

Having previously gone for refuge after hearing the Vāseṭṭha Sutta in the Majjhimapaṇṇāsa, they went for refuge a second time after hearing this Tevijja Sutta.

After some time, having gone forth and having attained full ordination (upasampadā) and arahantship in the Aggañña Sutta.

The rest is clear throughout.
