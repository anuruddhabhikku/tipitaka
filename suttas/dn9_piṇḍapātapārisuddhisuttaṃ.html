438. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary.

Then, the venerable Śāriputta, in the evening, arose from meditation and went to the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side.

As the venerable Śāriputta was sitting to one side, the Blessed One said to him:

“Śāriputta, your faculties are clear. The color of your skin is pure and bright. In what kind of dwelling ( vihāra) are you mostly dwelling at present?”

“At present, venerable sir, I am mostly dwelling in the dwelling of emptiness (śūnyatāvihāra).”

“Good, good, Śāriputta! Indeed, Śāriputta, at present you are mostly dwelling in the dwelling of a great man (mahāpurisavihāra). This, Śāriputta, is the dwelling of a great man, namely emptiness (śūnyatā). Therefore, Śāriputta, if a bhikkhu should wish, ‘May I mostly dwell in the dwelling of emptiness (śūnyatāvihāra)’, then, Śāriputta, that bhikkhu should consider thus: ‘On the path by which I went to the village for alms, or in the place where I wandered for alms, or on the path by which I returned from the village after the alms round, was there any desire (chanda) or lust (rāga) or hatred (dosa) or delusion (moha) or aversion (paṭigha) in my mind regarding forms cognizable by the eye?’ If, Śāriputta, a bhikkhu, upon reflection, knows thus: ‘On the path by which I went to the village for alms, or in the place where I wandered for alms, or on the path by which I returned from the village after the alms round, there was desire (chanda) or lust (rāga) or hatred (dosa) or delusion (moha) or aversion (paṭigha) in my mind regarding forms cognizable by the eye,’ then, Śāriputta, that bhikkhu should strive for the abandoning of those evil, unwholesome states.

But if, Śāriputta, a bhikkhu, upon reflection, knows thus: ‘On the path by which I went to the village for alms, or in the place where I wandered for alms, or on the path by which I returned from the village after the alms round, there was no desire (chanda) or lust (rāga) or hatred (dosa) or delusion (moha) or aversion (paṭigha) in my mind regarding forms cognizable by the eye,’ then, Śāriputta, that bhikkhu can abide filled with joy and gladness (prītipramodya), training day and night in wholesome states (kuśalāḥ dharmāḥ).

Again, Sāriputta, a bhikkhu should consider thus: 'On the path by which I went to the village for alms, or in the place where I wandered for alms, or on the path by which I returned from the almsround, was there any desire (chando), lust (rāgo), hate (doso), delusion (moho), or aversion (paṭighaṃ) in my mind regarding sounds cognizable by the ear?...regarding odours cognizable by the nose?...regarding flavours cognizable by the tongue?...regarding tangibles cognizable by the body?...regarding mindobjects cognizable by the mind?'

If, Sāriputta, a bhikkhu, by reviewing, knows thus: 'On the path by which I went to the village for alms, or in the place where I wandered for alms, or on the path by which I returned from the almsround, there was desire, lust, hate, delusion, or aversion in my mind regarding mindobjects cognizable by the mind,' then, Sāriputta, that bhikkhu should make an effort to abandon those evil unwholesome states.

But if, Sāriputta, a bhikkhu, by reviewing, knows thus: 'On the path by which I went to the village for alms, and in the place where I wandered for alms, and on the path by which I returned from the almsround, there was no desire, lust, hate, delusion, or aversion in my mind regarding mindobjects cognizable by the mind,' then, Sāriputta, that bhikkhu can abide happy and glad, training day and night in wholesome states.

Furthermore, Sāriputta, a monk should consider thus: ‘Are serenity (śamatha) and insight (vipaśyanā) developed in me?’ If, Sāriputta, by reviewing, he knows thus: ‘Serenity and insight are not developed in me,’ then, Sāriputta, he should make an effort to develop serenity and insight. But if, Sāriputta, by reviewing, he knows thus: ‘Serenity and insight are developed in me,’ then, Sāriputta, he can abide happy and glad, training day and night in wholesome states (kuśalāni dharmāṇi).
451
<p n="451" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">451</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, sāriputta, bhikkhunā iti paṭisañcikkhitabbaṃ – ‘sacchikatā nu kho me vijjā ca vimutti cā’ti? Sace, sāriputta, bhikkhu paccavekkhamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘asacchikatā kho me vijjā ca vimutti cā’ti, tena, sāriputta, bhikkhunā vijjāya vimuttiyā sacchikiriyāya vāyamitabbaṃ. Sace pana, sāriputta, bhikkhu paccavekkhamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘sacchikatā kho me vijjā ca vimutti cā’ti, tena, sāriputta, bhikkhunā teneva pītipāmojjena vihātabbaṃ ahorattānusikkhinā kusalesu dhammesu.

451. Furthermore, Sāriputta, it should occur to a bhikkhu: "Is true knowledge (vidjā) and liberation (vimutti) realized by me?" If, Sāriputta, a bhikkhu reviewing, knows thus: "True knowledge (vidjā) and liberation (vimutti) are not realized by me," then, Sāriputta, effort should be made by that bhikkhu for the realization of true knowledge (vidjā) and liberation (vimutti).

But if, Sāriputta, a bhikkhu reviewing, knows thus: "True knowledge (vidjā) and liberation (vimutti) are realized by me," then, Sāriputta, he can abide happy and glad, training day and night in wholesome states (dhamma).
452
<p n="452" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">452</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Ye hi keci, sāriputta, atītamaddhānaṃ samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā piṇḍapātaṃ parisodhesuṃ, sabbe te evameva paccavekkhitvā <pb ed="T" n="3.0540"/> paccavekkhitvā piṇḍapātaṃ parisodhesuṃ. Yepi hi keci, sāriputta, anāgatamaddhānaṃ samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā piṇḍapātaṃ parisodhessanti, sabbe te evameva paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā piṇḍapātaṃ parisodhessanti. Yepi hi keci, sāriputta, etarahi samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā piṇḍapātaṃ parisodhenti, sabbe te evameva paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā piṇḍapātaṃ parisodhenti. Tasmātiha, sāriputta <note>vo sāriputta evaṃ sikkhitabbaṃ (sī. pī.)</note>, ‘paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā piṇḍapātaṃ parisodhessāmā’ti – evañhi vo, sāriputta, sikkhitabba’’nti.

<p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā sāriputto bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti.

<p rend="centre">Piṇḍapātapārisuddhisuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ navamaṃ.

452. “Sāriputta, whatever ascetics (śramaṇa) and priests (brāhmaṇa) in the past have purified their almsfood (piṇḍapāta) have all done so by repeatedly reviewing thus.

Whatever ascetics (śramaṇa) and priests (brāhmaṇa) in the future will purify their almsfood (piṇḍapāta) will all do so by repeatedly reflecting thus.

Whatever ascetics (śramaṇa) and priests (brāhmaṇa) in the present are purifying their almsfood (piṇḍapāta) are all doing so by repeatedly reflecting thus.

Therefore, Sāriputta, you should train thus: ‘We will purify our almsfood (piṇḍapāta) by repeatedly reviewing thus’,”

This was said by the Blessed One (bhagavā).

The venerable Sāriputta was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One's (bhagavato) words (bhāsitaṃ).

The Piṇḍapātapārisuddhisutta, the ninth, is finished.
