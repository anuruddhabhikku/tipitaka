376. Thus have I heard: At one time the Blessed One (Bhagavān) was staying among the Sakyas (Sakkesu), at Kapilavatthu in the Banyan Tree Park (nigrodhārāme).

Then Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, taking a new pair of cloths (navaṃ dussayugaṃ), went to where the Blessed One (Bhagavā) was; having gone and bowed down to the Blessed One (Bhagavantaṃ), she sat down on one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdi).

Seated on one side (ekamantaṃ nisinnā), Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī said this to the Blessed One (Bhagavantaṃ): "This new pair of cloths (navaṃ dussayugaṃ), venerable sir (bhante), has been spun by myself (sāmaṃ kantaṃ) and woven by myself (sāmaṃ vāyitaṃ), intending it for the Blessed One (Bhagavantaṃ). May the Blessed One (Bhagavā) accept it from me, taking compassion (anukampaṃ upādāya)."

When this was said, the Blessed One (Bhagavā) said this to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī: "Give (dehi), Gotamī, to the Saṅgha (saṅghe). When it is given to the Saṅgha (saṅghe te dinne), both I (ahañceva) and the Saṅgha (saṅgho ca) will be honored (pūjito bhavissāmi)."

A second time (dutiyampi) Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī said this to the Blessed One (Bhagavantaṃ): "This new pair of cloths (navaṃ dussayugaṃ), venerable sir (bhante), has been spun by myself (sāmaṃ kantaṃ) and woven by myself (sāmaṃ vāyitaṃ), intending it for the Blessed One (Bhagavantaṃ). May the Blessed One (Bhagavā) accept it from me, taking compassion (anukampaṃ upādāya)."

A second time (dutiyampi) the Blessed One (Bhagavā) said this to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī: "Give (dehi), Gotamī, to the Saṅgha (saṅghe). When it is given to the Saṅgha (saṅghe te dinne), both I (ahañceva) and the Saṅgha (saṅgho ca) will be honored (pūjito bhavissāmi)."

A third time (tatiyampi) Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī said this to the Blessed One (Bhagavantaṃ): "This new pair of cloths (navaṃ dussayugaṃ), venerable sir (bhante), has been spun by myself (sāmaṃ kantaṃ) and woven by myself (sāmaṃ vāyitaṃ), intending it for the Blessed One (Bhagavantaṃ). May the Blessed One (Bhagavā) accept it from me, taking compassion (anukampaṃ upādāya)."

A third time (tatiyampi) the Blessed One (Bhagavā) said this to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī: "Give (dehi), Gotamī, to the Saṅgha (saṅghe). When it is given to the Saṅgha (saṅghe te dinne), both I (ahañceva) and the Saṅgha (saṅgho ca) will be honored (pūjito bhavissāmi)."

<p n="377" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">377</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ vutte, āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘paṭiggaṇhātu, bhante, bhagavā mahāpajāpatiyā gotamiyā navaṃ dussayugaṃ. Bahūpakārā <note>bahukārā (syā. kaṃ.)</note>, bhante, mahāpajāpati gotamī bhagavato mātucchā āpādikā posikā khīrassa dāyikā; bhagavantaṃ janettiyā kālaṅkatāya thaññaṃ pāyesi. Bhagavāpi, bhante, bahūpakāro mahāpajāpatiyā gotamiyā. Bhagavantaṃ, bhante, āgamma mahāpajāpati gotamī buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā, dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā, saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā. Bhagavantaṃ, bhante, āgamma mahāpajāpati gotamī pāṇātipātā paṭiviratā adinnādānā paṭiviratā kāmesumicchācārā paṭiviratā musāvādā paṭiviratā surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā paṭiviratā. Bhagavantaṃ, bhante, āgamma mahāpajāpati gotamī buddhe aveccappasādena samannāgatā <pb ed="V" n="3.0304"/>, dhamme aveccappasādena samannāgatā, saṅghe aveccappasādena samannāgatā ariyakantehi <pb ed="P" n="3.0254"/> sīlehi samannāgatā. Bhagavantaṃ, bhante, āgamma mahāpajāpati gotamī dukkhe nikkaṅkhā, dukkhasamudaye nikkaṅkhā, dukkhanirodhe nikkaṅkhā, dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya nikkaṅkhā. Bhagavāpi, bhante, bahūpakāro mahāpajāpatiyā gotamiyā’’ti.

When this was said, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, let the Blessed One accept the new pair of cloths from Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī.

Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī has been very helpful to the Blessed One, venerable sir. As his mother’s sister, she was his nurse, his foster mother, the one who gave him milk. She suckled the Blessed One when his own mother died.

The Blessed One too has been very helpful to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, venerable sir.

It is owing to the Blessed One that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī has gone for refuge to the Buddha (Buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā), the Dharma (dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā), and the Saṅgha (saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā).

It is owing to the Blessed One that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭiviratā), from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭiviratā), from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā paṭiviratā), from false speech (musāvādā paṭiviratā), and from wine, liquor, and intoxicants, which are the basis of negligence (surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā paṭiviratā).

It is owing to the Blessed One that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī possesses perfect confidence (aveccappasādena samannāgatā) in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṅgha, and that she possesses the virtues loved by noble ones (ariyakantehi sīlehi samannāgatā).

It is owing to the Blessed One that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī is free from doubt (nikkaṅkhā) about suffering (dukkhe), about the origin of suffering (dukkhasamudaye), about the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhe), and about the way leading to the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya).

The Blessed One too has been very helpful to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, venerable sir.”

“So it is, Ānanda.

When one person, owing to another, has gone for refuge to the Buddha (Buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti), the Dharma (dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti), and the Saṃgha (saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti), I say that it is not easy for the former to repay the latter by paying homage to him, rising up for him, according him reverential salutation, polite services, robes (cīvara), almsfood (piṇḍapāta), resting places (senāsana), and medicinal requisites (gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārānuppadānena).

“When one person, owing to another, has come to abstain from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti), from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti), from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato hoti), from false speech (musāvādā paṭivirato hoti), and from wine, liquor, and intoxicants, which are the basis of negligence (surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā paṭivirato hoti), I say that it is not easy for the former to repay the latter by paying homage to him…and medicinal requisites.

“When one person, owing to another, has come to possess perfect confidence in the Buddha (buddhe aveccappasādena samannāgato hoti), the Dharma (dhamme)…, and the Saṃgha (saṅghe)…, and to possess the virtues loved by noble ones (ariyakantehi sīlehi samannāgato hoti), I say that it is not easy for the former to repay the latter by paying homage to him…and medicinal requisites.

“When one person, owing to another, has become free from doubt about suffering (dukkhe nikkaṅkho hoti), about the origin of suffering (dukkhasamudaye nikkaṅkho hoti), about the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhe nikkaṅkho hoti), and about the way leading to the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya nikkaṅkho hoti), I say that it is not easy for the former to repay the latter by paying homage to him…and medicinal requisites.

379. "There are, Ānanda, fourteen kinds of personal offerings (pāṭipuggalikā dakkhiṇā).

What are the fourteen? One gives a gift to the Tathāgata, the worthy one, the perfectly enlightened one (tathāgate arahante sammāsambuddhe dānaṃ deti) – this is the first kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to a paccekabuddha – this is the second kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to a worthy disciple of the Tathāgata – this is the third kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to one who has entered upon the path to the realization of the fruit of arahantship – this is the fourth kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to a non-returner (anāgāmissa dānaṃ deti) – this is the fifth kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to one who has entered upon the path to the realization of the fruit of non-return – this is the sixth kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to a once-returner – this is the seventh kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to one who has entered upon the path to the realization of the fruit of once-return – this is the eighth kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to a stream-enterer (sotāpanne dānaṃ deti) – this is the ninth kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to one who has entered upon the path to the realization of the fruit of stream-entry – this is the tenth kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to one outside the Dispensation who is free from lust for sensual pleasures – this is the eleventh kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to a virtuous ordinary person – this is the twelfth kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to an immoral ordinary person – this is the thirteenth kind of personal offering. One gives a gift to an animal – this is the fourteenth kind of personal offering.

“Herein, Ānanda, by giving a gift to an animal, the offering may be expected to repay a hundredfold. By giving a gift to an immoral ordinary person, the offering may be expected to repay a thousandfold. By giving a gift to a virtuous ordinary person, the offering may be expected to repay a hundred-thousandfold. By giving a gift to one outside the Dispensation who is free from lust for sensual pleasures, the offering may be expected to repay a hundred-thousand times a hundred-thousandfold. By giving a gift to one who has entered upon the path to the realization of the fruit of stream-entry, the offering may be expected to repay the incalculable and immeasurable. What need to speak of a stream-enterer? What need to speak of one who has entered upon the path to the realization of the fruit of once-return? What need to speak of a once-returner? What need to speak of one who has entered upon the path to the realization of the fruit of non-return? What need to speak of a non-returner? What need to speak of one who has entered upon the path to the realization of the fruit of arahantship? What need to speak of a worthy one? What need to speak of a paccekasambuddha? What need to speak of the Tathāgata, the worthy one, the perfectly enlightened one!

Buddhappamukhe ubhatosaṅghe dānaṃ deti – ayaṃ paṭhamā saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā. Tathāgate parinibbute ubhatosaṅghe dānaṃ deti – ayaṃ dutiyā saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā. Bhikkhusaṅghe dānaṃ deti – ayaṃ tatiyā saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā. Bhikkhunisaṅghe dānaṃ deti – ayaṃ catutthī saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā. ‘Ettakā me bhikkhū ca bhikkhuniyo ca <pb ed="P" n="3.0256"/> saṅghato uddissathā’ti dānaṃ deti – ayaṃ pañcamī saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā. ‘Ettakā <pb ed="V" n="3.0306"/> me bhikkhū saṅghato uddissathā’ti dānaṃ deti – ayaṃ chaṭṭhī saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā. ‘Ettakā me bhikkhuniyo saṅghato uddissathā’ti dānaṃ deti – ayaṃ sattamī saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Bhavissanti <pb ed="M" n="3.0299"/> kho panānanda, anāgatamaddhānaṃ gotrabhuno kāsāvakaṇṭhā <pb ed="T" n="3.0461"/> dussīlā pāpadhammā. Tesu dussīlesu saṅghaṃ uddissa dānaṃ dassanti. Tadāpāhaṃ, ānanda, saṅghagataṃ dakkhiṇaṃ asaṅkheyyaṃ appameyyaṃ vadāmi. Na tvevāhaṃ, ānanda, kenaci pariyāyena saṅghagatāya dakkhiṇāya pāṭipuggalikaṃ dānaṃ mahapphalataraṃ vadāmi.

<pb ed="M" n="3.0299"/> <pb ed="T" n="3.0461"/>
“There are these seven kinds of offerings made to the Saṅgha, Ānanda. Which seven?

One gives a gift to a Saṅgha of both bhikkhus and bhikkhunīs headed by the Buddha; this is the first kind of offering made to the Saṅgha ( saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā).

One gives a gift to a Saṅgha of both bhikkhus and bhikkhunīs after the Tathāgata has attained final Nibbāna; this is the second kind of offering made to the Saṅgha ( saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā).

One gives a gift to a Saṅgha of bhikkhus; this is the third kind of offering made to the Saṅgha ( saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā).

One gives a gift to a Saṅgha of bhikkhunīs; this is the fourth kind of offering made to the Saṅgha ( saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā).

One gives a gift, saying: ‘Appoint so many bhikkhus and bhikkhunīs for me from the Saṅgha’; this is the fifth kind of offering made to the Saṅgha ( saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā).

One gives a gift, saying: ‘Appoint so many bhikkhus for me from the Saṅgha’; this is the sixth kind of offering made to the Saṅgha ( saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā).

One gives a gift, saying: ‘Appoint so many bhikkhunīs for me from the Saṅgha’; this is the seventh kind of offering made to the Saṅgha ( saṅghagatā dakkhiṇā).

“But in the future, Ānanda, there will be clansmen with an ochre robe around their necks who are unvirtuous ( dussīla) and bad-natured (pāpadhamma). People will give gifts to those immoral persons for the sake of the Saṅgha. Even then, Ānanda, I say that an offering made to the Saṅgha is incalculable (asaṅkheyyaṃ), immeasurable (appameyyaṃ). And I say that in no way does a gift to a person individually (pāṭipuggalikaṃ dānaṃ) ever have greater fruit than an offering made to the Saṅgha ( saṅghagatāya dakkhiṇāya).

381. “Ānanda, there are these four kinds of purification of offerings (dakkhiṇā visuddhiyo). What are the four? Ānanda, there is the offering that is purified by the giver (dāyaka), not by the receiver (paṭiggāhaka). Ānanda, there is the offering that is purified by the receiver, not by the giver. Ānanda, there is the offering that is purified neither by the giver nor by the receiver. Ānanda, there is the offering that is purified both by the giver and by the receiver.

“And how, Ānanda, is the offering purified by the giver, not by the receiver? Here, Ānanda, the giver is virtuous (sīlavā), of good qualities (kalyāṇadhammo), and the receivers are immoral (dussīlā), of evil qualities (pāpadhammā). In this way, Ānanda, the offering is purified by the giver, not by the receiver.

“And how, Ānanda, is the offering purified by the receiver, not by the giver? Here, Ānanda, the giver is immoral, of evil qualities, and the receivers are virtuous (sīlavanto), of good qualities. In this way, Ānanda, the offering is purified by the receiver, not by the giver.

“And how, Ānanda, is the offering purified neither by the giver nor by the receiver? Here, Ānanda, the giver is immoral, of evil qualities, and the receivers are also immoral, of evil qualities. In this way, Ānanda, the offering is purified neither by the giver nor by the receiver.

“And how, Ānanda, is the offering purified both by the giver and by the receiver? Here, Ānanda, the giver is virtuous, of good qualities, and the receivers are also virtuous, of good qualities. In this way, Ānanda, the offering is purified both by the giver and by the receiver. These, Ānanda, are these four purifications of offerings.”

382. Thus spoke the Blessed One. Having said this, the Fortunate One, the Teacher, then said further:

“One who is virtuous gives a gift to the immoral,
Having acquired it righteously, with a very pleased mind,
Believing in the eminent result of karma (kamma phalaṃ),
That offering is purified by the giver.

“One who is immoral gives a gift to the virtuous,
Having acquired it unrighteously, with a displeased mind,
Not believing in the eminent result of karma,
That offering is purified by the receiver.

“One who is immoral gives a gift to the immoral,
Having acquired it unrighteously, with a displeased mind,
Not believing in the eminent result of karma,
I do not say that offering is of great fruit.

“One who is virtuous gives a gift to the virtuous,
Having acquired it righteously, with a very pleased mind,
Believing in the eminent result of karma,
That offering, I say, is of great fruit.

“One who is without craving gives a gift to those without craving,
Having acquired it righteously, with a very pleased mind,
Believing in the eminent result of karma,
That offering is the foremost of material gifts."
That gift, I say, is abundant (sī.).
