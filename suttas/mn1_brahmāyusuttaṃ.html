THUS HAVE I HEARD.
On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the country of the Videhans with a large Sangha of bhikkhus, with about five hundred bhikkhus.

Now on that occasion the brāhmaṇa Brahmāyu was living at Mithilā. He was old, aged, burdened with years, advanced in life, and come to the last stage; he was one hundred and twenty years old. He was a master of the Three Vedas with their vocabularies ( nighaṇḍu), etymology (keṭubha), phonology and etymology (sākkharappabheda), and the histories as a fifth; skilled in philology and grammar (veyyākaraṇo), he was fully versed in natural philosophy (lokāyata) and in the marks of a Great Man (mahāpurisalakkhaṇa).

The brāhmaṇa Brahmāyu heard: “The recluse Gotama, the son of the Sakyans who went forth from a Sakyan clan, has been wandering in the country of the Videhans with a large Sangha of bhikkhus, with about five hundred bhikkhus.

Now a good report of Master Gotama has been spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an Arhat, a fully enlightened Buddha (sammāsambuddho), perfect in knowledge and conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), sublime (sugata), knower of the world (lokavidū), incomparable leader of persons to be tamed (anuttaro purisadammasārathi), teacher of gods and humans (devamanussānaṃ satthā), enlightened (buddha), blessed (bhagavā)’.

He, having realized it by his own super-knowledge (abhiññā), proclaims this world with its gods (sadevakaṃ), Māras (samārakaṃ) and Brahmās (sabrahmakaṃ), its śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas (sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ), its princes and people (sadevamanussaṃ).

He teaches the Dharma that is lovely in its beginning (ādikalyāṇaṃ), lovely in its middle (majjhekalyāṇaṃ), lovely in its ending (pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ), in the spirit (sātthaṃ) and in the letter (sabyañjanaṃ); he reveals the spiritual life (brahmacariyaṃ) that is utterly perfect (kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ) and pure (parisuddhaṃ).

It is good to see such Arhats (arahataṃ)’."

383. It is like this: “Thus I have heard” is the Brahmāyusutta.

There, "mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhi”—mahatā means great by greatness of qualities, and also by greatness of number.

Because in that bhikkhusaṅgha he was greatly endowed with noble qualities such as fewness of wishes, etc., and great also in number, being numbered as five hundred.

By the bhikkhūnaṃ saṅghena, the bhikkhusaṅgha, the meaning is the community of śramaṇas designated as a collection of common view and morality.

Saddhi means together.

"Pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehī"—pañca mattā etesanti pañcamattāni.

Mattāti means pamāṇaṃ vuccati, therefore, just as when " bhojane mattaññūti vutte," one knows the measure in eating, one knows the pamāṇa, that is the meaning; even here, the pañcamattā of those five hundred monks, pañcapamāṇanti, thus the meaning should be understood.

Bhikkhūnaṃ satāni bhikkhusatāni.

By those "pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi."

Vīsavassasatiko means one who is twenty years over a hundred years old.

Tiṇṇaṃ vedāna means of the Ṛgveda, Yajurveda, and Sāmaveda.

Because he has gone to the far shore through the act of lip-striking, therefore pāragū.

Together with the nighaṇḍu and with the keṭubha, sanighaṇḍukeṭubhānaṃ, nighaṇḍū means a treatise that reveals synonyms of names, nighaṇṭu, trees, and so forth.

Keṭubha means a treatise which is a differentiation of actions and kappas for the assistance of poets.

Together with the distinction of syllables, sakkharappabhedānaṃ.

Akkharappabhedo means śikṣā and nirukti.

Itihāsapañcamāna means having made the Atharvaveda the fourth, "Thus it was, thus it was," connected with such statements, the itihāsa known as ancient stories is the fifth for them, therefore itihāsapañcamā, of those itihāsapañcamānaṃ.

And the pada, and the remainder of it, and grammar, he studies and explains, therefore padako vaiyākaraṇo.

Lokāyataṃ is called the treatise on destructive criticism.

Mahāpurisalakkhaṇa means a treatise illuminating the characteristics of great men, such as Buddhas, etc., with a measure of twelve thousand granthas, in which, with a measure of sixteen thousand gāthās, buddhamantā they were called, by whose power "Those endowed with this characteristic are called Buddhas, these are called Pratyekabuddhas, by this the two chief disciples, the eighty great disciples, the Buddha's mother, the Buddha's father, the chief attendant, the chief female attendant, the rājā cakravartī”—this distinction is known.

Anavayo means among these lokāyata characteristics of great men, not less, one who does complete fulfillment, avayo does not exist, it is said.

Avayo means one who is not able to maintain those things in meaning and in text.

At that time, the brahmin Brahmāyu had a young brahmin student named Uttara who was a resident disciple, a master of the Three Vedas, with their vocabularies, etymology, phonology, and the histories as the fifth; skilled in philology and grammar, he was fully versed in natural philosophy (lokāyata) and in the marks of a Great Man (mahāpurisalakkhaṇa).

Then the brahmin Brahmāyu addressed the young brahmin Uttara: "Come, my dear Uttara, the renunciate Gotama, the son of the Śākyans, who went forth from a Śākyan clan, wanders in the country of the Videhans with a large saṃgha of bhikkhus, with about five hundred bhikkhus.

Now concerning that venerable Gotama, such a good reputation has spread: 'Indeed, that Blessed One is an arhat, a fully enlightened Buddha (sammāsambuddha)… it is good to see such arhats.'

Come, my dear Uttara, go to the renunciate Gotama; having gone, find out about the renunciate Gotama: whether the report about that venerable Gotama is actually true, or not; whether that venerable Gotama is such, or not.

Thus, we will know that venerable Gotama."

"But how shall I find out, sir, about that venerable Gotama, whether the report about that venerable Gotama is actually true, or not; whether that venerable Gotama is such, or not?"

"My dear Uttara, the thirty-two marks of a Great Man (dvattiṃsamahāpurisalakkhaṇa) have come down to us in our hymns, and the Great Man who is endowed with them has only two possible destinies, no other.

If he lives the household life, he will become a ruler, a wheel-turning monarch (cakravartin), a righteous king (dharmarājā) of the law, conqueror of the four quarters, who has established the security of his realm and is possessed of the seven treasures (sattaratana).

He has these seven treasures (satta ratanāni): the wheel treasure (cakkaratana), the elephant treasure (hatthiratana), the horse treasure (assaratana), the jewel treasure (maṇiratana), the woman treasure (itthiratana), the steward treasure (gahapatiratana), and the counselor treasure (pariṇāyakaratana) as the seventh.

Moreover, he has more than a thousand sons who are heroes, of heroic stature, conquerors of the hostile army.

He dwells having conquered this earth bounded by the ocean without stick or sword, by the law (dhamma).

But if he goes forth from the household life into homelessness, then he will become an arhat, a fully enlightened Buddha (sammāsambuddho), one who draws back the veil from the world.

But I, my dear Uttara, am the giver of the mantras; you are the receiver of the mantras."

When he walks, he does not raise his thighs, nor does he lower his thighs; he does not bend his thighs forwards, nor does he bend his thighs back.

When the venerable Gotama walks, only the lower part of his body oscillates, and he does not walk with bodily effort.

When the venerable Gotama turns to look, he does so with his whole body; he does not look straight up; he does not look straight down; he does not walk looking about; he looks a plough-yoke’s length before him; beyond that he has unhindered knowledge and vision.

When he goes indoors, he does not raise his body, nor does he lower his body; he does not bend his body forwards, nor does he bend his body back.

He does not turn round too far from the seat nor too near it, and he does not lean on the seat with his hand; he does not throw his body onto the seat.

When seated indoors, he does not fidget with his hands; he does not fidget with his feet; he does not sit with his knees crossed; he does not sit with his ankles crossed; he does not sit with his hand holding his chin.

When seated indoors he is not afraid, he does not shiver and tremble, he is not nervous.

He is without fear, without wavering, without quaking, without trembling, his hair does not stand on end.

The venerable Gotama dwells in seclusion when seated indoors.

When receiving water for the bowl, he does not raise the bowl, nor does he lower the bowl; he does not bend the bowl forwards, nor does he bend the bowl back.

He receives neither too little nor too much water for the bowl.

He washes the bowl without making a splashing noise, he washes the bowl without turning it round, he does not put the bowl on the ground to wash his hands; when his hands are washed, the bowl is washed, and when the bowl is washed, his hands are washed.

He pours the water for the bowl neither too far nor too near, and he does not pour it about.

When receiving rice, he does not raise the bowl, nor does he lower the bowl; he does not bend the bowl forwards, nor does he bend the bowl back.

He receives neither too little rice nor too much rice.

The venerable Gotama takes sauces in the right proportion; he does not exceed the right amount of sauce in the mouthful.

The venerable Gotama turns the mouthful over two or three times in his mouth and then swallows it, and no rice kernel enters his body unchewed, and no rice kernel remains in his mouth; then he takes another mouthful.

The venerable Gotama takes his food experiencing the taste, though not experiencing greed for the taste.

Such is the Master Gotama; such he is, and more than that.”

Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena brahmāyussa brāhmaṇassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṅghena. Atha kho brahmāyu brāhmaṇo sattāhaṃ buddhappamukhaṃ bhikkhusaṅghaṃ paṇītena khādanīyena bhojanīyena sahatthā santappesi sampavāresi. Atha kho bhagavā tassa sattāhassa accayena videhesu cārikaṃ pakkāmi. Atha kho brahmāyu brāhmaṇo acirapakkantassa bhagavato kālamakāsi. Atha kho sambahulā bhikkhū yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘brahmāyu, bhante, brāhmaṇo kālaṅkato. Tassa kā gati, ko abhisamparāyo’’ti? ‘‘Paṇḍito, bhikkhave, brahmāyu brāhmaṇo paccapādi dhammassānudhammaṃ, na ca maṃ dhammādhikaraṇaṃ vihesesi. Brahmāyu, bhikkhave, brāhmaṇo pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti, tattha parinibbāyī, anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Brahmāyusuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.
Then the Blessed One gave Brahmāyu the Brahmin a graduated talk, that is to say: talk on giving (dānakathā), talk on virtue (sīlakathā), talk on heaven (saggakathā); he revealed the danger (ādīnava), degradation (okāra), and defilement (saṃkilesa) in sensual pleasures (kāmānaṃ), and the blessing (ānisaṃsaṃ) of renunciation (nekkhamme).

When the Blessed One knew that the mind of Brahmāyu the Brahmin was ready (kallacittaṃ), receptive (muducittaṃ), free from hindrances (vinīvaraṇacittaṃ), elated (udaggacittaṃ), and confident (pasannacittaṃ), he then revealed the teaching special to the Buddhas (buddhānaṃ sāmukkaṃsikā dhammadesanā): suffering (dukkhaṃ), its origin (samudayaṃ), its cessation (nirodhaṃ), and the path (maggaṃ).

Just as a clean cloth with all marks removed would take dye evenly, so too, while the brahmin Brahmāyu sat there, the spotless immaculate vision of the Dhamma (dhammacakkhuṃ) arose in him: “All that is subject to arising (samudayadhammaṃ) is subject to cessation (nirodhadhammaṃ).”

Then the brahmin Brahmāyu, having seen the Dhamma (diṭṭhadhammo), attained the Dhamma (pattadhammo), understood the Dhamma (viditadhammo), fathomed the Dhamma (pariyogāḷhadhammo), having crossed beyond doubt (tiṇṇavicikiccho), having done away with perplexity (vigatakathaṃkatho), having gained intrepidity (vesārajjappatto), and having become independent of others (aparappaccayo) in the Teacher’s Dispensation (satthusāsane), said this to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ): “Magnificent (abhikkantaṃ), Master Gotama (bho Gotama), Magnificent (abhikkantaṃ), Master Gotama (bho Gotama)! Just as, Master Gotama (bho Gotama), one might set upright what was overturned, or reveal what was hidden, or show the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark so that those with eyes might see forms, just so has the Dhamma (dhammo) been made clear in many ways (anekapariyāyena) by Master Gotama (Gotamena).

I go to the Blessed One Gotama (bhavantaṃ Gotamaṃ) for refuge (saraṇaṃ gacchāmi), and to the Dhamma (dhammañca), and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus (bhikkhusaṅghañca).

May the revered Gotama (bhavaṃ Gotamo) remember me (maṃ dhāretu) as a lay follower (upāsakaṃ) who has gone for refuge (saraṇaṃ gataṃ) from this day forth (ajjatagge), as long as life shall last (pāṇupetaṃ).

May the revered Gotama (bhavaṃ Gotamo) consent (adhivāsetu) to accept tomorrow’s meal (svātanāya bhattaṃ) from me (me), together with the Sangha of bhikkhus (saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṅghenā'ti).”

The Blessed One (bhagavā) consented by maintaining silence (tuṇhībhāvena).

Then, knowing that the Blessed One (bhagavato) had consented (adhivāsanaṃ viditvā), the brahmin Brahmāyu rose from his seat (uṭṭhāyāsanā), and after paying homage to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā), keeping him on his right (padakkhiṇaṃ katvā), he departed (pakkāmi).

Then, when the night had ended (tassā rattiyā accayena), the brahmin Brahmāyu had good food (paṇītaṃ khādanīyaṃ bhojanīyaṃ) of various kinds prepared (paṭiyādāpetvā) in his residence (sake nivesane), and he had the time announced (kālaṃ ārocāpesi) to the Blessed One (bhagavato): “It is time (kālo), Master Gotama (bho Gotama), the meal (bhattaṃ) is ready (niṭṭhitaṃ).”

Then, it being morning (pubbaṇhasamayaṃ), the Blessed One (bhagavā) dressed (nivāsetvā), and taking his bowl and outer robe (pattacīvaramādāya), he went (upasaṅkami) with the Sangha of bhikkhus (saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṅghena) to the brahmin Brahmāyu’s residence (brahmāyussa brāhmaṇassa nivesanaṃ) and sat down (nisīdi) on the seat made ready (paññatte āsane).

Then the brahmin Brahmāyu, for a week (sattāhaṃ), with his own hands (sahatthā), served and satisfied (santappesi sampavāresi) the Sangha of bhikkhus (bhikkhusaṅghaṃ) headed by the Buddha (buddhappamukhaṃ) with various kinds of good food (paṇītena khādanīyena bhojanīyena).

Then, at the end of that week (tassa sattāhassa accayena), the Blessed One (bhagavā) set out to wander (cārikaṃ pakkāmi) in the country of the Videhans (videhesu).

Soon after he had gone (acirapakkantassa), the brahmin Brahmāyu died (kālamakāsi)

Then a number of bhikkhus (sambahulā bhikkhū) went (upasaṅkamiṃsu) to the Blessed One (bhagavā); having approached (upasaṅkamitvā), having greeted the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā), they sat down (nisīdiṃsu) at one side (ekamantaṃ).

As they were sitting at one side (ekamantaṃ nisinnā), those bhikkhus (te bhikkhū) said this (etadavocuṃ) to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ): “Brahmāyu (Brahmāyu), venerable sir (bhante), the brahmin (brāhmaṇo) has died (kālaṅkato). What is his destiny (kā gati), what is his future state (ko abhisamparāyo)?” “Wise (paṇḍito), bhikkhus (bhikkhave), Brahmāyu the brahmin (brahmāyu brāhmaṇo) practiced (paccapādi) the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhamma (dhammassānudhammaṃ), and he did not harass me (na ca maṃ vihesesi) in matters of the Dhamma (dhammādhikaraṇaṃ).

Brahmāyu (Brahmāyu), bhikkhus (bhikkhave), the brahmin (brāhmaṇo), with the destruction (parikkhayā) of the five lower fetters (pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ), has reappeared spontaneously (opapātiko hoti), and there (tattha) he will attain final Nibbāna (parinibbāyī), never to return (anāvattidhammo) from that world (tasmā lokā).”

This (idaṃ) the Blessed One (bhagavā) said (avoca).

Elated (attamanā), those bhikkhus (te bhikkhū) delighted (abhinandunti) in the Blessed One’s statement (bhagavato bhāsitaṃ).

The Brahmāyusutta is finished, the first.
395.Dānakathantiādīni heṭṭhā sutte vitthāritāneva. Paccapādīti paṭipajji. Dhammassānudhammanti imasmiṃ sutte dhammo nāma arahattamaggo, anudhammo nāma heṭṭhimā tayo maggā tīṇi ca sāmaññaphalāni, tāni paṭipāṭiyā paṭilabhīti attho. Na ca maṃ dhammādhikaraṇaṃ vihesesīti mañca dhammakāraṇā na kilamesi, na punappunaṃ kathāpesīti vuttaṃ hoti. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānameva. Tattha parinibbāyīti pana padena desanāya arahatteneva kūṭaṃ gahitanti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Brahmāyusuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
395. dānakathā etc. were already extensively explained in the sutta below. paccapādī means he undertook. dhammassānudhammaṃ means in this sutta, dhamma is the path of Arhatship, anudhammo means the lower three paths and the three fruits of the ascetic life; the meaning is that he attained them in order. na ca maṃ dhammādhikaraṇaṃ vihesesī means he did not weary me because of the Dhamma, he did not make me speak again and again. The rest is clear throughout. However, with the word tattha parinibbāyī, it is as if the peak was seized by the Dhamma through Arhatship.

The Brahmāyu Sutta Commentary in the Papañcasūdanī, Commentary to the Middle Length Discourses, is finished.
