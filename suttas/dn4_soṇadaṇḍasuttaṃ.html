Paragraph 300
Mūla (Source Text)
300. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā aṅgesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi yena campā tadavasari. Tatra sudaṃ bhagavā campāyaṃ viharati gaggarāya pokkharaṇiyā tīre. Tena kho pana samayena soṇadaṇḍo brāhmaṇo campaṃ ajjhāvasati sattussadaṃ satiṇakaṭṭhodakaṃ sadhaññaṃ rājabhoggaṃ raññā māgadhena seniyena bimbisārena dinnaṃ rājadāyaṃ brahmadeyyaṃ.
English Translation
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was traveling among the Aṅgas with a large Saṅgha of monks, about five hundred in all, and he arrived at Campā. There, it is said, the Blessed One was staying near Campā, on the bank of the Gaggarā lotus pond. At that time, the Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa was living in Campā, a place teeming with life, abundant in grass, timber, and water, and rich in grain. It was a royal domain, granted by King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha as a royal gift (rājadāya) and a sacred grant (brahmadeyya).
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
300. Evaṃ me sutaṃ…pe… aṅgesūti soṇadaṇḍasuttaṃ. Tatrāyaṃ apubbapadavaṇṇanā. Aṅgesūti aṅgā nāma aṅgapāsādikatāya evaṃ laddhavohārā jānapadino rājakumārā, tesaṃ nivāso ekopi janapado rūḷhisaddena aṅgāti vuccati, tasmiṃ aṅgesu janapade. Cārikanti idhāpi aturitacārikā ceva nibaddhacārikā ca adhippetā. Tadā kira bhagavato dasasahassilokadhātuṃ olokentassa soṇadaṇḍo brāhmaṇo ñāṇajālassa anto paññāyittha. Atha bhagavā ayaṃ brāhmaṇo mayhaṃ ñāṇajāle paññāyati. ‘Atthi nu khvassupanissayo’ti vīmaṃsanto addasa. ‘Mayi tattha gate etassa antevāsino dvādasahākārehi brāhmaṇassa vaṇṇaṃ bhāsitvā mama santike āgantuṃ na dassanti. So pana tesaṃ vādaṃ bhinditvā ekūnatiṃsa ākārehi mama vaṇṇaṃ bhāsitvā maṃ upasaṅkamitvā pañhaṃ pucchissati. So pañhavissajjanapariyosāne saraṇaṃ gamissatī’ti, disvā pañcasatabhikkhuparivāro taṃ janapadaṃ paṭipanno. Tena vuttaṃ – aṅgesu cārikaṃ caramāno…pe… yena campā tadavasarīti. Gaggarāya pokkharaṇiyā tīreti tassa campānagarassa avidūre gaggarāya nāma rājaggamahesiyā khaṇitattā gaggarāti laddhavohārā pokkharaṇī atthi. Tassā tīre samantato nīlādipañcavaṇṇakusumapaṭimaṇḍitaṃ mahantaṃ campakavanaṃ. Tasmiṃ bhagavā kusumagandhasugandhe campakavane viharati. Taṃ sandhāya gaggarāya pokkharaṇiyā tīreti vuttaṃ. Māgadhena seniyena bimbisārenāti ettha so rājā magadhānaṃ issarattā māgadho. Mahatiyā senāya samannāgatattā seniyo. Bimbīti suvaṇṇaṃ. Tasmā sārasuvaṇṇasadisavaṇṇatāya bimbisāroti vuccati.
English Commentary
Thus I have heard…pe… Soṇadaṇḍasuttaṃ [ends] with "in Aṅga". Here begins the explanation of unusual words. "In Aṅga" means: the *jānapadino rājakumārā*, the princes of the countryside, who have obtained the designation *aṅga* because of their charming and pleasing limbs, their residence, even a single one, is called Aṅga with a conventional term; [that is], in that Aṅga country. "*Cārika*" means: here, both unhurried wandering (*aturitacārikā*) and constant wandering (*nibaddhacārikā*) are intended. Then, it seems that when the Blessed One was looking at the ten-thousand world-system, the brāhmaṇa Soṇadaṇḍa appeared within the net of his knowledge (*ñāṇajāla*). Then the Blessed One [thought]: "This brāhmaṇa appears in my net of knowledge." Reflecting, "'Does he have a supporting condition? (*upanissaya*)'," he saw. "'When I go there, his pupils will not allow me to come near him, having spoken of the virtues (*vaṇṇa*) of the brāhmaṇa with twelve characteristics (*dvādasahākārehi*). But, having broken their argument (*vādaṃ bhinditvā*), having spoken of my virtues (*vaṇṇaṃ bhāsitvā*) with twenty-nine characteristics (*ekūnatiṃsa ākārehi*), he will approach me (*upasaṅkamitvā*) and ask a question (*pañhaṃ pucchissati*). He will go for refuge (*saraṇaṃ gamissati*) at the end of the question and answer (*pañhavissajjanapariyosāne*,'" having seen [this], he set out for that country accompanied by a retinue of five hundred monks (*pañcasatabhikkhuparivāro taṃ janapadaṃ paṭipanno*). Therefore it was said: "wandering in Aṅga (*aṅgesu cārikaṃ caramāno*)…pe… to which Campā he arrived (*yena campā tadavasarīti*)." "On the bank of Gaggara's lotus pond (*gaggarāya pokkharaṇiyā tīreti*)" means: not far from that city of Campā there is a lotus pond (*pokkharaṇī*) named Gaggarā, having obtained the designation "Gaggarā" because it was dug by a chief queen (*rājaggamahesiyā*) named Gaggarā (*gaggarāya nāma*). On its bank, all around, [is] a large *campaka* forest (*mahantaṃ campakavanaṃ*) adorned with flowers of blue (*nīla*), and other five colors (*pañcavaṇṇa*). In that *campaka* forest, fragrant with the scent of flowers (*kusumagandhasugandhe campakavane*), the Blessed One stayed (*viharati*). Referring to that, it was said, "On the bank of Gaggara's lotus pond (*gaggarāya pokkharaṇiyā tīreti vuttaṃ*)." "By Māgadha, Seniya, Bimbisāra (*māgadhena seniyena bimbisārenāti*)" here, that king is called *māgadha* because he is the lord (*issarattā*) of the Magadhas. He is called Seniya because he is endowed with a great army (*mahatiyā senāya samannāgatattā seniyo*). "*Bimbī*" means gold (*suvaṇṇaṃ*). Therefore, he is called Bimbisāra because his complexion (*vaṇṇatāya*) is like excellent gold (*sārasuvaṇṇa*).
Paragraph 301
Mūla (Source Text)
301. Assosuṃ kho campeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā – ‘‘samaṇo khalu bho gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito aṅgesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi campaṃ anuppatto campāyaṃ viharati gaggarāya pokkharaṇiyā tīre. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. So imaṃ lokaṃ sadevakaṃ samārakaṃ sabrahmakaṃ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ pajaṃ sadevamanussaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti. So dhammaṃ deseti ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’ti. Atha kho campeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā campāya nikkhamitvā saṅghasaṅghī saṅghā saṅghī (sī. syā. pī.) gaṇībhūtā yena gaggarā pokkharaṇī tenupasaṅkamanti.
English Translation
301. The *brāhmaṇa* householders of Campā heard: "The *śramaṇa* Gotama, a son of the Śākyas who went forth from the Śākya clan, is wandering in the land of the Aṅgas with a large *bhikkhu-saṅgha*, with about five hundred *bhikkhus*, and he has now arrived at Campā and is living on the banks of Gaggara's lotus pond. Now, a good report of Master Gotama has been spread to this effect: ‘That Blessed One is an *arhat*, a fully-enlightened Buddha (*sammāsambuddho*), perfected in knowledge and conduct (*vijjācaraṇasampanno*), a Well-Farer (*sugato*), Knower of the worlds (*lokavidū*), unequalled Trainer of men to be tamed (*anuttaro purisadammasārathi*), Teacher of gods and humans (*satthā devamanussānaṃ*), a Buddha (*buddho*), a Blessed Lord (*bhagavā*)’." He proclaims this world with its gods (*sadevakaṃ*), its Māras (*samārakaṃ*), and its Brahmās (*sabrahmakaṃ*), this generation with its *śramaṇa*s and *brāhmaṇa*s (*sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ*), its rulers and its people, which he has himself realised by direct knowledge (*abhiññā*). He teaches the *dharma* that is good at the beginning (*ādikalyāṇaṃ*), good in the middle (*majjhekalyāṇaṃ*), and good at the end (*pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ*), with its true meaning (*sātthaṃ*) and well-articulated expression (*sabyañjanaṃ*); he reveals the spiritual life (*brahmacariyaṃ*) that is utterly perfect (*kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ*) and pure (*parisuddhaṃ*). "It's good to see such *arhat*s." Then the *brāhmaṇa* householders of Campā, leaving Campā in crowds, gathered together and went to Gaggara's lotus pond.
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
301-302. Bahū bahū hutvā saṃhatāti saṅghā. Ekekissāya disāya saṅgho etesaṃ atthīti saṅghī. Pubbe nagarassa anto agaṇā bahi nikkhamitvā gaṇataṃ pattāti gaṇībhūtā. Khattaṃ āmantesīti. Khattā vuccati pucchitapañhe byākaraṇasamattho mahāmatto, taṃ āmantesi āgamentūti muhuttaṃ paṭimānentu, mā gacchantūti vuttaṃ hoti.
English Commentary
301-302. *Saṅgha* means many coming together having sacrificed much. *Saṅghī* means that each direction has a *saṅgha*. *Gaṇībhūtā* means previously, those that were not a group inside the city, having exited outside, attained a group. He addressed the steward (*khattaṃ āmantesi*). *Khatta* means a great minister (*mahāmatta*) capable of answering a question that has been asked. He addressed him, meaning, "Let them come, wait a moment, do not let them go," is what was said.
Paragraph 302
Mūla (Source Text)
302. Tena kho pana samayena soṇadaṇḍo brāhmaṇo uparipāsāde divāseyyaṃ upagato hoti. Addasā kho soṇadaṇḍo brāhmaṇo campeyyake brāhmaṇagahapatike campāya nikkhamitvā saṅghasaṅghī saṅghe saṅghī (sī. pī.) saṅghā saṅghī (syā.) gaṇībhūte yena gaggarā pokkharaṇī tenupasaṅkamante. Disvā khattaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘kiṃ nu kho, bho khatte, campeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā campāya nikkhamitvā saṅghasaṅghī gaṇībhūtā yena gaggarā pokkharaṇī tenupasaṅkamantī’’ti? ‘‘Atthi kho, bho, samaṇo gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito aṅgesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi campaṃ anuppatto campāyaṃ viharati gaggarāya pokkharaṇiyā tīre. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. Tamete bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamantī’’ti. ‘‘Tena hi, bho khatte, yena campeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā tenupasaṅkama, upasaṅkamitvā campeyyake brāhmaṇagahapatike evaṃ vadehi – ‘soṇadaṇḍo, bho, brāhmaṇo evamāha – āgamentu kira bhavanto, soṇadaṇḍopi brāhmaṇo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissatī’’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bho’’ti kho so khattā soṇadaṇḍassa brāhmaṇassa paṭissutvā yena campeyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā campeyyake brāhmaṇagahapatike etadavoca – ‘‘soṇadaṇḍo bho brāhmaṇo evamāha – ‘āgamentu kira bhavanto, soṇadaṇḍopi brāhmaṇo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissatī’’’ti.
English Translation
Just then, the Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa had gone up to the upper story of his palace for his midday rest. Soṇadaṇḍa the Brahmin saw the Brahmins and householders of Campā going out from Campā in crowds and groups, in large numbers, heading towards Gaggara's lotus pond. Seeing this, he addressed his steward: "What is the reason, good steward, that the Brahmins and householders of Campā are leaving Campā in such crowds and numbers, heading towards Gaggara's lotus pond?" "There is, sir, the ascetic Gotama, a son of the Śākya clan, who has gone forth from the Śākya family. He is wandering in the Aṅga region with a large Saṅgha of monks, about five hundred monks, and has arrived at Campā and is staying near Gaggara’s lotus pond. And concerning that Venerable Gotama, a good report has spread about: ‘Indeed, the Blessed One is an Arhat, a fully enlightened Buddha ( *sammāsambuddha* ), perfect in knowledge and conduct ( *vijjācaraṇasampanno* ), a Well-Farer ( *sugato* ), knower of the worlds ( *lokavidū* ), incomparable leader of persons to be tamed ( *anuttaro purisadammasārathi* ), teacher of gods and humans ( *satthā devamanussānaṃ* ), enlightened ( *buddho* ), blessed ( *bhagavā* ). That is why they are going to see the Venerable Gotama.’" "Then, good steward, go to the Brahmins and householders of Campā and tell them: 'The Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa says this: "Please wait, gentlemen, the Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa will also go to see the *samaṇa* Gotama."'" "As you say, sir," the steward replied to Soṇadaṇḍa the Brahmin, and then went to the Brahmins and householders of Campā. Approaching them, he said, "The Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa says this: 'Please wait, gentlemen, the Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa will also go to see the *samaṇa* Gotama.'"

Khattā vuccati pucchitapañhe byākaraṇasamattho mahāmatto, taṃ āmantesi āgamentūti muhuttaṃ paṭimānentu, mā gacchantūti vuttaṃ hoti.

301-302. saṅgha means many coming together, offering many sacrifices. saṅghī means each direction has a community/assemblage for them. gaṇībhūtā means previously, those who were not a group inside the city, went outside and attained the state of being a group. He addressed the steward. *khattā* means a great minister capable of explaining questions that are asked. He addressed him, meaning, "Let them come; let them wait a moment; let them not go," that is what was said.
Paragraph 303
Mūla (Source Text)
303. Tena kho pana samayena nānāverajjakānaṃ brāhmaṇānaṃ pañcamattāni brāhmaṇasatāni campāyaṃ paṭivasanti kenacideva karaṇīyena. Assosuṃ kho te brāhmaṇā – ‘‘soṇadaṇḍo kira brāhmaṇo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissatī’’ti. Atha kho te brāhmaṇā yena soṇadaṇḍo brāhmaṇo tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā soṇadaṇḍaṃ brāhmaṇaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘saccaṃ kira bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissatī’’ti? ‘‘Evaṃ kho me, bho, hoti – ‘ahampi samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissāmī’’’ti. ‘‘Mā bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkami. Na arahati bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamituṃ. Sace bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissati, bhoto soṇadaṇḍassa yaso hāyissati, samaṇassa gotamassa yaso abhivaḍḍhissati. Yampi bhoto soṇadaṇḍassa yaso hāyissati, samaṇassa gotamassa yaso abhivaḍḍhissati , imināpaṅgena na arahati bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamituṃ; samaṇotveva gotamo arahati bhavantaṃ soṇadaṇḍaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamituṃ. ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo ubhato sujāto mātito ca pitito ca, saṃsuddhagahaṇiko yāva sattamā pitāmahayugā akkhitto anupakkuṭṭho jātivādena. Yampi bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo ubhato sujāto mātito ca pitito ca, saṃsuddhagahaṇiko yāva sattamā pitāmahayugā akkhitto anupakkuṭṭho jātivādena, imināpaṅgena na arahati bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamituṃ; samaṇotveva gotamo arahati bhavantaṃ soṇadaṇḍaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamituṃ. ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo aḍḍho mahaddhano mahābhogo…pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo ajjhāyako , mantadharo, tiṇṇaṃ vedānaṃ pāragū sanighaṇḍukeṭubhānaṃ sākkharappabhedānaṃ itihāsapañcamānaṃ padako veyyākaraṇo, lokāyatamahāpurisalakkhaṇesu anavayo…pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo abhirūpo dassanīyo pāsādiko paramāya vaṇṇapokkharatāya samannāgato brahmavaṇṇī brahmavacchasī brahmaḍḍhī (sī.), brahmavaccasī (pī.) akhuddāvakāso dassanāya…pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo sīlavā vuddhasīlī vuddhasīlena samannāgato…pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo kalyāṇavāco kalyāṇavākkaraṇo poriyā vācāya samannāgato vissaṭṭhāya anelagalāya aneḷagalāya (sī. pī.), anelagaḷāya (ka) atthassa viññāpaniyā…pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo bahūnaṃ ācariyapācariyo tīṇi māṇavakasatāni mante vāceti. Bahū kho pana nānādisā nānājanapadā māṇavakā āgacchanti bhoto soṇadaṇḍassa santike mantatthikā mante adhiyitukāmā …pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo jiṇṇo vuddho mahallako addhagato vayoanuppatto; samaṇo gotamo taruṇo ceva taruṇapabbajito ca…pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo rañño māgadhassa seniyassa bimbisārassa sakkato garukato mānito pūjito apacito…pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo brāhmaṇassa pokkharasātissa sakkato garukato mānito pūjito apacito…pe… ‘‘Bhavañhi soṇadaṇḍo campaṃ ajjhāvasati sattussadaṃ satiṇakaṭṭhodakaṃ sadhaññaṃ rājabhoggaṃ, raññā māgadhena seniyena bimbisārena dinnaṃ, rājadāyaṃ brahmadeyyaṃ. Yampi bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo campaṃ ajjhāvasati sattussadaṃ satiṇakaṭṭhodakaṃ sadhaññaṃ rājabhoggaṃ, raññā māgadhena seniyena bimbisārena dinnaṃ, rājadāyaṃ brahmadeyyaṃ. Imināpaṅgena na arahati bhavaṃ soṇadaṇḍo samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamituṃ; samaṇotveva gotamo arahati bhavantaṃ soṇadaṇḍaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamitu’’nti.
English Translation
Now at that time, some five hundred Brahmins from various provinces were residing in Campā (Campā) on some business. The Brahmins heard, "It is said that the Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa (Śoṇadaṇḍa) will visit the ascetic Gotama (Gautama) to see him." Then those Brahmins approached the Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa; having approached, they said this to the Brahmin Soṇadaṇḍa: "Is it true, sir, that you, Soṇadaṇḍa, will visit the ascetic Gotama to see him?" "Yes, indeed, sirs, it occurs to me: 'I too will visit the ascetic Gotama to see him.'" "Sir, do not visit the ascetic Gotama to see him. It is not proper that you, Soṇadaṇḍa, should visit the ascetic Gotama to see him. If you, Soṇadaṇḍa, visit the ascetic Gotama to see him, your, Soṇadaṇḍa's, fame will decrease, and the ascetic Gotama's fame will increase. Moreover, your, Soṇadaṇḍa's, fame will decrease, and the ascetic Gotama's fame will increase; for this reason, it is not proper that you, Soṇadaṇḍa, should visit the ascetic Gotama to see him; rather, the ascetic Gotama should visit you, Soṇadaṇḍa, to see you. "For you, Soṇadaṇḍa, are well-born on both sides, from mother and father, of pure descent up to the seventh generation of ancestors, unblemished, irreproachable in regard to birth. Moreover, because you, Soṇadaṇḍa, are well-born on both sides, from mother and father, of pure descent up to the seventh generation of ancestors, unblemished, irreproachable in regard to birth, for this reason it is not proper that you, Soṇadaṇḍa, should visit the ascetic Gotama to see him; rather, the ascetic Gotama should visit you, Soṇadaṇḍa, to see you. "For you, Soṇadaṇḍa, are rich, with great wealth and great possessions... Furthermore, the Reverend Soṇadaṇḍa is a scholar, versed in the mantras, accomplished in the Three Vedas (Tri-veda), a skilled expounder of the rules and rituals, the lore of sounds and meanings and, fifthly, oral tradition — an expounder, fully versed in natural philosophy and the marks of a Great Man… Moreover, the Reverend Soṇadaṇḍa is handsome, comely, and graceful, possessing supreme beauty of complexion, with sublime beauty and sublime presence... )... irreproachable in appearance... Moreover, the Reverend Soṇadaṇḍa is virtuous, mature in virtue, endowed with mature virtue... Furthermore, the Reverend Soṇadaṇḍa is of good speech, speaks pleasantly, endowed with polished speech, fluent, without hesitation, understandable in meaning... Furthermore, the Reverend Soṇadaṇḍa is the teacher of many teachers, he teaches the *mantras* (mantra) to three hundred young Brahmins. 303. *Nānāverajjakānanti* means born in various kingdoms, in different kingdoms like Kāśi, Kosala, etc.; or those kingdoms are their residences, or from there they have come, therefore *nānāverajjakā*; to those various foreigners. *Kenacideva karaṇīyenāti* means in that city, brahmins gather for two reasons—either for experiencing sacrifice, or for the purpose of reciting mantras. Then in that city, there was no *yamaññā*. But near Soṇadaṇḍa, they gathered for the purpose of reciting mantras. Referring to that, it was said, "*kenacideva karaṇīyenā*." Having heard of his going, they thought, "This Soṇadaṇḍa is a well-known *brāhmaṇa*, and most of the other *brāhmaṇas* have gone to the *śramaṇa* Gotama for refuge; only he has not gone. If he goes there himself, surely he will be captivated by the captivating illusion of the *śramaṇa* Gotama, and he will go to him for refuge. Then there will be no gathering of *brāhmaṇas* even at his house-door." "Let us create an obstacle to his going," having conferred, they went there. Referring to that, it was said, "*atha kho te brāhmaṇātiādi vuttaṃ*." There, *imināpaṅgenāti* means also by this reason. Thus, having spoken this reason, again, "There is no one named *atussanakasatto* when his own qualities are being spoken. We will prevent his going by speaking about Handa's qualities," thinking thus, indeed, Soṇadaṇḍa is well-born on both sides, they spoke such reasons. *Ubhatoti* means by two sides. "*Mātito ca pitito cāti* means Bhoto's mother is a *brāhmaṇī*, his mother's mother is a *brāhmaṇī*, even her mother is a *brāhmaṇī*; his father is a *brāhmaṇa*, his father's father is a *brāhmaṇa*, even his father is a *brāhmaṇa*. Thus, Bhavaṃ is well-born on both sides, from the mother's side and from the father's side. *Saṃsuddhagahaṇikoti* means their mothers' wombs are pure, this is the meaning. "*Samavepākiniyā gahaṇiyāti*" here, however, the *kammajatejodhātu* is called "*gahaṇī*." *Yāva sattamā pitāmahayugāti* here, the father's father is the paternal grandfather, the grandfather's lineage is the paternal grandfather's lineage. *Yuganti* means it is called the measure of life. This is merely a verbal expression. In reality, however, the paternal grandfather himself is the *pitāmahayuga*. From that upward, all the former men are included by the term *pitāmahaggahaṇeneva*. Thus, up to the seventh person, he is *saṃsuddhagahaṇiko*. Or else, they show that he is *akkhitto anupakuṭṭho jātivādenāti*. *Akkhittoti* means "*apanetha etaṃ, kiṃ iminā*," thus, *akkhitto anavakkhitto*. *Anupakuṭṭhoti* means he is not *upakuṭṭho*, he has never received abuse or scorn. By what reason? By reason of caste. *Itipi* means "*hīnajātiko eso*," the meaning is by such words. *Aḍḍhoti* means he is powerful ( *īśvara*). *Mahaddhanoti* means he is endowed with great wealth. Indeed, in Bhavaṃ's house, there is much wealth like gravel on the earth, but the *śramaṇa* Gotama is without wealth, filling his stomach with alms food and maintaining himself, they show. *Mahābhogoti* means having great enjoyment, having great experience of the five desirable qualities. Thus, whatever qualities they speak of, thinking, "We will show the opposite of those qualities, indeed, the lack of qualities of the Bhagavat," they speak. *Abhirūpoti* means he is more handsome, more beautiful than other men. *Dassanīyoti* means he is fit to be seen, since he does not cause satiety even to those who see him for a day. *Dassaneneva cittapasādajananato pāsādiko* means he is pleasing since he produces clarity of mind just by being seen. *Pokkharatā vuccati sundarabhāvo*, *vaṇṇassa pokkharatā vaṇṇapokkharatā*, by that, he is endowed with excellence of complexion, this is the meaning. Thus, *vaṇṇapokkharatā* refers to the supreme, excellent, and utterly pure complexion, as well as the perfection of the body's form. This is the meaning. *Brahmavaṇṇī* means having the finest complexion. *Parisuddhavaṇṇesupi seṭṭhena suvaṇṇavaṇṇena samannāgato* means possessing a golden complexion that is superior even among pure complexions. If while someone is speaking, saliva drips, spittle flows, or phlegm comes out, that speech is called impeded (*elagaḷaṃ*). The meaning is the opposite of that (*tabbiparitāyāti*). *Atthassa viññāpaniyāti ādimajjhapariyosānaṃ pākaṭaṃ katvā bhāsitatthassa viññāpanasamatthāya* means capable of making the meaning known, making clear the meaning of what is spoken by making the beginning, middle, and end evident. *Jiṇṇoti jarājiṇṇatāya jiṇṇo* means aged, old due to old age. *Vuddhoti aṅgapaccaṅgānaṃ vuddhibhāvamariyādappatto* means increased, having attained the limit of the state of growth of the limbs and features. *Mahallakoti jātimahallakatāya samannāgato* means elderly, possessing the elderyness of birth. *Cirakālappasutoti vuttaṃ hoti* means it is said to be long-lived. *Addhagatoti addhānaṃ gato, dve tayo rājaparivaṭṭe atītoti adhippāyo* means aged, he has traveled the road, the intention is that two or three reigns have passed. *Vayoanuppattoti pacchimavayaṃ sampatto, pacchimavayo nāma vassasatassa pacchimo tatiyabhāgo* means he has attained the last age, the last age is the last third part of a hundred years. 311-313. "Sujaṃ paggaṇhantānaṃ" means that among the *brāhmaṇas* (Brahmins) who receive the *sujaṃ* for the purpose of sacrifice and offering, whether the first or the second. Tasmā āsanā me etaṃ bhavaṃ gotamo paccuṭṭhānaṃ dhāretūti, iminā kira sadiso kuhako dullabho, bhagavati panassa agāravaṃ nāma natthi, tasmā bhoganāsanabhayā kuhanavasena evaṃ vadati. Parapadesupi eseva nayo. Dhammiyā kathāyātiādīsu taṅkhaṇānurūpāya dhammiyā kathāya diṭṭhadhammikasamparāyikaṃ atthaṃ sandassetvā kusale dhamme samādapetvā gaṇhāpetvā. Tattha naṃ samuttejetvā saussāhaṃ katvā tāya ca saussāhatāya aññehi ca vijjamānaguṇehi sampahaṃsetvā dhammaratanavassaṃ vassitvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkāmi. Brāhmaṇo pana attano kuhakatāya evampi bhagavati dhammavassaṃ vassite visesaṃ nibbattetuṃ nāsakkhi. Kevalamassa āyatiṃ nibbānatthāya vāsanābhāgiyāya ca sabbā purimapacchimakathā ahosīti.
English Commentary
319-322. The meaning of the word *svātanāyāti* should be understood in the same way as the word *ajjatanāyāti* was explained here. "Lest that assembly despise me," thinking thus, "because you, seeing me from afar, [think,] 'This Soṇadaṇḍa is old, having lived a long time; Gotama, however, is young and youthful, not even his grandson is a match for him.' That one rises from his seat for someone who has not even attained the status of his own grandson!" thus, they would despise me. "Gotama rises to greet me from (his) seat" means there is no non-rising of mine due to disrespect; however, I will not rise due to fear of disturbing my meal, this you should know, indeed, both you and I. Therefore, "Let Gotama retain (his) rising to greet me from (his) seat," thus (he thought). Truly, a cheat like this is hard to find; however, there is no disrespect for the Blessed One in him, therefore he speaks thus through pretense due to fear of disturbing the meal. And the same rule applies to other instances. In the case of "through talk consistent with the Dharma," and so on, having shown the benefit that is visible in this life and the benefit in the future life through talk consistent with the Dharma appropriate to the occasion, having induced (him) in wholesome principles, having made (him) grasp (them). There, having roused him, having made (him) enthusiastic, and having gladdened (him) with that enthusiasm and with other existing virtues, having rained down a rain of the jewel of the Dharma, having risen from his seat, he departed. However, the *brāhmaṇa* was unable to bring about a distinction in the Blessed One, even in this way, when the rain of the Dharma was rained down, due to his own deceitfulness. The entire previous and subsequent talk of his was only for the sake of inclination towards the element of aspiration for liberation in the future.
Paragraph 322
Mūla (Source Text)
322. Atha kho bhagavā soṇadaṇḍaṃ brāhmaṇaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassetvā samādapetvā samuttejetvā sampahaṃsetvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkāmīti.
English Translation
Then the Blessed One, having instructed the Brahman Sonadanda with a talk on Dhamma, inspired him, encouraged him, gladdened him, rose from his seat and departed.
