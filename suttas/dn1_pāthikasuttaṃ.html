1. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā mallesu viharati anupiyaṃ nāma anuppiyaṃ nāma (syā.) mallānaṃ nigamo. Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya anupiyaṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi. Atha kho bhagavato etadahosi – ‘‘atippago kho tāva anupiyāyaṃ anupiyaṃ (ka.) piṇḍāya carituṃ. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ yena bhaggavagottassa paribbājakassa ārāmo, yena bhaggavagotto paribbājako tenupasaṅkameyya’’nti.
1. Thus have I heard.

Once, the Blessed One (bhagavān) was staying among the Mallas in a Malla town called Anupiya (anupiyaṃ nāma mallānaṃ nigamo).

Then, in the morning, having dressed and taken his bowl and robe, the Blessed One entered Anupiya for alms (piṇḍāya pāvisi).

Then the Blessed One thought: "It is still too early to walk for alms in Anupiya (anupiyāyaṃ piṇḍāya carituṃ)."

"Suppose I were to visit the hermitage of the wanderer Bhaggava-gotta (bhaggavagottassa paribbājakassa ārāmo), or rather, visit the wanderer Bhaggava-gotta himself (bhaggavagotto paribbājako)?"
1.Evaṃme sutaṃ…pe… mallesu viharatīti pāthikasuttaṃ. Tatrāyaṃ apubbapadavaṇṇanā. Mallesu viharatīti mallā nāma jānapadino rājakumārā, tesaṃ nivāso ekopi janapado ruḷhīsaddena ‘‘mallā’’ti vuccati, tasmiṃ mallesu janapade. ‘‘Anupiyaṃ nāma mallānaṃ nigamo’’ti anupiyanti evaṃnāmako mallānaṃ janapadassa eko nigamo, taṃ gocaragāmaṃ katvā ekasmiṃ chāyūdakasampanne vanasaṇḍe viharatīti attho. Anopiyantipi pāṭho. Pāvisīti paviṭṭho. Bhagavā pana na tāva paviṭṭho, pavisissāmīti nikkhantattā pana pāvisīti vutto. Yathā kiṃ, yathā ‘‘gāmaṃ gamissāmī’’ti nikkhanto puriso taṃ gāmaṃ apattopi ‘‘kuhiṃ itthannāmo’’ti vutte ‘‘gāmaṃ gato’’ti vuccati, evaṃ. Etadahosīti gāmasamīpe ṭhatvā sūriyaṃ olokentassa etadahosi. Atippago khoti ativiya pago kho, na tāva kulesu yāgubhattaṃ niṭṭhitanti. Kiṃ pana bhagavā kālaṃ ajānitvā nikkhantoti? Na ajānitvā. Paccūsakāleyeva hi bhagavā ñāṇajālaṃ pattharitvā lokaṃ volokento ñāṇajālassa anto paviṭṭhaṃ bhaggavagottaṃ channaparibbājakaṃ disvā ‘‘ajjāhaṃ imassa paribbājakassa mayā pubbe katakāraṇaṃ samāharitvā dhammaṃ kathessāmi, sā dhammakathā assa mayi pasādappaṭilābhavasena saphalā bhavissatī’’ti ñatvāva paribbājakārāmaṃ pavisitukāmo atippagova nikkhami. Tasmā tattha pavisitukāmatāya evaṃ cittaṃ uppādesi.
1. Thus it was heard by me…pe… he was dwelling among the Mallas, this is the Pāthika Sutta. Herein is an explanation of the apubbapadavaṇṇanā.

Mallesu viharati means: Mallā were the name of royal princes of the country. Their residence, even a single district, is called Mallā by convention, in that Mallā district.

"Anupiyaṃ nāma mallānaṃ nigamo" means: Anupiya is the name of a market town of the Mallas' country. Having made that a pasture village, he was dwelling in a certain grove endowed with shade and water, is the meaning. Anopiyaṃ is also a reading.

Pāvisī means: he entered. But the Blessed One had not yet entered; he was said to have entered because he had departed intending to enter. How is it? Just as a man who has set out, saying, "I will go to the village," is said to have "gone to the village" even if he has not reached that village when asked, "Where is so-and-so?". Thus it is.

Etadahosī means: while standing near the village and looking at the sun, this thought occurred to him.

Atippago kho means: it is very early, the rice gruel meal is not yet finished in the families.

But did the Blessed One set out without knowing the time? No, not without knowing. For in the early morning, the Blessed One, having spread the net of knowledge, surveyed the world. Seeing Bhaggava Gotra, the hidden wanderer, enter within the net of knowledge, he thought, "Today, having gathered the cause formerly made by me for this wanderer, I will preach the Dharma. That Dharma talk will be fruitful for him because of gaining faith in me," and knowing thus, intending to enter the wanderer's park, he set out very early. Therefore, because of the desire to enter there, he produced such a thought.

2. Atha kho bhagavā yena bhaggavagottassa paribbājakassa ārāmo, yena bhaggavagotto paribbājako tenupasaṅkami. Atha kho bhaggavagotto paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘etu kho, bhante, bhagavā. Svāgataṃ, bhante, bhagavato. Cirassaṃ kho, bhante, bhagavā imaṃ pariyāyamakāsi yadidaṃ idhāgamanāya. Nisīdatu, bhante, bhagavā, idamāsanaṃ paññatta’’nti. Nisīdi bhagavā paññatte āsane. Bhaggavagottopi kho paribbājako aññataraṃ nīcaṃ āsanaṃ gahetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho bhaggavagotto paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘purimāni, bhante, divasāni purimatarāni sunakkhatto licchaviputto yenāhaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā maṃ etadavoca – ‘paccakkhāto dāni mayā, bhaggava, bhagavā. Na dānāhaṃ bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharāmī’ti. Kaccetaṃ, bhante, tatheva, yathā sunakkhatto licchaviputto avacā’’ti? ‘‘Tatheva kho etaṃ, bhaggava, yathā sunakkhatto licchaviputto avaca’’.
2. Then the Blessed One went to the hermitage of the wanderer Bhaggava-gotta, and approached the wanderer Bhaggava-gotta.

Then the wanderer Bhaggava-gotta said this to the Blessed One: "Come, Blessed Lord. Welcome, Blessed Lord! It has been long, Blessed One, since you arranged to come here. Be seated, Blessed One, this seat is prepared."

The Blessed One sat down on the prepared seat.

The wanderer Bhaggava-gotta also took a low seat, sat down on one side.

Sitting to one side, the wanderer Bhaggava-gotta said this to the Blessed One: "Some days ago Sunakkhatta the Licchavi came to me and said: 'Bhaggava, I have renounced the Blessed One. I am no longer under the Lord’s rule.'"

"Is that as it is, venerable sir, just as Sunakkhatta the Licchavi said?" "It is just as Sunakkhatta the Licchavi said, Bhaggava."
2.Etadavocāti bhagavantaṃ disvā mānathaddhataṃ akatvā satthāraṃ paccuggantvā etaṃ etu kho, bhantetiādikaṃ vacanaṃ avoca. Imaṃ pariyāyanti imaṃ vāraṃ, ajja imaṃ āgamanavāranti attho. Kiṃ pana bhagavā pubbepi tattha gatapubboti? Na gatapubbo, lokasamudācāravasena pana evamāha. Lokiyā hi cirassaṃ āgatampi anāgatapubbampi manāpajātikaṃ āgataṃ disvā ‘‘kuto bhavaṃ āgato, cirassaṃ bhavaṃ āgato, kathaṃ te idhāgamanamaggo ñāto, kiṃ maggamūḷhosī’’tiādīni vadanti. Tasmā ayampi lokasamudācāravasena evamāhāti veditabbo. Idamāsananti attano nisinnāsanaṃ papphoṭetvā sampādetvā dadamāno evamāha. Sunakkhatto licchaviputtoti sunakkhatto nāma licchavirājaputto. So kira tassa gihisahāyo hoti, kālena kālaṃ tassa santikaṃ gacchati. Paccakkhātoti ‘‘paccakkhāmi dānāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ na dānāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharissāmī’’ti evaṃ paṭiakkhāto nissaṭṭho pariccatto.
2. " Etadavocā ti": Having seen the Blessed One, without making a show of pride, having gone to greet the teacher ( satthāraṃ), he spoke these words beginning with "etu kho, bhante."
"Imaṃ pariyāyanti": This turn ( vāraṃ), today is the turn for arrival ( āgamanavāraṃ), this is the meaning.
What, was the Blessed One there before? No, he was not there before; however, he spoke thus according to worldly custom ( lokasamudācāravasena).
For worldly people ( lokiyā) having seen an agreeable person who has arrived after a long time or who has not arrived before say, "From where have you come, you have come after a long time, how was the path to here known by you, have you lost your way?" and so on.
Therefore, he also spoke thus according to worldly custom; this should be understood.
"Idamāsananti": Having prepared and offered his own sitting place ( nisinnāsanaṃ), he spoke thus.
"Sunakkhatto licchaviputtoti": Sunakkhatta was the name of the Licchavi prince ( licchavirājaputto).
It seems he was a householder friend ( gihisahāyo) of his; he goes to his presence from time to time.
"Paccakkhātoti": "I reject ( paccakkhāmi) giving, venerable sir, to the Blessed One; I will live intending the Blessed One" - thus, he was rejected ( paṭiakkhāto), cast out ( nissaṭṭho), abandoned ( pariccatto).

3. Purimāni, bhaggava, divasāni purimatarāni sunakkhatto licchaviputto yenāhaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā maṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho, bhaggava, sunakkhatto licchaviputto maṃ etadavoca – ‘paccakkhāmi dānāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ. Na dānāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharissāmī’ti. ‘Evaṃ vutte, ahaṃ, bhaggava, sunakkhattaṃ licchaviputtaṃ etadavocaṃ – ‘api nu tāhaṃ, sunakkhatta, evaṃ avacaṃ, ehi tvaṃ, sunakkhatta, mamaṃ uddissa viharāhī’ti? ‘No hetaṃ, bhante’. ‘Tvaṃ vā pana maṃ evaṃ avaca – ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharissāmī’ti? ‘No hetaṃ, bhante’. ‘Iti kira, sunakkhatta, nevāhaṃ taṃ vadāmi – ehi tvaṃ, sunakkhatta, mamaṃ uddissa viharāhīti. Napi kira maṃ tvaṃ vadesi – ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharissāmīti. Evaṃ sante, moghapurisa, ko santo kaṃ paccācikkhasi? Passa, moghapurisa, yāvañca yāva ca (ka.) te idaṃ aparaddha’nti.
3. “Bhaggava, a few days ago, Sunakkhatta the Licchavi came to me, saluted me, and sat down to one side.

Sitting to one side, Bhaggava, Sunakkhatta the Licchavi said this to me: ‘I renounce the Blessed One, Bhante (venerable sir).

And I will no longer live under the authority of the Blessed One, Bhante.’

“When this was said, I said this to Sunakkhatta the Licchavi: ‘Sunakkhatta, did I ever say to you, "Come, Sunakkhatta, live under my authority?"’ ‘No, Bhante.’

“Or did you ever say this to me: ‘I will live under the authority of the Blessed One, Bhante’?” ‘No, Bhante.’

“So, Sunakkhatta, I never said to you, ‘Come, Sunakkhatta, live under my authority.’

And you never said to me, ‘I will live under the authority of the Blessed One, Bhante.’

This being so, foolish man, who are you renouncing? Look, foolish man, how much wrong (aparaddha) has been done by you.”
3.Bhagavantaṃ uddissāti bhagavā me satthā ‘‘bhagavato ahaṃ ovādaṃ paṭikaromī’’ti evaṃ apadisitvā. Ko santo kaṃ paccācikkhasīti yācako vā yācitakaṃ paccācikkheyya, yācitako vā yācakaṃ. Tvaṃ pana neva yācako na yācitako, evaṃ sante, moghapurisa, ko santo ko samāno kaṃ paccācikkhasīti dasseti. Passa moghapurisāti passa tucchapurisa. Yāvañca te idaṃ aparaddhanti yattakaṃ idaṃ tava aparaddhaṃ, yattako te aparādho tattako dosoti evāhaṃ bhaggava tassa dosaṃ āropesinti dasseti.
3. bhagavantaṃ uddissā means "Bhagavān is my teacher; thus, having indicated, 'I will follow the advice of the Bhagavān.'"

ko santo kaṃ paccācikkhasī means, "Should a beggar refuse a borrowed object, or should a borrower refuse a beggar?"

It indicates, "You are neither a beggar nor a borrower; thus, foolish man, who are you to refuse someone?"

passa moghapurisā means "See, foolish man."

yāvañca te idaṃ aparaddha means, "However much this is your fault, Bhaggava, to that extent I will attribute blame to him."

4. ‘Na hi pana me, bhante, bhagavā uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ karotī’ti. ‘Api nu tāhaṃ, sunakkhatta, evaṃ avacaṃ – ehi tvaṃ, sunakkhatta, mamaṃ uddissa viharāhi, ahaṃ te uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ karissāmī’ti? ‘No hetaṃ, bhante’. ‘Tvaṃ vā pana maṃ evaṃ avaca – ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharissāmi, bhagavā me uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ karissatī’ti? ‘No hetaṃ, bhante’. ‘Iti kira, sunakkhatta, nevāhaṃ taṃ vadāmi – ehi tvaṃ, sunakkhatta, mamaṃ uddissa viharāhi, ahaṃ te uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ karissāmī’ti; napi kira maṃ tvaṃ vadesi – ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharissāmi, bhagavā me uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ karissatī’ti. Evaṃ sante, moghapurisa , ko santo kaṃ paccācikkhasi? Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, sunakkhatta, kate vā uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriye akate vā uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriye yassatthāya mayā dhammo desito so niyyāti takkarassa sammā dukkhakkhayāyā’ti? ‘Kate vā, bhante, uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriye akate vā uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriye yassatthāya bhagavatā dhammo desito so niyyāti takkarassa sammā dukkhakkhayāyā’ti. ‘Iti kira, sunakkhatta, kate vā uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriye, akate vā uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriye, yassatthāya mayā dhammo desito, so niyyāti takkarassa sammā dukkhakkhayāya. Tatra, sunakkhatta, kiṃ uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ kataṃ karissati? Passa, moghapurisa, yāvañca te idaṃ aparaddha’nti.
4. ”Well, Lord bhante, the Blessed One bhagavā does not perform superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ.”

“And did I ever say to you: ‘Come under my rule, Sunakkhatta, and I will perform superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ for you?’” “No, Lord bhante.”

“Or did you ever say to me: ‘Lord bhante, I will be under your rule if you will perform superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ for me?’” “No, Lord bhante.”

“Then it appears, Sunakkhatta, that I made no such promises – ‘Come under my rule, Sunakkhatta, and I will perform superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ for you’ – and you made no such conditions – ‘Lord bhante, I will be under your rule if you will perform superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ for me.’”

“That being the case, foolish man moghapurisa, who are you contradicting? What do you think, Sunakkhatta, whether superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ are performed or not, the Dharma dhamma I have taught leads whoever practices it to the total destruction of suffering dukkhakkhayāya.” “Whether superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ are performed or not, Lord bhante, the Dharma dhamma the Blessed One bhagavatā has taught leads whoever practices it to the total destruction of suffering dukkhakkhayāya.”

“So, Sunakkhatta, whether superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ are performed or not, the Dharma dhamma I have taught, leads whoever practises it to the total destruction of suffering dukkhakkhayāya.

Then what purpose would the performance of superhuman uttarimanussa marvels iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ serve? Look, foolish man moghapurisa, how far you have gone wrong!”
4.Uttarimanussadhammāti pañcasīladasasīlasaṅkhātā manussadhammāuttari. Iddhipāṭihāriyanti iddhibhūtaṃ pāṭihāriyaṃ. Kate vāti katamhi vā. Yassatthāyāti yassa dukkhakkhayassa atthāya. Soniyyāti takkarassāti so dhammo takkarassa yathā mayā dhammo desito, tathā kārakassa sammā paṭipannassa puggalassa sabbavaṭṭadukkhakkhayāya amatanibbānasacchikiriyāya gacchati, na gacchati, saṃvattati, na saṃvattatīti pucchati. Tatra sunakkhattāti tasmiṃ sunakkhatta mayā desite dhamme takkarassa sammā dukkhakkhayāya saṃvattamāne kiṃ uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ kataṃ karissati, ko tena katena attho. Tasmiñhi katepi akatepi mama sāsanassa parihāni natthi, devamanussānañhi amatanibbānasampāpanatthāya ahaṃ pāramiyo pūresiṃ, na pāṭihāriyakaraṇatthāyāti pāṭihāriyassa niratthakataṃ dassetvā ‘‘passa, moghapurisā’’ti dutiyaṃ dosaṃ āropesi.
4. Uttarimanussadhammā: manussa-dhammā that are superior, enumerated as the five precepts and the ten precepts.

Iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ: miraculous power (iddhi) is a miracle (pāṭihāriyaṃ).

Kate vā: or in which one?

Yassatthāyā: for the sake of the cessation of which suffering?

So niyyāti takkarassā: that dhamma, just as the dhamma has been taught by me, goes for the sake of the cessation of all rounds of suffering, for the realization of deathless liberation (amatanibbānasacchikiriyāya) for the individual who is properly practicing it, to the one who does it (kārakassa). Does it go, does it not go, does it lead, does it not lead, he asks.

Tatra sunakkhattā: In that dhamma taught by me, Sunakkhatta, while it leads to the proper cessation of suffering for the one who does it, what superior human qualities (uttarimanussadhammā) or miracle of power (iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ) has been done or will be done, what is the purpose with that having been done?

Indeed, whether that has been done or not done, there is no decline of my teaching (sāsana). For the sake of leading gods and humans to deathless liberation (amatanibbānasampāpanatthāya), I have fulfilled the perfections (pāramiyo), not for the sake of doing miracles (pāṭihāriyakaraṇatthāya), thus having shown the meaninglessness of miracles, he imputed a second fault, saying, "Look, foolish men!"

5. ‘Na hi pana me, bhante, bhagavā aggaññaṃ paññapetī’ti paññāpetīti (pī.)? ‘Api nu tāhaṃ, sunakkhatta, evaṃ avacaṃ – ehi tvaṃ, sunakkhatta, mamaṃ uddissa viharāhi, ahaṃ te aggaññaṃ paññapessāmī’ti? ‘No hetaṃ, bhante’. ‘Tvaṃ vā pana maṃ evaṃ avaca – ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharissāmi, bhagavā me aggaññaṃ paññapessatī’ti? ‘No hetaṃ, bhante’. ‘Iti kira, sunakkhatta, nevāhaṃ taṃ vadāmi – ehi tvaṃ, sunakkhatta, mamaṃ uddissa viharāhi, ahaṃ te aggaññaṃ paññapessāmīti. Napi kira maṃ tvaṃ vadesi – ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ uddissa viharissāmi, bhagavā me aggaññaṃ paññapessatī’ti. Evaṃ sante, moghapurisa, ko santo kaṃ paccācikkhasi? Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, sunakkhatta, paññatte vā aggaññe, apaññatte vā aggaññe, yassatthāya mayā dhammo desito, so niyyāti takkarassa sammā dukkhakkhayāyā’ti? ‘Paññatte vā, bhante, aggaññe, apaññatte vā aggaññe, yassatthāya bhagavatā dhammo desito, so niyyāti takkarassa sammā dukkhakkhayāyā’ti. ‘Iti kira, sunakkhatta, paññatte vā aggaññe, apaññatte vā aggaññe, yassatthāya mayā dhammo desito, so niyyāti takkarassa sammā dukkhakkhayāya. Tatra, sunakkhatta, kiṃ aggaññaṃ paññattaṃ karissati? Passa, moghapurisa, yāvañca te idaṃ aparaddhaṃ’.
5. ‘But surely, venerable, the Blessed One does not teach about the origin (aggaññaṃ paññapetī)’? ‘And did I ever say to you, Sunakkhatta: “Come, Sunakkhatta, live under my guidance, and I will teach you about the origin (aggaññaṃ paññapessāmī)?”’ ‘No, venerable.’

‘Or did you ever say to me: “Venerable, I will live under the Blessed One’s guidance, and the Blessed One will teach me about the origin (aggaññaṃ paññapessatī)?”’ ‘No, venerable.’

‘Then, Sunakkhatta, it seems that neither did I say to you: “Come, Sunakkhatta, live under my guidance, and I will teach you about the origin (aggaññaṃ paññapessāmīti)," nor did you say to me: “Venerable, I will live under the Blessed One’s guidance, and the Blessed One will teach me about the origin (aggaññaṃ paññapessatī).”’

Such being the case, foolish man (moghapurisa), who are you to renounce what? What do you think, Sunakkhatta, whether the origin (aggaññe) is taught or not taught (apaññatte vā aggaññe), the Dhamma I teach leads one who practices it rightly to the complete destruction of suffering (dukkhakkhayāyā)’? ‘Whether the origin (aggaññe) is taught or not taught (apaññatte vā aggaññe), venerable, the Dhamma taught by the Blessed One leads one who practices it rightly to the complete destruction of suffering (dukkhakkhayāyā).’

‘Then, Sunakkhatta, whether the origin (aggaññe) is taught or not taught (apaññatte vā aggaññe), the Dhamma I teach leads one who practices it rightly to the complete destruction of suffering (dukkhakkhayāya).

So what origin (aggaññaṃ) will be taught? Look, foolish man (moghapurisa), how very wrong you are!’
5.Aggaññanti lokapaññattiṃ. ‘‘Idaṃ nāma lokassa agga’’nti evaṃ jānitabbampi aggaṃ mariyādaṃ na taṃ paññapetīti vadati. Sesamettha anantaravādānusāreneva veditabbaṃ.
5.
aggaññanti lokapaññattiṃ.

It is a designation of the world called aggañña.

‘‘idaṃ nāma lokassa agga’’nti evaṃ jānitabbampi aggaṃ mariyādaṃ na taṃ paññapetīti vadati.

Saying "this is called the highest in the world," one does not make known the limit ( maryādā) of what should be known in this way.

sesamettha anantaravādānusāreneva veditabbaṃ.

The rest should be understood according to the immediately preceding statement.

6. Why was this begun by saying "anekapariyāyena kho ti"? Sunakkhatta, after rambling on to this extent, "I will disparage the Blessed One's virtues, I will point out his faults," stood there distressed, without support, and without shelter while listening to the Blessed One's teaching.

Then the Blessed One said, "Sunakkhatta, in this way, remaining in a state of disparagement, you will only incur blame yourself." To show the danger in disparagement, he began with "anekapariyāyenā ti," and so on.

There, anekapariyāyenā ti means with many reasons (anekakāraṇena).

Vajjigāme ti means in the village of the Vajji kings; in the city of Vesālī, no visahī ti means he was not able (nāsakkhi).

So avisahanto ti means that Sunakkhatta, whose mouth was previously insufficient to describe the virtues of the three jewels ( tiṇṇaṃ ratanānaṃ), now speaks disparagingly with that very mouth. Indeed, being unable to practice the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ carituṃ), he has regressed to a lower state, having spoken disparagingly due to his own foolishness (bālatāya avaṇṇaṃ kathetvā hīnāyāvatto).

The Buddha, however, is well-awakened (subuddhova), the Dharma is well-proclaimed (svākkhātova), and the Saṅgha is well-practiced (suppaṭipannova).

Thus, people praising the three jewels will only show your faults (tuyheva dosaṃ dassessantīti).

Iti kho te ti means in this way, Sunakkhatta, they will be speakers (vattāro bhavissanti).

Therefore, when such a fault arises, the Teacher, whose knowledge is unobstructed in the past and future (atītānāgate appaṭihatañāṇo), shows that you will not be able to say, "Even though he knew that such a fault would arise in me, he did not speak beforehand (puretaraṃ na kathesīti)."

Apakkamevā ti, having departed, or the meaning is: "having fallen away" (cuto ti attho).

Yathā taṃ āpāyiko ti, just as a being worthy of being born in the lower realms (apāye nibbattanāraho satto) would depart (apakkameyya), so too, he departed (evameva apakkamīti attho).

7. " Ekamidāhanti": What does this mean? " Iminā kiṃ dasseti?" This sutta is bound by two padas, meaning it does not perform the miracle of power (iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ) and does not explain the origin of the world (aggaññaṃ).
There, “does not explain the origin of the world (aggaññaṃ na paññapeti)” — this pada will show at the end of the sutta.
"Does not perform the miracle of power (pāṭihāriyaṃ na karoti)" — this teaching (desanā) began in accordance with the connectional way of seeing of this pada.

There, "ekamidāhanti": I was in one.
"Samayanti": At one time, it means.
"Thūlūsūti": Thūlū is the name of a country (janapado); there, I live (viharāmi).
"Uttarakā nāmāti": By way of the feminine gender (itthiliṅgavasena), Uttarakā is a town (nigamo) of the country (janapadassa) of the Thūlūs; it means having made that town a pasture village (gocaragāmaṃ katvāti attho).
"Aceloti": Naked (naggo).
"Korakkhattiyoti": A Khattiya whose feet are inside the vaṅka (antovaṅkapādo khattiyo).
"Kukkuravatikoti": One who has undertaken the dog duty (samādinnakukkuravato), he eats having smelled like a dog (sunakho viya ghāyitvā khādati), lies down in another place (uddhanantare nipajjati), and does just the action of a dog (aññampi sunakhakiriyameva karoti).
"Catukkuṇḍikoti": One who is assembled on four sides (catusaṅghaṭṭito), having placed two knees (dve jāṇūni) and two elbows (dve ca kappare) on the ground (bhūmiyaṃ ṭhapetvā), he wanders (vicarati).
"Chamānikiṇṇanti": Strewn on the ground (bhūmiyaṃ nikiṇṇaṃ), thrown, established.

"Mukhenevāti": Without touching with the hand (hatthena aparāmasitvā), he eats the edible (khādanīyaṃ) just with the mouth (mukheneva khādati), he enjoys the enjoyable (bhojanīyampi) just with the mouth (mukheneva bhuñjati).
"Sādhurūpoti": Beautiful form (sundararūpo).
"Ayaṃ samaṇoti": This is a samaṇa of the arahats, it means.
There, "vatāti": It is a particle (nipāto) in the sense of desire (patthanatthe).
Thus, there was a desire in him: “There is no other samaṇa like this samaṇa (iminā samaṇena sadiso añño samaṇo nāma natthi), because this one, due to having few desires (appicchatāya), does not wear clothes (vatthaṃ na nivāseti), thinking ‘this is proliferation (esa papañcoti maññamāno)’, does not even care for a begging bowl (bhikkhābhājanampi na pariharati), he eats just what is strewn on the ground (chamānikiṇṇameva khādati), this is a samaṇa*!
But what are we, samaṇas?" Thus, while walking behind the all-knowing Buddha (sabbaññubuddhassa pacchato carantova), he pondered (vitakkesi) this evil thought (imaṃ pāpakaṃ vitakkaṃ).

Without turning away, he spoke this word beginning with "tvampi nāmāti*".
There, "tvampi nāmāti": The pikāro is in the sense of reproach (garahatthe).
Indeed, the Blessed One (bhagavā), while reproaching him (garahanto hi naṃ), said: "Tvampi nāmāti."
“You too, with such a base intention (tvampi nāma evaṃ hīnajjhāsayo), will declare yourself to be a samaṇa, a son of the Sakyas (ahaṃ samaṇo sakyaputtiyoti evaṃ paṭijānissasī)?" This is the intention here (ayañhettha adhippāyo).

12. "[Because] he did not go well" -- having made the Blessed One's round, he approached the naked ascetic Kaḷāramaṭṭaka.

"He asked a question" -- he asked a profound question that struck at the three characteristics.

"[Because] he does not go well" -- he does not go by means of right knowledge; like a blind man in an uneven place, he slips right there.

He did not see either the beginning or the end.

Then, "He does not go well" -- he did not accomplish it; having accomplished it, he was not able to speak.

"Not going well" -- turning his disheveled hair around, looking, saying, "You have gone forth near an uneducated person; even in an improper place, having gone forth, you wander about asking questions; go away, do not stand in this place!"

"He manifested anger, displeasure, and discontent" -- he made manifest anger in the manner of being angry, displeasure in the manner of being displeased, and discontent, which is of the nature of unhappiness, in the manner of being displeased.

"We assailed" -- we assailed, we struck.

"May it not be for us!" -- Oh, may it not be for me!

There is also the reading, "May it not be for me!"

There, "ma" means a word of use in the sense of ownership; the meaning is, "Oh, may it be mine!"

And further, having thought thus, squatting down, he propitiated him, saying, "Forgive me, venerable sir!"

He too, from here on, [said,] "You will not ask any other question by name."

"Yes, I will not ask."

"If so, go, I forgive you," he sent him away.

There is the following graduated talk (anupubbikathā): One day, it seems, that lion, returning from his range (gocara), saw that jackal (siṅgāla) fleeing in fear. Having become full of compassion (karuññajāta), he said, “Friend (vayasa), do not fear! Stop! What, pray, is your name (ko nāma tva)?”

tañca pajānāmī "and I understand that" means, not only the highest knowledge, but I understand that highest knowledge.

yadabhijānaṃ tathāgato "whatever the Tathāgata has directly known" means, whatever extinction of defilements the Tathāgata knows.

no anayaṃ āpajjatī "he does not fall into misfortune" means, unlike those of other sects who do not know cessation, he does not fall into misfortune, suffering, and calamity.

37. Now, to show what the Tīrthikas assert as the foremost knowledge (aggañña), [the text] begins with "santi bhaggavā," and so forth.

There, "issarakuttaṃ brahmakuttaṃ" means made by Īśvara, made by Brahmā; the meaning is created by Īśvara, created by Brahmā. For Brahmā himself here should be understood as Īśvara in the sense of having supremacy.

"ācariyakaṃ" means the state of a teacher, the doctrine of a teacher (ācariyavādaṃ). There, the doctrine of the teacher is the foremost knowledge (aggaññaṃ). But having made it taught here as the foremost knowledge (aggaññaṃ), he is indeed called the foremost knowledge (aggaññaṃ).

"kathaṃ vihitakaṃ" means by whom was it done? How was it done?

And I am not under the sway of what I know (tañca pajānaṃ na parāmasāmi), and not being under its sway I have come to know for myself that quenching (aparāmasato ca me paccattaññeva nibbuti viditā), by the realization of which the Tathāgata cannot fall into perilous paths (yadabhijānaṃ tathāgato no anayaṃ āpajjati).

47. ‘‘Evaṃvādiṃ kho maṃ, bhaggava, evamakkhāyiṃ eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā asatā tucchā musā abhūtena abbhācikkhanti – ‘viparīto samaṇo gotamo bhikkhavo ca. Samaṇo gotamo evamāha – yasmiṃ samaye subhaṃ vimokkhaṃ upasampajja viharati, sabbaṃ tasmiṃ samaye asubhantveva asubhanteva (sī. syā. pī.) pajānātī’ti sañjānātīti (sī. pī.). Na kho panāhaṃ, bhaggava, evaṃ vadāmi – ‘yasmiṃ samaye subhaṃ vimokkhaṃ upasampajja viharati, sabbaṃ tasmiṃ samaye asubhantveva pajānātī’ti. Evañca khvāhaṃ, bhaggava, vadāmi – ‘yasmiṃ samaye subhaṃ vimokkhaṃ upasampajja viharati, subhantveva tasmiṃ samaye pajānātī’ti. ‘‘Te ca, bhante, viparītā, ye bhagavantaṃ viparītato dahanti bhikkhavo ca. Evaṃpasanno ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavati. Pahoti me bhagavā tathā dhammaṃ desetuṃ, yathā ahaṃ subhaṃ vimokkhaṃ upasampajja vihareyya’’nti.
47. “And I, Bhaggava, who teach this and declare this am wrongly, vainly, lyingly and falsely accused by some ascetics and Brahmins who say: ”The ascetic Gotama is on the wrong track, and so are his monks.
The ascetic Gotama has declared that whoever has attained to the stage of deliverance called ‘the Beautiful’ finds everything repulsive.”

But I, Bhaggava, do not say that whoever has attained to the deliverance called ‘the Beautiful’ finds everything repulsive.
What l say is that whenever anyone has attained to the stage of deliverance called ”the Beautiful”, he knows ‘that it is beautiful.’

“Indeed, Lord, they are on the wrong track themselves who accuse the Lord and his monks of error.
I am so delighted with the Lord
that I think the Lord is able to teach me the dharma in such a way that I can attain and remain in the deliverance called ”the Beautiful”.’
47.Asatāti avijjamānena, asaṃvijjamānaṭṭhenāti attho. Tucchāti tucchena antosāravirahitena. Musāti musāvādena. Abhūtenāti bhūtatthavirahitena. Abbhācikkhantīti abhiācikkhanti. Viparītoti viparītasañño viparītacitto. Bhikkhavo cāti na kevalaṃ samaṇo gotamoyeva, ye ca assa anusiṭṭhiṃ karonti, te bhikkhū ca viparītā. Atha yaṃ sandhāya viparītoti vadanti, taṃ dassetuṃ samaṇo gotamotiādi vuttaṃ. Subhaṃ vimokkhanti vaṇṇakasiṇaṃ. Asubhantvevāti subhañca asubhañca sabbaṃ asubhanti evaṃ pajānāti. Subhantveva tasmiṃ samayeti subhanti eva ca tasmiṃ samaye pajānāti, na asubhaṃ. Bhikkhavo cāti ye te evaṃ vadanti, tesaṃ bhikkhavo ca antevāsikasamaṇā viparītā. Pahotīti samattho paṭibalo.
47. Asatā means by that which is non-existent, in the sense of not being found.

Tucchā means by that which is empty, devoid of inner essence.

Musā means by false speech (mṛṣāvāda).

Abhūtenā means by that which is devoid of truth.

Abbhācikkhantī means they accuse.

Viparīto means with perverted perception (viparītasaṃjño), with perverted mind (viparītacitto).

Bhikkhavo cā means not only the renunciate Gautama, but also those monks (bhikṣu) who do his teaching, those monks are also perverted.

Then, with reference to what do they say "perverted"? To show that, samaṇo gotamo etc. was said.

Subhaṃ vimokkha means color meditation (varṇakasiṇa).

Asubhantvevā means understanding thus, "Both the beautiful (śubha) and the unbeautiful (aśubha) are all unbeautiful (aśubha)."

Subhantveva tasmiṃ samaye means understanding thus, "Only the beautiful (śubha) at that time," not the unbeautiful (aśubha).

Bhikkhavo cā means those renunciates who speak thus, their renunciate disciples are perverted.

Pahotī means capable, competent.

48. ‘‘Dukkaraṃ kho etaṃ, bhaggava, tayā aññadiṭṭhikena aññakhantikena aññarucikena aññatrāyogena aññatrācariyakena subhaṃ vimokkhaṃ upasampajja viharituṃ. Iṅgha tvaṃ, bhaggava, yo ca te ayaṃ mayi pasādo, tameva tvaṃ sādhukamanurakkhā’’ti. ‘‘Sace taṃ, bhante, mayā dukkaraṃ aññadiṭṭhikena aññakhantikena aññarucikena aññatrāyogena aññatrācariyakena subhaṃ vimokkhaṃ upasampajja viharituṃ. Yo ca me ayaṃ, bhante, bhagavati pasādo, tamevāhaṃ sādhukamanurakkhissāmī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano bhaggavagotto paribbājako bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Pāthikasuttaṃ pāṭikasuttantaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ. pāṭikasuttantaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)
48. “It is difficult, Bhaggava, for you, holding different views (aññaditṭhikena), being of different inclinations (aññakhantikena), of different tastes (aññarucikena), following a different discipline (aññatrāyogena), and having had a different teacher (aññatrācariyakena), to attain and abide in the deliverance called "the Beautiful" (subhaṃ vimokkhaṃ).

You must strive hard, Bhaggava, putting your trust (prasāda) in me, you must safeguard that trust (sādhukamanurakkhā) in me."

"Lord (bhante), even if it is hard for me to attain and abide in the deliverance called "the Beautiful" (subhaṃ vimokkhaṃ), with different views (aññadiṭṭhikena), different inclinations (aññakhantikena), different tastes (aññarucikena), a different discipline (aññatrāyogena), and a different teacher (aññatrācariyakena), still I will place my trust (prasāda) in the Blessed One (bhagavati), I will carefully safeguard that trust (sādhukamanurakkhissāmi)."

Thus it was said by the Blessed One (bhagavā).

Bhaggava of the Bhaggava clan, the wanderer (paribbājako), was delighted and rejoiced at the Blessed One's (bhagavato) words (bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti).

The Pāthikasuttaṃ is finished, the first.
48.Dukkaraṃ khoti ayaṃ paribbājako yadidaṃ ‘‘evaṃpasanno ahaṃ, bhante’’tiādimāha, taṃ sāṭheyyena kohaññena āha. Evaṃ kirassa ahosi – ‘‘samaṇo gotamo mayhaṃ ettakaṃ dhammakathaṃ kathesi, tamahaṃ sutvāpi pabbajituṃ na sakkomi, mayā etassa sāsanaṃ paṭipannasadisena bhavituṃ vaṭṭatī’’ti. Tato so sāṭheyyena kohaññena evamāha. Tenassa bhagavā mammaṃ ghaṭṭento viya ‘‘dukkaraṃ kho etaṃ, bhaggava tayā aññadiṭṭhikenā’’tiādimāha. Taṃ poṭṭhapādasutte vuttatthameva. Sādhukamanurakkhāti suṭṭhu anurakkha. Iti bhagavā pasādamattānurakkhaṇe paribbājakaṃ niyojesi. Sopi evaṃ mahantaṃ suttantaṃ sutvāpi nāsakkhi kilesakkhayaṃ kātuṃ. Desanā panassa āyatiṃ vāsanāya paccayo ahosi. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti. Sumaṅgalavilāsiniyā dīghanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Pāthikasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
"Difficult indeed ( dukkaraṃ kho )": This mendicant ( paribbājaka ) said this, beginning with "I am pleased in this way, venerable sir ( evaṃpasanno ahaṃ, bhante )", deceitfully and hypocritically ( sāṭheyyena kohaññena ).

Thus it occurred to him: “The śramaṇa Gautama ( samaṇo gotamo ) spoke this much discourse on the dharma ( dhammakathaṃ ), but having heard it, I am not able to go forth as a renunciate ( pabbajituṃ na sakkomi ); it is fitting that I be one who is similar to one committed to his teaching ( etassa sāsanaṃ paṭipannasadisena bhavituṃ vaṭṭatī )."

Then he said thus deceitfully and hypocritically ( sāṭheyyena kohaññena evamāha ).

Because of that, the Blessed One ( bhagavā ), as if touching a vital spot ( mammaṃ ghaṭṭento viya ), said, "Difficult indeed is this ( dukkaraṃ kho etaṃ ), Bhaggava, for you, holding another view ( tayā aññadiṭṭhikenā )."

That is just the meaning as stated in the Poṭṭhapāda Sūtra ( taṃ poṭṭhapādasutte vuttatthameva ).

"Well guarded ( sādhukamanurakkhā )": Guard well ( suṭṭhu anurakkha ).

Thus, the Blessed One ( bhagavā ) directed the mendicant ( paribbājakaṃ niyojesi ) in guarding only his own serenity ( pasādamattānurakkhaṇe ).

Even he, having heard such a great sūtra ( mahantaṃ suttantaṃ sutvāpi ), was not able to make an end to the afflictions ( nāsakkhi kilesakkhayaṃ kātuṃ ).

The teaching ( desanā ), however, was a condition for the development of his disposition in the future ( āyatiṃ vāsanāya paccayo ahosi ).

The rest is clear throughout ( sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti ).

The Pāthikasuttavaṇṇanā is finished.
