160. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘saṅkhārupapattiṃ saṅkhārūpapattiṃ (syā. kaṃ.), saṅkhāruppattiṃ (sī. pī.) vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi, taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –
160. Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapindika's Park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: "Monks!"

"Venerable sir," those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: "I will teach you the attainment of saṃkhāra (saṅkhārupapattiṃ), monks. Listen to that, pay close attention, I will speak."

"Yes, lord," those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said:
160.Evaṃme sutanti saṅkhārupapattisuttaṃ. Tattha saṅkhārupapattinti saṅkhārānaṃyeva upapattiṃ, na sattassa, na posassa, puññābhisaṅkhārena vā bhavūpagakkhandhānaṃ upapattiṃ.
160.
Thus, me sutanti saṃkhārupapattisuttaṃ.

There, saṃkhārupapattinti is the arising of the saṃkhārānaṃ, not of a being (sattasya), not of a person (posasya), but the arising of the aggregates associated with existence (bhavūpagakkhandhānaṃ) through meritorious intentional action (puṇyābhisaṃkhāreṇa).

161. ‘‘Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu saddhāya samannāgato hoti, sīlena samannāgato hoti, sutena samannāgato hoti, cāgena samannāgato hoti, paññāya samannāgato hoti. Tassa evaṃ hoti – ‘aho vatāhaṃ kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā khattiyamahāsālānaṃ khattiyamahāsālānaṃ vā (syā. kaṃ. pī.) sahabyataṃ upapajjeyya’nti. So taṃ cittaṃ dahati, taṃ cittaṃ adhiṭṭhāti, taṃ cittaṃ bhāveti . Tassa te saṅkhārā ca vihārā vihāro (sī. pī.) ca evaṃ bhāvitā evaṃ bahulīkatā tatrupapattiyā tatrūpapattiyā (syā. kaṃ.), tatruppattiyā (sī. pī.) saṃvattanti. Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, maggo ayaṃ paṭipadā tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati.
“Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu possesses faith (śraddhā), possesses virtue (śīla), possesses learning, possesses generosity (tyāga), and possesses wisdom (prajñā).

It occurs to him: ‘Oh, that on the dissolution of the body (kāya), after death (maraṇa), I might reappear in the company of well-to-do nobles (khattiyamahāsālānaṃ)!’

He fixes his mind on that (cittaṃ), establishes it, develops it.

His mental formations (saṅkhārā) and abodes, thus developed and cultivated, conduce to rebirth there.

This, bhikkhus, is the path (maggo), the way (paṭipadā) that leads to reappearance there.
161.Saddhāya samannāgatoti saddhādayo pañca dhammā lokikā vaṭṭanti. Dahatīti ṭhapeti. Adhiṭṭhātīti patiṭṭhāpeti. Saṅkhārā ca vihārā cāti saha patthanāya saddhādayova pañca dhammā. Tatrupapattiyāti tasmiṃ ṭhāne nibbattanatthāya. Ayaṃ maggo ayaṃ paṭipadāti saha patthanāya pañca dhammāva. Yassa hi pañca dhammā atthi, na patthanā, tassa gati anibaddhā. Yassa patthanā atthi, na pañca dhammā, tassapi anibaddhā. Yesaṃ ubhayaṃ atthi, tesaṃ gati nibaddhā. Yathā hi ākāse khittadaṇḍo aggena vā majjhena vā mūlena vā nipatissatīti niyamo natthi, evaṃ sattānaṃ paṭisandhiggahaṇaṃ aniyataṃ. Tasmā kusalaṃ kammaṃ katvā ekasmiṃ ṭhāne patthanaṃ kātuṃ vaṭṭati.
161. “Being endowed with śraddhā” means the five mundane dharmas beginning with śraddhā endure.

“Burning” means establishing.

“Resolving” means instituting.

"Saṃkhārā and vihārā" means together with aspiration, the five dharmas, beginning with śraddhā.

"Tatrupapattiyā" means for the purpose of rebirth in that place.

“This is the path, this is the practice” means together with aspiration, the five dharmas.

Indeed, for whom there are five dharmas, but no aspiration, for them the destiny is unfixed.

For whom there is aspiration, but not five dharmas, even for them [the destiny is] unfixed.

For whom there are both, for them the destiny is fixed.

Just as in the sky, there is no rule that a thrown stick will fall with its tip, middle, or root, so too, the seizing of rebirth by beings (sattvānām paṭisandhiggahaṇaṃ) is uncertain (aniyataṃ).

Therefore, having performed wholesome karma (kusalaṃ kammaṃ), it is fitting to make aspiration in one place.

Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, maggo ayaṃ paṭipadā tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati.
Again, O monks, a monk possesses faith (saddhā), possesses virtue (sīla), possesses learning (suta), possesses generosity (cāga), and possesses wisdom (paññā).

It occurs to him: ‘Oh, that on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), I might reappear in the company of well-to-do brāhmaṇas (brāhmaṇamahāsālānaṃ)…pe… in the company of well-to-do householders (gahapatimahāsālānaṃ)!’

He fixes his mind on that (taṃ cittaṃ dahati), establishes it (taṃ cittaṃ adhiṭṭhāti), develops it (taṃ cittaṃ bhāveti).

These aspirations (saṅkhārā) and this abiding (vihārā) of his, thus developed (bhāvitā) and cultivated (bahulīkatā), lead to his reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattanti).

This, monks, is the path (maggo), this is the way (paṭipadā) that leads to reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati).

It occurs to him: ‘Oh, that on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), I might reappear in the company of the Four Great Kings gods (cātumahārājikānaṃ devānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjeyya)!’

He fixes his mind on this (taṃ cittaṃ dahati), establishes his mind on this (taṃ cittaṃ adhiṭṭhāti), develops his mind on this (taṃ cittaṃ bhāveti).

These intentions (saṅkhārā) and these practices (vihārā) of his, thus developed (bhāvitā) and cultivated (bahulīkatā), lead to his reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattanti).

This, O bhikkhus, is the path (maggo), this is the way (paṭipadā) that leads to reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati).

These aspirations (saṅkhārā) and this abiding (vihārā) of his, thus developed and cultivated, lead to his reappearance there.

This, bhikkhus, is the path (magga), the way (pratipadā) that leads to reappearance there.

165. “Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu possesses faith (saddhā), possesses virtue (sīla), possesses learning (suta), possesses generosity (cāga), and possesses wisdom (paññā).

He hears that the Brahma of a Thousand (sahasso brahmā) is long-lived (dīghāyuka), beautiful (vaṇṇavā), and enjoys great happiness (sukhabahula).

The Brahma of a Thousand (sahasso brahmā), bhikkhus, pervades and is intent on pervading a world-system of a thousand worlds (sahassilokadhātuṃ), and abides.

And he abides intent on pervading the beings (sattā) that have reappeared there (tattha).

Just as, bhikkhus, a man with good sight (cakkhumā puriso) might take a gallnut (āmaṇḍaṃ) in his hand (hatthe) and review it, so the Brahma of a Thousand (sahasso brahmā), bhikkhus, pervades and is intent on pervading a world-system of a thousand worlds (sahassilokadhātuṃ), and abides.

And he abides intent on pervading the beings (sattā) that have reappeared there (tattha).

It occurs to him: ‘Oh, that on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), I might reappear in the company of the Brahma of a Thousand (sahassassa brahmuno sahabyataṃ upapajjeyya)!’

He fixes his mind (cittaṃ) on that, establishes that thought, develops that thought.

These determinations (saṅkhārā) and abidings (vihārā) of his, thus maintained in being (bhāvitā) and developed (bahulīkatā), lead to his reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattanti).

This, bhikkhus, is the path (maggo), this is the way (paṭipadā) that leads to reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati).
165.Āmaṇḍanti āmalakaṃ. Yathā taṃ parisuddhacakkhussa purisassa sabbasova pākaṭaṃ hoti, evaṃ tassa brahmuno saddhiṃ tattha nibbattasattehi sahassī lokadhātu. Esa nayo sabbattha.
165. āmaṇḍa means āmalakaṃ.

Just as that is entirely clear to a man with perfectly clear eyes, so too is the thousandfold world-system together with the beings reborn there to that Brahmā.

This method applies everywhere.

The bhikkhu thinks: ‘Oh, that on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), I might reappear in the company of the Brahma of a Hundred Thousand (satasahassassa brahmuno sahabyataṃ upapajjeyya)!’

He fixes his mind on this (so taṃ cittaṃ dahati), establishes it (taṃ cittaṃ adhiṭṭhāti), develops it (taṃ cittaṃ bhāveti).

This, bhikkhus, is the path (maggo), the way (paṭipadā) that leads to reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati).

That which is said, "having pervaded and resolved" in all respects, there are five kinds of pervasion: mind-pervasion (cetopharaṇa), kasiṇa-pervasion, divine-eye-pervasion (dibbacakkhupharaṇa), light-pervasion (ālokapharaṇa), and body-pervasion (sarīrapharaṇa).

There, mind-pervasion (cetopharaṇa) is the knowing of the minds of beings in a thousand world-systems (lokadhātu).

Kasiṇa-pervasion is the spreading of the kasiṇa in a thousand world-systems (lokadhātu).

Divine-eye-pervasion (dibbacakkhupharaṇa) is, having increased the light, seeing a thousand world-systems (sahassalokadhātu) with the divine eye (dibba cakkhu).

Light-pervasion (ālokapharaṇa) is just that.

Body-pervasion (sarīrapharaṇa) is the spreading of the light of the body in a thousand world-systems (lokadhātu).

Everywhere, these five pervasions should be spoken without destroying [their meaning].
