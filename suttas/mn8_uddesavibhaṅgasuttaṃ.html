313. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘uddesavibhaṅgaṃ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Tathā tathā, bhikkhave, bhikkhu upaparikkheyya yathā yathā yathā yathāssa (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) upaparikkhato bahiddhā cassa viññāṇaṃ avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ, ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ anupādāya na paritasseyya. Bahiddhā, bhikkhave, viññāṇe avikkhitte avisaṭe sati ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhite anupādāya aparitassato āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇadukkhasamudayasambhavo na hotī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Idaṃ vatvāna sugato uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṃ pāvisi. yathā yathāssa (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)
Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: “Bhikkhus!”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: “Bhikkhus, I will teach you a summary and an exposition (uddesavibhaṅgaṃ).

Listen to that, pay close attention, I will speak.”

“As you say, lord,” those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Bhikkhus, a bhikkhu should examine things in such a way that as he examines, his consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) is neither externally scattered (avikkhittaṃ) and diffused (avisaṭaṃ), nor internally fixated (ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ), and not clinging (anupādāya) he does not become agitated (na paritasseyya).

Bhikkhus, when consciousness (viññāṇe) is not externally scattered (avikkhitte) and diffused (avisaṭe), and not internally fixated (ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhite), and not clinging (anupādāya), he is unagitated (aparitassato), then there is no coming into being of the origination of future birth (jāti), aging (jarā), death (maraṇa), suffering (dukkha), and arising (samudayasambhavo).”

This is what the Blessed One said.

Having said this, the Happy One (sugato) rose from his seat (utthāyāsanā) and went into his dwelling (vihāraṃ pāvisi).
313.Evaṃme sutanti uddesavibhaṅgasuttaṃ. Tattha uddesavibhaṅganti uddesañca vibhaṅgañca, mātikañca vibhajanañcāti attho. Upaparikkheyyāti tuleyya tīreyya pariggaṇheyya paricchindeyya. Bahiddhāti bahiddhāārammaṇesu. Avikkhittaṃ avisaṭanti nikantivasena ārammaṇe tiṭṭhamānaṃ vikkhittaṃ visaṭaṃ nāma hoti, taṃ paṭisedhento evamāha. Ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitanti gocarajjhatte nikantivasena asaṇṭhitaṃ. Anupādāya na paritasseyyāti anupādiyitvā aggahetvā taṃ viññāṇaṃ na paritasseyya. Yathā viññāṇaṃ bahiddhā avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ, ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ anupādāya na paritasseyya, evaṃ bhikkhu upaparikkheyyāti vuttaṃ hoti. Jātijarāmaraṇadukkhasamudayasambhavoti jātijarāmaraṇassa ceva avasesassa ca dukkhassa nibbatti na hotīti attho.
Thus, it is what I have heard (evaṃ me suta), the uddesavibhaṅgasutta.

There, uddesavibhaṅga means uddesa and vibhaṅga, mātika and vibhajana, this is the meaning (uddesaṃca vibhaṅgaṃca, mātikaṃca vibhajanaṃcāti attho).

Upaparikkheyyā means to weigh, to cross over, to grasp, to discriminate (tuleyya tīreyya pariggaṇheyya paricchindeyya).

Bahiddhā means in external objects (bahiddhāārammaṇesu).

Avikkhittaṃ avisaṭa means when the mind stands in an object by way of desire, it is called scattered and diffused. Preventing that, he says thus (nikantivasena ārammaṇe tiṭṭhamānaṃ vikkhittaṃ visaṭaṃ nāma hoti, taṃ paṭisedhento evamāha).

Ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhita means unestablished in the inner sphere of experience (gocarajjhatte nikantivasena asaṇṭhitaṃ).

Anupādāya na paritasseyyā means not clinging, not grasping, he is not agitated by that consciousness (anupādiyitvā aggahetvā taṃ viññāṇaṃ na paritasseyya).

Just as consciousness is neither externally scattered and diffused, nor internally positioned, not clinging he is not agitated, thus should a bhikṣu investigate; it is said (yathā viññāṇaṃ bahiddhā avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ, ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ anupādāya na paritasseyya, evaṃ bhikkhu upaparikkheyyāti vuttaṃ hoti).

Jātijarāmaraṇadukkhasamudayasambhavo means the arising of birth, aging, death, suffering, does not occur, this is the meaning (jātijarāmaraṇassa ceva avasesassa ca dukkhassa nibbatti na hotīti attho).

Atha kho tesaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ, acirapakkantassa bhagavato, etadahosi – ‘‘idaṃ kho no, āvuso, bhagavā saṃkhittena uddesaṃ uddisitvā vitthārena atthaṃ avibhajitvā uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṃ paviṭṭho – ‘tathā tathā, bhikkhave , bhikkhu upaparikkheyya yathā yathā upaparikkhato bahiddhā cassa viññāṇaṃ avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ, ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ anupādāya na paritasseyya. Bahiddhā , bhikkhave, viññāṇe avikkhitte avisaṭe sati ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhite anupādāya aparitassato āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇadukkhasamudayasambhavo na hotī’ti. Ko nu kho imassa bhagavatā saṃkhittena uddesassa uddiṭṭhassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa vitthārena atthaṃ vibhajeyyā’’ti? Atha kho tesaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ etadahosi – ‘‘ayaṃ kho āyasmā mahākaccāno satthu ceva saṃvaṇṇito sambhāvito ca viññūnaṃ sabrahmacārīnaṃ; pahoti cāyasmā mahākaccāno imassa bhagavatā saṃkhittena uddesassa uddiṭṭhassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa vitthārena atthaṃ vibhajituṃ. Yaṃnūna mayaṃ yenāyasmā mahākaccāno tenupasaṅkameyyāma; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ mahākaccānaṃ etamatthaṃ paṭipuccheyyāmā’’ti. Atha kho te bhikkhū yenāyasmā mahākaccāno tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā mahākaccānena saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū āyasmantaṃ mahākaccānaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘Idaṃ kho no, āvuso kaccāna, bhagavā saṃkhittena uddesaṃ uddisitvā vitthārena atthaṃ avibhajitvā uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṃ paviṭṭho – ‘tathā tathā, bhikkhave, bhikkhu upaparikkheyya yathā yathā upaparikkhato bahiddhā cassa viññāṇaṃ avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ, ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ anupādāya na paritasseyya. Bahiddhā, bhikkhave, viññāṇe avikkhitte avisaṭe sati ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhite anupādāya aparitassato āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇadukkhasamudayasambhavo na hotī’ti. Tesaṃ no, āvuso kaccāna, amhākaṃ, acirapakkantassa bhagavato, etadahosi – ‘idaṃ kho no, āvuso, bhagavā saṃkhittena uddesaṃ uddisitvā vitthārena atthaṃ avibhajitvā uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṃ paviṭṭho – tathā tathā, bhikkhave, bhikkhu upaparikkheyya, yathā yathā upaparikkhato bahiddhā cassa viññāṇaṃ avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ anupādāya na paritasseyya. Bahiddhā, bhikkhave, viññāṇe avikkhitte avisaṭe sati ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhite anupādāya aparitassato āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇadukkhasamudayasambhavo na hotī’ti. Ko nu kho imassa bhagavatā saṃkhittena uddesassa uddiṭṭhassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa vitthārena atthaṃ vibhajeyyā’’ti. ‘‘Tesaṃ no, āvuso kaccāna, amhākaṃ etadahosi – ‘ayaṃ kho āyasmā mahākaccāno satthu ceva saṃvaṇṇito, sambhāvito ca viññūnaṃ sabrahmacārīnaṃ. Pahoti cāyasmā mahākaccāno imassa bhagavatā saṃkhittena uddesassa uddiṭṭhassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa vitthārena atthaṃ vibhajituṃ. Yaṃnūna mayaṃ yenāyasmā mahākaccāno tenupasaṅkameyyāma; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ mahākaccānaṃ etamatthaṃ paṭipuccheyyāmā’ti – vibhajatāyasmā mahākaccāno’’ti.

Then, soon after the Blessed One had departed, the monks considered: "Friends, the Blessed One gave a summary in brief without expounding the detailed meaning, and then rose from his seat and entered his dwelling: ‘Monks, a monk should investigate in such a way that–his consciousness neither externally scattered & diffused, nor internally positioned–and by not clinging he does not become agitated.’

If his consciousness is not distracted and scattered externally nor stuck internally, and if by not clinging he does not become agitated, then for him there is no origination of suffering - of birth, ageing, and death in the future.”

Now who will expound in detail the meaning of this summary given in brief by the Blessed One?"

Then the monks considered: "The venerable Mahākaccāna is praised by the Teacher and esteemed by his wise companions in the holy life. The venerable Mahākaccāna is capable of expounding the detailed meaning of this summary given in brief by the Blessed One.

Suppose we went to him and asked him the meaning of this."

Then those monks went to the venerable Mahākaccāna and exchanged greetings with him. When they had concluded their greetings and cordial talk, they sat down to one side.

Sitting to one side, those monks said to the venerable Mahākaccāna:

“The Blessed One gave a summary in brief without expounding the detailed meaning, and then rose from his seat and entered his dwelling: ‘Monks, a monk should investigate in such a way that–his consciousness neither externally scattered & diffused, nor internally positioned–and by not clinging he does not become agitated.’

If his consciousness is not distracted and scattered externally nor stuck internally, and if by not clinging he does not become agitated, then for him there is no origination of suffering - of birth, ageing, and death in the future.”

315. ‘‘‘Seyyathāpi, āvuso, puriso sāratthiko sāragavesī sārapariyesanaṃ caramāno mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato atikkammeva mūlaṃ atikkamma khandhaṃ sākhāpalāse sāraṃ pariyesitabbaṃ maññeyya, evaṃ sampadamidaṃ āyasmantānaṃ satthari sammukhībhūte taṃ bhagavantaṃ atisitvā amhe etamatthaṃ paṭipucchitabbaṃ maññatha. So hāvuso, bhagavā jānaṃ jānāti, passaṃ passati, cakkhubhūto ñāṇabhūto dhammabhūto brahmabhūto vattā pavattā atthassa ninnetā amatassa dātā dhammassāmī tathāgato. So ceva panetassa kālo ahosi yaṃ bhagavantaṃyeva etamatthaṃ paṭipuccheyyātha; yathā vo bhagavā byākareyya tathā naṃ dhāreyyāthā’’’ti. ‘Addhāvuso kaccāna, bhagavā jānaṃ jānāti, passaṃ passati, cakkhubhūto ñāṇabhūto dhammabhūto brahmabhūto vattā pavattā atthassa ninnetā amatassa dātā dhammassāmī tathāgato. So ceva panetassa kālo ahosi yaṃ bhagavantaṃyeva etamatthaṃ paṭipuccheyyāma; yathā no bhagavā byākareyya tathā naṃ dhāreyyāma. Api cāyasmā mahākaccāno satthu ceva saṃvaṇṇito sambhāvito ca viññūnaṃ sabrahmacārīnaṃ. Pahoti cāyasmā mahākaccāno imassa bhagavatā saṃkhittena uddesassa uddiṭṭhassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa vitthārena atthaṃ vibhajituṃ. Vibhajatāyasmā mahākaccāno agaruṃ karitvā’ti. ‘Tena hāvuso, suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’ti. ‘Evamāvuso’ti kho te bhikkhū āyasmato mahākaccānassa paccassosuṃ. Āyasmā mahākaccāno etadavoca – ‘Yaṃ kho no, āvuso, bhagavā saṃkhittena uddesaṃ uddisitvā vitthārena atthaṃ avibhajitvā uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṃ paviṭṭho – tathā tathā, bhikkhave, bhikkhu upaparikkheyya, yathā yathā upaparikkhato bahiddhā cassa viññāṇaṃ avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ anupādāya na paritasseyya, bahiddhā, bhikkhave, viññāṇe avikkhitte avisaṭe sati ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhite anupādāya aparitassato āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇadukkhasamudayasambhavo na hotī’ti. Imassa kho ahaṃ, āvuso, bhagavatā saṃkhittena uddesassa uddiṭṭhassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa evaṃ vitthārena atthaṃ ājānāmi.

316. ‘‘Kathañcāvuso, bahiddhā viññāṇaṃ vikkhittaṃ visaṭanti vuccati? Idhāvuso, bhikkhuno cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā rūpanimittānusāri viññāṇaṃ hoti rūpanimittassādagadhitaṃ …gathitaṃ (sī. pī.) rūpanimittassādavinibandhaṃ …vinibandhaṃ (sī. pī.) rūpanimittassādasaṃyojanasaṃyuttaṃ bahiddhā viññāṇaṃ vikkhittaṃ visaṭanti vuccati. Sotena saddaṃ sutvā…pe… ghānena gandhaṃ ghāyitvā… jivhāya rasaṃ sāyitvā… kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ phusitvā… manasā dhammaṃ viññāya dhammanimittānusārī viññāṇaṃ hoti; dhammanimittassādagadhitaṃ dhammanimittassādavinibandhaṃ dhammanimittassādasaṃyojanasaṃyuttaṃ bahiddhā viññāṇaṃ vikkhittaṃ visaṭanti vuccati. Evaṃ kho āvuso, bahiddhā viññāṇaṃ vikkhittaṃ visaṭanti vuccati.
“How, friends, is consciousness called ‘distracted and scattered externally?’ Here, friends, when a bhikkhu has seen a form with the eye, if his consciousness follows after the sign of form, is tied and shackled by gratification in the sign of form, is fettered by the fetter of gratification in the sign of form, then his consciousness is called ‘distracted and scattered externally.’ When he has heard a sound with the ear…smelt an odour with the nose…tasted a flavour with the tongue…touched a tangible with the body…cognized a mind-object with the mind, if his consciousness follows after the sign of the mind-object, is tied and shackled by gratification in the sign of the mind-object, is fettered by the fetter of gratification in the sign of the mind-object, then his consciousness is called ‘distracted and scattered externally.’ Thus, friends, is consciousness called ‘distracted and scattered externally.’

317. “And how, friends, is consciousness called ‘not distracted and scattered externally’? Here, when a bhikkhu has seen a form with the eye, if his consciousness does not follow after the sign of form (rūpanimitta), is not tied and shackled by gratification in the sign of form (rūpanimittassāda), is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the sign of form, then his consciousness is called ‘not distracted and scattered externally.’

When he has heard a sound with the ear…smelt an odor with the nose…tasted a flavor with the tongue…touched a tangible with the body, cognized a mind-object with the mind, if his consciousness does not follow after the sign of the mind-object (dhammanimitta), is not tied and shackled by gratification in the sign of the mind-object (dhammanimittassāda), is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the sign of the mind-object, then his consciousness is called ‘not distracted and scattered externally.’

Thus, friends, is consciousness called ‘not distracted and scattered externally.’”

318. ‘‘Kathañcāvuso, ajjhattaṃ ajjhattaṃ cittaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) saṇṭhitanti vuccati? Idhāvuso, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Tassa vivekajapītisukhānusāri viññāṇaṃ hoti vivekajapītisukhassādagadhitaṃ vivekajapītisukhassādavinibandhaṃ vivekajapītisukhassādasaṃyojanasaṃyuttaṃ ajjhattaṃ cittaṃ saṇṭhitanti vuccati. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, āvuso, bhikkhu vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Tassa samādhijapītisukhānusāri viññāṇaṃ hoti samādhijapītisukhassādagadhitaṃ samādhijapītisukhassādavinibandhaṃ samādhijapītisukhassādasaṃyojanasaṃyuttaṃ ajjhattaṃ cittaṃ saṇṭhitanti vuccati. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, āvuso, bhikkhu pītiyā ca virāgā upekkhako ca viharati sato ca sampajāno sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṃvedeti, yaṃ taṃ ariyā ācikkhanti – ‘upekkhako satimā sukhavihārī’ti tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Tassa upekkhānusāri viññāṇaṃ hoti upekkhāsukhassādagadhitaṃ upekkhāsukhassādavinibandhaṃ upekkhāsukhassādasaṃyojanasaṃyuttaṃ ajjhattaṃ cittaṃ saṇṭhitanti vuccati. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, āvuso, bhikkhu sukhassa ca pahānā dukkhassa ca pahānā pubbeva somanassadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamā adukkhamasukhaṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Tassa adukkhamasukhānusāri viññāṇaṃ hoti adukkhamasukhassādagadhitaṃ adukkhamasukhassādavinibandhaṃ adukkhamasukhassādasaṃyojanasaṃyuttaṃ ajjhattaṃ cittaṃ saṇṭhitanti vuccati. Evaṃ kho, āvuso, ajjhattaṃ ajjhattaṃ cittaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) saṇṭhitanti vuccati.
318. “And how, friend, is the mind called ‘stuck internally’ (ajjhattaṃ saṇṭhitaṃ)? Here, friend, quite secluded from sensual pleasures (kāmehi), secluded from unwholesome states (akusalehi dhammehi), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought (savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ), with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion (vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ).
If his consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) follows after the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion (vivekajapītisukha), is tied and shackled by gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, is fettered by the fetter of gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, then his mind is called ‘stuck internally’ (ajjhattaṃ cittaṃ saṇṭhitaṃ).

“Again, friend, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought (vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the second jhāna, which has self-confidence and singleness of mind without applied and sustained thought (avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ), with rapture and pleasure born of concentration (samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ).
If his consciousness follows after the rapture and pleasure born of concentration (samādhijapītisukha), is tied and shackled by gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, is fettered by the fetter of gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, then his mind is called ‘stuck internally’.

“Again, friend, with the fading away of delight (pītiyā virāgā), remaining imperturbable (upekkhako), mindful (sato) and clearly aware (sampajāno), he experiences in himself the joy (sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṃvedeti) of which the Noble Ones (ariyā) say: “Happy is he who dwells with equanimity (upekkhako), and mindfulness (satimā), he enters the third jhāna (tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ).
If his consciousness follows after the equanimity (upekkhā), is tied and shackled by gratification in the equanimity and pleasure, is fettered by the fetter of gratification in the equanimity and pleasure, then his mind is called ‘stuck internally’.

“Again, friend, with the abandoning of pleasure (sukhassa pahānā) and pain (dukkhassa pahānā), and with the previous disappearance of joy (somanassa) and grief (domanassānaṃ atthaṅgamā), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna, which has neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukhaṃ) and purity of mindfulness due to equanimity (upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ).
If his consciousness follows after the neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukha), is tied and shackled by gratification in the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, is fettered by the fetter of gratification in the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, then his mind is called ‘stuck internally’.
That is how, friend, the mind is called ‘stuck internally’ (ajjhattaṃ saṇṭhitaṃ).
318.Evaṃ kho, āvuso, ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitanti nikantivasena asaṇṭhitaṃ. Nikantivasena hi atiṭṭhamānaṃ hānabhāgiyaṃ na hoti, visesabhāgiyameva hoti.
Thus indeed, friend, "unestablished internally" means unestablished in the sense of rejection. For, being unestablished in the sense of rejection, it does not partake of decline, but rather, it partakes of distinction.

If his consciousness does not follow after the rapture and pleasure born of concentration (samādhijapītisukha), is not tied and shackled by gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, then his mind is called ‘not stuck internally.’

“Again, with the fading away as well of rapture (pītiyā ca virāgā), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the third jhāna. If his consciousness does not follow after the equanimity (upekkhā), is not tied and shackled by gratification in the pleasure of equanimity, is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the pleasure of equanimity, then his mind is called ‘not stuck internally.’

“Again, with the abandoning of pleasure and pain (sukkhassa ca pahānā dukkhassa ca pahānā), and with the previous disappearance of joy and grief (somanassadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamā), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna, which has neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukhaṃ) and purity of mindfulness due to equanimity (upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ). If his consciousness does not follow after the neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukha), is not tied and shackled by gratification in the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, then his mind is called ‘not stuck internally.’

320. “But how, friend, is there agitation due to non-clinging? Here, friend, an untaught ordinary person who has no regard for noble ones and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, who has no regard for true men and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, regards material form as self, or self as possessed of material form, or material form as in self, or self as in material form. That form of his changes and alters. With the change and becoming otherwise of that material form, his consciousness is preoccupied with the change of material form. Agitation and a constellation of mental states born of preoccupation with the change of form remain obsessing his mind. Because his mind is obsessed, he is frightened, distressed, and anxious, and through non-clinging he becomes agitated. He regards feeling as self … perception as self … volitional formations as self … consciousness as self, or self as possessing consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness. That consciousness of his changes and alters. With the change and becoming otherwise of that consciousness, his consciousness is preoccupied with the change of consciousness. Agitation and a constellation of mental states born of preoccupation with the change of consciousness remain obsessing his mind. Because his mind is obsessed, he is frightened, distressed, and anxious, and through non-clinging he becomes agitated. That is how, friend, there is agitation due to non-clinging.
320.Anupādā paritassanāti satthārā khandhiyavagge ‘‘upādāparitassanañca vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi anupādāaparitassanañcā’’ti (saṃ. ni. 3.7) evaṃ gahetvā paritassanā, aggahetvāva aparitassanā ca kathitā, taṃ mahāthero upādāparitassanameva anupādāparitassananti katvā dassento evamāha. Kathaṃ panesā anupādāparitassanā hotīti. Upādātabbassa abhāvato. Yadi hi koci saṅkhāro nicco vā dhuvo vā attā vā attaniyo vāti gahetabbayuttako abhavissa, ayaṃ paritassanā upādāparitassanāva assa. Yasmā pana evaṃ upādātabbo saṅkhāro nāma natthi, tasmā rūpaṃ attātiādinā nayena rūpādayo upādinnāpi anupādinnāva honti. Evamesā diṭṭhivasena upādāparitassanāpi samānā atthato anupādāparitassanāyeva nāma hotīti veditabbā. Vipariṇāmānuparivattajāti vipariṇāmassa anuparivattanato vipariṇāmārammaṇacittato jātā. Paritassanā dhammasamuppādāti taṇhāparitassanā ca akusaladhammasamuppādā ca. Cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhantīti kusalacittaṃ pariyādiyitvā gahetvā khepetvā tiṭṭhanti. Uttāsavāti bhayatāsenapi sauttāso taṇhātāsenapi sauttāso. Vighātavāti savighāto sadukkho. Apekkhavāti sālayo sasineho. Evaṃ kho, āvuso, anupādā paritassanā hotīti evaṃ maṇikaraṇḍakasaññāya tucchakaraṇḍakaṃ gahetvā tasmiṃ naṭṭhe pacchā vighātaṃ āpajjantassa viya pacchā aggahetvā paritassanā hoti. Aññathā hotī rūpavipariṇāmānuparivattī Vipariṇāmānuparivattajā Paritassanā dhammasamuppādā Cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhantī Uttāsavā Vighātavā Apekkhavā Evaṃ kho, āvuso, anupādā paritassanā hotī
320. “Anupādā paritassanā": The Teacher in the Khandhiya Vagga said, “I will teach you upādāparitassanā and anupādāaparitassanā” (Saṃ. Ni. 3.7). Thus, paritassanā is explained as seizing, and aparitassanā as not seizing. The great elder, showing that upādāparitassana is the same as anupādāparitassana, said thus:

How then does this anupādāparitassanā come to be? From the absence of what should be seized. If there were any saṃkhāra that were permanent (nicca) or lasting (dhruva) or a self (ātmā) or belonged to a self (āttaniyo) that could be rightly seized, then this paritassanā would be just upādāparitassanā. But since there is no saṃkhāra called that which should be seized, therefore, by the method beginning with “form is self” (rūpaṃ attā), forms (rūpādayo) are seized, yet they are just not seized (anupādinnāva honti). Thus, this upādāparitassanā, though the same from the viewpoint of views (diṭṭhivasena), is actually called just anupādāparitassanā in meaning (atthato).

"Aññathā hotī": it changes, it perishes by abandoning its nature. "Rūpavipariṇāmānuparivattī": “My form has changed (mama rūpaṃ vipariṇata)” or “What was, that is not mine (yaṃ ahu, taṃ vata me natthī)”, etc. (Ma. Ni. 1.242). By this method, consciousness of karma (kammaviññāṇaṃ) follows the dissolution (bheda) of form (rūpassa). "Vipariṇāmānuparivattajā": born from the transformation (vipariṇāma) following the mind that has the transformation as its object (vipariṇāmārammaṇacittato jātā). "Paritassanā dhammasamuppādā": craving- paritassanā (taṇhāparitassanā) and the arising of unwholesome things (akusaladhammasamuppādā ca). "Cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhantī": having completely taken possession of the wholesome mind (kusalacittaṃ pariyādiyitvā), they seize it, destroy it, and remain. "Uttāsavā": fearful (bhayatāsenapi) and very anxious (sauttāso), and also very anxious (sauttāso) with craving-fear (taṇhātāsenapi). "Vighātavā": with obstruction (savighāto), with suffering (sadukkho). "Apekkhavā": with attachment (sālayo), with affection (sasineho). "Evaṃ kho, āvuso, anupādā paritassanā hotī": Thus, having taken an empty basket (tucchakaraṇḍakaṃ gahetvā) with the perception of a jeweled basket (maṇikaraṇḍakasaññāya), when that is lost (tasmiṃ naṭṭhe), just as for one who experiences obstruction afterwards (pacchā vighātaṃ āpajjantassa viya), so afterwards, not seizing (aggahetvā), there is paritassanā.

Aññathā hotī, rūpavipariṇāmānuparivattī, vipariṇāmānuparivattajā, paritassanā dhammasamuppādā, cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhantī, uttāsavā, vighātavā, apekkhavā, evaṃ kho, āvuso, anupādā paritassanā hotī.

321. "But how, friend, does non-clinging lead to non-agitation? Here, friend, a well-taught noble disciple who has regard for noble ones, is skilled in their Dhamma, and is well-trained in their Dhamma, who has regard for true men, is skilled in their Dhamma, and is well-trained in their Dhamma, does not regard material form (rūpa) as self (ātmā), or self as possessed of material form, or material form as in self, or self as in material form.

That material form of his changes and becomes otherwise.

With the change and becoming otherwise of that material form, his consciousness (vijñāna) is not preoccupied with the change of material form.

Agitated mental states born of preoccupation with the change of material form do not arise together and remain obsessing his mind.

Because his mind is not obsessed, he is not anxious, distressed, and concerned, and due to non-clinging he does not become agitated.

He does not regard feeling (vedanā) as self… He does not regard perception (saṃjñā) as self… He does not regard formations (saṃskhāra) as self… He does not regard consciousness (vijñāna) as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness.

That consciousness of his changes and becomes otherwise.

With the change and becoming otherwise of that consciousness, his consciousness is not preoccupied with the change of consciousness.

Agitated mental states born of preoccupation with the change of consciousness do not arise together and remain obsessing his mind.

Because his mind is not obsessed, he is not anxious, distressed, and concerned, and through nonclinging he does not become agitated.

That is how, friend, non-clinging leads to non-agitation."

"Friends, when the Blessed One rose from his seat and went into his dwelling after giving a summary in brief without expounding the detailed meaning, that is: ‘Monks, a monk should investigate in such a way that–his consciousness neither externally scattered & diffused, nor internally positioned–by not clinging he does not become agitated.

If his consciousness is not distracted and scattered externally nor stuck internally, and if by not clinging he does not become agitated, then for him there is no origination of suffering - of birth, ageing, and death in the future,’ I understand the detailed meaning of this summary to be thus.

Now, friends, if you wish, go to the Blessed One and ask him about the meaning of this. As the Blessed One explains it to you, so you should remember it."

Pahoti cāyasmā mahākaccāno imassa bhagavatā saṃkhittena uddesassa uddiṭṭhassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa vitthārena atthaṃ vibhajituṃ. Yaṃnūna mayaṃ yenāyasmā mahākaccāno tenupasaṅkameyyāma; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ mahākaccānaṃ etamatthaṃ paṭipuccheyyāmā’ti. ‘‘Atha kho mayaṃ, bhante, yenāyasmā mahākaccāno tenupasaṅkamimha; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ mahākaccānaṃ etamatthaṃ paṭipucchimha. Tesaṃ no, bhante, āyasmatā mahākaccānena imehi ākārehi imehi padehi imehi byañjanehi attho vibhatto’’ti. ‘‘Paṇḍito, bhikkhave, mahākaccāno; mahāpañño, bhikkhave, mahākaccāno. Maṃ cepi tumhe, bhikkhave, etamatthaṃ paṭipuccheyyātha, ahampi evamevaṃ byākareyyaṃ yathā taṃ mahākaccānena byākataṃ. Eso cevetassa eso cetassa (sī. pī.), eso ceva tassa (syā. kaṃ.), esoyeva tassa (ka.) attho. Evañca naṃ dhāreyyāthā’’ti. eso cetassa (sī. pī.), eso ceva tassa (syā. kaṃ.), esoyeva tassa (ka.) Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Uddesavibhaṅgasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.
322. Then those monks, having rejoiced and delighted in the words spoken by the venerable Mahākaccāna, rose from their seats and went to where the Blessed One was. Having approached the Blessed One, and having paid homage to him, they sat down to one side.

Sitting to one side, those monks said this to the Blessed One:

“Venerable sir, when the Blessed One, after giving a summary in brief, without expounding the detailed meaning, rose from his seat and entered his dwelling, [saying]: ‘Bhikkhus, a bhikkhu should examine things in such a way that while he is examining them, his consciousness is not distracted and scattered externally nor stuck internally, and by not clinging he does not become agitated. If his consciousness is not distracted and scattered externally nor stuck internally, and if by not clinging he does not become agitated, then for him there is no origination of suffering - of birth, ageing, and death in the future,’

“Venerable sir, it occurred to us, after the Blessed One had recently departed: ‘The Blessed One, after giving a summary in brief, without expounding the detailed meaning, rose from his seat and entered his dwelling, [saying]: “Bhikkhus, a bhikkhu should examine things in such a way that while he is examining them, his consciousness is not distracted and scattered externally nor stuck internally, and by not clinging he does not become agitated. If his consciousness is not distracted and scattered externally nor stuck internally, and if by not clinging he does not become agitated, then for him there is no origination of suffering - of birth, ageing, and death in the future.” Who now could expound the detailed meaning of this summary given by the Blessed One without expounding the detailed meaning?’ Then, venerable sir, it occurred to us: ‘The venerable Mahākaccāna is praised by the Teacher and esteemed by his wise fellow monks in the spiritual life. The venerable Mahākaccāna is capable of expounding the detailed meaning of this summary given by the Blessed One without expounding the detailed meaning. Suppose we went to him and asked him the meaning of this.’

“Then, venerable sir, we went to the venerable Mahākaccāna and asked him about the meaning. The venerable Mahākaccāna has expounded the meaning to us with these terms, with these statements, with these phrases.”

“Bhikkhus, Mahākaccāna is wise; bhikkhus, Mahākaccāna has great wisdom. If you were to ask me the meaning of this, I would explain it to you in the same way that Mahākaccāna has explained it. This is its meaning, and so you should remember it.”

The Blessed One said this. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Udesavibhanga Sutta, the eighth, is finished.
