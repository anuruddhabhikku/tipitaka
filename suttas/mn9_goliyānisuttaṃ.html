173. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary.

At that time, a monk named Goliyāni, a forest-dweller (āraññika), well-behaved, had come to the Saṃgha for some reason.

Then, venerable Sāriputta, concerning the monk Goliyāni, addressed the monks:

"Friends (āvuso), a forest-dwelling monk (āraññika) who has come to the Saṃgha and is living in the Saṃgha should be respectful (sagārava) and deferential (sappatissa) towards his companions in the holy life (sabrahmacārī). If, friends (āvuso), a forest-dwelling monk (āraññika) who has come to the Saṃgha and is living in the Saṃgha is disrespectful (agārava) and undeferential (appatissa) towards his companions in the holy life (sabrahmacārī), there will be those who would say of him: ‘What has this venerable (āyasmato) forest-dweller (āraññikassa) gained by his dwelling alone in the forest, doing as he likes, since he is disrespectful (agāravo) and undeferential (appatissa) towards his companions in the holy life (sabrahmacārī)?’ There will be those who would say this of him. Therefore, a forest-dwelling monk (āraññikena bhikkhunā) who has come to the Saṃgha and is living in the Saṃgha should be respectful (sagāravena) and deferential (sappatissena) towards his companions in the holy life (sabrahmacārī).

"Friends (āvuso), a forest-dwelling monk (āraññika) who has come to the Saṃgha and is living in the Saṃgha should be skilled in good behaviour regarding seats (āsanakusala) thus: ‘I shall sit down in such a way that I do not encroach upon elder monks (there bhikkhū) and do not deny new monks (nave bhikkhū) a seat (āsana).’ If, friends (āvuso), a forest-dwelling monk (āraññika) who has come to the Saṃgha and is living in the Saṃgha is not skilled in good behaviour regarding seats (na āsanakusalo), there will be those who would say of him: ‘What has this venerable (āyasmato) forest-dweller (āraññikassa) gained by his dwelling alone in the forest, doing as he likes, since he is not even skilled in good behaviour regarding seats (āsanakusalo na hoti)?’ There will be those who would say this of him. Therefore, a forest-dwelling monk (āraññikena bhikkhunā) who has come to the Saṃgha and is living in the Saṃgha should be skilled in good behaviour regarding seats (āsanakusalena bhavitabbaṃ).

"Friends (āvuso), a forest-dwelling monk (āraññika) who has come to the Saṃgha and is living in the Saṃgha should know the rules of conduct (ābhisaṃācārika). If, friends (āvuso), a forest-dwelling monk (āraññika) who has come to the Saṃgha and is living in the Saṃgha does not know the rules of conduct (ābhisaṃācārikaṃ dhammaṃ na jānāti), there will be those who would say of him: ‘What has this venerable (āyasmato) forest-dweller (āraññikassa) gained by his dwelling alone in the forest, since he does not even know the rules of conduct (ābhisaṃācārikaṃpi dhammaṃ)?"

If a forest-dwelling bhikkhu who has come to the saṅgha and is living in the saṅgha is talkative and speaks loosely (vikiṇṇavāco), there will be those who would say of him: ‘What has this venerable forest-dweller gained by his dwelling alone in the forest, doing as he likes, since this venerable one is talkative and speaks loosely?’ Therefore, a forest-dwelling bhikkhu who has come to the saṅgha and is living in the saṅgha should not be talkative and should not speak loosely.

“A forest-dwelling bhikkhu who has come to the saṅgha and is living in the saṅgha should be easy to correct and should associate with good friends (kalyāṇamittena). If a forest-dwelling bhikkhu who has come to the saṅgha and is living in the saṅgha is difficult to correct and associates with bad friends, there will be those who would say of him: ‘What has this venerable forest-dweller gained by his dwelling alone in the forest, doing as he likes, since this venerable one is difficult to correct and associates with bad friends?’ Therefore, a forest-dwelling bhikkhu who has come to the saṅgha and is living in the saṅgha should be easy to correct and should associate with good friends.

“A forest-dwelling bhikkhu should guard the doors of his sense faculties (indriyesu guttadvārena bhavitabbaṃ). If a forest-dwelling bhikkhu does not guard the doors of his sense faculties (indriyesu aguttadvāro), there will be those who would say of him: ‘What has this venerable forest-dweller gained by his dwelling alone in the forest, doing as he likes, since this venerable one does not guard the doors of his sense faculties?’ Therefore, a forest-dwelling bhikkhu should guard the doors of his sense faculties.

a forest-dwelling bhikkhu should apply himself (yogo karaṇīyo) to the higher Dhamma (abhidhamma) and the higher Vinaya (abhivinaya).

“A forest-dwelling bhikkhu should apply himself (yogo karaṇīyo) to those liberations (vimokkhā) that are peaceful (santā) and immaterial (āruppā), transcending forms (atikkamma rūpe).

For there are those who ask a forest-dwelling bhikkhu questions on the liberations (vimokkhā) that are peaceful (santā) and immaterial (āruppā), transcending forms (atikkamma rūpe).

If, friend, a forest-dwelling bhikkhu, when asked a question on those liberations (vimokkhā) that are peaceful (santā) and immaterial (āruppā), transcending forms (atikkamma rūpe), does not apply himself (na sampāyati), there will be those who would say of him:

‘What has this venerable forest-dweller gained by his dwelling alone in the forest, doing as he likes, since he does not apply himself (na sampāyati) to those liberations (vimokkhā) that are peaceful (santā) and immaterial (āruppā), transcending forms (atikkamma rūpe)?’ Since there would be those who would say this of him,

a forest-dwelling bhikkhu should apply himself (yogo karaṇīyo) to those liberations (vimokkhā) that are peaceful (santā) and immaterial (āruppā), transcending forms (atikkamma rūpe).

“A forest-dwelling bhikkhu should apply himself (yogo karaṇīyo) to the superhuman states (uttari manussadhamme).

For there are those who ask a forest-dwelling bhikkhu questions on the superhuman states (uttari manussadhamme).

If, friend, a forest-dwelling bhikkhu, when asked a question on the superhuman states (uttari manussadhamme), does not apply himself (na sampāyati), there will be those who would say of him:

‘What has this venerable forest-dweller gained by his dwelling alone in the forest, doing as he likes, since he does not know (na jānāti) the goal (atthaṃ) for which he went forth (pabbajito)?’ Since there would be those who would say this of him,

a forest-dwelling bhikkhu should apply himself (yogo karaṇīyo) to superhuman states (uttari manussadhamme).”

When this was said, the venerable Mahā Moggallāna asked the venerable Sāriputta: “Friend Sāriputta, should these things be undertaken and practised only by a forest-dwelling bhikkhu, or by a town-dwelling bhikkhu as well?” “Friend Moggallāna, these things should be undertaken and practised not only by a forest-dwelling bhikkhu, but by a town-dwelling bhikkhu as well.”

173. Thus have I heard: the Goliyāni Sutta.

There, padasamācāro means having a weak demeanor, an imposing demeanor, being dependent on requisites, like the Elder Mahārakkhita. It seems that while he was sitting in the house of a supporter, the supporter said, "Venerable sir, a robe was given to me for such-and-such an elder." He said, "Good, you have done well by giving a robe to him who lives considering only that." "Venerable sir, shall I also give to you?" He said, "You will do well, giving to him who considers only that." This too was such an imposing demeanor. Sappatissenā means with his elder, one should not live making oneself the elder. Serivihārenā means with an independent dwelling, with an unrestrained dwelling.

Nānūpakhajjā means not intruding, not entering. There, when two great elders are sitting on either side, one sits touching them with a robe or knee without asking permission; this is called sitting intrusively. But if one does not do this, but stands near one's own allotted seat and is told, "Sit down, friend," one should sit down. If they do not say [anything], one should ask permission and sit down, [saying] "Venerable sirs, may I sit down?" Even if it is said or not said after the time of asking permission, it is indeed proper to sit down.

Na paṭibāhissāmī: Here, one who, passing beyond one's own allotted seat, sits in a place for newcomers, this is called obstructing the new monks with a seat. For when he is sitting thus, the new monks, complaining, "He does not allow us to sit," stand or wander about seeking a seat. Therefore, one should sit in one's own allotted seat. Thus, one does not obstruct.

Nānūpakhajjā na paṭibāhissāmī ābhisamācārikampi dhamma: Ābhisamācārikaṃ dhamma means even just proper conduct and practice. Nātikālenā: one should not enter too early, one should not leave too late; one should enter and leave only with the Saṅgha of monks. For the observances of the courtyard of the shrine, the courtyard of the Bodhi tree, etc., of one who enters too early and leaves too late, are diminished. Having washed one's face early, cutting monkey threads, letting drops of saliva fall, entering the village, seeking rice-gruel, sitting down until alms-time inside the village itself conversing various kinds of animal talk, having done the meal duty, and leaving during the day, one must return to the monastery at the time of washing the monks' feet. Na purebhattaṃ pacchābhattaṃ kulesu cārittaṃ āpajjitabba means, "A monk who, being invited for a meal, without asking a monk who is present, should go wandering in families before or after the meal, except at the right time, commits an offense entailing confession" (Pācittiya 299); one should not undertake wandering before or after the meal, as stated in the analysis of that training rule, guarding that training rule. Uddhato hoti capalo: he is indeed of a haughty nature and is endowed with the fickleness of a young boy, which is called adorning robes, adorning bowls, adorning lodgings, or playing with this stinking body.

Ābhisamācārikampi dhamma nātikālenā na purebhattaṃ pacchābhattaṃ kulesu cārittaṃ āpajjitabba uddhato hoti capalo

Paññavatā bhavitabba: one should be endowed with skillful wisdom in things to be done, such as making robes, etc. Abhidhamme abhivinaye: effort should be made in the Abhidhamma Piṭaka and in the Vinaya Piṭaka, both by way of the Pali text and by way of the commentary. For, in the very last section, the Dhammahadayavibhaṅga is not proper without the pairs and triplets of the Abhidhamma. In the Vinaya, however, the two well-decided Pātimokkhas are not proper without the determination of actions and non-actions.

Āruppā: Thus, all eight attainments ( samāpatti) are stated. However, if one is entirely unable [to attain all eight], effort should be made in seven, or six... or five. With the very last limit, having made one preliminary meditation exercise on a kasiṇa proficient, one should wander taking it up, without this much it is not fitting.

Uttarimanussadhamme: By this, he indicates all supramundane (lokuttara) qualities. Therefore, one should dwell having become an arhat, one who does not realize arhatship should be established in the fruit of non-returning (anāgāmiphala), the fruit of once-returning (sakadāgāmiphala), or the fruit of stream-entry (sotāpattiphala). With the very last method, having made one gateway to insight (vipassanāmukha) proficient up to arhatship, one should wander taking it up. The rest is clear throughout. However, venerable Sāriputta, with reference to a person who needs guidance (neyyapuggala), from the practice of proper conduct (ābhisamācārikavatta) onwards, gradually having caused [him] to attain arhatship, completed this teaching.
