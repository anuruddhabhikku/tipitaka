Paragraph 444
Mūla (Source Text)
444. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ āyasmā ānando sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme aciraparinibbute bhagavati. Tena kho pana samayena subho māṇavo todeyyaputto sāvatthiyaṃ paṭivasati kenacideva karaṇīyena.
English Translation
Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the venerable Ānanda was staying near Śrāvastī, in the Jetavana, at Anāthapindika’s monastery, not long after the Blessed One's *parinirvāṇa*. And at that time, Subha, the young *brāhmaṇa* Todeyyaputta, was staying in Śrāvastī on some business or other.
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
444.Evaṃme sutaṃ…pe… sāvatthiyanti subhasuttaṃ. Tatrāyaṃ anuttānapadavaṇṇanā. Aciraparinibbute bhagavatīti aciraṃ parinibbute bhagavati, parinibbānato uddhaṃ māsamatte kāle. Nidānavaṇṇanāyaṃ vuttanayeneva bhagavato pattacīvaraṃ ādāya āgantvā khīravirecanaṃ pivitvā vihāre nisinnadivasaṃ sandhāyetaṃ vuttaṃ. Todeyyaputtoti todeyyabrāhmaṇassa putto, so kira sāvatthiyā avidūre tudigāmo nāma atthi, tassa adhipatittā todeyyoti saṅkhyaṃ gato. Mahaddhano pana hoti pañcacattālīsakoṭivibhavo, paramamaccharī – ‘‘dadato bhogānaṃ aparikkhayo nāma natthī’’ti cintetvā kassaci kiñci na deti, puttampi āha – Evaṃ adānameva sikkhāpetvā kāyassa bhedā tasmiṃyeva ghare sunakho hutvā nibbatto. Subho taṃ sunakhaṃ ativiya piyāyati. Attano bhuñjanakabhattaṃyeva bhojeti, ukkhipitvā varasayane sayāpeti. Atha bhagavā ekadivasaṃ nikkhante māṇave taṃ gharaṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi. Sunakho bhagavantaṃ disvā bhukkāraṃ karonto bhagavato samīpaṃ gato. Tato naṃ bhagavā avoca ‘‘todeyya tvaṃ pubbepi maṃ ‘bho, bho’ti paribhavitvā sunakho jāto, idānipi bhukkāraṃ katvā avīciṃ gamissasī’’ti. Sunakho taṃ kathaṃ sutvā vippaṭisārī hutvā uddhanantare chārikāya nipanno, manussā naṃ ukkhipitvā sayane sayāpetuṃ nāsakkhiṃsu . Subho āgantvā ‘‘kenāyaṃ sunakho sayanā oropito’’ti āha. Manussā ‘‘na kenacī’’ti vatvā taṃ pavattiṃ ārocesuṃ. Māṇavo sutvā ‘‘mama pitā brahmaloke nibbatto, samaṇo pana gotamo me pitaraṃ sunakhaṃ karoti yaṃ kiñci esa mukhārūḷhaṃ bhāsatī’’ti kujjhitvā bhagavantaṃ musāvādena codetukāmo vihāraṃ gantvā taṃ pavattiṃ pucchi. Bhagavā tassa tatheva vatvā avisaṃvādanatthaṃ āha – ‘‘atthi pana te, māṇava, pitarā na akkhātaṃ dhana’’nti. Atthi, bho gotama, satasahassagghanikā suvaṇṇamālā, satasahassagghanikā suvaṇṇapādukā, satasahassagghanikā suvaṇṇapāti, satasahassañca kahāpaṇanti. Gaccha taṃ sunakhaṃ appodakaṃ madhupāyāsaṃ bhojetvā sayanaṃ āropetvā īsakaṃ niddaṃ okkantakāle puccha, sabbaṃ te ācikkhissati, atha naṃ jāneyyāsi – ‘‘pitā me eso’’ti. So tathā akāsi. Sunakho sabbaṃ ācikkhi, tadā naṃ – ‘‘pitā me’’ti ñatvā bhagavati pasannacitto gantvā bhagavantaṃcuddasa pañhe pucchitvā vissajjanapariyosāne bhagavantaṃ saraṇaṃ gato, taṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ ‘‘subho māṇavo todeyyaputto’’ti. Sāvatthiyaṃ paṭivasatīti attano bhogagāmato āgantvā vasati.
English Commentary
Thus have I heard…pe… the Subha-sūtra in Śrāvastī. Herein is an elucidation of the terms that are not clear. "When the Blessed One had recently attained complete extinction": [This means] when the Blessed One had recently attained complete extinction, [that is,] in the period of about a month after the complete extinction. This is said in connection with the day the Blessed One, having gone to the place for the explanation of the origin, taking his bowl and robe, and having drunk a purgative made with milk, was sitting in the monastery. "Todeyya's son": [He was] the son of the *brāhmaṇa* Todeyya. It seems that near Śrāvastī there was a village named Tuḍi, and because he was the chief of that, he was called Todeyya. Moreover, he was very wealthy, possessing forty-five *koṭis* of riches, [and] extremely miserly, [thinking,] “There is no such thing as the exhaustion of enjoyment in giving,” he gave nothing to anyone. He even instructed his son, teaching only nongiving. After the breakup of his body, he was reborn as a dog in that very house. Subha was very fond of that dog. He would feed it with the same rice and curry that he himself ate, and lifting it up, would make it sleep on a fine bed. Then, one day when the youth had gone out, the Blessed One entered that house for alms. The dog, seeing the Blessed One, barking, went near the Blessed One. Then the Blessed One said to him, “Todeyya, in the past too, having slighted me, [saying,] ‘Hey, you!’ you were born as a dog. Now too, having barked, you will go to Avīci hell.” The dog, having heard that speech, remorseful, lay down on a heap of ashes nearby. The people were not able to lift it up and make it sleep on the bed. Subha came and said, “By whom was this dog taken down from the bed?” The people, saying, “By no one,” reported that happening. The youth, having heard, [thinking,] “My father was reborn in the Brahma-world, [but] the *śramaṇa* Gotama makes my father into a dog, whatever he says that comes to his mouth,” angry, wanting to accuse the Blessed One with a lie, going to the monastery, asked about that happening. The Blessed One, having spoken to him just as before, for the sake of non-deception, said, “Is there any wealth, youth, that was not declared to you by your father?” “There is, O Gotama, a golden garland worth a hundred thousand, golden sandals worth a hundred thousand, a golden bowl worth a hundred thousand, and a hundred thousand *kahāpaṇas*.” "Go, feed that dog with little water and honey-rice, and lifting it up, placing it on the bed, at the time when sleep has overcome it a little, ask [it]. It will tell you everything. Then you will know him: ‘This is my father.’" He did so. The dog told everything. Then, knowing him – “This is my father,” his mind pleased with the Blessed One, going to the Blessed One, having asked the Blessed One fourteen questions, at the end of the explanation, he went for refuge to the Blessed One. With reference to that it was said, “Subha, the youth, Todeyya’s son.” "He dwells in Śrāvastī": [This means that] having come from his own village of enjoyment, he dwells [there].
Paragraph 445
Mūla (Source Text)
445. Atha kho subho māṇavo todeyyaputto aññataraṃ māṇavakaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘ehi tvaṃ, māṇavaka, yena samaṇo ānando tenupasaṅkama; upasaṅkamitvā mama vacanena samaṇaṃ ānandaṃ appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ puccha – ‘subho māṇavo todeyyaputto bhavantaṃ ānandaṃ appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ pucchatī’ti. Evañca vadehi – ‘sādhu kira bhavaṃ ānando yena subhassa māṇavassa todeyyaputtassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkamatu anukampaṃ upādāyā’’’ti.
English Translation
445. Then, Subha, the young Brahmin of the Todeyya clan, addressed a certain young man: "Come, young man, go to where the *śramaṇa* (ascetic) Ānanda is; having gone, ask the *śramaṇa* (ascetic) Ānanda, in my words, if he is free from illness (*appābādhaṃ*), free from affliction (*appātaṅkaṃ*), of light rising (*lahuṭṭhānaṃ*), strong (*balaṃ*), and living in comfort (*phāsuvihāraṃ*)—‘Subha, the young Brahmin of the Todeyya clan, asks the venerable Ānanda if he is free from illness, free from affliction, of light rising, strong, and living in comfort.’" And say thus: ‘It would be good if the venerable Ānanda would, out of compassion (*anukampaṃ upādāya*), visit the dwelling (*nivesanaṃ*) of Subha, the young Brahmin of the Todeyya clan.’"
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
445-446.Aññataraṃ māṇavakaṃ āmantesīti satthari parinibbute ‘‘ānandatthero kirassa pattacīvaraṃ gahetvā āgato, mahājano taṃ dassanatthāya upasaṅkamatī’’ti sutvā ‘‘vihāraṃ kho pana gantvā mahājanamajjhe na sakkā sukhena paṭisanthāraṃ vā kātuṃ, dhammakathaṃ vā sotuṃ gehaṃ āgataṃyeva naṃ disvā sukhena paṭisanthāraṃ karissāmi, ekā ca me kaṅkhā atthi, tampi naṃ pucchissāmī’’ti cintetvā aññataraṃ māṇavakaṃ āmantesi. Appābādhantiādīsu ābādhoti visabhāgavedanā vuccati, yā ekadese uppajjitvā cattāro iriyāpathe ayapaṭṭena ābandhitvā viya gaṇhati, tassā abhāvaṃ pucchāti vadati. Appātaṅkoti kicchajīvitakaro rogo vuccati, tassāpi abhāvaṃ pucchāti vadati. Gilānasseva ca uṭṭhānaṃ nāma garukaṃ hoti, kāye balaṃ na hoti, tasmā niggelaññabhāvañca balañca pucchāti vadati. Phāsuvihāranti gamanaṭhānanisajjasayanesu catūsu iriyāpathesu sukhavihāraṃ pucchāti vadati. Athassa pucchitabbākāraṃ dassento ‘‘subho’’tiādimāha.
English Commentary
445-446. "He addressed a certain youth," means that when the Teacher had attained *parinirvāṇa*, having heard that "The venerable Ānanda has arrived, taking his bowl and robe, and the great multitude is approaching him to see him," thinking, "Having gone to the monastery, it is not possible to exchange friendly greetings with ease or hear the *dharma* talk in the midst of the great multitude. Having seen him when he has come to my house, I will exchange friendly greetings with ease, and I have a certain doubt, and I will ask him about that," he addressed a certain youth. In "appābādha and so on," *ābādha* means a dissimilar feeling. Having arisen in one place, it seizes one as if binding the four postures (*iriyāpatha*) with an iron band. He asks about its absence, he says. "Appātaṅka" means a disease that makes living difficult. He asks about the absence of that also, he says. And for the sick, rising up is difficult, and there is no strength in the body. Therefore, he asks about the state of being without sickness (*niggelaññabhāva*) and strength (*bala*), he says. "Phāsuvihāra" means he asks about pleasant abiding (*sukhavihāra*) in the four postures (*catūsu iriyāpathesu*) of going (*gamana*), standing (*ṭhāna*), sitting (*nisajja*), and lying down (*sayana*), he says. Then, showing the manner of questioning him, he said, "Subha," and so on.
Paragraph 446
Mūla (Source Text)
446. ‘‘Evaṃ, bho’’ti kho so māṇavako subhassa māṇavassa todeyyaputtassa paṭissutvā yenāyasmā ānando tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā ānandena saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho so māṇavako āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘subho māṇavo todeyyaputto bhavantaṃ ānandaṃ appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ pucchati; evañca vadeti – ‘sādhu kira bhavaṃ ānando yena subhassa māṇavassa todeyyaputtassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkamatu anukampaṃ upādāyā’’’ti.
English Translation
446. "So be it, sir," the young man replied to Subha, the student, the son of Todeyya. Then he approached the venerable Ānanda, and after exchanging greetings with the venerable Ānanda, he sat down at one side after concluding their conversation with courteous and amiable talk. Having sat down to one side, that young man said to the venerable Ānanda: "Subha, the student, the son of Todeyya, inquires about your health, freedom from illness, lightness of body, strength and comfortable living, and further says: ‘May the venerable Ānanda be so kind as to visit the dwelling of Subha, the student, the son of Todeyya, out of compassion.’"

Pali Commentary

Appābādhantiādīsu ābādhoti visabhāgavedanā vuccati, yā ekadese uppajjitvā cattāro iriyāpathe ayapaṭṭena ābandhitvā viya gaṇhati, tassā abhāvaṃ pucchāti vadati. English Commentary
445-446. Having heard that when the Teacher had attained *parinirvāṇa*, "The venerable Ānanda has arrived, carrying his bowl and robes, and a great crowd is approaching him to see him," he thought, "It is not easy to go to the monastery and exchange greetings comfortably or hear the *dharma* talk in the midst of a large crowd. Having seen him when he has come to my house, I will greet him comfortably. And I have a question; I will ask him that as well." Having thought this, he addressed a certain youth ( *aññataraṃ māṇavakaṃ āmantesi*). In "freedom from disease," disease (*ābādha*) is said to be disagreeable feeling (*visabhāgavedanā*), which, having arisen in one place, seizes one as if binding one with iron bands in the four postures (*cattāro iriyāpathe*). He asks about the absence of that. In "freedom from sickness" (*appātaṅko*), sickness (*roga*) is said to be that which makes life difficult (*kicchajīvitakaro*). He asks about the absence of that as well. For one who is ill, rising up is difficult, and there is no strength (*balaṃ*) in the body (*kāye*). Therefore, he asks about freedom from illness (*niggelaññabhāvaṃ*) and strength (*balaṃ*). In "comfortable abiding" (*phāsuvihāraṃ*), he asks about a pleasant abiding (*sukhavihāraṃ*) in the four postures (*catūsu iriyāpathesu*) of going, standing, sitting, and lying down (*gamanaṭhānanisajjasayanesu*). Then, showing the way he should be asked, he said, "Well-born" (*subho*), and so forth.
Paragraph 447
Mūla (Source Text)
447. Evaṃ vutte, āyasmā ānando taṃ māṇavakaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘akālo kho, māṇavaka . Atthi me ajja bhesajjamattā pītā. Appevanāma svepi upasaṅkameyyāma kālañca samayañca upādāyā’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bho’’ti kho so māṇavako āyasmato ānandassa paṭissutvā uṭṭhāyāsanā yena subho māṇavo todeyyaputto tenupasaṅkami ; upasaṅkamitvā subhaṃ māṇavaṃ todeyyaputtaṃ etadavoca, ‘‘avocumhā kho mayaṃ bhoto vacanena taṃ bhavantaṃ ānandaṃ – ‘subho māṇavo todeyyaputto bhavantaṃ ānandaṃ appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ pucchati, evañca vadeti – ‘‘sādhu kira bhavaṃ ānando yena subhassa māṇavassa todeyyaputtassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkamatu anukampaṃ upādāyā’’’ti. Evaṃ vutte, bho, samaṇo ānando maṃ etadavoca – ‘akālo kho, māṇavaka. Atthi me ajja bhesajjamattā pītā. Appevanāma svepi upasaṅkameyyāma kālañca samayañca upādāyā’ti. Ettāvatāpi kho, bho, katameva etaṃ, yato kho so bhavaṃ ānando okāsamakāsi svātanāyapi upasaṅkamanāyā’’ti.
English Translation
447. When this was said, the venerable Ānanda said to that young man: "It is not the right time, young man. Today I have taken some medicine (bhesajjamattā pītā). Perhaps it will be possible to come tomorrow when the time (kāla) and the occasion (samaya) are suitable." "Yes, sir," the young man replied to the venerable Ānanda, and rising from his seat, he went to where Subha, the son of Todeyya, was. Having approached, he said to Subha, the son of Todeyya: "We have told that Reverend Ānanda, according to your words, that Subha, the son of Todeyya, asks about the Reverend Ānanda's freedom from illness (appābādhaṃ), freedom from disease (appātaṅkaṃ), quickness of recovery (lahuṭṭhānaṃ), strength (balaṃ), and comfortable living (phāsuvihāraṃ), and also says: 'It would be good if the Reverend Ānanda would come to the residence (nivesanaṃ) of Subha, the son of Todeyya, out of compassion (anukampaṃ).'" When this was said, sir, the *śramaṇa* Ānanda said to me: "It is not the right time, young man. Today I have taken some medicine (bhesajjamattā pītā). Perhaps it will be possible to come tomorrow when the time (kāla) and the occasion (samaya) are suitable." "Even so far, sir, this has been accomplished, that the Reverend Ānanda has probably made an opportunity even for coming tomorrow."
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
447.Kālañca samayañca upādāyāti kālañca samayañca paññāya gahetvā upadhāretvāti attho. Sace amhākaṃ sve gamanakālo bhavissati, kāye balamattā ceva pharissati, gamanapaccayā ca añño aphāsuvihāro na bhavissati, athetaṃ kālañca gamanakāraṇasamavāyasaṅkhātaṃ samayañca upadhāretvā – ‘‘api eva nāma sve āgaccheyyāmā’’ti vuttaṃ hoti.
English Commentary
447. Taking into consideration both time (*kāla*) and circumstance (*samaya*), means taking time (*kāla*) and circumstance (*samaya*) with wisdom (*paññā*), grasping and considering them, this is the meaning. If for us tomorrow will be the time for going (*gamanakāla*), and strength and measure will pervade the body (*kāya*), and another uncomfortable abiding (*aphāsuvihāra*) will not arise from the causes of going (*gamanapaccaya*), then considering this time (*kāla*), reckoned as the collection of causes of going (*gamanakāraṇasamavāya-saṅkhāta*), and also the circumstance (*samaya*), it is said, "Even so, may we come tomorrow."
Paragraph 448
Mūla (Source Text)
448. Atha kho āyasmā ānando tassā rattiyā accayena pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya cetakena bhikkhunā pacchāsamaṇena yena subhassa māṇavassa todeyyaputtassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Atha kho subho māṇavo todeyyaputto yenāyasmā ānando tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā ānandena saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho subho māṇavo todeyyaputto āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘bhavañhi ānando tassa bhoto gotamassa dīgharattaṃ upaṭṭhāko santikāvacaro samīpacārī. Bhavametaṃ ānando jāneyya, yesaṃ so bhavaṃ gotamo dhammānaṃ vaṇṇavādī ahosi, yattha ca imaṃ janataṃ samādapesi nivesesi patiṭṭhāpesi. Katamesānaṃ kho, bho ānanda, dhammānaṃ so bhavaṃ gotamo vaṇṇavādī ahosi; kattha ca imaṃ janataṃ samādapesi nivesesi patiṭṭhāpesī’’ti?
English Translation
448. Then, as that night ended, the venerable Ānanda, having dressed in the early morning, took his bowl and robe, and accompanied by the venerable Cetaka, a monk who was his attendant, went to the dwelling of Subha, the young man, the son of Todeyya; having arrived, he sat down on the seat that was prepared. Then Subha, the young man, the son of Todeyya, approached the venerable Ānanda; having approached, he exchanged greetings with the venerable Ānanda. Having concluded their conversation of courteous greetings, he sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Subha, the young man, the son of Todeyya, said this to the venerable Ānanda: “For a long time, venerable Ānanda, you have been the attendant of the esteemed Gotama, dwelling in his presence and near him. Venerable Ānanda, you would know what things the esteemed Gotama was an advocate of, and with which he encouraged, exhorted, and established the people. Which of those things, venerable Ānanda, was the esteemed Gotama an advocate of; and with what did he encourage, exhort, and establish these people?”
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
448.Cetakenabhikkhunāti cetiraṭṭhe jātattā cetakoti evaṃ laddhanāmena. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyanti bho, ānanda, dasabalassa ko nāma ābādho ahosi, kiṃ bhagavā paribhuñji. Api ca satthu parinibbānena tumhākaṃ soko udapādi, satthā nāma na kevalaṃ tumhākaṃyeva parinibbuto, sadevakassa lokassa mahājāni, ko dāni añño maraṇā muccissati, yatra so sadevakassa lokassa aggapuggalo parinibbuto, idāni kaṃ aññaṃ disvā maccurājā lajjissatīti evamādinā nayena maraṇapaṭisaṃyuttaṃ sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā therassa hiyyo pītabhesajjānurūpaṃ āhāraṃ datvā bhattakiccāvasāne ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Upaṭṭhāko santikāvacaroti upaṭṭhāko hutvā santikāvacaro, na randhagavesī. Na vīmaṃsanādhippāyo. Samīpacārīti idaṃ purimapadasseva vevacanaṃ. Yesaṃ so bhavaṃ gotamoti kasmā pucchati? Tassa kira evaṃ ahosi ‘‘yesu dhammesu bhavaṃ gotamo imaṃ lokaṃ patiṭṭhapesi, te tassa accayena naṭṭhā nu kho, dharanti nu kho, sace dharanti, ānando jānissati, handa naṃ pucchāmī’’ti, tasmā pucchi.
English Commentary
448. Because the *bhikkhu* (monk) was born in the Ceti country, he was named Cetaka in this way. "What kind of comforting and স্মরণীয় speech [should be given]?" But these beings who engaged in good conduct of body (*kāyasucarita*), speech (*vacīsucarita*), and mind (*manosucarita*), who did not abuse the noble ones (*ariyānaṃ anupavādakā*), who held right view (*sammādiṭṭhikā*) and acted accordingly (*sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā*), at the breaking up of the body (*kāyassa bhedā*), after death (*paraṃ maraṇā*), have reappeared in a good destination (*sugatiṃ*), in the world of heaven (*saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannā*.’) Thus, with the divine eye (*dibbena cakkhunā*)… he sees beings passing away and realizing. “Just as, Māṇava, if there were a palace in the middle of a crossroads (*majjhesiṅghāṭake pāsādo*), and a man with sight (*cakkhumā puriso*) stood there, he might see people entering a house, leaving, walking along the street (*rathikāya vīthiṃ sañcarante*), or sitting in the middle of the crossroads (*majjhesiṅghāṭake nisinnepi*). He might think: ‘These people are entering the house, these are leaving, these are walking along the street, and these are sitting in the middle of the crossroads.’ In the same way, Māṇava, a *bhikkhu* (monk)…when his mind is thus concentrated…attained to imperturbability, he directs it to knowledge of the passing away and reappearance of beings. With the divine eye (*dibbena cakkhunā*)… he sees beings passing away and realizing. That is known to him by wisdom (*paññāya*).
