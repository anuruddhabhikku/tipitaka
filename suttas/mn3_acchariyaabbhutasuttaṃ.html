197. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavān) was staying at Sāavatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park (Jetavane Anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

Then, after their meal, when the monks had returned from their alms round, a large number of monks (sambahulānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ) were sitting together, gathered in the service hall (upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ). This discussion arose among them: “It is wonderful (acchariyaṃ), friends (āvuso), it is marvellous (abbhutaṃ), friends (āvuso), how mighty (mahiddhikatā) and powerful (mahānubhāvatā) is the Tathāgata! For if the Tathāgata knows about the past Buddhas (atīte buddhe), who have attained final Nirvāṇa (parinibbute), cut off proliferation (chinnapapañce), broken the cycle (pariyādinnavaṭṭe), brought suffering to an end (sabbadukkhavītivatte), [namely:] ‘Such was the birth of those Blessed Ones (evaṃjaccā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ)’; ‘Such were the names of those Blessed Ones (evaṃnāmā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ)’; ‘Such was the lineage of those Blessed Ones (evaṃgottā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ)’; ‘Such was the virtue of those Blessed Ones (evaṃsīlā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ)’; ‘Such was the teaching of those Blessed Ones (evaṃdhammā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ)’; ‘Such was the wisdom of those Blessed Ones (evaṃpaññā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ)’; ‘Such was the conduct of those Blessed Ones (evaṃvihārī te bhagavanto ahesuṃ)’; ‘Such was the liberation of those Blessed Ones (evaṃvimuttā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ).’”

When this was said, venerable Ānanda (āyasmā ānando) said this to those monks: “The Tathāgatas are wonderful (acchariyā), friends (āvuso), and endowed with wonderful qualities (acchariyadhammasamannāgatā ca); the Tathāgatas are marvelous (abhutā), friends (āvuso), and endowed with marvelous qualities (abbhutadhammasamannāgatā ca).”

And this conversation of those monks was interrupted (ayañca hidaṃ tesaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ antarākathā vippakatā hoti).

197. "Thus have I heard"—the Achariyabbhutasutta.

There, " yatra hi nāmā " is a particle indicating astonishment.

yo nāma tathāgato means "who is called the Tathāgata."

In " chinnapapañce ," papañcā means these three defilements: craving, conceit, and views.

In " chinnavaṭume ," vaṭuma means the cycle of wholesome and unwholesome actions.

pariyādinnavaṭṭe is a synonym for that.

sabbadukkhavītivatte means one who has transcended all suffering, which is known as the cycle of ripening.

anussarissatī here, due to the particle yatrāti, is a future tense, but its meaning should be understood as past tense here.

The Blessed One recollected those Buddhas; he will not recollect them now.

evaṃjaccā means Vipassī and others were born into the khattiya caste, Kakusandha and others were born into the brāhmaṇa caste.

evaṃgottā means Vipassī and others were of the Koṇḍañña clan, Kakusandha and others were of the Kassapa clan.

evaṃsīlā means having such morality through mundane and supramundane morality.

evaṃdhammā here implies the qualities pertaining to concentration.

evaṃsamādhinoti means having such concentration through mundane and supramundane concentration.

evaṃpaññā means having such wisdom through mundane and supramundane wisdom.

As for evaṃvihārī, since qualities pertaining to concentration have already been mentioned below, the dwelling has already been mentioned. If one asks why it is mentioned again, it is not just a mere repetition.

This is in order to illuminate the cessation-attainment (nirodhasamāpatti). Therefore, evaṃnirodhasamāpattivihārīti is the meaning here.

evaṃvimuttā here refers to the five kinds of liberation: liberation by suppression (vikkhambhanavimutti), liberation by temporary suspension (tadaṅgavimutti), liberation by eradication (samucchedavimutti), liberation by tranquilization (paṭippassaddhivimutti), and liberation by escape (nissaraṇavimutti).

Therein, the eight attainments (samāpattis) are called vikkhambhanavimutti because they are liberated from hindrances and so on by suppression.

The seven contemplations beginning with the contemplation of impermanence are called tadaṅgavimutti because they are liberated from the perception of permanence and so on by temporarily abandoning them according to their respective opposing factors.

The four noble paths (ariyamagga) are called samucchedavimutti because they are liberated from defilements by eradication.

The four fruits of recluseship (sāmaññaphalāni) are called paṭippassaddhivimutti because they arise at the end of the tranquilization of defilements by the power of the paths.

Nibbāna is called nissaraṇavimutti because it has escaped, departed, and stands far away from all defilements.

Thus, based on these five kinds of liberation, evaṃvimuttāti should be understood to mean thus here.

evaṃvimuttā vikkhambhanavimutti tadaṅgavimutti samucchedavimutti paṭippassaddhivimutti nissaraṇavimutti

199. Therefore, because you say, "The tathāgatas are amazing," therefore, may the amazing and wonderful qualities of the tathāgata be revealed even more greatly!

Here, "mindful and fully aware" refers to the two kinds of full awareness in the human world and the world of the gods.

In the Vessantara Jātaka, after giving two sons, and on the following day, after giving the queen to Śakra, Śakra was pleased and gave eight boons, which he accepted, saying:

"When I am released from here, I will go to heaven, skilled in special knowledge; may I not return from there; this is the eighth excellent boon."

(Jā. 2.22.2300)

Thus, he grasped the boon, "May my rebirth be in the Tuṣita heaven," and from then on, he knows, "I will be reborn in the Tuṣita heaven"; this is full awareness in the human world.

Furthermore, having passed away from the state of being Vessantara and being reborn in the Tuṣita heaven, he knew, "I am reborn"; this is full awareness in the world of the gods.

But do the remaining deities not know? No, they do not know.

However, those who, having looked at the garden-palace wish-fulfilling trees, are awakened by the sounds of divine music from the divine dramas, are made to remember, "O venerable one, this is the world of the gods; you are reborn here," they know.

The bodhisattva does not know in the first moment of impulsion, but knows from the second moment of impulsion onward.

For this one, there is knowledge that is uncommon to others.

Here, "He stood firm" means that even though other gods are there, they know "we are standing here," but being overwhelmed in the six sense spheres by a powerful, desirable object, having abandoned mindfulness, and not even knowing their state of having eaten and drunk, they pass away due to the interruption of food.

Does the bodhisattva not have such an object? No, he does not.

Indeed, he grasps the remaining deities with ten qualities, but he does not allow the object to crush himself; having overcome that object, he stands firm.

Therefore it was said: "Mindful and fully aware, Ānanda, the bodhisattva stood in the Tuṣita heaven."

He stood firm.

The Middle Country (majjhimadeso), namely, in the Vinaya, it has been said in this manner: "In the eastern direction, there is a market-town called Gajaṅgala."
That, however, is three hundred yojanas in length, two and a half in width, and nine hundred yojanas in circumference.

207. Sampratijāto means born at the moment.

In the Pāli [text], it is shown as just having emerged from the mother's womb, but it should not be seen like that.

For when he had just emerged, first the Brahmās received him in a golden net, from whose hands the Four Great Kings received him on antelope skins agreeable for auspicious occasions and soft to the touch; from their hands men received him on a roll of fine cloth; [and] having been released from the hands of men, he stood on the ground.

Setamhi chatte anudhāriyamāne means while a white umbrella was being held, namely, a divine white umbrella was being held.

And here, the retinue of the umbrella, swords, etc., and the five royal emblems of royalty also arrived.

But in the Pāli [text], in the king's procession, only the umbrella is mentioned, like a king.

Among those, only the umbrella is evident, not the umbrella-bearers.

Likewise, the sword, palm-leaf fan, peacock-feather fan, yak-tail whisk, and turban are evident by themselves, not their bearers.

It is said that all those deities of invisible form took [them].

It was also said:

Sabbā ca disā means this statement that he looked in all directions is like [saying that] he stood on the seven-pace path, but it should not be seen like that.

For the great being, having been released from the hands of men, looked toward the east. Thousands of world systems became a single open space.

There, gods and humans, making offerings with fragrant garlands, etc., said, "Great man, there is no one equal to you here, how could there be one greater?"

Thus, having looked in the four directions, the four intermediate directions, below, and above—ten directions in all—and not seeing anyone equal to himself, he went seven paces, [saying,] "This is the north direction." Thus, the meaning should be understood here.

Āsabhi means excellent.

Aggo means first in qualities.

The other two words are synonyms of this.

Ayamantimā jāti, natthi dāni punabbhavo means he declared arhatship, which is to be attained in this very existence, with [these] two lines.

Sabbā ca disā, āsabhi, aggo, ayamantimā jāti, natthi dāni punabbhavo

Here, standing on the earth with even feet was a preliminary sign of obtaining the four bases of miraculous power; having his face turned toward the north was a preliminary sign of going, overwhelming and surpassing the great multitude; going seven paces was a preliminary sign of obtaining the seven jewels of enlightenment factors; holding a divine white umbrella was a preliminary sign of obtaining the umbrella of liberation; the five royal emblems of royalty were a preliminary sign of being released by the five liberations; looking in all directions was a preliminary sign of obtaining unobstructed knowledge; and speaking a fearless speech was a preliminary sign of turning the irreversible wheel of Dharma.

"This is the last birth" – this lion's roar should be understood as a preliminary sign of parinirvāṇa in the realm of nirvāṇa without remainder.

These passages have come in the Pāli [text], and many passages have come; they should be brought and explained.

On the day of the great being's birth, the ten-thousandfold world system shook.

In the ten-thousandfold world system, the deities gathered in one cakravāḍa.

First the gods received [him], then the humans.

Stringed vīṇās and leather-covered drums played by themselves without being played by anyone, and the chains, bonds, etc., of the humans shattered into pieces.

All diseases disappeared like dirt on copper washed with acid, and those born blind saw forms.

Those born deaf heard sounds, the lame became swift, mindfulness arose even in the congenitally dull and mute, ships sailing to foreign lands reached safe harbors, celestial and terrestrial jewels shone with their own radiance, enemies obtained minds of loving-kindness, and the fire in Avīci went out.

Here they ask a question: "When the great man (mahāpurisa) having stood on the earth, goes facing north, and speaks an authoritative speech, then does he go from the earth , or through the sky? Does he go seen, or does he go unseen? Does he go naked (acelaka), or does he go adorned and prepared? Does he go [as] a youth (dahara), or does he go [as] an old man (mahallaka)? Afterwards, will he be just like that, or will he again be a foolish boy?" This question, however, was answered below in the Iron Palace, in the assembly of the saṃgha, by the Tripitaka Cūḷābhaya Thera.
The Thera, indeed, there, having spoken much according to the school of fate, former action, lord (īśvara), [and] creation (nirmāṇa), at the end declared thus: "The great man went on the earth, but to the multitude, it was as if he were going in the sky.
He went seen, but to the multitude, it was as if he were going unseen.
He went naked, but to the multitude, he appeared adorned and prepared.
He went [as] a youth, but to the multitude, it was as if he were about sixteen years old.
Afterwards, however, he was just a foolish boy, not like that."
Having been said thus, the assembly, saying, "Having become like a Buddha, the question has been spoken by the venerable Thera," was delighted.
The lokantarika-vāra [is] just as said.
Known means having become manifest.
Just as, indeed, the disciples, at the time of bathing, face-washing, eating, drinking, etc., are not able to reflect upon the past aggregates in a place where there is no opportunity (anokāsa), but they reflect only when they have obtained an opportunity, not so the Buddhas.
For the Buddhas, indeed, having reflected upon the aggregates established within seven days, having imposed the three characteristics from the beginning, dismiss [them], there is no Dharma un-discerned by them, therefore he said "known".
