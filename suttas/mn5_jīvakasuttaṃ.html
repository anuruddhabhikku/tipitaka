51. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati jīvakassa komārabhaccassa ambavane. Atha kho jīvako komārabhacco yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi . Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho jīvako komārabhacco bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘sutaṃ metaṃ, bhante – ‘samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ uddissa pāṇaṃ ārabhanti ārambhanti (ka.), taṃ samaṇo gotamo jānaṃ uddissakataṃ uddissakaṭaṃ (sī. pī.) maṃsaṃ paribhuñjati paṭiccakamma’nti. Ye te, bhante, evamāhaṃsu – ‘samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ uddissa pāṇaṃ ārabhanti, taṃ samaṇo gotamo jānaṃ uddissakataṃ maṃsaṃ paribhuñjati paṭiccakamma’nti, kacci te, bhante, bhagavato vuttavādino, na ca bhagavantaṃ abhūtena abbhācikkhanti, dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākaronti, na ca koci sahadhammiko vādānuvādo gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchatī’’ti?
THUS HAVE I HEARD.

On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Mango Grove of Jīvaka Komārabhacca.

Then Jīvaka Komārabhacca went to the Blessed One and, on arrival, having bowed down to him, sat to one side.

Seated to one side, Jīvaka Komārabhacca said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, I have heard this: 'They slaughter living beings for the śramaṇa Gotama; the śramaṇa Gotama knowingly eats meat prepared for him from animals killed for his sake.'"

"Venerable sir, do those who speak thus say what has been said by the Blessed One, and not misrepresent him with what is contrary to fact? Do they explain in accordance with the dharma in such a way that nothing which provides a ground for censure can be legitimately deduced from their assertions?"
51.Evaṃme sutanti jīvakasuttaṃ. Tattha jīvakassa komārabhaccassa ambavaneti ettha jīvatīti jīvako. Kumārena bhatoti komārabhacco. Yathāha ‘‘kiṃ etaṃ bhaṇe kākehi samparikiṇṇanti? Dārako devāti. Jīvati bhaṇeti? Jīvati devāti. Tena hi bhaṇe taṃ dārakaṃ amhākaṃ antepuraṃ netvā dhātīnaṃ detha posetunti. Tassa jīvatīti jīvakoti nāmaṃ akaṃsu, kumārena posāpitoti komārabhaccoti nāmaṃ akaṃsū’’ti (mahāva. 328). Ayamettha saṅkhepo. Vitthārena pana jīvakavatthu khandhake āgatameva. Vinicchayakathāpissa samantapāsādikāya vinayaṭṭhakathāya vuttā. Ayaṃ pana jīvako ekasmiṃ samaye bhagavato dosābhisannaṃ kāyaṃ virecetvā sīveyyakaṃ dussayugaṃ datvā vatthānumodanapariyosāne sotāpattiphale patiṭṭhāya cintesi – ‘‘mayā divasassa dvattikkhattuṃ buddhupaṭṭhānaṃ gantabbaṃ, idañca veḷuvanaṃ atidūre, mayhaṃ uyyānaṃ ambavanaṃ āsannataraṃ, yaṃnūnāhamettha bhagavato vihāraṃ kāreyya’’nti. So tasmiṃ ambavane rattiṭṭhānadivāṭṭhānaleṇakuṭimaṇḍapādīni sampādetvā bhagavato anucchavikaṃ gandhakuṭiṃ kāretvā ambavanaṃ aṭṭhārasahatthubbedhena tambapaṭṭavaṇṇena pākārena parikkhipāpetvā buddhappamukhaṃ bhikkhusaṅghaṃ cīvarabhattena santappetvā dakkhiṇodakaṃ pātetvā vihāraṃ niyyātesi. Taṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ – ‘‘jīvakassa komārabhaccassa ambavane’’ti. Ārabhantīti ghātenti. Uddissakatanti uddisitvā kataṃ. Paṭiccakammanti attānaṃ paṭicca kataṃ. Atha vā paṭiccakammanti nimittakammassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ, taṃ paṭicca kammamettha atthīti maṃsaṃ ‘‘paṭiccakamma’’nti vuttaṃ hoti yo evarūpaṃ maṃsaṃ paribhuñjati, sopi tassa kammassa dāyādo hoti, vadhakassa viya tassāpi pāṇaghātakammaṃ hotīti tesaṃ laddhi. Dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākarontīti bhagavatā vuttakāraṇassa anukāraṇaṃ kathenti. Ettha ca kāraṇaṃ nāma tikoṭiparisuddhamacchamaṃsaparibhogo, anukāraṇaṃ nāma mahājanassa tathā byākaraṇaṃ. Yasmā pana bhagavā uddissakataṃ na paribhuñjati, tasmā neva taṃ kāraṇaṃ hoti, na titthiyānaṃ tathā byākaraṇaṃ anukāraṇaṃ. Sahadhammiko vādānuvādoti parehi vuttakāraṇena sakāraṇo hutvā tumhākaṃ vādo vā anuvādo vā viññūhi garahitabbakāraṇaṃ koci appamattakopi kiṃ na āgacchati . Idaṃ vuttaṃ hoti – ‘‘kiṃ sabbākārenapi tumhākaṃ vāde gārayhaṃ kāraṇaṃ natthī’’ti. Abbhācikkhantīti abhibhavitvā ācikkhanti. 51. Thus have I heard: This is the Jīvaka Sūtra.

There, "at the Mango Grove of Jīvaka Komārabhacca," the name Jīvaka means, “he lives.”

Komārabhacca means "reared by a prince."

As it was said: “What is this, sir, surrounded by crows?” “A boy, your highness.”

“Is he alive, I say?” “He is alive, your highness.”

“Well then, I say, take that boy into our palace and give him to the nurses to raise.”

Because it was said of him, “He is alive,” they gave him the name Jīvaka; and because he was raised by a prince, they gave him the name Komārabhacca” (Mahāvagga 328).

This is a brief account.

The story of Jīvaka is related at length in the Khandhakas.

The story of the judgment is also told in the Samantapāsādikā Vinayaṭṭhakathā.

Once, Jīvaka purged the body of the Blessed One when it was afflicted by a disorder of the humors, and having given a pair of Siveyyaka cloths, at the conclusion of the thanksgiving talk for the cloths, he was established in the fruit of stream-entry. He thought: “I should go to wait upon the Buddha two or three times a day, but the Bamboo Grove is too far away. My Mango Grove park is closer. Let me build a dwelling there for the Blessed One.”

He set up night-quarters, day-quarters, caves, huts, pavilions, etc, in that Mango Grove, built a Fragrant Cottage suitable for the Exalted One, and erected a copper-colored wall eighteen yards high surrounding the Mango Grove. He treated the Sangha of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha to an offering of a meal and robes, and after pouring the gift-water, presented the dwelling.

With reference to that, it was said: “at the Mango Grove of Jīvaka Komārabhacca.”

Ārabhantī means they kill.

Uddissakata means done after having been intended.

Paṭiccakamma means done in relation to oneself.

Alternatively, paṭiccakamma is an appellation for an act based on a cause. Meat is called paṭiccakamma because an action is present in relation to it. Whoever consumes such meat is an heir to that action; like the butcher, that person also has an act of taking life, such is their view.

Dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākarontī means they tell an imitation of the reason spoken by the Blessed One.

Here, kāraṇaṃ means the consumption of fish or meat that is pure in three respects, and anukāraṇaṃ means a similar explanation by the general populace.

Since the Blessed One does not consume what was done after having been intended, therefore that is not a reason, nor is that an imitation of the explanation of the Tīrthikas.

Sahadhammiko vādānuvādo means, having become causal by a reason spoken by others, does not even a small reason for which your speech or translation is to be censured by the wise come?

This is what was said: “Is there not in every way a reason in your speech that is censurable?”

Abbhācikkhantī means they speak after having overcome.

Ārabhantī, uddissakata, paṭiccakamma, paṭiccakamma, dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākarontī, sahadhammiko vādānuvādo, abbhācikkhantī

Tīhi kho ahaṃ, jīvaka, ṭhānehi maṃsaṃ paribhoganti vadāmi. Adiṭṭhaṃ, asutaṃ, aparisaṅkitaṃ – imehi kho ahaṃ, jīvaka, tīhi ṭhānehi maṃsaṃ paribhoganti vadāmi.
“Jīvaka, those who speak thus, saying, ‘The śramaṇa Gotama slaughters living beings for the sake of (himself), the śramaṇa Gotama knowingly eats meat killed for his sake based on the causal principle’ do not speak according to what I have said; they misrepresent me with what is untrue and contrary to fact.

“Jīvaka, I say that there are three instances in which meat should not be eaten:

when it is seen (dṛṣṭaṃ), heard (śrutaṃ), or suspected (parisaṅkitaṃ) – Jīvaka, I say that meat should not be eaten in these three instances.

Jīvaka, I say that there are three instances in which meat may be eaten:

when it is not seen (adṛṣṭaṃ), not heard (aśrutaṃ), and not suspected (aparisaṅkitaṃ) – Jīvaka, I say that meat may be eaten in these three instances.

52. "For these reasons" means because of these causes.

Among things seen, "seen" (diṭṭhaṃ) means that which is seen, that is, after hunting animals or fish for the sake of the bhikkhus.

"Heard" (sutaṃ) means that which is heard, that is, after hunting animals or fish for the sake of the bhikkhus, it is taken.

"Suspected" (parisaṅkitaṃ) means three kinds: suspected because of seeing (diṭṭhaparisaṅkitaṃ), suspected because of hearing (sutaparisaṅkitaṃ), and suspected of being freed from both (tadubhayavimuttaparisaṅkitanti).

There is this comprehensive judgment: Here, the bhikkhus see people going out from a village or wandering in a forest with nets, traps, etc., in their hands.

On the second day, they bring alms-food mixed with meat to that village for the bhikkhus who have entered for alms.

They suspect because of that seeing, "Was this done for the sake of the bhikkhus?" This is called "suspected because of seeing" (diṭṭhaparisaṅkitaṃ), it is not proper to take this.

Whatever is not suspected in this way, that is proper.

If those people ask, "Venerable Sirs, why do you not accept?" and having heard the matter, say, "This was not done for the sake of the bhikkhus, but it was done by us for our own sake or for the sake of royal officials, etc.," it is allowable.

Suspected because of seeing (diṭṭhaparisaṅkitaṃ): The bhikkhus do not see, but they hear, "People are going out from a village or wandering in a forest with nets, traps, etc., in their hands."

On the second day, they bring alms-food mixed with meat to that village for the bhikkhus who have entered for alms.

They suspect because of that hearing, "Was this done for the sake of the bhikkhus?" This is called "suspected because of hearing" (sutaparisaṅkitaṃ), it is not proper to take this.

Whatever is not suspected in this way, that is proper.

If those people ask, "Venerable Sirs, why do you not accept?" and having heard the matter, say, "This was not done for the sake of the bhikkhus, but it was done by us for our own sake or for the sake of royal officials, etc.," it is allowable.

Suspected because of hearing (sutaparisaṅkitaṃ): The bhikkhus neither see nor hear, but having entered that village for alms, they take the bowl, mix the alms-food with meat, and bring it.

They suspect, "Was this done for the sake of the bhikkhus?" This is called "suspected of being freed from both" (tadubhayavimuttaparisaṅkitaṃ).

It is not proper to take even this.

Whatever is not suspected in this way, that is proper.

If those people ask, "Venerable Sirs, why do you not accept?" and having heard the matter, say, "This was not done for the sake of the bhikkhus, but it was done by us for our own sake or for the sake of royal officials, etc., or it was made from meat already killed, having obtained what is allowable, it was prepared for the sake of the bhikkhus," it is allowable.

Suspected of being freed from both (tadubhayavimuttaparisaṅkitaṃ): This same method applies even if it is done for the sake of deceased ancestors or for auspicious ceremonies, etc.

Whatever is not done specifically for the sake of the bhikkhus, and in which there is no uncertainty, all of that is allowable.

If, however, something is done in a certain monastery intending it for the bhikkhus, and they do not know that it was done for their own sake, but others know,

those who know, it is not proper for them; for the others, it is proper.

If others do not know, but they themselves know, it is not proper for them alone; for the others, it is proper.

If they also know, "This was done for our sake," and others also know, "This was done for the sake of these ones," it is not proper for any of them.

If all do not know, it is proper for all.

Indeed, if something is done intending it for the sake of anyone among five fellow dhamma-practitioners, it is not allowable for all.

If, however, someone kills a living being intending it for a certain bhikkhu, and having filled his bowl, gives it, and that bhikkhu, knowing that it was done for his own sake, takes it and gives it to another bhikkhu, that one enjoys it out of faith.

To whom does the offense apply? There is no offense for either of the two.

Indeed, because it was done intentionally, there is no offense for him because of not eating it; for the other, it is because of not knowing.

Indeed, there is no offense in accepting allowable meat.

And if, without knowing that it was done intentionally, it is eaten, afterwards, upon knowing, there is no matter of confession of the offense.
However, if one unknowingly eats unsuitable meat (akappiyamaṃsa) and later realizes it, the offense (āpatti) should be confessed.

Indeed, if one knows that [the meat] was specifically intended [for him] and then eats it, there is an offense (āpatti). Even if one unknowingly eats unsuitable meat (akappiyamaṃsa), there is still an offense (āpattiyeva).

Therefore, one who is afraid of offenses (āpattibhīrukena) should carefully examine the appearance (rūpaṃ sallakkhentenāpi) and ask before accepting meat (maṃsaṃ paṭiggahetabbaṃ), or, at the time of consumption (paribhogakāle) having taken it, should ask: "Will I consume it after asking?" (pucchitvā paribhuñjissāmīti) and then should consume it only after asking (pucchitvāva paribhuñjitabbaṃ).

Why is that? Because it is difficult to discern (duviññeyyattā).

For example, bear meat (acchamaṃsaṃ) is similar to pig meat (sūkaramaṃsasadisaṃ hoti), and leopard meat (dīpimaṃsādīni) etc., is similar to deer meat (migamaṃsasadisāni). Therefore, they say that it is only proper to take it after asking (pucchitvā gahaṇameva vaṭṭatīti vadanti).

Unseen (adiṭṭha) means that it was killed for the monks, but not received by them.

Unheard (asuta) means that it was killed for the monks and received by them.

Unsuspected (aparisaṅkita) means that it is free from suspicion due to having been seen, or other reasons.

I declare it is proper to consume (paribhoganti vadāmī) - thus, because of these three reasons, it is called perfectly pure (parisuddhaṃ), "threefold-pure" (tikoṭiparisuddhaṃ). Its consumption (paribhogo) is like the consumption of soup and vegetables (sūpeyyasākaparibhogasadiso) that grow in the wilderness (araññe). For a monk who dwells in loving-kindness (mettāvihārissa), who consumes such [food], there is no fault (doso) or blame (vajjaṃ) (bhikkhuno doso vā vajjaṃ vā natthi). Therefore, the meaning (attho) is, "I declare that it is proper to consume it (taṃ paribhuñjitabbanti vadāmīti)."

53. ‘‘Idha, jīvaka, bhikkhu aññataraṃ gāmaṃ vā nigamaṃ vā upanissāya viharati. So mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ, tathā tatiyaṃ, tathā catutthaṃ. Iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharati. Tamenaṃ gahapati vā gahapatiputto vā upasaṅkamitvā svātanāya bhattena nimanteti. Ākaṅkhamānova ākaṅkhamāno (syā. kaṃ.), jīvaka, bhikkhu adhivāseti . Here, Jīvaka, a bhikkhu dwells in dependence upon a certain village or town.

He abides pervading one quarter with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (maitrī), likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth.

Thus above, below, around, everywhere, in its entirety, he dwells pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with loving-kindness, abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, without ill will.

Then a householder (gṛhapati) or a householder’s son approaches him and invites him for the next day’s meal.

If the bhikkhu, desiring, accepts,

when that night has passed, in the morning, having dressed, and taking his bowl (pātra) and robe (cīvara), he approaches the residence of that householder or householder’s son; having approached, he sits down on a seat made ready.

Then that householder or householder’s son serves him with choice almsfood (piṇḍapāta).

It does not occur to him: ‘How good that this householder or householder’s son serves me with choice almsfood!’ If only this householder or householder’s son might serve me with such choice almsfood in the future!’—it does not occur to him thus.

He partakes of that almsfood unattached, uninfatuated, not adhering to it, seeing the danger in it, understanding the escape from it.

What do you think, Jīvaka, would that bhikkhu on that occasion choose for his own affliction, or for another’s affliction, or for the affliction of both?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Does not that bhikkhu, Jīvaka, on that occasion partake of food that is blameless?”

“Yes, venerable sir. I have heard this, venerable sir: ‘Brahmā abides in loving-kindness (maitrī)’”.

“Venerable sir, the Blessed One (bhagavat) is my visible witness to that; for the Blessed One (bhagavat) abides in loving-kindness (maitrī).”

“Jīvaka, any lust (rāga), any hate (dveṣa), any delusion (moha) whereby ill will (vyāpāda) might arise have been abandoned by the Tathāgata, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, done away with so that they are no longer subject to future arising.

If what you said referred to that, then I allow it to you.”

“That is exactly what I meant, venerable sir.”

53. Now, to show the faultlessness even in the use of such things by one abiding in loving-kindness, he said, "Here, Jīvaka, bhikkhus," and so forth.

There, although it is said vaguely, "bhikkhus," it should be understood that this was said referring to himself.

For the Blessed One, in the Mahāvacchagotta Sutta, the Caṅkī Sutta, and in this Sutta, in these three instances, the discourse was given referring to himself.

"Paṇītena piṇḍapātena": below, in the Anaṅgaṇa Sutta, any alms-food that is very valuable is intended as paṇīta-piṇḍapāta; here, however, only food with meat sauce is intended.

"Agathito": not attached to craving ( taṇhā).

"Amucchito": not infatuated with the infatuation of craving (taṇhā).

"Anajjhopanno": not adhering; not overcome, not desiring to swallow everything, having plundered it all, with a single strike, like a crow—this is the meaning.

"Ādīnavadassāvī": seeing the danger ( ādīnava) in the manner: "having entered the abdominal cavity in one night, it will exit through nine wound-openings," and so forth.

"Nissaraṇapañño paribhuñjatī": he consumes, having thoroughly analyzed with wisdom ( paññā) that this use of food is for this purpose.

"Attabyābādhāya vā cetetī": or he intends for his own suffering.

"Sutameta": "This was heard by me previously; this is merely my hearing," he shows.

"Sace kho te, Jīvaka, idaṃ sandhāya bhāsita": Jīvaka, the defilements, beginning with ill-will (byāpāda), are abandoned by Mahābrahmā by means of suppression ( vikkhambhanappahāna); therefore, that one abiding in loving-kindness [is faultless]. By my abandonment through eradication ( samucchedappahāna), if this was spoken by you intending this, in this case, I approve this statement of yours—this is the meaning.

He consented.

He abides pervading one quarter with a mind imbued with compassion…with a mind imbued with sympathetic joy…with a mind imbued with equanimity, likewise the second quarter, the third quarter, and the fourth quarter.

The bhikkhu accepts, if he likes, Jivaka.

55. “If anyone slaughters a living being for the Tathāgata or his disciple, he lays up much demerit in five instances.

When he says: ‘Go and fetch that living being,’ this is the first instance in which he lays up much demerit.

When that living being experiences pain and grief on being led along with a neck-halter, this is the second instance in which he lays up much demerit.

When he says: ‘Go and slaughter that living being,’ this is the third instance in which he lays up much demerit.

When that living being experiences pain and grief on being slaughtered, this is the fourth instance in which he lays up much demerit.

When he provides the Tathāgata or his disciple with food that is not permissible, this is the fifth instance in which he lays up much demerit.

Anyone who slaughters a living being for the Tathāgata or his disciple lays up much demerit in these five instances.”

When this was said, Jīvaka Komārabhacca said to the Blessed One: “Wonderful, O Lord, most excellent, O Lord! The bhikkhus sustain themselves with permissible food. The bhikkhus sustain themselves with blameless food.

Excellent, O Lord, most excellent, O Lord! May the Blessed Lord accept me from this day forth as a lay-follower as long as life shall last!”

The Jīvaka Sutta, the fifth, is ended.
