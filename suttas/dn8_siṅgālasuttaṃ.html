At that time a young householder named Siṅgālaka arose early and set out from Rājagaha with freshly washed clothes and hair. With palms together held up in reverence, he was paying respect towards the six directions: that is east (purastimaṃ diśaṃ), south (dakṣiṇaṃ diśaṃ), west (paścimaṃ diśaṃ), north (uttaraṃ diśaṃ), lower (heṭṭhimaṃ diśaṃ) and upper (uparimaṃ diśaṃ).

242. Thus have I heard.

This is the explanation of the words that are not plain: veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe, "in the Bamboo Grove, at the Kalandaka feeding ground." Veḷuvana means the name of that park.

It was enclosed by bamboos, delightful to behold with a wall of eighteen hatthas, complete with a gateway and watchtower, and gleaming with blue. Therefore, it is called Veḷuvana.

There, they gave food to the kalandakas; therefore, it is called Kalandakanivāpa.

Once upon a time, a king came there to play in the park, and, drunk with liquor, fell asleep during the day.

His attendants, thinking, "The king is asleep," went away here and there, enticed by flowers and fruits, and so on.

Then, drawn by the smell of liquor, a black snake came out of a hollow tree and approached the king. Seeing this, the tree deity, thinking, "I will give the king life," came in the guise of a crow and made a sound in his ear.

The king awoke.

The black snake retreated.

Seeing it, he thought, "This crow gave me life." Therefore, he established a feeding ground there for crows, and proclaimed an assurance of safety.

Therefore, from that time on, it was called Kalandakanivāpa.

Kalandaka is a name for crows.

kalandakā.

"At that time," means at the time when the Blessed One, having made Rājagṛha his alms-gathering village, was dwelling in the Bamboo Grove, at the Kalandaka feeding ground.

"Siṅgālako gahapatiputto" means Siṅgālaka was his name.

"Gahapatiputto" means the son of the householder is the gahapatiputto.

His father was a very wealthy householder. Forty dhanakoṭis were deposited and stored in his house.

He was a lay follower who had gone for refuge to the Blessed One, a stream-enterer; his wife too was a stream-enterer.

But his son was faithless and displeased.

Then his mother and father constantly advised him thus: "Dear son, go to the Teacher, go to the general of the Dhamma, Mahāmoggallāna, go to Mahākassapa, [go] to the eighty great disciples."

He said thus: "I have no business going to your ascetics. Having gone to the presence of the ascetics, one must pay homage. As one bows down, one's back breaks, one's knees become stiff, one must sit on the ground.

Having sat there, one's clothes get soiled and worn out. From the time of sitting nearby onward, there is conversation and discussion. When that is the case, trust arises; then, having invited them, robes, alms-food, and so on must be given.

When that is the case, wealth declines. I have no business going to your ascetics."

Thus, though his mother and father advised him for as long as he lived, they were not able to bring him to the Teaching.

Then, when his father was lying on his deathbed, he thought, "It is fitting to give an instruction to my son." Thinking again, he decided: "I will give him this instruction: 'Dear son, worship the directions!'" Not understanding the meaning, he will worship the directions. Then the Teacher or a disciple will see him and ask, "What are you doing?"

Then he will say, "My father instructed me, 'Worship the directions!'"

Then they will teach him the Dhamma, saying, "Your father does not command you to worship these directions, but he commands you to worship these directions."

Having known the good qualities in the Buddha's teaching, he will do meritorious deeds.

Then, having summoned him, he said, "Dear son, having gotten up early in the morning, you should worship the six directions."

A word from one lying on one's deathbed is indeed something to be remembered for as long as life lasts.

Therefore, that householder's son, remembering that word of his father, did so.

Therefore, it was said, "Having gotten up early in the morning, having gone out from Rājagṛha," and so on.

Then, when it was morning, the Blessed One, having dressed, took his bowl and robe and entered Rājagaha for alms.

The Blessed One saw Sigālaka, a householder's son, who had risen early and left Rājagaha, with wet clothes and wet hair, paying homage to the different directions with his palms together—the east direction, the south direction, the west direction, the north direction, the lower direction, and the upper direction.

Seeing Sigālaka, the householder's son, the Blessed One said to him: "Young householder, why do you, having risen early and left Rājagaha, with wet clothes and wet hair, pay homage to the different directions with your palms together—the east direction, the south direction, the west direction, the north direction, the lower direction, and the upper direction?"

"Venerable Sir, my father, when he was about to die, told me: 'Dear son, you should pay homage to the directions.' And so, venerable sir, honoring, respecting, esteeming, and venerating my father's words, I rise early and leave Rājagaha, with wet clothes and wet hair, paying homage to the different directions with my palms together—the east direction, the south direction, the west direction, the north direction, the lower direction, and the upper direction."

244. “Young householder, that is not how the six directions (ṣaḍdiśa, Sī., Pī.) should be worshipped according to the discipline (vinaya) of the noble ones (ārya).”

“Then how, dear sir (bhante), should the six directions (ṣaḍdiśa, Sī., Pī.) be worshipped according to the discipline (vinaya) of the noble ones (ārya)? It would be good if the Blessed One (bhagavān) would teach me the dharma in such a way that the six directions (ṣaḍdiśa, Sī., Pī.) are [properly] worshipped according to the discipline (vinaya) of the noble ones (ārya).”

“Very well, young man, listen and pay careful attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, dear sir (bhante),” replied Siṅgālaka, the householder's son, to the Blessed One (bhagavat).

The Blessed One (bhagavān) said this:

“Young man, when a noble disciple (ariyasāvaka) has abandoned the four defilements of action (cattāro kammakilesā), does not commit evil deeds from four causes, and does not pursue the six ways of wasting wealth, then, having avoided these fourteen evil ways, that noble disciple (ariyasāvaka) covers the six directions and has embarked on the path to conquer both worlds (ubholoka). For him, this world is secured, and the next world (para loka) as well. At the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (maraṇā), he attains a good destiny (sugati), even a heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ).

Sakilesoyeva hi pāṇaṃ hanati, nikkileso na hanati, tasmā pāṇātipāto ‘‘kammakileso’’ti vutto. Adinnādānādīsupi eseva nayo. Athāparanti aparampi etadatthaparidīpakameva gāthābandhaṃ avocāti attho. Catuṭhānādivaṇṇanā
245. Thus, the Blessed One, having established the summary (mātikā) in brief, now expanding on that very thing, said, beginning with, "What are the four defilements of action (kammakilesā)?"

Kammakileso means action (kamma) and defilement (kilesa), since it is associated with defilements; therefore, it is a defilement of action (kammakileso).

Indeed, one kills a living being with defilement, not without defilement; therefore, taking life (pāṇātipāto) is called "defilement of action" (kammakileso).

The same method (naya) applies to taking what is not given (adinnādāna), and so forth (adinnādānādīsu).

Athāparaṃ means "Furthermore (aparaṃ)," he spoke another verse (gāthābandhaṃ) that illuminates this meaning (etadatthaparidīpakameva), that is the meaning (attho).

246. Which are the four ways in which one does not commit evil deeds? Going astray through desire (chandāgati), one commits evil deeds; going astray through hatred (dosāgati), one commits evil deeds; going astray through delusion (mohāgati), one commits evil deeds; going astray through fear (bhayāgati), one commits evil deeds.

But, young man, when a noble disciple (ariyasāvaka) does not go astray through desire, does not go astray through hatred, does not go astray through delusion, does not go astray through fear, in these four ways, he does not commit evil deeds.

The Blessed One said this. Having spoken thus, the Well-Gone (sugata), the Teacher (satthā) then said further:

“Desire (chandā), hatred (dosā), fear (bhayā), delusion (mohā): whoever transgresses the Dharma (dhamma),

his glory wanes, like the moon in the dark fortnight."

246. Pāpakammaṃ karotī (he does an evil action): Because when the agent is shown, the non-agent becomes clear, therefore the Blessed One, after establishing the topic "he does not do an evil action," showing the agent first, said, out of skill in teaching.

There, chandāgatiṃ gacchanto (while taking a wrong course of action because of partiality): going the wrong course of action due to desire and affection, doing what should not be done.

And so with the other three wrong courses of action.

There, "This is my friend, my acquaintance, my companion, or my relative, or he is giving me a bribe," thus, because of partiality, he treats one who is not the owner as the owner; this one, while taking a wrong course of action because of partiality (chandāgatiṃ gacchanto), does an evil action (pāpakammaṃ karoti) indeed.

"This is my enemy," thus, because of natural hostility or because of an access of anger arisen in that moment, he treats one who is the owner as one who is not the owner; this one, while taking a wrong course of action because of hatred (dosāgatiṃ gacchanto), does an evil action (pāpakammaṃ karoti) indeed.

Furthermore, out of dullness or out of delusion, saying this or that, he treats one who is not the owner as the owner; this one, while taking a wrong course of action because of delusion (mohāgatiṃ gacchanto), does an evil action (pāpakammaṃ karoti) indeed.

Furthermore, fearing that "This is either a king's favourite or one who is entrenched in wrongdoing, he could even harm me," he treats one who is not the owner as the owner; this one, while taking a wrong course of action because of fear (bhayāgatiṃ gacchanto), does an evil action (pāpakammaṃ karoti) indeed.

Furthermore, while distributing something, "This is my acquaintance or my companion," thus, out of affection, he gives more; "This is my enemy," thus, out of hatred, he gives less; out of delusion, not knowing what is given and what is not given, he gives less to someone and more to someone else; fearing that "If this is not given to him, he could even harm me," he gives more to someone; this one, in all these four ways, following these wrong courses of action such as partiality, does an evil action (pāpakammaṃ karoti) indeed.

But a noble disciple (ariyasāvako), even reaching the destruction of life, does not take these wrong courses of action such as partiality.

Therefore it was said: "By these four conditions, he does not do an evil action (pāpakammaṃ)."

273. Alamatto means capable of dwelling in a house after having made provision for sons and wife.

Paṇḍito means being wise in the place of honoring the directions.

Saṇho means being gentle by seeing subtle meanings and speaking gentle words, or having become gentle.

Paṭibhānavā means being quick-witted in the place of honoring the directions, nivātavuttī means humble conduct.

Atthaddho means without stiffness.

Uṭṭhānako means endowed with energetic vigor.

Analaso means without laziness.

Acchinnavuttī means unbroken conduct due to continuous action.

Medhāvī means endowed with wisdom through the attainment of a basis.

Saṅgāhako means one who gathers with the four bases of sympathy ( saṃgrahavastu ).

Mittakaro means seeking friends.

Vadaññū means, by a former act, knowing the spoken word.

When a friend has gone to his house, understanding the spoken word, recalling, "Give my friend a wrap, give a cloth, give the servants their food wages," he repays the arriving friend with that much or more, that is the meaning.

Moreover, vadaññū is the name of one who, going to a friend's house and being unable to receive the arriving friend out of shame, thinking, "I will take this name," knowing his speech even though it is unuttered, accomplishes that with which he has come.

Or whatever is lacking for a friend, looking after that, even giving that, is indeed vadaññū.

Netā means showing that meaning, a leader by wisdom.

Showing various reasons, netīti vinetā means a guide.

Punaḥ punaḥ netīti anunetā means repeatedly guiding.

Saṅgāhako, mittakaro, vadaññū, netā, vinetā, anunetā.

Tattha tatthā means in that that person.

Rathassāṇīva yāyato means just as a chariot goes only when there is a pin, and does not go when there is not, so also in these gatherings the world goes on only when there is, and does not go on when there is not.

Therefore it was said - "These gatherings in the world are like the pin of a chariot going."

Tattha tatthā, rathassāṇīva yāyato.

Na mātā puttakāraṇā means if a mother would not do these gatherings for her son, she would not gain honor or worship on account of her son.

Na mātā puttakāraṇā, saṅgahā ete means individually in the expression of use.

Or " saṅgahe ete " is the reading.

Sammapekkhantī means rightly regarding.

Pāsaṃsā ca bhavantī means and they are praiseworthy.

Saṅgahā ete, sammapekkhantī, pāsaṃsā ca bhavantī.

274. Thus, when this was said, Siṅgālaka, the householder's son, said this to the Blessed One: “Excellent, venerable sir! Excellent, venerable sir! Just as, venerable sir, one might set upright what has been overturned, or reveal what has been hidden, or show the way to one who is lost, or carry an oil lamp into the darkness so that those with eyes might see what is there—just so has the Blessed Lord expounded the dharma in various ways (anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito). I go for refuge to the Buddha (bhagavantam saraṇaṃ gacchāmi), the dharma (dhammañca), and the Saṃgha of monks (bhikkhusaṃghañca). May the Blessed Lord accept me from this day forth as a lay-follower (upāsakaṃ) as long as life shall last!”

Imasmiñca pana sutte yaṃ gihīhi kattabbaṃ kammaṃ nāma, taṃ akathitaṃ natthi, gihivinayo nāmāyaṃ suttanto. Tasmā imaṃ sutvā yathānusiṭṭhaṃ paṭipajjamānassa vuddhiyeva pāṭikaṅkhā, no parihānīti. Sumaṅgalavilāsiniyā dīghanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Siṅgālasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
Thus, the Bhagavān, referring to the direction that your householder son's father spoke of, said, "You should pay homage to the directions." These are the six directions.

If you do the word of your father, showing Siṅgāla that you should pay homage to these directions, having stood to ask, bringing the teaching to its peak, he entered Rājagaha for alms.

Siṅgālaka also, having been established in the refuges, having scattered forty million wealth in the Buddha's teaching, having done meritorious action, became destined for heaven.

Moreover, in this sutta, that action which should be done by householders, there is nothing that is not spoken of. This sutta is called the "Vinaya for Householders."

Therefore, having heard this, for one who practices according to the instruction, only growth is to be expected, not decline.
