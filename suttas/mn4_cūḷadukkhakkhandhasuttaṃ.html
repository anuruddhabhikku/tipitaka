175. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sakkesu viharati kapilavatthusmiṃ nigrodhārāme. Atha kho mahānāmo sakko yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho mahānāmo sakko bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘dīgharattāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavatā evaṃ dhammaṃ desitaṃ ājānāmi – ‘lobho cittassa upakkileso, doso cittassa upakkileso, moho cittassa upakkileso’ti. Evañcāhaṃ evaṃpāhaṃ (ka.), bhante, bhagavatā dhammaṃ desitaṃ ājānāmi – ‘lobho cittassa upakkileso, doso cittassa upakkileso, moho cittassa upakkileso’ti. Atha ca pana me ekadā lobhadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti, dosadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti, mohadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti. Tassa mayhaṃ, bhante, evaṃ hoti – ‘kosu nāma me dhammo ajjhattaṃ appahīno yena me ekadā lobhadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti, dosadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti, mohadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhantī’’’ti.
Thus have I heard:

On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavān) was dwelling among the Sakyans, at Kapilavatthu in the Banyan Tree Park (nigrodhārāme).

Then Mahānāma the Sakyan approached the Blessed One; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, Mahānāma the Sakyan said this to the Blessed One: "For a long time, venerable sir (bhante), I have understood the Dharma (dhamma) as taught by the Blessed One thus: ‘Greed (lobha) is a defilement (upakkileso) of the mind (citta), hatred (dosa) is a defilement of the mind, delusion (moha) is a defilement of the mind.’

And thus, venerable sir, I understand the Dharma as taught by the Blessed One: ‘Greed is a defilement of the mind, hatred is a defilement of the mind, delusion is a defilement of the mind.’

But sometimes greed-related states (lobhadhammā) persist, overwhelming my mind, hatred-related states (dosadhammā) persist, overwhelming my mind, delusion-related states (mohadhammā) persist, overwhelming my mind.

It occurs to me, venerable sir: ‘What quality (dhammo) is not yet abandoned within me (ajjhattaṃ appahīno) such that these greed-related states persist, overwhelming my mind, hatred-related states persist, overwhelming my mind, delusion-related states persist, overwhelming my mind?’”

There, sakkesū means in a country of such a name.

That country was called the land of the Śākyas (śakyānaṃ) because it was the dwelling place of the Śākya princes (rājakumārānaṃ).

Moreover, the origin (uppatti) of the Śākyas (sakyānaṃ) is mentioned in the Ambaṭṭha Sutta.

kapilavatthusmi means in a city of such a name.

Because it was made in the dwelling place of the sage (iṣi) Kapila, it was called Kapilavatthu, having made that a pasture village.

nigrodhārāme means that Sakko by the name of Nigrodha, when the Blessed One (bhagavati) arrived at Kapilavatthuṃ at the time of the meeting of relatives, caused a monastery (vihāraṃ) to be made in his own garden (ārāme), and presented it to the Blessed One. The meaning is, he dwelt in that.

mahānāmo means the brother (bhātā) of Anuruddha, the son of the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) paternal uncle (cuḷapituputto).

Suddhodana, Sukkodana, Sakkodana, Dhotodana, and Amitodana, these five people (janā) were brothers (bhātaro).

amitā was the name of the goddess (devī), their sister (bhaginī).

Tissatthero was her son (putto).

The Tathāgata, Nanda, and Suddhodana were sons (puttā), as were Mahānāma, Anuruddha, and Sukkodana.

Ānandatthero was the son of Amitodana, he was the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) younger brother.

Mahānāma was quite old, a once-returner (sakadāgāmī), a noble disciple (ariyasāvako).

dīgharatta means he shows that “I have known for a long time, since the arising of the fruit of once-returning (sakadāgāmiphaluppattito).”

lobhadhammā means greed (lobha), these are called things (dhammā), speaking with reference to various kinds of greed (lobhaṃyeva).

The same method (nayo) applies in the other two.

pariyādāya tiṭṭhantī means they stand having destroyed (khepetvā).

Indeed, that is called destruction (pariyādānaṃ), namely, “having destroyed all the elephant divisions (hatthikāyaṃ), all the horse divisions (assakāyaṃ), all the chariot divisions (rathakāyaṃ), all the infantry divisions (pattikāyaṃ), he would throw him down while still alive” (saṃ. ni. 1. 126), which has come in this connection.

“The perception of impermanence (aniccasaññā), O monks (bhikkhave), cultivated (bhāvitā) and made abundant (bahulīkatā), destroys all sensual desire (kāmarāgaṃ)” (saṃ. ni. 3. 102), here in destruction.

Here also destruction (khepane) is intended (adhippetaṃ).

Therefore it was said, “having destroyed (pariyādiyitvā) means having wasted away (khepetvā).”

dīgharatta lobhadhammā pariyādāya tiṭṭhantī

yena me ekadā lobhadhammāpī means, “through which, at one time (ekekasmiṃ kāle), greed (lobhadhammāpi) also destroys the mind (cittaṃ), so he asks.”

It seems that this king (rājā) was aware (saññī) that “through the path of once-returning (sakadāgāmimaggena), greed (lobhadosamohā), hatred, and delusion are completely abandoned (pahīyantī),” and he also knows that “there is something unabandoned (appahīnaṃ me atthi),” he is aware (saññī hoti) that, taking up even what has been abandoned (pahīnakampi), it returns again from behind.

Does doubt (sandeho) arise (uppajjati) for a noble disciple (ariyasāvakassa) in this way (evaṃ)? Yes (āma), it arises (uppajjati).

Why (kasmā)? Because of ignorance (akovidattā) of the teaching (paṇṇattiyā).

“This defilement (kileso) is to be killed by such and such a path (asukamaggavajjho),” thus, for one ignorant (akovidassa) of this teaching (paṇṇattiyā), it is like this even for a noble disciple (ariyasāvakassapi).

Does he not have reflection (paccavekkhaṇā)? He has (atthi).
But that is not complete for everyone.
For one considers only the abandoned defilements.
One considers only the remaining defilements, one considers only the path (magga), one considers only the fruit (phala), one considers only nirvāṇa (nibbāna).
But in these five contemplations, it is not fitting not to obtain one or two.
Thus, for whom the contemplation is not complete, for him, due to inexperience in the designation of path-killing defilements, it is thus.
Because at one time even greed arises in me.

176. ‘‘So eva kho te, mahānāma, dhammo ajjhattaṃ appahīno yena te ekadā lobhadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti, dosadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti, mohadhammāpi cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti. So ca hi te, mahānāma, dhammo ajjhattaṃ pahīno abhavissa, na tvaṃ agāraṃ ajjhāvaseyyāsi, na kāme paribhuñjeyyāsi. Yasmā ca kho te, mahānāma, so eva dhammo ajjhattaṃ appahīno tasmā tvaṃ agāraṃ ajjhāvasasi, kāme paribhuñjasi.

Indeed, Mahānāma, that principle (dhammo) is not abandoned within you (ajjhattaṃ appahīno), because of which, sometimes, even the principles of greed (lobhadhammāpi) occupy your mind (cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhanti), even the principles of aversion (dosadhammāpi) occupy your mind, even the principles of delusion (mohadhammāpi) occupy your mind.

And if that principle (dhammo), Mahānāma, had been abandoned within you (ajjhattaṃ pahīno abhavissa), you would not live in a house (agāraṃ ajjhāvaseyyāsi), you would not enjoy sensual pleasures (kāme paribhuñjeyyāsi).

But because that very principle (so eva dhammo), Mahānāma, is not abandoned within you (ajjhattaṃ appahīno), therefore you live in a house (agāraṃ ajjhāvasasi), you enjoy sensual pleasures (kāme paribhuñjasi).

177. Appassādā means "of little pleasure," or limited happiness.

Bahudukkhā means "of much suffering," that is, there is much suffering here related to immediate circumstances and future lives.

Bahupāyāsā means "of much tribulation," that is, there are many afflictive tribulations related to immediate circumstances and future lives.

Ādīnavo means "misfortune," that is, trouble related to immediate circumstances and future lives.

Ettha bhiyyo means "more here," that is, in these sensual pleasures there is much misfortune.

But the enjoyment is small, limited, like a mustard seed compared to the Himavant mountain.

Iti cepi mahānāmā means, "Thus, even, Mahānāma," that is, thus it is, even for a noble disciple.

Yathābhūtanti means "according to reality," or according to its own nature.

It shows that it is well seen by right method, reason, and wisdom.

There, paññāyā means with the wisdom of vipaśyanā, or with the knowledge of the two lower paths, this is the meaning.

So cā means that noble disciple sees the misfortunes of sensual pleasures through those two paths.

Pītisukhanti, with this, it shows the two jhānas that are with joy.

Aññaṃ vā tato santataranti, different or more continuous than that: different, more continuous than those two jhānas are the two higher jhānas as well as the two paths.

Neva tāva anāvaṭṭī kāmesu hotīti, as for "he does not return to sensual pleasures" - even though that noble disciple is established, having penetrated the two paths, because he has not attained the higher jhānas or paths, he does not return to sensual pleasures, the non-returning is not without enjoyment.

The returning is with enjoyment.

Why? Because of the absence of abandonment by suppression through the four jhānas, and the absence of abandonment by eradication through the two paths.

Moreover, mayhampi kho means, "not only for you, but also for me."

Pubbeva sambodhā means "before awakening," or rather, immediately before the awakening of the path.

Paññāya sudiṭṭhaṃ hotīti, here, orodhanāṭakā means the wisdom of abandoning is intended.

Pītisukhaṃ nājjhagamanti means "did not attain joy and happiness," or "did not obtain the two jhānas that are with joy."

Aññaṃ vā tato santataranti, here, the two higher jhānas as well as the four paths are intended.

Paccaññāsinti means "I acknowledged."

Moreover, mayhampi kho pubbeva sambodhā paññāya sudiṭṭhaṃ hotī pītisukhaṃ nājjhagama paccaññāsi

180. "Thus having spoken, I, Mahānāma, said this to those Niganthas: 'But, friends, do you know that you existed in the past, and that it is not the case that you did not exist?' 'No, friend.'
'But, friends, do you know that you did evil actions in the past and did not abstain from them?' 'No, friend.'
'But, friends, do you know that you did such and such evil actions?' 'No, friend.'
'But, friends, do you know that so much suffering has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering has been exhausted all suffering will have been exhausted?' 'No, friend.'
'But, friends, do you know what the abandoning of unwholesome states is and what the cultivation of wholesome states is here and now?' 'No, friend.'

"‘“So, friends, it seems that you do not know that you existed in the past and that it is not the case that you did not exist; or that you did evil actions in the past and did not abstain from them; or that you did such and such evil actions; or that so much suffering has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering has been exhausted all suffering will have been exhausted.
You do not know what the abandoning of unwholesome states is and what the cultivation of wholesome states is here and now.
In this being the case, friends Niganthas, are those reborn among men who are cruel, bloody-handed, and practice cruelty in the world then ordained among the Niganthas?” ‘No, friend Gotama, pleasure is not to be gained through pleasure; pleasure is to be gained through pain; if, friend Gotama, pleasure was to be gained through pleasure, then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha would gain pleasure; King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha would live a more pleasant life than venerable Gotama.’”

“'Assuredly, friends, the Niganthas spoke words hastily, without consideration: “Friend Gotama, pleasure is not to be gained through pleasure; pleasure is to be gained through pain; if, friend Gotama, pleasure was to be gained through pleasure, then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha would gain pleasure; King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha would live a more pleasant life than venerable Gotama.”'
Moreover, I should be questioned there: “Who now lives a more pleasant life, venerable sirs, King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha or venerable Gotama?” Assuredly, friend Gotama, we spoke words hastily, without consideration: “Friend Gotama, pleasure is not to be gained through pleasure; pleasure is to be gained through pain; if, friend Gotama, pleasure was to be gained through pleasure, then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha would gain pleasure; King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha would live a more pleasant life than venerable Gotama.”
Moreover, let this be; even now we ask venerable Gotama: “Who now lives a more pleasant life, venerable sirs, King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha or venerable Gotama?”’

“‘Then, friends Niganthas, I will question you there; as it suits you, so explain it.
What do you think, friends Niganthas, is King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha able, without moving his body, without speaking, to experience exclusively pleasant feelings for seven days and nights?’” ‘No, friend.’

“‘What do you think, friends Niganthas, is King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha able, without moving his body, without speaking, for six days and nights…pe…five days and nights…four days and nights…three days and nights…two days and nights…one day and night to experience exclusively pleasant feelings?’” ‘No, friend.’

“‘I, friends Niganthas, am able, without moving my body, without speaking, for one day and night to experience exclusively pleasant feelings.
I, friends Niganthas, am able, without moving my body, without speaking, for two days and nights…three days and nights…four days and nights…five days and nights…six days and nights…seven days and nights to experience exclusively pleasant feelings.
What do you think, friends Niganthas, in this being the case, who lives a more pleasant life, King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha or I?’” ‘In this being the case, venerable Gotama lives a more pleasant life than King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha.’”

Thus spoke the Blessed One.
The venerable Mahānāma the Sakyan was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

§ 180. "But, friends, do the Nigaṇṭhas know, ‘So much unsatisfactoriness has been destroyed’?"

The Blessed One [replied]: A person knows what he does. For example, having taken a loan of twenty kahāpaṇas, he knows, "I have given ten, ten remain." Having given those too, he knows, "All is given." Having cultivated a third of a field, he knows, "One part is cultivated, two remain." Having cultivated another, he knows, "Two are cultivated, one remains." When even that is cultivated, he knows, "All is finished." Thus, in all activities, he knows what is done and what is not done. It is shown that you should also know in the same way.

"Abandoning unwholesome qualities": With this, he asks whether there is a Nigaṇṭha in your teaching who, having abandoned the unwholesome and cultivated the wholesome, has reached the pure end.

"Thus being": Since you are thus without knowledge.

"Cruel": Of cruel conduct.

"With bloodstained hands": Those who kill living beings, with hands smeared with blood. Even if the hand of one who kills a living being is not smeared with blood, he is still called "with bloodstained hands."

"Of cruel deeds": Of terrible deeds. Those who have committed offenses against mother, father, righteous ascetics and brahmins, and so on. Or māgavikās and others, of harsh deeds.

"Thus being cruel, with bloodstained hands, of cruel deeds."

"No, friend Gotama": The Nigaṇṭhas, thinking, "This one finds fault with our doctrine; let us also impute a fault to him," began. Its meaning [is]: "Friend Gotama, as you, wearing fine robes, eating rice and meat, living in a perfumed hut resembling a divine mansion, attain happiness through happiness, happiness should not be attained through happiness. But as we experience various kinds of suffering through the practice of squatting and so on, happiness should be attained through suffering."

"And easily, friend": This was said to show that if happiness were to be attained through happiness, the king would attain it.

There, māgadho: The lord of the country of Magadha.

Seniyo: His name.

Bimbī: The name of his person. Because of the prosperity of his beautiful and pleasing person, which is the essence of him, he is called Bimbisāro.

"Living more happily": The Nigaṇṭhas are saying this with reference to the king's experience of prosperity together with plays of three kinds in three palaces for the three ages.

Addhā: Certainly.

Sahasā appaṭisaṅkhā: Having done violence, without reflecting, just as one who is passionate speaks because of passion, one who is angry because of anger, one who is deluded speaks because of delusion, just so this speech was spoken, he shows.

"No, friend Gotama," "and easily, friend," māgadho, seniyo, bimbī, bimbisāro, sukhavihāritaro, addhā, sahasā appaṭisaṅkhā.

"There I will ask in return": In that matter I will ask.

"As it may please you": As it may suit you.

Pahotī: Is able.

Aniñjamāno: Not wavering.

Ekantasukhaṃ paṭisaṃvedī: Experiencing uninterrupted happiness.

"I, friend Nigaṇṭha, am able... experiencing uninterrupted happiness": Showing the happiness of his own attainment of the fruit, he said [this].

And here, for the purpose of establishing the topic of conversation, beginning with the king's turn, seven questions were asked. For if it were said that he is not able for seven days and nights, then one would have to ask about six, five, four, and so on.
When it is said that there are seven in the pure series, it is not surprising to say again that there are six, five, [or] four. Therefore, having made one the beginning, the teaching was given.

The rest is clear throughout.
