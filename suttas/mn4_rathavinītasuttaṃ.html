252. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe. Atha kho sambahulā jātibhūmakā bhikkhū jātibhūmiyaṃ vassaṃvuṭṭhā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinne kho te bhikkhū bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Ko nu kho, bhikkhave, jātibhūmiyaṃ jātibhūmakānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ sabrahmacārīnaṃ evaṃ sambhāvito – ‘attanā ca appiccho appicchakathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca santuṭṭho santuṭṭhikathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca pavivitto pavivekakathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca asaṃsaṭṭho asaṃsaggakathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca āraddhavīriyo vīriyārambhakathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca sīlasampanno sīlasampadākathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca samādhisampanno samādhisampadākathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca paññāsampanno paññāsampadākathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca vimuttisampanno vimuttisampadākathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca vimuttiñāṇadassanasampanno vimuttiñāṇadassanasampadākathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, ovādako viññāpako sandassako samādapako samuttejako sampahaṃsako sabrahmacārīna’’’nti? ‘‘Puṇṇo nāma, bhante, āyasmā mantāṇiputto jātibhūmiyaṃ jātibhūmakānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ sabrahmacārīnaṃ evaṃ sambhāvito – ‘attanā ca appiccho appicchakathañca bhikkhūnaṃ kattā, attanā ca santuṭṭho…pe… ovādako viññāpako sandassako samādapako samuttejako sampahaṃsako sabrahmacārīna’’’nti.
252. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary.

Then a number of bhikkhus from the Blessed One’s native land (jātibhūmakā), who had spent the Rains there, went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, sat down at one side.

Sitting to one side, these monks said this to the Blessed One:

“Venerable sir, is there any among the monks (bhikkhu) of the native land (jātibhūmi), who are your fellow-brahmacārins, who is regarded thus: ‘He himself is of few desires (appiccho) and speaks in praise of being of few desires; he himself is content (santuṭṭho) and speaks in praise of contentment; he himself is secluded (pavivitto) and speaks in praise of seclusion; he himself is unassociated (asaṃsaṭṭho) and speaks in praise of non-association; he himself is energetic (āraddhavīriyo) and speaks in praise of the instigation of energy; he himself is accomplished in virtue (sīlasampanno) and speaks in praise of the accomplishment of virtue; he himself is accomplished in concentration (samādhisampanno) and speaks in praise of the accomplishment of concentration (samādhi); he himself is accomplished in wisdom (paññāsampanno) and speaks in praise of the accomplishment of wisdom (paññā); he himself is accomplished in liberation (vimuttisampanno) and speaks in praise of the accomplishment of liberation (vimutti); he himself is accomplished in the knowledge and vision of liberation (vimuttiñāṇadassanasampanno) and speaks in praise of the accomplishment of the knowledge and vision of liberation, is an instructor (ovādako), an informer (viññāpako), an inspirer (sandassako), an encourager (samādapako), an exhorter (samuttejako), a rejoicer (sampahaṃsako) of his fellow-brahmacārins’?”

“Venerable sir, the venerable Puṇṇa Mantāṇiputta is regarded thus among the monks of the native land who are your fellow-brahmacārins: ‘He himself is of few desires (appiccho) and speaks in praise of being of few desires; he himself is content (santuṭṭho)…pe… is an instructor (ovādako), an informer (viññāpako), an inspirer (sandassako), an encourager (samādapako), an exhorter (samuttejako), a rejoicer (sampahaṃsako) of his fellow-brahmacārins.’”
252.Evaṃme sutanti rathavinītasuttaṃ. Tattha rājagaheti evaṃnāmake nagare, tañhi mandhātumahāgovindādīhi pariggahitattā rājagahanti vuccati. Aññepettha pakāre vaṇṇayanti. Kiṃ tehi? Nāmametaṃ tassa nagarassa. Taṃ panetaṃ buddhakāle ca cakkavattikāle ca nagaraṃ hoti, sesakāle suññaṃ hoti yakkhapariggahitaṃ, tesaṃ vasantavanaṃ hutvā tiṭṭhati. Veḷuvane kalandakanivāpeti veḷuvananti tassa uyyānassa nāmaṃ, taṃ kira veḷūhi parikkhittaṃ ahosi aṭṭhārasahatthena ca pākārena, gopuraṭṭālakayuttaṃ nīlobhāsaṃ manoramaṃ, tena veḷuvananti vuccati. Kalandakānañcettha nivāpaṃ adaṃsu, tena kalandakanivāpoti vuccati. Pubbe kira aññataro rājā tattha uyyānakīḷanatthaṃ āgato surāmadena matto divāseyyaṃ upagato supi. Parijanopissa, ‘‘sutto rājā’’ti pupphaphalādīhi palobhiyamāno ito cito ca pakkāmi, atha surāgandhena aññatarasmā susirarukkhā kaṇhasappo nikkhamitvā raññābhimukho āgacchati. Taṃ disvā rukkhadevatā, ‘‘rañño jīvitaṃ dammī’’ti kāḷakavesena āgantvā kaṇṇamūle saddamakāsi. Rājā paṭibujjhi, kaṇhasappo nivatto. So taṃ disvā, ‘‘imāya mama jīvitaṃ dinna’’nti kāḷakānaṃ tattha nivāpaṃ paṭṭhapesi, abhayaghosanañca ghosāpesi. Tasmā taṃ tato pabhuti kalandakanivāpanti saṅkhyaṃ gataṃ. Kalandakāti kāḷakānaṃ nāmaṃ. kalandakanivāpa Kalandakā Jātibhūmikāti jātibhūmivāsino. Tattha jātibhūmīti jātaṭṭhānaṃ. Taṃ kho panetaṃ neva kosalamahārājādīnaṃ na caṅkībrāhamaṇādīnaṃ na sakkasuyāmasantusitādīnaṃ na asītimahāsāvakādīnaṃ na aññesaṃ sattānaṃ jātaṭṭhānaṃ ‘‘jātibhūmī’’ti vuccati. Yassa pana jātadivase dasasahassilokadhātu ekaddhajamālāvippakiṇṇakusumavāsacuṇṇagandhasugandhā sabbapāliphullamiva nandanavanaṃ virocamānā paduminipaṇṇe udakabindu viya akampittha, jaccandhādīnañca rūpadassanādīni anekāni pāṭihāriyāni pavattiṃsu, tassa sabbaññubodhisattassa jātaṭṭhānasākiyajanapado kapilavatthāhāro, sā ‘‘jātibhūmī’’ti vuccati. Jātibhūmikā jātibhūmī Dhammagarubhāvavaṇṇanā Vassaṃvuṭṭhāti temāsaṃ vassaṃvuṭṭhā pavāritapavāraṇā hutvā. Bhagavā etadavocāti ‘‘kacci, bhikkhave, khamanīya’’ntiādīhi vacanehi āgantukapaṭisanthāraṃ katvā etaṃ, ‘‘ko nu kho, bhikkhave’’tiādivacanamavoca. Te kira bhikkhu, – ‘‘kacci, bhikkhave, khamanīyaṃ kacci yāpanīyaṃ, kaccittha appakilamathena addhānaṃ āgatā, na ca piṇḍakena kilamittha, kuto ca tumhe, bhikkhave, āgacchathā’’ti paṭisanthāravasena pucchitā – ‘‘bhagavā sākiyajanapade kapilavatthāhārato jātibhūmito āgacchāmā’’ti āhaṃsu. Atha bhagavā neva suddhodanamahārājassa, na sakkodanassa, na sukkodanassa, na dhotodanassa, na amitodanassa, na amittāya deviyā, na mahāpajāpatiyā, na sakalassa sākiyamaṇḍalassa ārogyaṃ pucchi. Atha kho attanā ca dasakathāvatthulābhiṃ parañca tattha samādapetāraṃ paṭipattisampannaṃ bhikkhuṃ pucchanto idaṃ – ‘‘ko nu kho, bhikkhave’’tiādivacanaṃ avoca. Vassaṃvuṭṭhā Bhagavā etadavocā Kasmā pana bhagavā suddhodanādīnaṃ ārogyaṃ apucchitvā evarūpaṃ bhikkhumeva pucchati? Piyatāya. Buddhānañhi paṭipannakā bhikkhū bhikkhuniyo upāsakā upāsikāyo ca piyā honti manāpā. Kiṃ kāraṇā? Dhammagarutāya. Dhammagaruno hi tathāgatā, so ca nesaṃ dhammagarubhāvo, ‘‘dukkhaṃ kho agāravo viharati, appatisso’’ti (a. ni. 4.21) iminā ajapālanigrodhamūle uppannajjhāsayena veditabbo. Dhammagarutāyeva hi bhagavā mahākassapattherassa abhinikkhamanadivase paccuggamanaṃ karonto tigāvutaṃ maggaṃ agamāsi. Atirekatiyojanasataṃ maggaṃ gantvā gaṅgātīre dhammaṃ desetvā mahākappinaṃ saparisaṃ arahatte patiṭṭhapesi. Ekasmiṃ pacchābhatte pañcacattālīsayojanaṃ maggaṃ gantvā kumbhakārassa nivesane tiyāmarattiṃ dhammakathaṃ katvā pukkusātikulaputtaṃ anāgāmiphale patiṭṭhapesi. Vīsayojanasataṃ gantvā vanavāsisāmaṇerassa anuggahaṃ akāsi. Saṭṭhiyojanamaggaṃ gantvā khadiravaniyattherassa dhammaṃ desesi. Anuruddhatthero pācīnavaṃsadāye nisinno mahāpurisavitakkaṃ vitakketīti ñatvā tattha ākāsena gantvā therassa purato oruyha sādhukāramadāsi. Koṭikaṇṇasoṇattherassa ekagandhakuṭiyaṃ senāsanaṃ paññapāpetvā paccūsakāle dhammadesanaṃ ajjhesitvā sarabhaññapariyosāne sādhukāramadāsi. Tigāvutaṃ maggaṃ gantvā tiṇṇaṃ kulaputtānaṃ vasanaṭṭhāne gosiṅgasālavane sāmaggirasānisaṃsaṃ kathesi. Kassapopi bhagavā – ‘‘anāgāmiphale patiṭṭhito ariyasāvako aya’’nti vissāsaṃ uppādetvā ghaṭikārassa kumbhakārassa nivesanaṃ gantvā sahatthā āmisaṃ gahetvā paribhuñji. Amhākaṃyeva bhagavā upakaṭṭhāya vassūpanāyikāya jetavanato bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto cārikaṃ nikkhami. Kosalamahārājaanāthapiṇḍikādayo nivattetuṃ nāsakkhiṃsu. Anāthapiṇḍiko gharaṃ āgantvā domanassappatto nisīdi. Atha naṃ puṇṇā nāma dāsī domanassappattosi sāmīti āha. ‘‘Āma je, satthāraṃ nivattetuṃ nāsakkhiṃ, atha me imaṃ temāsaṃ dhammaṃ vā sotuṃ, yathādhippāyaṃ vā dānaṃ dātuṃ na labhissāmī’’ti cintā uppannāti. Ahampi sāmi satthāraṃ nivattessāmīti. Sace nivattetuṃ sakkosi, bhujissāyeva tvanti. Sā gantvā dasabalassa pādamūle nipajjitvā ‘‘nivattatha bhagavā’’ti āha. Puṇṇe tvaṃ parapaṭibaddhajīvikā kiṃ me karissasīti. Bhagavā mayhaṃ deyyadhammo natthīti tumhepi jānātha, tumhākaṃ nivattanapaccayā panāhaṃ tīsu saraṇesu pañcasu sīlesu patiṭṭhahissāmīti. Bhagavā sādhu sādhu puṇṇeti sādhukāraṃ katvā nivattetvā jetavanameva paviṭṭho. Ayaṃ kathā pākaṭā ahosi. Seṭṭhi sutvā puṇṇāya kira bhagavā nivattitoti taṃ bhujissaṃ katvā dhītuṭṭhāne ṭhapesi. Sā pabbajjaṃ yācitvā pabbaji, pabbajitvā vipassanaṃ ārabhi. Athassā satthā āraddhavipassakabhāvaṃ ñatvā imaṃ obhāsagāthaṃ vissajjesi – ‘‘Puṇṇe pūresi saddhammaṃ, cando pannaraso yathā; Paripuṇṇāya paññāya, dukkhassantaṃ karissasī’’ti. Thero āgantvā vanditvā, ‘‘kāya velāya, bhante, āgatatthā’’ti pucchi. Tayā suttante āraddhamatteti. Dukkaraṃ karittha, bhante, buddhasukhumālā tumheti. Sace tvaṃ, nanda, kappaṃ desetuṃ sakkuṇeyyāsi, kappamattampāhaṃ ṭhitakova suṇeyyanti bhagavā avoca. Evaṃ dhammagaruno tathāgatā. Tesaṃ dhammagarutāya paṭipannakā piyā honti, tasmā paṭipannake pucchi. Ayaṃ attahitāya paṭipanno nāma no parahitāya paṭipanno, evarūpaṃ bhikkhuṃ bhagavā na pucchati. Kasmā? Na mayhaṃ sāsanassa vaḍḍhipakkhe ṭhitoti. Yo pana dasannaṃ kathāvatthūnaṃ alābhī, paraṃ tehi ovadati tena katavattasādiyanatthaṃ upanando sakyaputto viya, ayaṃ parahitāya paṭipanno nāma no attahitāya, evarūpampi na pucchati. Kasmā? Assa taṇhā mahāpacchi viya appahīnāti. Yo attanāpi dasannaṃ kathāvatthūnaṃ alābhī, parampi tehi na ovadati, lāḷudāyī viya, ayaṃ neva attahitāya paṭipanno na parahitāya, evarūpampi na pucchati. Kasmā? Assa anto kilesā pharasuchejjā viya mahantāti. Yo pana sayaṃ dasannaṃ kathāvatthūnaṃ lābhī, parampi tehi ovadati, ayaṃ attahitāya ceva parahitāya ca paṭipanno nāma sāriputtamoggallānamahākassapādayo asītimahātherā viya, evarūpaṃ bhikkhuṃ pucchati. Evaṃ bhagavatā puṭṭhānaṃ pana tesaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ bhagavā attano jātibhūmiyaṃ ubhayahitāya paṭipannaṃ dasakathāvatthulābhiṃ bhikkhuṃ pucchati, ko nu kho tattha evarūpoti na aññamaññaṃ cintanā vā samantanā vā ahosi. "In Veḷuvana, in the kalandakanivāpa" means that Veḷuvana was the name of that park. It is said that it was enclosed by bamboo and surrounded by an eighteen-cubit-high wall, complete with gate towers and watchtowers, beautiful and gleaming with blue light. Therefore it was called Veḷuvana.
There, they gave alms to the squirrels; therefore, it was called Kalandakanivāpa.

Once upon a time, a king came there to enjoy the park. Overcome with intoxication from strong drink, he went to a day-bed and fell asleep.
His attendants, thinking, "The king is asleep," went away, enticed by flowers, fruits, and other things. Then, a black snake came out of a certain hollow tree because of the smell of liquor and approached the king.
Seeing him, a tree goddess, thinking, "I will give life to the king," came and made a sound in his ear in the form of a black crow.
The king awoke, and the black snake retreated.
Seeing it, he thought, "She gave me life!" and established an offering for the crows there, and proclaimed a guarantee of safety.
Therefore, from that time on, it was called Kalandakanivāpa.
"Kalandakā" means the name of the crows.

Ascertaining the Desirelessness of Achievement

Venerable Puṇṇa, having abandoned excessive desire ( atricchatā), evil desire (pāpicchatā), and great desire (mahicchatā), was entirely endowed with desirelessness (appicchatā), which is the opposite of desire, reckoned as non-greed (alobha), and perfectly pure. Thus, he was known as "Desireless" (appiccha).

He also taught the monks, saying, "Friends, these things—excessive desire (atricchatā), evil desire (pāpicchatā), and great desire (mahicchatā)—should be abandoned," showing them the dangers in those things, and encouraging them to cultivate such desirelessness (appicchatā). Thus, he told the story of desirelessness (appicchakathaṃ).

Therefore, it was said of him, "He himself was desireless (appiccha), and he was a maker of the story of desirelessness (appicchakatha) for the monks."

Explanation of the Twelvefold Contentment

Now, we will explain the meaning of the particulars in "Content with himself (attanā ca santuṭṭho)," and so on.

The interpretation should be understood according to the method stated.

"Content (santuṭṭho)" means endowed with contentment based on mutual conditions.

And that contentment (santosa) is twelvefold.

That is to say, in regard to the robe ( cīvara), there is contentment with what one obtains ( yathālābhasantosa), contentment with one's strength (yathābalasantosa), and contentment with suitability (yathāsāruppasantosa); thus it is threefold, and so also in regard to alms-food ( piṇḍapāta) and so forth.

Here is the explanation of its divisions:

Content with himself (attanā ca santuṭṭho), Content (santuṭṭho)

Here, a monk obtains a robe (cīvara) that is beautiful (sundaraṃ) or not beautiful (asundaraṃ).

He lives with just that, he does not desire another. Even when obtaining [another], he does not take it. This is his contentment with what one obtains ( yathālābhasantosa) in regard to the robe (cīvara).

Then, if one is naturally weak (pakatidubbalo) or is overcome with sickness and old age (ābādhajarābhibhūto), and wearing a heavy robe (garucīvaraṃ) he tires (kilamati), then having exchanged it with a fellow monk (sabhāgena bhikkhunā), he is content living with a light one (lahukena yāpentopi santuṭṭhova hoti). This is his contentment with strength (yathābalasantoso) in regard to the robe (cīvara).

Another, having obtained a choice requisite (paṇītapaccayalābhī), having obtained one of the valuable robes (mahagghacīvaraṃ) such as a silk robe ( paṭṭacīvara) and so on, or even many robes (bahūni vā pana cīvarāni), [thinking] "This is suitable for the elders ( therānaṃ), those long gone forth ( cirapabbajitānaṃ), this is fitting for the learned ( bahussutānaṃ), this for the sick ( gilānānaṃ), this for those of little gain (appalābhānaṃ)," having given [them away] and even wearing their old robes ( purāṇacīvaraṃ), or having gathered cast-off cloths from a heap of rubbish ( saṅkārakūṭādito vā nantakāni uccinitvā) and having made a double-robe ( saṅghāṭiṃ katvā) out of them, he is content. This is his contentment with suitability (yathāsāruppasantoso) in regard to the robe (cīvara).

Unassociated means: separated from the five kinds of association (saṃsagga).

Buddhānañhi, for Buddhas, when dwelling in one place, there is no lack of adversity such as problems with shade and water, or uncomfortable lodging, or the faithlessness of humans.

There is also no prolonged dwelling after delighting in their prosperity, thinking "Here, we shall live comfortably."

But where, while the tathāgata is dwelling, beings take refuge, or undertake the precepts, or go forth, then for them there is a basis for the path to stream-entry and so on.

There, Buddhas dwell with the intention of establishing beings in those prosperities; in the absence of those, they depart.

Therefore it was said: "Having lived as one pleases."

Once, it seems, the elder said, "Bhante, I will go to Tissasāmaṇera."

There are two kinds of upādāna (grasping): grasping of acquisition (gahaṇūpādāna) and grasping of condition (paccayūpādāna).

"Gahaṇūpādānaṃ" means the fourfold grasping of sense pleasures etc., "paccayūpādānaṃ" means the conditioned things (saṅkhārā) because of ignorance, thus are the conditioned things spoken of.

There, the teachers who advocate grasping of acquisition (gahaṇūpādānavādino) say that anupādāparinibbāna is the fruition of arhatship that occurs without grasping any dhamma by any of the four graspings, and thus they speak of anupādāparinibbāna.

For indeed, it is neither associated with grasping nor does it grasp any dhamma, and because the defilements have ceased, it is called parinibbāna.

But the advocates of conditional grasping (paccayūpādānavādino) say that anupādāparinibbāna is appaccayaparinibbāna (cessation without conditions).
They declare that the unconditioned asaṅkhataṃ deathless element amatadhātu which has not arisen due to conditions, is indeed anupādāparinibbāna.

This is the end anta, this is the limit koṭi, this is the completion niṭṭhā.

It is a gain for his companions in the holy life, a great gain for his companions in the holy life, that they have the opportunity to see and honour the venerable Śāriputra.

Even if it were by carrying the venerable Śāriputra about on a cushion on their heads that his companions in the holy life would get the opportunity to see and honour him, it would be a gain for them, a great gain for them. And it is a gain for us, a great gain for us that we have the opportunity to see and honor the venerable Śāriputra."

Thus it was that these two great beings rejoiced in each other’s good words.

The Rathavinīta Sutta, the fourth, is finished.
