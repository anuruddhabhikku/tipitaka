<p n="276" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">276</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0352"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0230"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā ānando upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandasseti samādapeti samuttejeti <pb ed="P" n="3.0190"/> sampahaṃseti, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca bhāsati.

<p rend="bodytext">Atha kho bhagavā sāyanhasamayaṃ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yenupaṭṭhānasālā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Nisajja kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘ko nu kho, bhikkhave, upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsī’’ti? ‘‘Āyasmā, bhante, ānando upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsī’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Atha kho bhagavā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘yathā kathaṃ pana tvaṃ, ānanda, bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi <pb ed="M" n="3.0229"/>, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsī’’ti? ‘‘Evaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, bhikkhūnaṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassesiṃ samādapesiṃ samuttejesiṃ sampahaṃsesiṃ, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsiṃ –

<pb ed="M" n="3.0229"/> <p rend="gatha1">‘‘Atītaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0353"/> nānvāgameyya, nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ;

<pb ed="T" n="3.0353"/> <p rend="gathalast">Yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ, appattañca anāgataṃ.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ, tattha tattha vipassati;

<p rend="gathalast">Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ, taṃ vidvā manubrūhaye.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Ajjeva <pb ed="V" n="3.0231"/> kiccamātappaṃ, ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve;

<pb ed="V" n="3.0231"/> <p rend="gathalast">Na hi no saṅgaraṃ tena, mahāsenena maccunā.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ, ahorattamatanditaṃ;

<p rend="gathalast">Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate muni’’.

Thus have I heard:

Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Sāvatthī (sāvatthiyaṃ), in the Jeta Grove (jetavane), at Anāthapindika’s (anāthapiṇḍikassa) monastery (ārāme).

Now on that occasion, the venerable Ānanda (āyasmā ānando) was instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) in the assembly hall (upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ) with talk on the Dharma (dhammiyā kathāya), and he was reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of the "Auspicious One Night" (bhaddekarattassa).

Then, in the evening (sāyanhasamayaṃ), the Blessed One (bhagavā), rose from seclusion (paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito) and went to the assembly hall (upaṭṭhānasālā) and, on arrival, sat down on a seat (āsane) made ready (paññatte nisīdi).

Having sat down (nisajja), the Blessed One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū) – “Who, O monks (bhikkhave), has been instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) in the assembly hall (upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ) with talk on the Dharma (dhammiyā kathāya), and reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of the "Auspicious One Night" (bhaddekarattassa) (abhāsī)?”

“[It was] the venerable Ānanda (āyasmā ānando), O Blessed One (bhante), [who] in the assembly hall (upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ) was instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) with talk on the Dharma (dhammiyā kathāya), and reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of the "Auspicious One Night" (bhaddekarattassa),”

Then the Blessed One (bhagavā) addressed the venerable Ānanda (āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ) – “How (yathā kathaṃ) were you, Ānanda (ānanda), instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) with talk on the Dharma (dhammiyā kathāya), and reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of the "Auspicious One Night" (bhaddekarattassa) (abhāsī)?”

“In this way (evaṃ kho), O Blessed One (bhante), I was instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) with talk on the Dharma (dhammiyā kathāya), and reciting the summary (uddesaṃ ca) and exposition (vibhaṅgaṃ ca) of the "Auspicious One Night" (bhaddekarattassa) (abhāsiṃ) –

"One should not yearn for the past (atītaṃ nānvāgameyya), nor should one long for the future (nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ);

For the past is gone (yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ), and the future is yet unreached (appattañca anāgataṃ).

But whoever discerns (vipassati) the present (paccuppannaṃ) Dharma (dhammaṃ) in each and every instance (tattha tattha);

Unwavering (asaṃhīraṃ), unshakeable (asaṃkuppaṃ), knowing this (taṃ vidvā) one should cultivate it (manubrūhaye).

Today (ajjeva) the effort (kiccamātappaṃ) must be made (kiccamātappaṃ), who knows (ko jaññā) if death (maraṇaṃ) will come tomorrow (suve);

For there is no bargaining (na hi no saṅgaraṃ) with Mortality (maccunā) and his mighty horde (mahāsenena).

One who dwells (vihāriṃ) thus, ardent (ātāpiṃ), unwearied (atanditaṃ) day and night (ahorattamatan);

That one (taṃ ve) truly (ve) is called (iti) "Auspicious One Night" (bhaddekarattoti), so (iti) the sage (muni) declares (ācikkhate)."

277. “And how, friend, does one revive the past? Thinking, ‘I had such material form in the past,’ one finds delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such feeling in the past’…! had such perception in the past’…! had such formations in the past,’…! had such consciousness in the past,’ one finds delight in that – that is how, friend, one revives the past.

“And how, friend, does one not revive the past? Thinking, ‘I had such material form in the past,’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such feeling in the past’…! had such perception in the past’…! had such formations in the past,’…! had such consciousness in the past’ one does not find delight in that. That is how, friend, one does not revive the past.

“And how, friend, does one build up hope upon the future? Thinking, ‘I may have such material form in the future’ one finds delight in that. Thinking, ‘I may have such feeling in the future,’… ‘I may have such perception in the future’…! may have such formations in the future’… ‘I may have such consciousness in the future,’ one finds delight in that – that is how, friend, one builds up hope upon the future.

“And how, friend, does one not build up hope upon the future? Thinking, ‘I may have such material form in the future’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I may have such feeling in the future,’… ‘I may have such perception in the future’…! may have such formations in the future’… ‘I may have such consciousness in the future,’ one does not find delight in that – that is how, friend, one does not build up hope upon the future.

“And how, friend, does one get carried away with regard to present phenomena? Here, friend, an uninstructed run-of-the-mill person who has not seen the noble ones, is not versed in the teachings of the noble ones, is not trained in the teachings of the noble ones, has not seen good people, is not versed in the teachings of good people, is not trained in the teachings of good people, sees form as self ( ātman), or self as possessing form, or form as in self, or self as in form. He/she sees feeling… perception… formations… consciousness as self, or self as possessing consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness - that is how, friend, one gets carried away with regard to present phenomena.

“And how, friend, does one not get carried away with regard to present phenomena? Here, friend, a well-instructed noble disciple who has seen the noble ones, is versed in the teachings of the noble ones, is well-trained in the teachings of the noble ones, has seen good people, is versed in the teachings of good people, is well-trained in the teachings of good people, does not see form as self, or self as possessing form, or form as in self, or self as in form. He/she does not see feeling… perception… formations… consciousness as self, or self as possessing consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness - that is how, friend, one does not get carried away with regard to present phenomena.

“Do not revive the past, nor build up hope upon the future;

For the past is left behind, and the future not yet reached.

“But one sees each present phenomenon

With discernment, without getting carried away, unshaken; having known it, one should develop it.

“Today the effort must be made; who knows, death may come tomorrow?

For there is no bargaining with Death and his mighty host.

“The sage calls that person truly fortunate who lives thus, ardent, untiring day and night.”

“Thus, venerable sir, I was instructing, urging, rousing, and encouraging the monks with talk on the Dharma, and reciting the summary and exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’."

278. "Excellent, excellent, Ānanda! Excellent, Ānanda, that you teach, train, encourage, and gladden the monks with a talk on the Dharma, and that you have spoken on both the outline and the detailed explanation of the auspicious one who has had a single night."

"One should not pursue the past…pe…

The sages call him truly auspicious who has had a single night."

"And how, Ānanda, does one revive the past? …pe… Thus, Ānanda, does one revive the past.

And how, Ānanda, does one not revive the past? …pe… Thus, Ānanda, does one not revive the past.

And how, Ānanda, does one build up hope upon the future? …pe… Thus, Ānanda, does one build up hope upon the future.

And how, Ānanda, does one not build up hope upon the future? …pe… Thus, Ānanda, does one not build up hope upon the future.

And how, Ānanda, is one invincible in regard to presently arisen states? …pe… Thus, Ānanda, is one invincible in regard to presently arisen states.

And how, Ānanda, is one not invincible in regard to presently arisen states? …pe… Thus, Ānanda, is one not invincible in regard to presently arisen states."

"One should not pursue the past…pe…"

"The sages call him truly auspicious who has had a single night."

This the Blessed One said. The venerable Ānanda, being pleased, rejoiced in the Exalted One’s words.

The Ānandabhaddekarattasuttaṃ, the second [sutta], is finished.
