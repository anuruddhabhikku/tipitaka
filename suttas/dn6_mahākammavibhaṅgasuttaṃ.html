298. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagṛha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels' Sanctuary.

At that time, the venerable Samiddhi was staying in a forest hut.

Then the wanderer Potaliputta, while wandering and walking for exercise, went to the venerable Samiddhi, and exchanged greetings with him.

Having exchanged courteous greetings and amiable talk, he sat down at one side.

Seated to one side, the wanderer Potaliputta said to the venerable Samiddhi: “Friend Samiddhi, I have heard and learned this directly from the mouth of the Śramaṇa Gautama: ‘Bodily action (kāyakarma) is vain, verbal action (vacīkarma) is vain, only mental action (manokarma) is real.’

But there is that attainment (samāpatti) on entering which one does not feel anything at all?”

“No, friend Potaliputta, do not speak like that; (do not speak like that, friend Potaliputta;) do not misrepresent the Blessed One.

It is not good to misrepresent the Blessed One.

The Blessed One would never say this: ‘Bodily action (kāyakarma) is vain, verbal action (vacīkarma) is vain, only mental action (manokarma) is real.’

But there is, friend, that attainment (samāpatti) on entering which one does not feel anything at all.”

“How long have you been ordained, friend Samiddhi?”

“Not long, friend! Three years.”

“Now, what should we say to the elder monks, when such a new monk thinks the Teacher should be defended?

Having done an intentional action (karma), friend Samiddhi, through body, speech, or mind, what does one feel?”

“Having done an intentional action (karma), friend Potaliputta, through body, speech, or mind, one feels suffering (duḥkha).”

Then, neither approving nor disapproving of the venerable Samiddhi’s words, the wanderer Potaliputta rose from his seat and departed.

299. Then, after the wanderer Potaliputta had recently departed, the Venerable Samiddhi approached the Venerable Ānanda; having approached, he exchanged greetings with the Venerable Ānanda. When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side. Seated to one side, the Venerable Samiddhi reported to the Venerable Ānanda his entire conversation with the wanderer Potaliputta.

When this was said, the Venerable Ānanda said to the Venerable Samiddhi: “Friend Samiddhi, this conversation warrants being shown to the Blessed One. Come, friend Samiddhi, let us approach the Blessed One; having approached, we will report this matter to the Blessed One. As the Blessed One explains it to us, so we shall bear it in mind.” “Yes, friend,” the Venerable Samiddhi replied to the Venerable Ānanda.

Then the Venerable Ānanda and the Venerable Samiddhi approached the Blessed One; having approached, they paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the Venerable Ānanda reported to the Blessed One his entire conversation with the Venerable Samiddhi and the wanderer Potaliputta.

When this was said, the Blessed One said to the Venerable Ānanda: “Ānanda, I do not even recognize the sight of the wanderer Potaliputta, how much less a conversation with him? And, Ānanda, this foolish man Samiddhi has explained a question that should be answered with a detailed analysis to the wanderer Potaliputta with a one-sided answer.”

When this was said, the Venerable Udāyī said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, perhaps the Venerable Samiddhi spoke thus referring to the principle: ‘Whatever is felt is suffering.’”

Then the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “See, Ānanda, how this misguided man Udāyin interferes? I knew, Ānanda, that this misguided man Udāyin would unduly interfere right now.”

From the start the wanderer Potaliputta had asked about the three kinds of feeling.

This misguided man Samiddhi would have answered the wanderer Potaliputta rightly if, when asked thus, he would have explained: ‘Friend Potaliputta, having done an intentional action by way of body, speech, or mind whose result is to be felt as pleasant, one feels pleasure. Having done an intentional action by way of body, speech, or mind whose result is to be felt as painful, one feels pain. Having done an intentional action by way of body, speech, or mind whose result is to be felt as neither painful nor pleasant, one feels neither pain nor pleasure.’

But who are these foolish, thoughtless wanderers of other sects, that they could understand the Tathāgata’s great exposition of action? If you, Ānanda, were to hear the Tathāgata expounding the great exposition of action!”

“This is the time, Blessed One, this is the time, Sublime One, for the Blessed One to expound the great exposition of action. Having heard it from the Blessed One, the bhikkhus will remember it.”

“Then listen, Ānanda, and attend closely to what I shall say.”

“So be it, O Lord.”

The Blessed One said this:

“There are these four kinds of individuals who are found existing in the world, Ānanda. Which four? Here, Ānanda, some person kills living beings (prāṇātipātī), takes what is not given (adinnādāyī), misconducts himself in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārī), speaks falsehood (musāvādī), speaks maliciously (pisuṇavāco), speaks harshly (pharusavāco), gossips (samphappalāpī); he is covetous (abhijjhālu), has a mind of ill will (byāpannacitto), and holds wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi). On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, perdition, even in hell (apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjati).”

“But here, Ānanda, some person kills living beings (prāṇātipātī), takes what is not given (adinnādāyī), misconducts himself in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārī), speaks falsehood (musāvādī), speaks maliciously (pisuṇavāco), speaks harshly (pharusavāco), gossips (samphappalāpī); he is covetous (abhijjhālu), has a mind of ill will (byāpannacitto), and holds wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi). On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a happy destination, even in the heavenly world (sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapajjati).”

“Here, Ānanda, some person abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato), from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato), from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato), from false speech (musāvādā paṭivirato), from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato), from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato), from gossip (samphappalāpā paṭivirato); he is not covetous (anabhijjhālu), his mind is without ill will (abyāpannacitto), and he holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi). Because of that, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, in the downfall, even in hell. But since he has here killed living beings, taken what is not given…and held wrong view (mithyādṛṣṭi), he will experience the result of that either here and now, or in his next rebirth, or in some subsequent existence.

“Therein, Ānanda, as to the person here who kills living beings, takes what is not given… and holds wrong view (mithyādṛṣṭi), on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappears in a happy destination, even in the heavenly world (sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ); either earlier he did a good action to be felt as pleasant, or later he did a good action to be felt as pleasant, or at the time of death he acquired and undertook right view (sammādiṭṭhi). Because of that, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a happy destination, even in the heavenly world (sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ). But since he has here killed living beings, taken what is not given…and held wrong view (mithyādṛṣṭi), he will experience the result of that either here and now, or in his next rebirth, or in some subsequent existence.

“Therein, Ānanda, as to the person here who abstains from killing living beings, abstains from taking what is not given…and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappears in a happy destination, even in the heavenly world (sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ); either earlier he did a good action to be felt as pleasant, or later he did a good action to be felt as pleasant, or at the time of death he acquired and undertook right view (sammādiṭṭhi). Because of that, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a happy destination, even in the heavenly world (sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ). But since he has here abstained from killing living beings, abstains from taking what is not given…and held right view (sammādiṭṭhi), he will experience the result of that either here and now, or in his next rebirth, or in some subsequent existence.

“Therein, Ānanda, as to the person here who abstains from killing living beings, abstains from taking what is not given…and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappears in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, in the downfall, even in hell; either earlier he did an evil action to be felt as painful, or later he did an evil action to be felt as painful, or at the time of death he acquired and undertook wrong view (mithyādṛṣṭhi). Because of that, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, in the downfall, even in hell. But since he has here abstained from killing living beings, abstains from taking what is not given…and held right view (sammādiṭṭhi), he will experience the result of that either here and now, or in his next rebirth, or in some subsequent existence.

“Thus, Ānanda, there is action that is incapable of good result and appears incapable; there is action that is incapable of good result and appears capable; there is action that is capable of good result and appears capable; and there is action that is capable of good result and appears incapable.”

Thus spoke the Lord. The venerable Ānanda was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Great Analysis of Action Discourse, the Sixth, is finished.
