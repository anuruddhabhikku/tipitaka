92. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā kosalesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ yena sālā nāma kosalānaṃ brāhmaṇagāmo tadavasari. Assosuṃ kho sāleyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā – ‘‘samaṇo khalu bho gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito kosalesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ sālaṃ anuppatto. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. So imaṃ lokaṃ sadevakaṃ samārakaṃ sabrahmakaṃ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ pajaṃ sadevamanussaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti. So dhammaṃ deseti ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’ti. Atha kho sāleyyakā brāhmaṇagahapatikā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā appekacce bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu; sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce yena bhagavā tenañjaliṃ paṇāmetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce bhagavato santike nāmagottaṃ sāvetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Appekacce tuṇhībhūtā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu.
92. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the Kosala country with a large Saṅgha of monks, and he arrived at a town of Brahmins in Kosala, called Sāla.

The householders who were Brahmins of Sāla heard, "The ascetic Gotama, the son of the Sakyans, who went forth from the Sakyan clan, is wandering in the Kosala country with a large Saṅgha of monks, and has arrived at Sāla."

Now a good report of that Blessed Gotama has spread to this effect: 'Indeed, the Blessed One is an arhat, a perfectly enlightened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, a Well-Farer, knower of the worlds, unsurpassed trainer of persons to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans, enlightened, blessed.' ( arahant = worthy one)

He, having realized it by his own super-knowledge, proclaims this world with its gods ( devas), Māras, and Brahmās, its ascetics and Brahmins, its princes and people.

He teaches the Dhamma ( dharma) that is good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with the right meaning and phrasing; he reveals the spiritual life ( brahmacariya) that is utterly perfect and pure.

"It is good to see such arahants."

Then the householders of Sāla, who were Brahmins, went to the Blessed One. On arrival, some bowed down to the Blessed One and sat to one side. Some exchanged greetings with the Blessed One, and when this courteous and amiable talk was finished, sat down to one side. Some extended their hands in reverential salutation towards the Blessed One and sat down to one side.

Some announced their name and clan in the Blessed One's presence and sat down to one side. Some kept silent and sat down to one side.

93.Atthi pana vo gahapatayoti kasmā āha? So kira gāmo aṭavidvāre niviṭṭho. Nānāvidhā samaṇabrāhmaṇā divasaṃ maggaṃ gantvā sāyaṃ taṃ gāmaṃ vāsatthāya upenti, tesaṃ te manussā mañcapīṭhāni pattharitvā pāde dhovitvā pāde makkhetvā kappiyapānakāni datvā punadivase nimantetvā dānaṃ denti. Te pasannacittā tehi saddhiṃ sammantayamānā evaṃ vadanti ‘‘atthi pana vo gahapatayo kiñci dassanaṃ gahita’’nti? Natthi, bhanteti. ‘‘Gahapatayo vinā dassanena loko na niyyāti, ekaṃ dassanaṃ ruccitvā khamāpetvā gahetuṃ vaṭṭati, ‘sassato loko’ti dassanaṃ gaṇhathā’’ti vatvā pakkantā. Aparadivase aññe āgatā. Tepi tatheva pucchiṃsu. Te tesaṃ ‘‘āma, bhante, purimesu divasesu tumhādisā samaṇabrāhmaṇā āgantvā ‘sassato loko’ti amhe idaṃ dassanaṃ gāhāpetvā gatā’’ti ārocesuṃ. ‘‘Te bālā kiṃ jānanti? ‘Ucchijjati ayaṃ loko’ti ucchedadassanaṃ gaṇhathā’’ti evaṃ tepi ucchedadassanaṃ gaṇhāpetvā pakkantā. Etenupāyena aññe ekaccasassataṃ, aññe antānantaṃ , aññe amarāvikkhepanti evaṃ dvāsaṭṭhi diṭṭhiyo uggaṇhāpesuṃ. Te pana ekadiṭṭhiyampi patiṭṭhātuṃ nāsakkhiṃsu. Sabbapacchā bhagavā agamāsi. So tesaṃ hitatthāya pucchanto ‘‘atthi pana vo gahapatayo’’tiādimāha. Tattha ākāravatīti kāraṇavatī sahetukā. Apaṇṇakoti aviraddho advejjhagāmī ekaṃsagāhiko.
93. Why did he say, "Are there householders present?" It seems that the village was situated at the Aṭavī gate.

Various śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas went on the road during the day, and in the evening they approached that village for lodging. Those people spread out seats and benches for them, washed their feet, anointed their feet, gave them suitable drinks, and invited them for the next day, giving alms.

Those with pleased minds, consulting with them, said thus: "Is there any doctrine accepted by you householders?" "No, venerable sirs."

"Householders, without a doctrine, the world does not proceed. It is fitting to accept one doctrine, having approved and condoned it. Accept the doctrine, 'The world is eternal!'" So saying, they departed.

On the next day, others came.

They asked the same way.

They told them, "Yes, venerable sirs, on previous days, śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas like you came and made us accept this doctrine, 'The world is eternal!' and left."

"What do those fools know? Accept the doctrine of annihilation, 'This world is annihilated!'" Thus, they too, having made [them] accept the doctrine of annihilation, departed.

In this way, some [taught] eternalism in some respects, others [taught] the infinity of the end, others [taught] the sport of the immortals. Thus, they taught sixty-two views.

But they were not able to be established in even one view.

The Blessed One came last of all.

He, asking for their benefit, spoke, beginning with "Are there householders?"

There, ākāravatī means having a cause (kāraṇavatī), having a reason (sahetukā).

Apaṇṇaka means non-erroneous (aviraddha), going to the non-dual (advejjhagāmī), grasping the one aspect (ekaṃsagāhiko).

94. ‘‘Santi , gahapatayo, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘natthi dinnaṃ, natthi yiṭṭhaṃ, natthi hutaṃ; natthi sukatadukkaṭānaṃ sukaṭadukkaṭānaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko, natthi ayaṃ loko, natthi paro loko; natthi mātā, natthi pitā; natthi sattā opapātikā; natthi loke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sammaggatā samaggatā (ka.) sammā paṭipannā ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī’ti. Tesaṃyeva kho, gahapatayo, samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā ujuvipaccanīkavādā. Te evamāhaṃsu – ‘atthi dinnaṃ, atthi yiṭṭhaṃ, atthi hutaṃ; atthi sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko; atthi ayaṃ loko, atthi paro loko; atthi mātā, atthi pitā; atthi sattā opapātikā; atthi loke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sammaggatā sammā paṭipannā ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī’ti. Taṃ kiṃ maññatha, gahapatayo – ‘nanume samaṇabrāhmaṇā aññamaññassa ujuvipaccanīkavādā’’’ti? ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’.
94. Householders, there are some ascetics and brāhmaṇas who maintain the following doctrine and view: ‘There is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed. There is no fruit or result of good or bad actions. There is no this world, no next world, no mother, no father; there are no spontaneously reborn beings; there are no ascetics or brāhmaṇas in the world who have rightly attained and practice rightly, and who after having directly known and realized this world and the next, proclaim them.’

However, householders, there are other ascetics and brāhmaṇas who hold directly opposing views. They say: ‘There is what is given, what is offered, what is sacrificed. There are fruits and results of good and bad actions. There is this world and the next world. There is mother and father. There are spontaneously reborn beings; there are ascetics and brāhmaṇas in the world who have rightly attained and practice rightly, and who after having directly known and realized this world and the next, proclaim them.’

What do you think, householders? Are not the assertions of these ascetics and brāhmaṇas directly opposed to one another?" "Yes, venerable sir."

95. ‘‘Tatra, gahapatayo, ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘natthi dinnaṃ, natthi yiṭṭhaṃ…pe… ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī’ti tesametaṃ pāṭikaṅkhaṃ? Yamidaṃ yadidaṃ (ka.) kāyasucaritaṃ, vacīsucaritaṃ, manosucaritaṃ – ime tayo kusale dhamme abhinivajjetvā abhinibbajjetvā (syā. kaṃ.), abhinibbijjitvā (ka.) yamidaṃ yadidaṃ (ka.) kāyaduccaritaṃ, vacīduccaritaṃ, manoduccaritaṃ – ime tayo akusale dhamme samādāya vattissanti. Taṃ kissa hetu? Na hi te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā passanti akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ādīnavaṃ okāraṃ saṃkilesaṃ, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ nekkhamme ānisaṃsaṃ vodānapakkhaṃ. Santaṃyeva pana paraṃ lokaṃ ‘natthi paro loko’ tissa diṭṭhi hoti; sāssa hoti micchādiṭṭhi. Santaṃyeva kho pana paraṃ lokaṃ ‘natthi paro loko’ti saṅkappeti; svāssa hoti micchāsaṅkappo. Santaṃyeva kho pana paraṃ lokaṃ ‘natthi paro loko’ti vācaṃ bhāsati; sāssa hoti micchāvācā. Santaṃyeva kho pana paraṃ lokaṃ ‘natthi paro loko’ti āha; ye te arahanto paralokaviduno tesamayaṃ paccanīkaṃ karoti. Santaṃyeva kho pana paraṃ lokaṃ ‘natthi paro loko’ti paraṃ saññāpeti paññāpeti (ka.); sāssa hoti asaddhammasaññatti assaddhammapaññatti (ka.). Tāya ca pana asaddhammasaññattiyā attānukkaṃseti, paraṃ vambheti. Iti pubbeva kho panassa susīlyaṃ pahīnaṃ hoti, dussīlyaṃ paccupaṭṭhitaṃ – ayañca micchādiṭṭhi micchāsaṅkappo micchāvācā ariyānaṃ paccanīkatā asaddhammasaññatti attukkaṃsanā paravambhanā. Evamassime evaṃ’si’me’ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) aneke pāpakā akusalā dhammā sambhavanti micchādiṭṭhipaccayā. ‘‘Tatra , gahapatayo, viññū puriso iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘sace kho natthi paro loko evamayaṃ bhavaṃ purisapuggalo kāyassa bhedā sotthimattānaṃ karissati; sace kho atthi paro loko evamayaṃ bhavaṃ purisapuggalo kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjissati. Kāmaṃ kho pana māhu paro loko, hotu nesaṃ bhavataṃ samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ saccaṃ vacanaṃ; atha ca panāyaṃ bhavaṃ purisapuggalo diṭṭheva dhamme viññūnaṃ gārayho – dussīlo purisapuggalo micchādiṭṭhi natthikavādo’ti. Sace kho attheva paro loko, evaṃ imassa bhoto purisapuggalassa ubhayattha kaliggaho – yañca diṭṭheva dhamme viññūnaṃ gārayho, yañca kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjissati. Evamassāyaṃ apaṇṇako dhammo dussamatto samādinno, ekaṃsaṃ pharitvā tiṭṭhati, riñcati kusalaṃ ṭhānaṃ.
95. “Now, householders, those ascetics and Brahmins who speak thus, who hold such views—’There is nothing given, there is nothing offered’…and so on… ‘those who declare this world and the next, having directly known and realized them by themselves with higher knowledge’—what is to be expected of them? That is, good bodily conduct kāyasucarita, good verbal conduct vacīsucarita, good mental conduct manosucarita—having abandoned these three wholesome dharmas, and having adopted bad bodily conduct kāyaduccarita, bad verbal conduct vacīduccarita, bad mental conduct manoduccarita—they will live according to these three unwholesome dharmas.

What is the reason for this? It is because those venerable ascetics and Brahmins do not see the danger, inferiority, and defilement in unwholesome dharmas, and the benefit, the cleansing side of renunciation in wholesome dharmas.

Indeed, while there is another world paraṃ lokaṃ, the view arises in him that ‘there is no other world paro loko’; that is his wrong view micchādiṭṭhi.

Indeed, while there is another world paraṃ lokaṃ, he resolves that ‘there is no other world paro loko’; that is his wrong resolve micchāsaṅkappo.

Indeed, while there is another world paraṃ lokaṃ, he utters the speech that ‘there is no other world paro loko’; that is his wrong speech micchāvācā.

Indeed, while there is another world paraṃ lokaṃ, he says that ‘there is no other world paro loko’; he makes himself an opponent of those ascetics (arahants) who know the other world paralokaviduno.

Indeed, while there is another world paraṃ lokaṃ, he persuades another that ‘there is no other world paro loko’; that is persuasion in what is not true Dhamma asaddhammasaññatti.

And through that persuasion in what is not true Dhamma asaddhammasaññattiyā, he exalts himself and disparages others.

Thus, even earlier his good morality susīlyaṃ is abandoned, and bad morality dussīlyaṃ arises. And this wrong view micchādiṭṭhi, wrong resolve micchāsaṅkappo, wrong speech micchāvācā, opposition to the noble ones (ariyānaṃ), persuasion in what is not true Dhamma asaddhammasaññatti, self-exaltation attukkaṃsanā, and disparagement of others paravambhanā—

Thus, in him, these many evil unwholesome dharmas arise, conditioned by wrong view micchādiṭṭhipaccayā.

“Now, householders, a wise person reflects thus: ‘If there is no other world paro loko, then this good person, this individual, at the breaking up of the body, will keep himself in safety; but if there is another world paro loko, then this good person, this individual, at the breaking up of the body, after death, will go to a state of loss (apāyaṃ), a bad destiny (duggatiṃ), a downfall (vinipātaṃ), hell (nirayaṃ).

Even if there is no other world paro loko, let the word of those ascetics and Brahmins be true; nevertheless, this good person, this individual, is still censured in this very life by the wise: ‘A person of bad character (dussīlo), a person of wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi), an annihilationist (natthikavādo).’

And if indeed there is another world paro loko, then for this good person, this individual, there is a losing throw in both respects: in that he is censured in this very life by the wise, and in that, at the breaking up of the body, after death, he will go to a state of loss (apāyaṃ), a bad destiny (duggatiṃ), a downfall (vinipātaṃ), hell (nirayaṃ).

Thus, this sure teaching (apaṇṇako dhammo), when badly undertaken and adopted, only touches one side, and leaves aside the possibility of the wholesome (kusalaṃ ṭhānaṃ).
95.Nekkhamme ānisaṃsanti yo nesaṃ akusalato nikkhantabhāve ānisaṃso, yo ca vodānapakkho visuddhipakkho, taṃ na passantīti attho. Asaddhammasaññattīti abhūtadhammasaññāpanā . Attānukkaṃsetīti ṭhapetvā maṃ ko añño attano dassanaṃ pare gaṇhāpetuṃ sakkotīti attānaṃ ukkhipati. Paraṃ vambhetīti ettakesu janesu ekopi attano dassanaṃ pare gaṇhāpetuṃ na sakkotīti evaṃ paraṃ heṭṭhā khipati. Pubbeva kho panāti pubbe micchādassanaṃ gaṇhantasseva susīlyaṃ pahīnaṃ hoti, dussīlabhāvo paccupaṭṭhito. Evamassimeti evaṃ assa ime micchādiṭṭhiādayo satta. Aparāparaṃ uppajjanavasena pana teyeva micchādiṭṭhipaccayā aneke pāpakā akusalā dhammā uppajjanti nāma. Tatrāti tāsu tesaṃ samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ laddhīsu. Kaliggahoti parājayaggāho. 95. Nekkhamme ānisaṃsa means the benefit in their having gone forth from the unwholesome, and it means they do not see the purification side, the side of cleansing.

Asaddhammasaññattī means misperceptions of false Dharma.

Attānukkaṃsetī means that having set me aside, who else is able to make others accept their views? He exalts himself.

Paraṃ vambhetī means that among so many people, not even one is able to make others accept their views. Thus, he puts others down.

Pubbeva kho panā means that even before, as soon as he grasps wrong views, his good morality is lost, and immorality is present.

Evamassime means thus, these seven—wrong view, and so forth—belong to him. Furthermore, through the force of the arising of one thing after another, indeed, through the condition of wrong view, many evil, unwholesome dharmas arise.

Tatrā means among those doctrines of those ascetics and Brahmins.

Kaliggaho means seizing quarrels.

Dussamatto samādinno means badly grasped, poorly comprehended.

Ekaṃsaṃ pharitvā tiṭṭhatī means he dwells having thoroughly pervaded only one aspect, one part, his own doctrine, having fixated on it, thinking, "If there is no other world," thus, even in that case, he is the bringer of well-being.

Riñcatī means he avoids.

There, seizing quarrels, badly grasped, poorly comprehended, dwelling having thoroughly pervaded only one aspect, he avoids.

96. ‘‘Tatra , gahapatayo, ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘atthi dinnaṃ…pe… ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī’ti tesametaṃ pāṭikaṅkhaṃ? It is said, "Therein, householders, those ascetics and brāhmaṇas who speak thus, who hold such views, namely, ‘There is what is given…pe… those who, having directly experienced this world and the next with their own higher knowledge, make them known’—this is to be expected of them: that they, having completely avoided these three unwholesome things—bodily misconduct (kāyaduccarita), verbal misconduct (vacīduccarita), and mental misconduct (manoduccarita)—will undertake and maintain these three wholesome things (kusala dhamma)—bodily good conduct (kāyasucarita), verbal good conduct (vacīsucarita), and mental good conduct (manosucarita)."

What is the reason for this? Because those venerable ascetics and brāhmaṇas see drawbacks (ādīnava), degradation (okāra), and defilement (saṃkilesa) in unwholesome qualities (akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ), and they see the rewards (ānisaṃsaṃ) of renunciation (nekkhamme), resembling cleansing (vodānapakkhaṃ), in wholesome qualities (kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ).

Because there actually is the next world (paraṃ lokaṃ), the view (diṭṭhi) of one who thinks, ‘There is a next world (atthi paro loko)’ is his right view (sammādiṭṭhi).

Because there actually is the next world (paraṃ lokaṃ), when he is resolved that ‘There is a next world (atthi paro loko)’, that is his right resolve (sammāsaṅkappo).

Because there actually is the next world (paraṃ lokaṃ), when he speaks the statement, ‘There is a next world (atthi paro loko)’, that is his right speech (sammāvācā).

Because there actually is the next world (paraṃ lokaṃ), when he says that ‘There is a next world (atthi paro loko)’, he doesn’t make himself an opponent to those arahants who know the next world (paralokaviduno).

Because there actually is the next world (paraṃ lokaṃ), when he persuades another that ‘There is a next world (atthi paro loko)’, that is persuasion in what is true dharma (saddhammasaññatti).

And in that persuasion in what is true dharma (saddhammasaññattiyā), he doesn’t exalt himself (nevattānukkaṃseti) or disparage others (na paraṃ vambheti).

Whatever bad habituation (dussīlyaṃ) he previously had is abandoned (pahīnaṃ hoti), while good habituation (susīlyaṃ) is manifested (paccupaṭṭhitaṃ). And this right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right resolve (sammāsaṅkappo), right speech (sammāvācā), non-opposition to the ariyas, persuasion in what is true dharma (saddhammasaññatti), non-exaltation of self (anattukkaṃsanā), & non-disparagement of others (aparavambhanā):

These many skillful activities (kusalā dhammā) come into play (sambhavanti), in dependence on right view (sammādiṭṭhipaccayā).

“Therein, householders, an observant person considers thus: ‘If there actually is the next world (atthi paro loko), then this venerable person (bhavaṃ purisapuggalo)—on the breakup of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā)—will reappear in a good destination (sugatiṃ), a heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ)’.”

Even if we didn’t speak of the next world (paro loko), and there weren’t the true statement (saccaṃ vacanaṃ) of those venerable ascetics and brāhmaṇas (samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ), this venerable person (bhavaṃ purisapuggalo) is still praised in the here & now (diṭṭheva dhamme) by the observant (viññūnaṃ pāsaṃso) as a person of good habits (sīlavā purisapuggalo) & right view (sammādiṭṭhi): one who holds to a doctrine of existence (atthikavādo).’

If there really is a next world (paro loko), then this venerable person (bhoto purisapuggalassa) has made a good throw twice (ubhayattha kaṭaggaho): in that he is praised by the observant (viññūnaṃ pāsaṃso) here & now (diṭṭheva dhamme); and in that—with the breakup of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā)—he will reappear in a good destination (sugatiṃ), a heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ).

Thus this safe-bet teaching (apaṇṇako dhammo), when well grasped (susamatto) & adopted (samādinno) by him, covers both sides (ubhayaṃsaṃ pharitvā tiṭṭhati), and leaves behind the possibility of the unskillful (riñcati akusalaṃ ṭhānaṃ).

103. "There are absolutely no formless realms" (natthi sabbaso āruppā) means the formless realm of Brahmā (arūpabrahmaloko) does not exist in any way.

"Mind-made" (manomayā) means made of the mind of jhāna (jhānacittamayā).

"Conception-made" (saññāmayā) means conception-made by the conception of formless jhāna (arūpajjhānasaññāya saññāmayā).

"He practices for disenchantment toward forms (rūpānaṃyeva nibbidāya), for dispassion toward forms (virāgāya), and for the cessation of forms (nirodhāya paṭipanno hotī)" means he is either a gainer (ayaṃ lābhī) or a reasoner (hoti takkī vā).

"Gainer" (lābhī) means a gainer of the form realm jhāna (rūpāvacarajjhānalābhī).

For him, there is no doubt in the form realm (rūpāvacare kaṅkhā natthi), but there is in the formless realm (arūpāvacaraloke atthi).

He [thinks]: "I hear those who say, 'There are formless realms (ahaṃ āruppā atthīti vadantānampi),' and I hear those who say, 'There are no formless realms (natthīti vadantānampi),' but I do not know whether there are or are not (atthi natthīti pana na jānāmi)."

Having established the fourth jhāna (catutthajjhānaṃ padaṭṭhānaṃ katvā), I will generate the formless realm jhāna (arūpāvacarajjhānaṃ nibbattessāmi).

If there are formless realms (sace āruppā atthi), I will be reborn there (tattha nibbattissāmi); if there are not (sace natthi), I will be reborn in the form realm of Brahmā (rūpāvacarabrahmaloke nibbattissāmi).

Thus, for me, the undeteriorated Dharma (evaṃ me apaṇṇako dhammo) will be undeteriorated and uncorrupted (apaṇṇakova aviraddhova bhavissatī’'ti), thus he practices (tathā paṭipajjati).

But the reasoner (takkī pana) is one who has not attained jhāna (appaṭiladdhajjhāno); for him too (tassāpi), there is no doubt in the form realm jhāna (rūpajjhāne kaṅkhā natthi), but there is in the formless realm (arūpaloke pana atthi).

He [thinks]: "I hear those who say, 'There are formless realms (ahaṃ āruppā atthīti vadantānampi),' and I hear those who say, 'There are no formless realms (natthīti vadantānampi),' but I do not know whether there are or are not (atthi natthīti pana na jānāmi)."

Having performed the kasiṇa preparation (kasiṇaparikammaṃ katvā), having generated the fourth jhāna (catutthajjhānaṃ nibbattetvā), and having established that as a basis (taṃ padaṭṭhānaṃ katvā), I will generate the formless realm jhāna (arūpāvacarajjhānaṃ nibbattessāmi).

If there are formless realms (sace āruppā atthi), I will be reborn there (tattha nibbattissāmi).

If there are not (sace natthi), I will be reborn in the form realm of Brahmā (rūpāvacarabrahmaloke nibbattissāmi).

Thus, for me, the undeteriorated Dharma (evaṃ me apaṇṇako dhammo) will be undeteriorated and uncorrupted (apaṇṇakova aviraddhova bhavissatī’'ti), thus he practices (tathā paṭipajjati).

Ye kho te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘natthi sabbaso bhavanirodho’ti, tesamayaṃ diṭṭhi sārāgāya sarāgāya (syā. kaṃ.) santike, saṃyogāya santike, abhinandanāya santike, ajjhosānāya santike, upādānāya santike. Ye pana te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘atthi sabbaso bhavanirodho’ti, tesamayaṃ diṭṭhi asārāgāya santike, asaṃyogāya santike, anabhinandanāya santike, anajjhosānāya santike, anupādānāya santike’’’ti. So iti paṭisaṅkhāya bhavānaṃyeva nibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya paṭipanno hoti.
“There are some householders, contemplatives and brāhmaṇas who hold this doctrine, hold this view: ‘There is no total cessation of becoming ( bhava-nirodha).’

“Some contemplatives and brāhmaṇas, speaking in direct opposition to those contemplatives and brāhmaṇas, say this: ‘There is total cessation of becoming (bhava-nirodha).'

“What do you think, householders? Don’t these contemplatives and brāhmaṇas speak in direct opposition to each other?” “Yes, venerable sir.”

“With regard to this, an observant person considers thus: ‘As for those venerable contemplatives and brāhmaṇas who hold this doctrine, hold this view—“There is no total cessation of becoming (bhava-nirodha)”—I haven’t seen that. As for those venerable contemplatives and brāhmaṇas who hold this doctrine, hold this view—“There is total cessation of becoming (bhava-nirodha)”—I haven’t known that.

“If I, not knowing, not seeing, were to take one side and declare, “Only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless,” that would not be fitting for me.

“As for those venerable contemplatives and brāhmaṇas who hold this doctrine, hold this view—“There is no total cessation of becoming (bhava-nirodha)”: If their statement is true, there’s the safe-bet possibility that I might reappear among the perception-made devas of no form (arūpino saññāmayā).

“As for those venerable contemplatives and brāhmaṇas who hold this doctrine, hold this view—“There is total cessation of becoming (bhava-nirodha)”: If their statement is true, it is possible that I will be totally unbound (parinibbāyissāmi) in the here and now (diṭṭheva dhamme).

“As for those venerable contemplatives and brāhmaṇas who hold this doctrine, hold this view—“There is no total cessation of becoming (bhava-nirodha)”: This view of theirs borders on passion (sārāga), borders on fettering (saṃyoga), borders on relishing (abhinandana), borders on grasping (ajjhosāna), borders on clinging (upādāna).

“As for those venerable contemplatives and brāhmaṇas who hold this doctrine, hold this view—“There is total cessation of becoming (bhava-nirodha)”: This view of theirs borders on non-passion (asārāga), borders on non-fettering (asaṃyoga), borders on non-relishing (anabhinandana), borders on non-grasping (anajjhosāna), borders on non-clinging (anupādāna).’"

Reflecting thus, he practices for disenchantment toward becomings (bhavānaṃ), for dispassion (virāgāya) toward becomings (bhavānaṃ), and for the cessation (nirodhāya) of becomings (bhavānaṃ).
104.Bhavanirodhoti nibbānaṃ. Sārāgāya santiketi rāgavasena vaṭṭe rajjanassa santike. Saṃyogāyāti taṇhāvasena saṃyojanatthāya. Abhinandanāyāti taṇhādiṭṭhivasena abhinandanāya. Paṭipanno hotīti ayampi lābhī vā hoti takkī vā. Lābhī nāma aṭṭhasamāpattilābhī. Tassa āruppe kaṅkhā natthi, nibbāne atthi. So – ‘‘ahaṃ nirodho atthītipi natthītipi suṇāmi, sayaṃ na jānāmi. Samāpattiṃ pādakaṃ katvā vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhessāmi. Sace nirodho bhavissati, arahattaṃ patvā parinibbāyissāmi. No ce bhavissati, āruppe nibbattissāmī’’ti evaṃ paṭipajjati. Takkī pana ekasamāpattiyāpi na lābhī, āruppe panassa kaṅkhā natthi, bhavanirodhe atthi. So – ‘‘ahaṃ nirodho atthītipi natthītipi suṇāmi, sayaṃ na jānāmi, kasiṇaparikammaṃ katvā aṭṭhasamāpattiyo nibbattetvā samāpattipadaṭṭhānaṃ vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhessāmi. Sace nirodho bhavissati, arahattaṃ patvā parinibbāyissāmi. No ce bhavissati, āruppe nibbattissāmī’’ti evaṃ paṭipajjati. Etthāha – ‘‘atthi dinnantiādīni tāva apaṇṇakāni bhavantu, natthi dinnantiādīni pana kathaṃ apaṇṇakānī’’ti. Gahaṇavasena. Tāni hi apaṇṇakaṃ apaṇṇakanti evaṃ gahitattā apaṇṇakāni nāma jātāni.
104. Bhavanirodho means nirvāṇa (Nibbānaṃ).

Sārāgāya santike means near the rope that colors with passion (rāgavasena vaṭṭe rajjanassa santike).

Saṃyogāyā means for the purpose of connection through craving (taṇhāvasena saṃyojanatthāya).

Abhinandanāyā means for the delighting through craving and views (taṇhādiṭṭhivasena abhinandanāya).

Paṭipanno hotī means this one is either a gainer (lābhī) or a speculator (takkī) (ayampi lābhī vā hoti takkī vā).

Lābhī means one who has gained the eight attainments (aṭṭhasamāpattilābhī).

For him, there is doubt about the immaterial (āruppe), but there is about nirvāṇa (nibbāne atthi).

He [thinks]: "I hear that there is cessation (nirodha) and that there is not, but I myself do not know (ahaṃ nirodho atthītipi natthītipi suṇāmi, sayaṃ na jānāmi).

Having made samāpatti a foundation, I will develop insight (vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhessāmi).

If there is cessation (nirodho bhavissati), having attained arhatta, I will be completely unbound (parinibbāyissāmi).

If there is not, I will be reborn in the immaterial realm (āruppe nibbattissāmī)"’—thus he proceeds (evaṃ paṭipajjati).

But a speculator (takkī) is not a gainer (lābhī) even by a single samāpatti (ekasamāpattiyāpi na lābhī), but for him, there is no doubt about the immaterial (āruppe), there is about the cessation of existence (bhavanirodhe atthi).

He [thinks]: "I hear that there is cessation (nirodho) and that there is not, but I myself do not know (ahaṃ nirodho atthītipi natthītipi suṇāmi, sayaṃ na jānāmi), having performed the preliminary work for the kasina, having produced the eight attainments (aṭṭhasamāpattiyo nibbattetvā), I will develop insight (vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhessāmi) based on the attainment-path (samāpattipadaṭṭhānaṃ).

If there is cessation (nirodho bhavissati), having attained arhatta, I will be completely unbound (parinibbāyissāmi).

If there is not, I will be reborn in the immaterial realm (āruppe nibbattissāmī)"’—thus he proceeds (evaṃ paṭipajjati).

Here he says (etthāha) – "Let these, beginning with ‘there is what is given,’ be unerring (apaṇṇakāni bhavantu), but how are these, beginning with ‘there is not what is given,’ unerring (natthi dinnantiādīni pana kathaṃ apaṇṇakānī’’ti)?"

By way of grasping (gahaṇavasena).

Indeed, because those are grasped as ‘unerring, unerring (apaṇṇakaṃ apaṇṇakanti),’ therefore they are called unerring (apaṇṇakāni nāma jātāni).

Here, householders, a certain individual neither torments himself nor is he devoted to the practice of torturing himself, neither torments others nor is he devoted to the practice of torturing others; who—neither tormenting himself ( anattantapo ) nor tormenting others ( aparantapo )—dwells in the here & now free of hunger ( nicchāto ), unbound ( nibbuto ), cooled ( sītībhūto ), sensitive to happiness ( sukhappaṭisaṃvedī ), with a Brahmā-like mind ( brahmabhūtena attanā viharati).

105. "Four" -- this is a related connection.

Nihilism (natthikavāda), the doctrine of no cause (ahetukavāda), the doctrine of no action (akiriyavāda), there are no immaterial realms (āruppā natthi), there is no cessation (nirodho natthi)—those who speak thus and the two others, these five individuals are inferior; only three individuals are superior.

The five beginning with the doctrine of existence (atthikavāda) are one; only the fourth individual is superior.

To show this meaning, the Blessed One began this teaching (desanaṃ ārabhi).
