Thus I have heard…pe…the Anaṅgaṇasutta, spoken by Venerable Sāriputta. Here, the explanation of words whose meaning is not obvious is just as in all the suttas. Therefore, from now on, without saying even this much, we will only give explanations of words which have not occurred before.

"Four" is a delimitation of number. "Individuals" (puggalā) means beings (sattā), persons (narā), men (posā). But one should not take it that the Great Elder is a proponent of the doctrine of the individual because of this; for this Venerable One is the best of the Buddha's sons, and he teaches the Blessed Buddha's teaching without distorting it.

Four Individuals

A Discussion of the Teaching of Conventional and Ultimate Truths

The Blessed Buddha's teaching is of two kinds: conventional teaching (sammutidesanā) and ultimate teaching (paramatthadesanā). Therein, "individual," "being," "woman," "man," "warrior," "brahmin," "god," "Māra"—such are conventional teachings. "Impermanent," "suffering," "not-self," aggregates (khandhā), elements (dhātū), sense-spheres (āyatanāni), mindfulness (satipaṭṭhānā)—such are ultimate teachings.

Conventional Teaching, Ultimate Teaching

Therein, the Blessed One teaches the conventional teaching to those who, having heard a teaching in conventional terms, are capable of understanding the meaning, abandoning delusion, and attaining distinction. But he teaches the ultimate teaching to those who, having heard a teaching in ultimate terms, are capable of understanding the meaning, abandoning delusion, and attaining distinction. Here, there is this simile: just as a teacher who is skilled in the language of the country, an expounder of the meaning of the three Vedas, explains in the Tamil language to those who understand the meaning of what is said in the Tamil language, and to those who [understand] in some other language such as the Andhra language, [he explains] in that [particular] language; in this way, those young men, approaching a clever, intelligent teacher, quickly learn the skill. There, the Blessed Buddha is like the teacher, the three Piṭakas are like the three Vedas that are established as something to be spoken of, skill in conventional and ultimate [truths] is like skill in the language of the country, trainee beings who are capable of understanding the teaching of conventional and ultimate [truths] are like young men skilled in various languages of the country, and the Blessed One's teaching in terms of conventional and ultimate [truths] is to be understood like the teacher's instruction in the Tamil language etc. And here he said:

"The Sambuddha, the best of speakers, declared two truths: conventional [truth] and ultimate truth; a third is not found.

A statement of convention (saṅketavacanaṃ) is true, because of worldly convention (lokasammutikāraṇā); a statement of ultimate meaning (paramatthavacanaṃ) is true, because of the real nature (bhūtakāraṇā) of phenomena (dhammānaṃ).

Therefore, for the teacher, the lord of the world, who is skilled in expression, while expressing convention (sammutiṃ), no false speech arises."

Moreover, the Blessed One speaks about individuals for eight reasons: to illuminate shame and moral dread (hirottappadīpanatthaṃ), to illuminate the fact that beings own their actions (kammassakatādīpanatthaṃ), to illuminate individual human agency (paccattapurisakāradīpanatthaṃ), to illuminate immediate retribution (ānantariyadīpanatthaṃ), to illuminate the divine abodes (brahmavihāradīpanatthaṃ), to illuminate former dwellings (pubbenivāsadīpanatthaṃ), to illuminate purity of offerings (dakkhiṇāvisuddhidīpanatthaṃ), and to not abandon worldly convention (lokasammutiyā appahānatthaṃ).

For if it is said that "the aggregates, elements, and sense-spheres feel shame and moral dread," the great mass of people does not understand, falls into confusion, and becomes hostile, [saying], "What is this that is called 'the aggregates, elements, and sense-spheres feel shame and moral dread'?" But when it is said that "a woman feels shame and moral dread, a man, a warrior, a brahmin, a god, [or] Māra," then [the great mass of people] understands, does not fall into confusion, and does not become hostile. Therefore, the Blessed One speaks about individuals to illuminate shame and moral dread (hirottappadīpanatthaṃ).

To Illuminate Shame and Moral Dread

The same method [applies] even when it is said that "the aggregates own action, the elements, [and] the sense-spheres." Therefore, the Blessed One speaks about individuals to illuminate the fact that beings own their actions (kammassakatādīpanatthaṃ).

To Illuminate the Fact That Beings Own Their Actions

The same method [applies] even when it is said that "the great monasteries such as the Bamboo Grove Monastery were made by the aggregates, by the elements, [and] by the sense-spheres." Therefore, the Blessed One speaks about individuals to illuminate individual human agency (paccattapurisakāradīpanatthaṃ).

60. ‘“Blemish, blemish,” is said, friend, but what is this word ‘blemish’ a term for?’ ‘“Blemish,” friend, is a term for the spheres of evil unwholesome wishes.’

It is possible, friend, that a bhikkhu here might wish: ‘If I commit an offense, let the bhikkhus not know that I have committed an offense.’
And it is possible that the bhikkhus come to know that that bhikkhu has committed an offense.
So he is angry and bitter thus: ‘The bhikkhus know I have committed an offense.’
The anger and the bitterness are both a blemish.

63. 'A comparison occurred to me, friend Sāriputta.' The meaning is, "To me, friend Sāriputta, a comparison appears."
The intention is, "I wish to speak a comparison."

'May it occur to you.' The meaning is, "May it occur to you, may it appear, you should say."

'ekamidāha' means here, "idā" is merely a particle. It means, "At one time, he said." It is a word used in the sense of the ground.

'rājagahe viharāmi giribbaje' means, rājagaha is the name of that city.

giribbaja is said because it is entirely surrounded by mountains, and its nature is like a vajra.

I dwell in that city. It means, "Relying on that, I dwell."

'atha khvāha' means, "atha kho ahaṃ."

Here, 'athā' is a particle at the beginning of a statement about a different authority.

'kho' is merely a filler of the sentence.

'pubbaṇhasamaya' means "the time of the early portion of the day."

pubbaṇhasamaya means the time of the early portion of the day; early at sunrise; thus, is the meaning; in other words at the early time of sun-rise is pubbaṇhasamaya ; a moment before sun-rise ; thus, it is what has been told, in this way in the constant connection the word of use is obtained.

'nivāsetvā' means having put on, this should be understood based on the context of the changing of the monastery, dwelling.
