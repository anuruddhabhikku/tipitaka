286. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā lomasakaṅgiyo lomasakakaṅgiyo (ṭīkā) sakkesu viharati kapilavatthusmiṃ nigrodhārāme. Atha kho candano devaputto abhikkantāya rattiyā abhikkantavaṇṇo kevalakappaṃ nigrodhārāmaṃ obhāsetvā yenāyasmā lomasakaṅgiyo tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho candano devaputto āyasmantaṃ lomasakaṅgiyaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘dhāresi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañcā’’ti? ‘‘Na kho ahaṃ, āvuso, dhāremi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Tvaṃ panāvuso, dhāresi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañcā’’ti? ‘‘Ahampi kho, bhikkhu, na dhāremi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Dhāresi pana tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’’ti? ‘‘Na kho ahaṃ, āvuso, dhāremi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā. Tvaṃ panāvuso, dhāresi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’’ti? ‘‘Dhāremi kho ahaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’’ti. ‘‘Yathā kathaṃ pana tvaṃ, āvuso, dhāresi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’’ti? ‘‘Ekamidaṃ, bhikkhu, samayaṃ bhagavā devesu tāvatiṃsesu viharati pāricchattakamūle paṇḍukambalasilāyaṃ. Tatra bhagavā devānaṃ tāvatiṃsānaṃ bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsi – ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya, nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ; Yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ, appattañca anāgataṃ. ‘‘Paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ, tattha tattha vipassati; Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ, taṃ vidvā manubrūhaye. ‘‘Ajjeva kiccamātappaṃ, ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve; Na hi no saṅgaraṃ tena, mahāsenena maccunā. ‘‘Evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ, ahorattamatanditaṃ; Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate munī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhikkhu, dhāremi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā. Uggaṇhāhi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca; pariyāpuṇāhi tvaṃ , bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca; dhārehi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Atthasaṃhito, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddeso ca vibhaṅgo ca ādibrahmacariyako’’ti. Idamavoca candano devaputto. Idaṃ vatvā tatthevantaradhāyi.
Thus have I heard:

Once the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī) in the Jeta Grove, at Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery (Anāthapiṇḍadasya ārāme).

Now at that time, the venerable Lomasakaṅgiya was living in the Sakyan country at Kapilavatthu (Kapilavastu) in Nigrodha’s Park.

Then Candana (Candano), a young deva (devaputto) of beautiful appearance, illuminating the entire Nigrodha’s Park as the night was far advanced, approached the venerable Lomasakaṅgiya; having approached, he stood to one side.

Standing to one side, Candana the young deva said this to the venerable Lomasakaṅgiya: “Bhikkhu (monk), do you remember the summary and the exposition of the bhaddekaratta (one who has one fortunate attachment)?”

“No, friend (āvuso), I do not remember the summary and the exposition of the bhaddekaratta.”

“Then, friend, do you remember the summary and the exposition of the bhaddekaratta?”

“I, too, bhikkhu, do not remember the summary and the exposition of the bhaddekaratta.”

“Then, bhikkhu, do you remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?”

“No, friend, I do not remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.”

“Then, friend, do you remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?”

“Yes, bhikkhu, I do remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.”

“In what way, friend, do you remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?”

“Once, bhikkhu, the Blessed One was dwelling among the gods (devesu) in Tāvatiṃsa (Trāyastriṃśa), at the foot of the Pāricchattaka tree, on the Paṇḍukambala stone.

There, the Blessed One spoke this summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta to the Tāvatiṃsa gods:

“One should not hark back to the past, nor long for the future;

For the past is left behind, and the future is not yet attained.

But one should contemplate the present moment, discerning the dharma (law) in each and every instance;

Unswayed, unperturbed, knowing this, may one cultivate it.

Today, the task must be done with zeal; who knows if death will come tomorrow?

For there is no bargaining with Death, nor with his mighty host.

Thus, one who dwells ardently, untiringly day and night;

That one, indeed, the sage declares to be ‘one with a fortunate attachment.’”

“Thus, bhikkhu, I remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.

Learn, bhikkhu, the summary and the exposition of the bhaddekaratta; master, bhikkhu, the summary and the exposition of the bhaddekaratta; remember, bhikkhu, the summary and the exposition of the bhaddekaratta.

The summary and the exposition of the bhaddekaratta is beneficial, bhikkhu, it belongs to the fundamentals of the holy life.”

That is what was said by Candana the young deva.

Having said this, he vanished at once.
286.Evaṃme sutanti lomasakaṅgiyabhaddekarattasuttaṃ. Tattha lomasakaṅgiyoti aṅgathero kira nāmesa, kāyassa pana īsakalomasākāratāya lomasakaṅgiyoti pākaṭo jāto. Candanodevaputtoti kassapasammāsambuddhakāle kiresa candano nāma upāsako aḍḍho mahaddhano tīṇi ratanāni catūhi paccayehi pūjetvā devaloke nibbatto, purimanāmena candano devaputtotveva saṅkhaṃ gato. Paṇḍukambalasilāyanti rattakambalasilāyaṃ. Tassā kira rattakambalasseva jayasumanapuppharāsi viya vaṇṇo, tasmā ‘‘paṇḍukambalasilā’’ti vuccati. Kadā pana tattha bhagavā vihāsīti? Bodhipattito sattame saṃvacchare sāvatthiyaṃ āsāḷhīmāsapuṇṇamāya dvādasayojanāya parisāya majjhe yamakapāṭihāriyaṃ katvā oruyha kaṇḍambamūle paññattavarabuddhāsane nisīditvā dhammadesanāya mahājanaṃ mahāviduggato uddharitvā buddhā nāma yasmā pāṭihāriyaṃ katvā manussapathe na vasanti, tasmā passamānasseva tassa janassa padavīkkamaṃ katvā tāvatiṃsabhavane pāricchattakamūle paṇḍukambalasilāyaṃ vassaṃ upagato, tasmiṃ samaye vihāsi. Tatra bhagavāti tatra viharanto bhagavā yebhuyyena dasahi cakkavāḷasahassehi sannipatitāhi devatāhi parivuto mātaraṃ kāyasakkhiṃ katvā abhidhammapiṭakaṃ kathento gambhīraṃ nipuṇaṃ tilakkhaṇāhataṃ rūpārūpaparicchedakathaṃ paṭivijjhituṃ asakkontānaṃ devānaṃ saṃvegajananatthaṃ antarantarā bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsi. Tatrāyaṃ devaputto uggaṇhanto imā gāthā saddhiṃ vibhaṅgena uggaṇhi, devattassa pana pamādādhiṭṭhānattā dibbehi ārammaṇehi nippīḷiyamāno anupubbena suttaṃ sammuṭṭho gāthāmattameva dhāresi. Tenāha ‘‘evaṃ kho ahaṃ bhikkhu dhāremi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’’ti. Tatra bhagavā abhidhammapiṭakaṃ Uggaṇhāhitvantiādīsu tuṇhībhūto nisīditvā suṇanto uggaṇhāti nāma, vācāya sajjhāyaṃ karonto pariyāpuṇāti nāma, aññesaṃ vācento dhāreti nāma. Sesamettha uttānamevāti. Uggaṇhāhi tva Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Lomasakaṅgiyabhaddekarattasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
286. "Thus I have heard": this is from the Lomasakaṅgiyabhaddekarattasutta.

There, Lomasakaṅgiyo means: It seems that was the name of an elder (thero) from Aṅga. However, because his body was somewhat in the shape of hair (lomasa), he became known as Lomasakaṅgiya.

Candano devaputto means: It seems that in the time of the Buddha Kassapa, there was a lay follower (upāsako) named Candana, who was rich and wealthy. Having venerated the three jewels with the four requisites, he was reborn in the world of the gods. By his former name, he went by the designation Candana, a god.

Paṇḍukambalasilāya means: On a red cloth stone (rattakambalasilāyaṃ). It seems its color was like a heap of jayasumana flowers on a red cloth; therefore, it is called "Paṇḍukambalasilā."

When did the Blessed One dwell there? In the seventh year from his enlightenment, in Sāvatthi, on the full moon of Āsāḷhī month, having performed the twin miracle in the midst of a twelve yojana assembly, he descended and sat on the excellent Buddha-seat prepared at the foot of the Kaṇḍamba tree. Having rescued the great multitude from the great quagmire by means of a dharma talk, because Buddhas do not dwell on the path of humans after performing a miracle, while that crowd was still watching, having made an imperceptible stride, he went to the Tāvatiṃsa heaven, to the foot of the Pāricchatta tree, to the Paṇḍukambalasilā, and observed the rainy season retreat (vassa). At that time, he dwelt there.

"There, the Blessed One" means: There, dwelling, the Blessed One, mostly surrounded by deities who had assembled from ten thousand cakkavāḷas, having made his mother a witness by [showing her] his body, while teaching the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, in order to generate a sense of urgency in the deities who were unable to penetrate the profound, subtle, and three characteristics-stricken, analysis of form and formless phenomena, in between, he spoke the summary and the analysis of the bhaddekaratta.

There, this devaputta, while learning, learned these verses together with the analysis. However, due to being based on negligence because of his divine nature, being oppressed by divine objects, gradually he forgot the sutta and retained only the verses. Therefore, he said, "Thus, I, bhikkhu, remember the verses of the bhaddekarattiya."

"There, the Blessed One [taught] the Abhidhamma Piṭaka"

"Learn you" and so on, means: remaining silent, sitting and listening, he is said to learn (uggaṇhāti); reciting with speech, he is said to master (pariyāpuṇāti); causing others to recite, he is said to retain (dhāreti).

The rest is evident here.

"Learn you"

The Lomasakaṅgiyabhaddekarattasuttavaṇṇanā is finished.

287. Atha kho āyasmā lomasakaṅgiyo tassā rattiyā accayena senāsanaṃ saṃsāmetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena sāvatthi tena cārikaṃ pakkāmi. Anupubbena cārikaṃ caramāno yena sāvatthi jetavanaṃ anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāmo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā lomasakaṅgiyo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘Ekamidāhaṃ, bhante, samayaṃ sakkesu viharāmi kapilavatthusmiṃ nigrodhārāme. Atha kho, bhante, aññataro devaputto abhikkantāya rattiyā abhikkantavaṇṇo kevalakappaṃ nigrodhārāmaṃ obhāsetvā yenāhaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho, bhante, so devaputto maṃ etadavoca – ‘dhāresi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañcā’ti? Evaṃ vutte ahaṃ, bhante, taṃ devaputtaṃ etadavocaṃ – ‘na kho ahaṃ, āvuso, dhāremi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Tvaṃ panāvuso, dhāresi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañcā’ti? ‘Ahampi kho, bhikkhu, na dhāremi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Dhāresi pana tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’ti? ‘Na kho ahaṃ, āvuso, dhāremi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā. Tvaṃ panāvuso, dhāresi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’ti? ‘Dhāremi kho ahaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’ti. ‘Yathā kathaṃ pana tvaṃ, āvuso, dhāresi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’ti? Ekamidaṃ, bhikkhu, samayaṃ bhagavā devesu tāvatiṃsesu viharati pāricchattakamūle paṇḍukambalasilāyaṃ . Tatra kho bhagavā devānaṃ tāvatiṃsānaṃ bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca abhāsi – ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya…pe… Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate munī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhikkhu, dhāremi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā. Uggaṇhāhi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca; pariyāpuṇāhi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca; dhārehi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Atthasaṃhito, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddeso ca vibhaṅgo ca ādibrahmacariyako’ti. Idamavoca, bhante, so devaputto; idaṃ vatvā tatthevantaradhāyi. Sādhu me, bhante, bhagavā bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca desetū’’ti.
Thereupon, the venerable Lomasakaṅgiya, at the end of that night, having arranged his bedding, taking his bowl and robe, left on a walking tour for Sāvatthī.

Wandering by stages, he arrived at Sāvatthī, Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. He went to the Blessed One and, on arrival, having bowed down to him, sat to one side.

As he was sitting to one side, the venerable Lomasakaṅgiya said this to the Blessed One:

“Once, lord, I was staying in the Sakyan country at Kapilavatthu in Nigrodha’s Park.

Then, lord, a certain young deity, in the far extreme of the night, his extreme radiance lighting up the entirety of Nigrodha’s Park, came to me and, on arrival, stood to one side.

Standing to one side, lord, that young deity said this to me: ‘Do you, bhikkhu, remember the summary and the analysis of the bhaddekaratta (one who has one fortunate attachment)?’ When this was said, I said this to that young deity: ‘No, friend, I do not remember the summary and the analysis of the bhaddekaratta.

Then, friend, do you remember the summary and the analysis of the bhaddekaratta?’ ‘I also, bhikkhu, do not remember the summary and the analysis of the bhaddekaratta.’

Then, do you, bhikkhu, remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?’ ‘No, friend, I do not remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.

Then, friend, do you remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?’ ‘Yes, bhikkhu, I remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.’

‘How then, friend, do you remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?’ Once, bhikkhu, the Blessed One was staying among the gods of the Tāvatiṃsa heaven at the foot of the pāricchattaka tree on the paṇḍukambala stone slab.

There, the Blessed One recited the summary and the analysis of the bhaddekaratta to the Tāvatiṃsa gods –

“One should not pursue the past…pe…

That one is truly fortunate, so the peaceful ones declare.”

“In this way, bhikkhu, I remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.

Bhikkhu, learn the summary and exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’ Bhikkhu, master the summary and exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’ Bhikkhu, remember the summary and exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’

Bhikkhu, the summary and exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ is beneficial, it belongs to the fundamentals of the holy life.”

That is what the young deity said, lord; having said this, he vanished right there.

It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would teach me the summary and the analysis of the bhaddekaratta."

288. ‘‘Jānāsi pana tvaṃ, bhikkhu, taṃ devaputta’’nti? ‘‘Na kho ahaṃ, bhante, jānāmi taṃ devaputta’’nti. ‘‘Candano nāma so, bhikkhu, devaputto. Candano, bhikkhu, devaputto aṭṭhiṃ katvā aṭṭhikatvā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) manasikatvā sabbacetasā sabbaṃ cetaso (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.), sabbaṃ cetasā (ka.) samannāharitvā ohitasoto dhammaṃ suṇāti. Tena hi, bhikkhu, suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā lomasakaṅgiyo bhagavato paccassosi. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya, nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ; Yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ, appattañca anāgataṃ. ‘‘Paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ, tattha tattha vipassati; Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ, taṃ vidvā manubrūhaye. ‘‘Ajjeva kiccamātappaṃ, ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve; Na hi no saṅgaraṃ tena, mahāsenena maccunā; ‘‘Evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ, ahorattamatanditaṃ; Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate muni’’. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhu, atītaṃ anvāgameti…pe… evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, atītaṃ anvāgameti. Kathañca , bhikkhu, atītaṃ nānvāgameti…pe… evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, atītaṃ nānvāgameti. Kathañca, bhikkhu, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati…pe… evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati. Kathañca, bhikkhu, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati…pe… evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati. Kathañca, bhikkhu, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīrati…pe… evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīrati. Kathañca, bhikkhu, paccuppannesu dhammesu na saṃhīrati…pe… evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, paccuppannesu dhammesu na saṃhīrati. ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya, nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ; Yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ, appattañca anāgataṃ. ‘‘Paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ, tattha tattha vipassati; Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ, taṃ vidvā manubrūhaye. ‘‘Ajjeva kiccamātappaṃ, ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve; Na hi no saṅgaraṃ tena, mahāsenena maccunā. ‘‘Evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ, ahorattamatanditaṃ; Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate munī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā lomasakaṅgiyo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Lomasakaṅgiyabhaddekarattasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ catutthaṃ.
288. “Monk, do you know that young deity?” “No, venerable sir, I do not know that young deity.”

“Bhikkhu, that young deity is named Candana. Candana, the young deity, gives it attention, engages it with all his mind, hears the Dhamma as with eager ears. Therefore, monk, listen and attend closely to what I shall say.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Lomasakangiya replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Let not a person revive the past
Or on the future build his hopes;

For the past is left behind,
And the future is not yet attained.

But whoever sees insightfully the present phenomenon
In each and every case,

Unshaken, unperturbed,
May he cultivate it, that sage."

“Ardently doing what should be done today, for — who knows? — tomorrow death.

There is no bargaining with Mortality & his mighty horde.

One who dwells thus ardently, relentlessly,
Is truly called blessed to live alone for a night by the peaceful sage.”

“And how, monk, does one revive the past? …So, monk, one revives the past. And how, monk, does one not revive the past? … So, monk, one does not revive the past. And how, monk, does one build hopes on the future? … So, monk, one builds hopes on the future. And how, monk, does one not build hopes on the future? … So, monk, one does not build hopes on the future. And how, monk, is one drawn together in present phenomena? … So, monk, one is drawn together in present phenomena. And how, monk, is one not drawn together in present phenomena? … So, monk, one is not drawn together in present phenomena.”

“Let not a person revive the past
Or on the future build his hopes;

For the past is left behind,
And the future is not yet attained.

But whoever sees insightfully the present phenomenon
In each and every case,

Unshaken, unperturbed,
May he cultivate it, that sage."

“Ardently doing what should be done today, for — who knows? — tomorrow death.
No bargaining with Death (maccu) and his mighty host (mahāsena) is possible for us.

"Thus, the sage (muni), at peace, describes one who lives diligently (ātāpi), ceaselessly (atanditaṃ) day and night as truly fortunate (bhaddekaratta)."

The Blessed One (bhagavā) spoke thus.

The venerable Āyasmā Lomasakangiya, being pleased (attamano), rejoiced (abhinandi) in the Blessed One's (bhagavato) words (bhāsitaṃ).

The Lomasakangiyabhaddekarattasuttaṃ, the fourth, is completed (niṭṭhitaṃ).
