260. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tena kho pana samayena uggāhamāno paribbājako samaṇamuṇḍikāputto samaṇamaṇḍikāputto (sī. pī.) samayappavādake tindukācīre ekasālake mallikāya ārāme paṭivasati mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṃ pañcamattehi paribbājakasatehi. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati sāvatthiyā nikkhami divā divassa bhagavantaṃ dassanāya. Atha kho pañcakaṅgassa thapatissa etadahosi – ‘‘akālo kho tāva bhagavantaṃ dassanāya; paṭisallīno bhagavā. Manobhāvaniyānampi bhikkhūnaṃ asamayo dassanāya; paṭisallīnā manobhāvaniyā bhikkhū. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ yena samayappavādako tindukācīro ekasālako mallikāya ārāmo yena uggāhamāno paribbājako samaṇamuṇḍikāputto tenupasaṅkameyya’’nti. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati yena samayappavādako tindukācīro ekasālako mallikāya ārāmo yena uggāhamāno paribbājako samaṇamuṇḍikāputto tenupasaṅkami. Tena kho pana samayena uggāhamāno paribbājako samaṇamuṇḍikāputto mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṃ nisinno hoti unnādiniyā uccāsaddamahāsaddāya anekavihitaṃ tiracchānakathaṃ kathentiyā, seyyathidaṃ – rājakathaṃ corakathaṃ mahāmattakathaṃ senākathaṃ bhayakathaṃ yuddhakathaṃ annakathaṃ pānakathaṃ vatthakathaṃ sayanakathaṃ mālākathaṃ gandhakathaṃ ñātikathaṃ yānakathaṃ gāmakathaṃ nigamakathaṃ nagarakathaṃ janapadakathaṃ itthikathaṃ sūrakathaṃ visikhākathaṃ kumbhaṭṭhānakathaṃ pubbapetakathaṃ nānattakathaṃ lokakkhāyikaṃ samuddakkhāyikaṃ itibhavābhavakathaṃ iti vā. Addasā kho uggāhamāno paribbājako samaṇamuṇḍikāputto pañcakaṅgaṃ thapatiṃ dūratova āgacchantaṃ. Disvāna sakaṃ parisaṃ saṇṭhāpesi – ‘‘appasaddā bhonto hontu, mā bhonto saddamakattha; ayaṃ samaṇassa gotamassa sāvako āgacchati pañcakaṅgo thapati. Yāvatā kho pana samaṇassa gotamassa sāvakā gihī odātavasanā sāvatthiyaṃ paṭivasanti ayaṃ tesaṃ aññataro pañcakaṅgo thapati. Appasaddakāmā kho pana te āyasmanto appasaddavinītā appasaddassa vaṇṇavādino; appeva nāma appasaddaṃ parisaṃ viditvā upasaṅkamitabbaṃ maññeyyā’’ti. Atha kho te paribbājakā tuṇhī ahesuṃ.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park.

Now on that occasion the wanderer Uggāhamāna Samaṇamuṇḍikaputta was staying in Mallikā’s Park, the single-hall Tinduka plantation for philosophical debates, together with a large following of wanderers, with as many as five hundred wanderers. Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga went out from Sāvatthī at midday in order to see the Blessed One. Then Pañcakaṅga the carpenter thought: “It is not the right time to see the Blessed One; he is still in retreat. And it is not the right time to see bhikkhus worthy of esteem; they are still in retreat. Suppose I went to Mallikā’s Park, to the debating hall near the Tinduka tree to see the wanderer Uggāhamāna Samaṇamuṇḍikaputta?” And he went to Mallikā’s Park.

Now on that occasion the wanderer Uggāhamāna Samaṇamuṇḍikaputta was sitting together with a large following of wanderers, making a great noise and uproar, engaged in manifold frivolous talk, such as: talk about kings, robbers, and ministers of state; talk about armies, dangers, and wars; talk about food, drink, garments, and lodgings; talk about garlands and scents; talk about relations, vehicles, villages, towns, cities, and countries; talk about women and talk about heroes; street talk and talk by the well; talk about those departed in days gone by; rambling chit-chat; speculations about the world and about the sea; talk about gain and loss.

The wanderer Uggāhamāna Samaṇamuṇḍikaputta saw the carpenter Pañcakaṅga coming in the distance. Seeing him, he quieted his own assembly thus: “Sirs, be quiet; sirs make no noise. Here comes the carpenter Pañcakaṅga, a disciple of the recluse Gotama. As many as there are lay disciples of the recluse Gotama, dressed in white, staying at Sāvatthī, this carpenter Pañcakaṅga is one of them. These venerable ones like quiet; they are disciplined in quiet; they commend quiet. Perhaps if he finds our assembly a quiet one, he will think to join us.” Then the wanderers became silent.
260.Evaṃme sutanti samaṇamuṇḍikasuttaṃ. Tattha uggāhamānoti tassa paribbājakassa nāmaṃ. Sumanoti pakatināmaṃ. Kiñci kiñci pana uggahituṃ uggāhetuṃ samatthatāya uggāhamānoti naṃ sañjānanti. Samayaṃ pavadanti etthāti samayappavādakaṃ. Tasmiṃ kira ṭhāne caṅkītārukkhapokkharasātippabhutayo brāhmaṇā nigaṇṭhācelakaparibbājakādayo ca pabbajitā sannipatitvā attano attano samayaṃ pavadanti kathenti dīpenti, tasmā so ārāmo samayappavādakoti vuccati. Sveva tindukācīrasaṅkhātāya timbarūsakarukkhapantiyā parikkhittattā tindukācīraṃ. Yasmā panettha paṭhamaṃ ekā sālā ahosi, pacchā mahāpuññaṃ poṭṭhapādaparibbājakaṃ nissāya bahū sālā katā, tasmā tameva ekaṃ sālaṃ upādāya laddhanāmavasena ekasālakoti vuccati. Mallikāya pana pasenadirañño deviyā uyyānabhūto so pupphaphalasañchanno ārāmoti katvā mallikāya ārāmoti saṅkhaṃ gato. Tasmiṃ samayappavādake tindukācīre ekasālake mallikāya ārāme. Paṭivasatīti vāsaphāsutāya vasati. Divā divassāti divasassa divā nāma majjhanhātikkamo, tasmiṃ divasassapi divābhūte atikkantamatte majjhanhike nikkhamīti attho. Paṭisallīnoti tato tato rūpādigocarato cittaṃ paṭisaṃharitvā līno, jhānaratisevanavasena ekībhāvaṃ gato. Manobhāvanīyānanti manavaḍḍhanakānaṃ, ye āvajjato manasikaroto cittaṃ vinīvaraṇaṃ hoti unnamati vaḍḍhati. Yāvatāti yattakā. Ayaṃ tesaṃ aññataroti ayaṃ tesaṃ abbhantaro eko sāvako. Appeva nāmāti tassa upasaṅkamanaṃ patthayamāno āha. Patthanākāraṇaṃ pana sandakasutte vuttameva.
260. Thus I have heard, the samaṇamuṇḍika discourse.

There, uggāhamāno means the name of that wanderer (parivrājaka).

sumano means the natural name.

Because of the ability to grasp and cause to grasp a little, they recognize him as uggāhamāno.

They declare the samayaṃ there, therefore samayappavādakaṃ.

In that place, brāhmaṇas, nigaṇṭhācelaka parivrājakas, and other renunciates, such as caṅkītārukkhapokkharasātippabhuta, having gathered, declare, speak, and illuminate their own doctrines (samaya). Therefore, that ārāma is called samayappavādako.

Because it is surrounded by a row of timbarūsakarukkhapanti, known as tindukācīra, it is called tindukācīraṃ.

Because here previously there was one hall (sālā), later many halls were made relying on the greatly meritorious poṭṭhapāda parivrājaka, therefore, taking up that same one hall, according to the name received, it is called ekasālako.

Mallikā's, the pasenadirañño devī's, garden-like, that flower and fruit covered park, having made it an ārāma, it went to the designation mallikāya ārāmo.

In that samayappavādaka tindukācīra ekasālaka mallikāya ārāme.

paṭivasatī means dwells in comfort.

divā divassā means by day of the day, namely after midday, at that time when the day has become day, when the midday has passed, he goes out, is the meaning.

paṭisallīno means having withdrawn and secluded the mind (citta) from this and that object of sense, such as form (rūpa), having gone to oneness through cultivating delight in meditation (jhāna).

manobhāvanīyāna means of those that increase the mind, which, when reflecting and attending, the mind becomes without hindrances, rises up, grows.

yāvatā means as much as.

ayaṃ tesaṃ aññataro means this one is another, an inner disciple of those.

appeva nāmā means wishing to approach him, he says.

But the cause of wishing has already been spoken in the sandakasutta.

261. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati yena uggāhamāno paribbājako samaṇamuṇḍikāputto tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā uggāhamānena paribbājakena samaṇamuṇḍikāputtena saddhiṃ sammodi . Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho pañcakaṅgaṃ thapatiṃ uggāhamāno paribbājako samaṇamuṇḍikāputto etadavoca – ‘‘catūhi kho ahaṃ, gahapati, dhammehi samannāgataṃ purisapuggalaṃ paññapemi sampannakusalaṃ paramakusalaṃ uttamapattipattaṃ samaṇaṃ ayojjhaṃ. Katamehi catūhi? Idha, gahapati, na kāyena pāpakammaṃ karoti, na pāpakaṃ vācaṃ bhāsati, na pāpakaṃ saṅkappaṃ saṅkappeti, na pāpakaṃ ājīvaṃ ājīvati – imehi kho ahaṃ, gahapati, catūhi dhammehi samannāgataṃ purisapuggalaṃ paññapemi sampannakusalaṃ paramakusalaṃ uttamapattipattaṃ samaṇaṃ ayojjha’’nti. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati uggāhamānassa paribbājakassa samaṇamuṇḍikāputtassa bhāsitaṃ neva abhinandi nappaṭikkosi. Anabhinanditvā appaṭikkositvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkāmi – ‘‘bhagavato santike etassa bhāsitassa atthaṃ ājānissāmī’’ti. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho pañcakaṅgo thapati yāvatako ahosi uggāhamānena paribbājakena samaṇamuṇḍikāputtena saddhiṃ kathāsallāpo taṃ sabbaṃ bhagavato ārocesi.
Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga went to the wanderer Uggāhamāna, the Samaṇamuṇḍikāputta, and exchanged greetings with Uggāhamāna, the wanderer, the Samaṇamuṇḍikāputta. When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

The wanderer Uggāhamāna, the Samaṇamuṇḍikāputta, then said to the carpenter Pañcakaṅga who was sitting to one side: "Householder, when a man possesses four qualities, I describe him as accomplished in what is wholesome (sampannakusalaṃ), perfected in what is wholesome (paramakusalaṃ), attained to the supreme attainment (uttamapattipattaṃ), an invincible ascetic (samaṇaṃ ayojjhaṃ). Which four? Here, householder, he does no evil bodily actions, he utters no evil speech, he has no evil intentions, and he does not make his living by any evil livelihood. When a man possesses these four qualities, I describe him as accomplished in what is wholesome, perfected in what is wholesome, attained to the supreme attainment, an invincible ascetic."

Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga neither approved nor disapproved of the words of the wanderer Uggāhamāna, the Samaṇamuṇḍikāputta. Without approving or disapproving, he rose from his seat and went away, thinking: “I shall learn the meaning of this statement in the presence of the Blessed One (bhagavato)."

Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga went to the Blessed One; and after paying homage to the Blessed One, he sat down at one side. As he sat there, the carpenter Pañcakaṅga reported to the Blessed One his entire conversation with the wanderer Uggāhamāna, the Samaṇamuṇḍikāputta.

Sampannakusalanti paripuṇṇakusalaṃ. Paramakusalanti uttamakusalaṃ. Ayojjhanti vādayuddhena yujjhitvā cāletuṃ asakkuṇeyyaṃ acalaṃ nikkampaṃ thiraṃ. Na karotīti akaraṇamattameva vadati, ettha pana saṃvarappahānaṃ vā paṭisevanappahānaṃ vā na vadati. Sesapadesupi eseva nayo. Neva abhinandīti titthiyā nāma jānitvāpi ajānitvāpi yaṃ vā taṃ vā vadantīti maññamāno nābhinandi. Na paṭikkosīti sāsanassa anulomaṃ viya pasannākāraṃ viya vadatīti maññamāno na paṭisedheti. 261. etadavocā means: "the wise householder, thinking, 'having gathered him with a dharma-talk, I will make him my disciple,' spoke this saying beginning with, 'by means of these four.'"
paññapemī means: "I will show," "I will establish."
sampannakusala means: complete merit.
paramakusala means: supreme merit, or uttamakusala.
ayojjha means: "having striven in verbal battle, [it is] impossible to move, unshakeable, unmoving, firm."
na karotī means: "he does not do" merely abstaining from action; here he does not speak of restraint-abandoning or recourse-abandoning.
The same method [applies] in the remaining phrases.

neva abhinandī means: thinking, "the tīrthikas speak thus, whether knowing or not knowing," he did not rejoice.
na paṭikkosī means: thinking, "he speaks as if in accordance with the teaching, as if with a pleased manner," he does not reject.

Evaṃ vutte, bhagavā pañcakaṅgaṃ thapatiṃ etadavoca – ‘‘evaṃ sante kho, thapati, daharo kumāro mando uttānaseyyako sampannakusalo bhavissati paramakusalo uttamapattipatto samaṇo ayojjho, yathā uggāhamānassa paribbājakassa samaṇamuṇḍikāputtassa vacanaṃ. Daharassa hi, thapati, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa kāyotipi na hoti, kuto pana kāyena pāpakammaṃ karissati, aññatra phanditamattā! Daharassa hi, thapati, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa vācātipi na hoti, kuto pana pāpakaṃ vācaṃ bhāsissati, aññatra roditamattā ! Daharassa hi, thapati, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa saṅkappotipi na hoti, kuto pana pāpakaṃ saṅkappaṃ saṅkappissati, aññatra vikūjitamattā vikujjitamattā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)! Daharassa hi, thapati, kumārassa mandassa uttānaseyyakassa ājīvotipi na hoti, kuto pana pāpakaṃ ājīvaṃ ājīvissati, aññatra mātuthaññā! Evaṃ sante kho, thapati, daharo kumāro mando uttānaseyyako sampannakusalo bhavissati paramakusalo uttamapattipatto samaṇo ayojjho, yathā uggāhamānassa paribbājakassa samaṇamuṇḍikāputtassa vacanaṃ.

Thus spoken, the Blessed One said the following to the carpenter Pañcakaṅga: "If that were so, carpenter, then a young, tender infant lying prone would be accomplished in what is wholesome, perfected in what is wholesome, attained to the supreme attainment, an invincible śramaṇa (ascetic), according to the statement of the wanderer Uggāhamāna, the son of a śramaṇa (ascetic) Muṇḍikā.

For a young, tender infant lying prone does not even have the notion 'body' (kāya), so how should he do an evil action (pāpakarma) with the body (kāya) beyond mere wriggling? For a young, tender infant lying prone does not even have speech, so how should he utter evil speech (pāpakaṃ vācaṃ) beyond mere crying? For a young, tender infant lying prone does not even have intention (saṅkappa), so how should he have an evil intention (pāpakaṃ saṅkappaṃ) beyond mere cooing? For a young, tender infant lying prone does not even have a livelihood (ājīva), so how should he make a living with an evil livelihood (pāpakaṃ ājīvaṃ) beyond his mother's milk? If that were so, carpenter, then a young, tender infant lying prone would be accomplished in what is wholesome, perfected in what is wholesome, attained to the supreme attainment, an invincible śramaṇa (ascetic), according to the statement of the wanderer Uggāhamāna, the son of a śramaṇa (ascetic) Muṇḍikā."
262.Yathā uggāhamānassāti yathā tassa vacanaṃ, evaṃ sante uttānaseyyako kumāro ayojjhasamaṇo thirasamaṇo bhavissati, mayaṃ pana evaṃ na vadāmāti dīpeti. Kāyotipi na hotīti sakakāyo parakāyotipi visesañāṇaṃ na hoti. Aññatraphanditamattāti paccattharaṇe valisamphassena vā maṅguladaṭṭhena vā kāyaphandanamattaṃ nāma hoti. Taṃ ṭhapetvā aññaṃ kāyena karaṇakammaṃ nāma natthi. Tampi ca kilesasahagatacitteneva hoti. Vācātipi na hotīti micchāvācā sammāvācātipi nānattaṃ na hoti. Roditamattāti jighacchāpipāsāparetassa pana roditamattaṃ hoti. Tampi kilesasahagatacitteneva. Saṅkappoti micchāsaṅkappo sammāsaṅkappotipi nānattaṃ na hoti. Vikūjitamattāti vikūjitamattaṃ rodanahasitamattaṃ hoti. Daharakumārakānañhi cittaṃ atītārammaṇaṃ pavattati, nirayato āgatā nirayadukkhaṃ saritvā rodanti, devalokato āgatā hasanti, tampi kilesasahagatacitteneva hoti. Ājīvoti micchājīvo sammājīvotipi nānattaṃ na hoti. Aññatra mātuthaññāti thaññacoradārakā nāma honti, mātari khīraṃ pāyantiyā apivitvā aññavihitakāle piṭṭhipassena āgantvā thaññaṃ pivanti. Ettakaṃ muñcitvā añño micchājīvo natthi. Ayampi kilesasahagatacitteneva hotīti dasseti.
262. "Just as [it is] for one who is learning," that is, just as his word [is]; thus, while he exists, the infant lying on his back will become non-contentious and stable; this is what he is explaining when he says, "But we do not speak thus."
"There is no body either," that is, there is no specific knowledge of one's own body or another's body.
"Except for a trembling," that is, at the time of spreading out a covering, either by the touch of a wrinkle or by the bite of a maṅgula, there is merely a bodily trembling.
Having set that aside, there is no other act of doing with the body.
Moreover, that too occurs only with a mind accompanied by defilements (kleśa).
"There is no speech either," that is, there is no difference between false speech and true speech.
"There is only crying," that is, for one overcome by hunger and thirst, there is only crying.
Moreover, that too [occurs] only with a mind accompanied by defilements.
"Saṅkappa," that is, there is no difference between wrong intention (micchāsaṅkappa) and right intention (sammāsaṅkappa).
"There is only humming," that is, there is only humming, only crying and laughing.
For the mind of young children tends towards past objects; those coming from hell cry, remembering the suffering of hell; those coming from the world of the gods laugh; moreover, that too occurs only with a mind accompanied by defilements.
"Ājīva," that is, there is no difference between wrong livelihood (micchājīva) and right livelihood (sammājīva).
"Except for the mother's breast," that is, they are called breast-thieving children; while the mother is giving milk, without drinking, they come to the side of her back at another prescribed time and drink milk.
Having abandoned so much, there is no other wrong livelihood.
He shows that even this occurs only with a mind accompanied by defilements.

267. “And what, householder, are wholesome intentions ( kusalā saṅkappā )? They are the intention of renunciation ( nekkhammasaṅkappo ), the intention of non-ill will ( abyāpādasaṅkappo ), and the intention of non-cruelty (avihiṃsāsaṅkappo) . These, householder, are called wholesome intentions.

“And what is the origin of these wholesome intentions, householder? Their origin, too, has been stated.
‘They should be said to originate from perception ( saññāsamuṭṭhānā’tissa vacanīyaṃ ).’
What is perception ( saññā )? Perception is varied, of many kinds, and diverse.
There is perception of renunciation ( nekkhammasaññā ), perception of non-ill will ( abyāpādasaññā ), and perception of non-cruelty ( avihiṃsāsaññā ). Wholesome intentions originate from this.

“And where do these wholesome intentions cease without remainder, householder? Their cessation, too, has been stated.
Here, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought ( vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā )…a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the second jhāna ( dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati ); it is here that these wholesome intentions cease without remainder.

“And how, householder, does one practice the way to the cessation of wholesome intentions? Here, a bhikkhu awakens zeal (chandaṃ janeti) for the non-arising of unarisen evil unwholesome states (anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya), and he makes effort ( vāyamati ), arouses energy ( vīriyaṃ ārabhati ), exerts his mind ( cittaṃ paggaṇhāti ), and strives ( padahati ); for the abandoning of arisen evil unwholesome states (uppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya)…for the arising of unarisen wholesome states (anuppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ uppādāya)…for the continuance, non-disappearance, strengthening, increase, and fulfilment by development of arisen wholesome states (uppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ṭhitiyā asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā), he awakens zeal, makes effort, arouses energy, exerts his mind, and strives.
One so practising, householder, practises the way to the cessation of wholesome intentions.
267.Ettheteti arahattaphale bhummaṃ. Dutiyajjhānikaṃ arahattaphalañhi pāpuṇitvā kusalasaṅkappā aparisesā nirujjhanti. Nirodhāya paṭipannoti ettha yāva arahattamaggā nirodhāya paṭipanno nāma hoti, phalapatte pana te nirodhitā nāma honti. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Samaṇamuṇḍikasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
"Here, there," means grounded in the fruit of Arahatship (arhattaphala).
Indeed, having attained the fruit of Arahatship in the second jhana, wholesome thoughts are completely stopped without remainder.
"Entered upon cessation" means here, up to the path of Arahatship, he is called "entered upon cessation"; but when the fruit is attained, they are called "those who have caused cessation."
All the rest is clear.

The Samaṇamuṇḍikasutta Commentary in the Papañcasūdanī, the Commentary to the Middle Length Discourses, is finished.

Idha, thapati, bhikkhu asekhāya sammādiṭṭhiyā samannāgato hoti, asekhena sammāsaṅkappena samannāgato hoti, asekhāya sammāvācāya samannāgato hoti, asekhena sammākammantena samannāgato hoti, asekhena sammāājīvena samannāgato hoti, asekhena sammāvāyāmena samannāgato hoti, asekhāya sammāsatiyā samannāgato hoti, asekhena sammāsamādhinā samannāgato hoti, asekhena sammāñāṇena samannāgato hoti, asekhāya sammāvimuttiyā samannāgato hoti – imehi kho ahaṃ, thapati, dasahi dhammehi samannāgataṃ purisapuggalaṃ paññapemi sampannakusalaṃ paramakusalaṃ uttamapattipattaṃ samaṇaṃ ayojjha’’nti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano pañcakaṅgo thapati bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Samaṇamuṇḍikasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.
268. “And furthermore, carpenter, by possessing which ten qualities do I describe an individual as accomplished in the good, supremely good, as having attained the highest attainment, an invincible samaṇa? Here, carpenter, a bhikkhu possesses the right view of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyagdṛṣṭi), the right intention of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyaksaṃkalpa), the right speech of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyagvāc), the right action of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyakkarmānta), the right livelihood of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyagājīva), the right effort of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyagvyāyāma), the right mindfulness of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyaksmṛti), the right concentration of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyaksamādhi), the right knowledge of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyagjñāna), and the right deliverance of one beyond training (aśaikṣa-samyagvimukti). It is by possessing these ten qualities that I describe an individual as accomplished in the good, supremely good, as having attained the highest attainment, an invincible samaṇa.”

Thus spoke the Blessed One. The carpenter Pañcakaṅga was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Samaṇamuṇḍika Sutta, the eighth, is finished.
