406. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tena kho pana samayena poṭṭhapādo paribbājako samayappavādake tindukācīre ekasālake mallikāya ārāme paṭivasati mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṃ tiṃsamattehi paribbājakasatehi. Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion, the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Sāvatthī in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park (jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

Now on that occasion, Poṭṭhapāda the wanderer (poṭṭhapādo paribbājako) was staying in the debating hall (samayappavādake) near the Tiṇḍuka tree (tindukācīre) in the single-pavilion park (ekasālake) of Queen Mallikā (mallikāya ārāme), together with a large following (mahatiyā) of about three hundred wanderers (tiṃsamattehi paribbājakasatehi).

Then, early in the morning (pubbaṇhasamayaṃ), the Blessed One (bhagavā), having dressed and taken his bowl and robe (pattacīvaramādāya), went into Sāvatthī for alms (sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi).
406.Evaṃme suttaṃ…pe… sāvatthiyanti poṭṭhapādasuttaṃ. Tatrāyaṃ apubbapadavaṇṇanā. Sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāmeti sāvatthiṃ upanissāya yo jetassa kumārassa vane anāthapiṇḍikena gahapatinā ārāmo kārito, tattha viharati. Poṭṭhapādo paribbājakoti nāmena poṭṭhapādo nāma channaparibbājako. So kira gihikāle brāhmaṇamahāsālo kāmesuādīnavaṃ disvā cattālīsakoṭiparimāṇaṃ bhogakkhandhaṃ pahāya pabbajitvā titthiyānaṃ gaṇācariyo jāto. Samayaṃ pavadanti etthāti samayappavādako, tasmiṃ kira ṭhāne caṅkītārukkhapokkharasātippabhutayo brāhmaṇā nigaṇṭhaacelakaparibbājakādayo ca pabbajitā sannipatitvā attano attano samayaṃ vadanti kathenti dīpenti, tasmā so ārāmo samayappavādakoti vuccati. Sveva ca tindukācīrasaṅkhātāya timbarūrukkhapantiyā parikkhittattā tindukācīro. Yasmā panettha paṭhamaṃ ekāva sālā ahosi, pacchā mahāpuññaṃ paribbājakaṃ nissāya bahū sālā katā. Tasmā tameva ekaṃ sālaṃ upādāya laddhanāmavasena ekasālakoti vuccati. Mallikāya pana pasenadirañño deviyā uyyānabhūto so pupphaphalasampanno ārāmoti katvā mallikāya ārāmoti saṅkhyaṃ gato. Tasmiṃ samayappavādake tindukācīre ekasālake mallikāya ārāme. Paṭivasatīti nivāsaphāsutāya vasati. Athekadivasaṃ bhagavā paccūsasamaye sabbaññutaññāṇaṃ pattharitvā lokaṃ pariggaṇhanto ñāṇajālassa antogataṃ paribbājakaṃ disvā – ‘‘ayaṃ poṭṭhapādo mayhaṃ ñāṇajāle paññāyati, kinnu kho bhavissatī’’ti upaparikkhanto addasa – ‘‘ahaṃ ajja tattha gamissāmi, atha maṃ poṭṭhapādo nirodhañca nirodhavuṭṭhānañca pucchissati, tassāhaṃ sabbabuddhānaṃ ñāṇena saṃsanditvā tadubhayaṃ kathessāmi, atha so katipāhaccayena cittaṃ hatthisāriputtaṃ gahetvā mama santikaṃ āgamissati, tesamahaṃ dhammaṃ desessāmi, desanāvasāne poṭṭhapādo maṃ saraṇaṃ gamissati, citto hatthisāriputto mama santike pabbajitvā arahattaṃ pāpuṇissatī’’ti. Tato pātova sarīrapaṭijagganaṃ katvā surattadupaṭṭaṃ nivāsetvā vijjulatāsadisaṃ kāyabandhanaṃ bandhitvā yugandharapabbataṃ parikkhipitvā ṭhitamahāmeghaṃ viya meghavaṇṇaṃ paṃsukūlaṃ ekaṃsavaragataṃ katvā paccagghaṃ selamayapattaṃ vāmaaṃsakūṭe laggetvā sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pavisissāmīti sīho viya himavantapādā vihārā nikkhami. Imamatthaṃ sandhāya – ‘‘atha kho bhagavā’’tiādi vuttaṃ.
406. Thus have I heard. ...at Sāvattha, this is the Poṭṭhapāda Sutta.

Herein is an explanation of uncommon words.

He dwells at Sāvatthi, in the Jeta Grove, in Anāthapiṇḍika's Park: that is, relying on Sāvatthi, he dwells in the park that Anāthapiṇḍika the householder had built in the Jeta Prince's Grove.

"Poṭṭhapāda the wanderer" means Poṭṭhapāda, by name, was a hidden wanderer.

It is said that in his householder days, he was a wealthy Brāhman, who, seeing the danger in sensual pleasures, renounced a fortune of forty koṭis, went forth, and became a teacher of a group of sectarians.

"They proclaim a doctrine here," therefore it is a place where doctrines are proclaimed. In that place, it is said, Brahmans such as Caṅkī, Tārukkha, Pokkharasāti, and others, as well as ascetics, naked ascetics, wanderers, and others who have gone forth, having assembled, proclaim, relate, and expound their own doctrines; therefore, that park is called a place where doctrines are proclaimed.

Moreover, because it is surrounded by a row of tinduka trees called tindukācīra, it is called tindukācīra.

Because at first there was only one hall here, but later many halls were built relying on a very meritorious wanderer.

Therefore, because of taking up that one hall according to the name received, it is called "single-hall".

Moreover, because it was a pleasure garden of Queen Mallikā of King Pasenadi, and because it was a park full of flowers and fruits, it was called "Mallikā's Park," and it went to the designation of Mallikā's Park.

In that place where doctrines are proclaimed, in Tindukācīra, in the single-hall, in Mallikā's Park.

"He dwells" means he lives in a dwelling that is comfortable for dwelling.

Then, one day, the Blessed One, in the early morning, having spread forth his all-knowing knowledge, surveying the world, seeing the wanderer within the net of knowledge – "This Poṭṭhapāda appears in my net of knowledge; what will happen?" – examining, he saw – "Today I will go there, and then Poṭṭhapāda will ask me about cessation and the rising from cessation, and then I, having conferred with the knowledge of all Buddhas, will explain both of those things. Then, after a few days, Citta Hatthīsariputta, having taken his mind, will come to my presence, and to them I will teach the Dharma, and at the end of the teaching, Poṭṭhapāda will go to me for refuge, and Citta Hatthīsariputta, having gone forth in my presence, will attain arahantship."

Then, in the morning, having attended to his bodily needs, having put on a fine red double-cloth, having tied on a body-band resembling a lightning flash, having circled Mount Yugandhara, making the pāṃsukūla robe of cloud color one-shouldered like a great cloud standing, having hung the invaluable bowl made of stone on the peak of his left shoulder, thinking, "I will enter Sāvatthi for alms," he went forth from the monastery like a lion from the foothills of the Himalayas.

Referring to this meaning, it was said, "Then, indeed, the Blessed One..." and so on.

407. Atha kho bhagavato etadahosi – ‘‘atippago kho tāva sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya carituṃ. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ yena samayappavādako tindukācīro ekasālako mallikāya ārāmo, yena poṭṭhapādo paribbājako tenupasaṅkameyya’’nti. Atha kho bhagavā yena samayappavādako tindukācīro ekasālako mallikāya ārāmo tenupasaṅkami.
Then the thought occurred to the Blessed One: "It is still too early to go into Sāvatthī for alms. Suppose I were to visit Poṭṭhapāda the wanderer at the debating hall near the Tiṇḍuka tree in the single-pavilion park of Queen Mallikā?"

Then the Blessed One went to the debating hall near the Tiṇḍuka tree in the single-pavilion park of Queen Mallikā.
407.Etadahosīti nagaradvārasamīpaṃ gantvā attano rucivasena sūriyaṃ oloketvā atippagabhāvameva disvā etaṃ ahosi. Yaṃnūnāhanti saṃsayaparidīpano viya nipāto, buddhānañca saṃsayo nāma natthi – ‘‘idaṃ karissāma, idaṃ na karissāma, imassa dhammaṃ desessāma, imassa na desessāmā’’ti evaṃ parivitakkapubbabhāgo panesa sabbabuddhānaṃ labbhati. Tenāha – ‘‘yaṃnūnāha’’nti, yadi panāhanti attho.
407.

Thus it occurred to him: "Having gone near the city gate, and having looked at the sun according to his own liking, and having seen an exceedingly bright state..."

Since it is like a word that dispels doubt, but for the Buddhas there is no such thing as doubt - "We will do this, we will not do this, we will teach the Dharma to this person, we will not teach it to this person" - thus, this kind of prior thought is obtained by all Buddhas.

Therefore he said, "Because...", if that is the meaning.

408. Tena kho pana samayena poṭṭhapādo paribbājako mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṃ nisinno hoti unnādiniyā uccāsaddamahāsaddāya anekavihitaṃ tiracchānakathaṃ kathentiyā. Seyyathidaṃ – rājakathaṃ corakathaṃ mahāmattakathaṃ senākathaṃ bhayakathaṃ yuddhakathaṃ annakathaṃ pānakathaṃ vatthakathaṃ sayanakathaṃ mālākathaṃ gandhakathaṃ ñātikathaṃ yānakathaṃ gāmakathaṃ nigamakathaṃ nagarakathaṃ janapadakathaṃ itthikathaṃ sūrakathaṃ visikhākathaṃ kumbhaṭṭhānakathaṃ pubbapetakathaṃ nānattakathaṃ lokakkhāyikaṃ samuddakkhāyikaṃ itibhavābhavakathaṃ iti vā.
Now at that time, the wanderer Poṭṭhapāda was sitting with a large assembly of wanderers, who were making a great noise and commotion, engaged in various kinds of trivial talk. Specifically, they were talking about kings (rājakathā), robbers (corakathā), ministers of state (mahāmattakathā), armies (senākathā), dangers (bhayakathā), wars (yuddhakathā), food (annakathā), drink (pānakathā), clothing (vatthakatha), beds (sayanakatha), garlands (mālākathā), perfumes (gandhakathā), relatives (ñātikathā), vehicles (yānakathā), villages (gāmakathā), towns (nigamakathā), cities (nagarakathā), countries (janapadakatha), women (itthikathā), heroes (sūrakathā), street gossip (visikhākatha), well gossip (kumbhaṭṭhānakatha), departed ancestors (pubbapetakatha), various trivialities (nānattakatha), world-stories (lokakkhāyika), sea-stories (samuddakkhāyika), and talk about being and non-being (itibhavābhavakatha)—and so on.
408.Unnādiniyāti uccaṃ nadamānāya, evaṃ nadamānāya cassā uddhaṃ gamanavasena ucco, disāsu patthaṭavasena mahā saddoti uccāsaddamahāsaddāya, tesañhi paribbājakānaṃ pātova vuṭṭhāya kattabbaṃ nāma cetiyavattaṃ vā bodhivattaṃ vā ācariyupajjhāyavattaṃ vā yoniso manasikāro vā natthi. Tena te pātova vuṭṭhāya bālātape nisinnā – ‘‘imassa hattho sobhano, imassa pādo’’ti evaṃ aññamaññassa hatthapādādīni vā ārabbha, itthipurisadārakadārikādīnaṃ vaṇṇe vā, aññaṃ vā kāmassādabhavassādādivatthuṃ ārabbha kathaṃ samuṭṭhāpetvā anupubbena rājakathādianekavidhaṃ tiracchānakathaṃ kathenti. Tena vuttaṃ – ‘‘unnādiniyā uccāsaddamahāsaddāya anekavihitaṃ tiracchānakathaṃ kathentiyā’’ti. Tato poṭṭhapādo paribbājako te paribbājake oloketvā – ‘‘ime paribbājakā ativiya aññamaññaṃ agāravā, mayañca samaṇassa gotamassa pātubhāvato paṭṭhāya sūriyuggamane khajjopanakūpamā jātā, lābhasakkāropi no parihīno. Sace panimaṃ ṭhānaṃ samaṇo gotamo vā gotamassa sāvako vā gihī upaṭṭhāko vā tassa āgaccheyya , ativiya lajjanīyaṃ bhavissati, parisadoso kho pana parisajeṭṭhakasseva upari ārohatī’’ti itocito ca vilokento bhagavantaṃ addasa. Tena vuttaṃ – ‘‘addasā kho poṭṭhapādo paribbājako…pe… tuṇhī ahesu’’nti.
408.
"Unnādiniyā" means on account of loudly sounding. Thus, on account of sounding in this way, "ucco" is because of going upwards, and "mahā saddo" is because of being spread in the directions. Therefore, (it is) "uccāsaddamahāsaddāya." For those wanderers, there is no required practice, such as worship of a cetiya shrine, a bodhi tree, or paying respects to their teacher or preceptor, nor wise reflection after rising early in the morning.

Therefore, having risen early in the morning, they sit in the sunshine, (saying,) "This one's hand is beautiful, this one's foot," and thus, concerning each other's hands, feet, and so on, or concerning the beauty of women, men, boys, girls, and so on, or concerning other things related to the enjoyment of sensual pleasures and the enjoyment of existence, having stirred up a conversation, they expound various kinds of pointless talk, such as talk of kings, and so forth, in due order.

Therefore, it was said, "On account of loudly sounding with a great and noisy sound, they expound various kinds of pointless talk."

Then, the wanderer Poṭṭhapāda, having looked at those wanderers, (thought,) "These wanderers are exceedingly disrespectful to each other, and we, since the appearance of the ascetic Gotama, have become like fireflies at sunrise, and our gain and honor have declined.

If, indeed, the ascetic Gotama, or a disciple of Gotama, or a householder supporter of his, should come to this place, it would be exceedingly shameful. Indeed, the fault of the assembly rises upon the elder of the assembly himself." Thus, looking here and there, he saw the Blessed One.

Therefore, it was said, "The wanderer Poṭṭhapāda saw... and was silent."

409. Addasā kho poṭṭhapādo paribbājako bhagavantaṃ dūratova āgacchantaṃ; disvāna sakaṃ parisaṃ saṇṭhapesi – ‘‘appasaddā bhonto hontu, mā bhonto saddamakattha. Ayaṃ samaṇo gotamo āgacchati. Appasaddakāmo kho so āyasmā appasaddassa vaṇṇavādī. Appeva nāma appasaddaṃ parisaṃ viditvā upasaṅkamitabbaṃ maññeyyā’’ti. Evaṃ vutte te paribbājakā tuṇhī ahesuṃ.
409. Then Poṭṭhapāda the wanderer saw the Blessed One coming from afar, and on seeing him, he quieted his following: “Be quiet, good sirs. Don’t make any noise.
Here comes the śramaṇa Gotama.
He, the venerable one, is fond of quietude and speaks in praise of quietude (appasaddakāmo kho so āyasmā appasaddassa vaṇṇavādī).
Maybe, if he perceives our group as quiet, he will consider it worthwhile to come our way."
When this was said, the wanderers fell silent (evaṃ vutte te paribbājakā tuṇhī ahesuṃ).
409. Tattha saṇṭhapesīti sikkhāpesi, vajjamassā paṭicchādesi. Yathā susaṇṭhitā hoti, tathā naṃ ṭhapesi. Yathā nāma parisamajjhaṃ pavisanto puriso vajjapaṭicchādanatthaṃ nivāsanaṃ saṇṭhapeti, pārupanaṃ saṇṭhapeti, rajokiṇṇaṭṭhānaṃ puñchati; evamassā vajjapaṭicchādanatthaṃ – ‘‘appasaddā bhonto’’ti sikkhāpento yathā susaṇṭhitā hoti, tathā naṃ ṭhapesīti attho. Appasaddakāmoti appasaddaṃ icchati, eko nisīdati, eko tiṭṭhati, na gaṇasaṅgaṇikāya yāpeti. Upasaṅkamitabbaṃ maññeyyāti idhāgantabbaṃ maññeyya. Kasmā panesa bhagavato upasaṅkamanaṃ paccāsīsatīti? Attano vuddhiṃ patthayamāno. Paribbājakā kira buddhesu vā buddhasāvakesu vā attano santikaṃ āgatesu – ‘‘ajja amhākaṃ santikaṃ samaṇo gotamo āgato , sāriputto āgato, na kho pana te yassa vā tassa vā santikaṃ gacchanti, passatha amhākaṃ uttamabhāva’’nti attano upaṭṭhākānaṃ santike attānaṃ ukkhipanti, ucce ṭhāne ṭhapenti, bhagavatopi upaṭṭhāke gaṇhituṃ vāyamanti. Te kira bhagavato upaṭṭhāke disvā evaṃ vadanti – ‘‘tumhākaṃ satthā bhavaṃ gotamopi gotamasāvakāpi amhākaṃ santikaṃ āgacchanti, mayaṃ aññamaññaṃ samaggā. Tumhe pana amhe akkhīhipi passituṃ na icchatha, sāmīcikammaṃ na karotha, kiṃ vo amhehi aparaddha’’nti. Athekacce manussā – ‘‘buddhāpi etesaṃ santikaṃ gacchanti kiṃ amhāka’’nti tato paṭṭhāya te disvā nappamajjanti. Tuṇhī ahesunti poṭṭhapādaṃ parivāretvā nissaddā nisīdiṃsu.
409. There he settled = he trained [him], he concealed his fault.

Just as it is well-settled, so he placed him.

Just as, for example, a man entering an assembly settles his lower robe for the purpose of concealing faults, settles his outer robe, wipes off a dusty place; likewise, for the purpose of concealing his fault, teaching, "Let there be little noise, good sirs," just as it is well-settled, so he placed him, is the meaning.

"Desiring little noise" means he wants little noise. He sits alone, he stands alone, he does not spend [his time] in a crowd or company.

He would think it fit to approach = he would think it fit to come here.

Why then does he expect to approach the Blessed One? Desiring his own growth.

Wanderers, when Buddhas or disciples of the Buddha have come to their presence, [say,] "Today the ascetic Gotama has come to our presence, Sāriputta has come. But they do not go to the presence of just anyone. See our superior state!" Thus, in the presence of their attendants, they exalt themselves, they place [themselves] in a high position, they strive to get even the Blessed One's attendants.

They, having seen the Blessed One's attendants, say thus: "Your teacher, venerable Gotama, and Gotama's disciples come to our presence. We are harmonious with one another.

But you do not wish to see us even with [your] eyes, you do not do proper service. What wrong has been done to you by us?"

Then some people [think,] "Even Buddhas go to their presence, what [need is there] for us?" From then on, having seen them, they are not negligent.

"They were silent" means having surrounded Poṭṭhapāda, they sat down silently.

410. Atha kho bhagavā yena poṭṭhapādo paribbājako tenupasaṅkami. Atha kho poṭṭhapādo paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘etu kho, bhante, bhagavā. Svāgataṃ, bhante, bhagavato. Cirassaṃ kho, bhante, bhagavā imaṃ pariyāyamakāsi, yadidaṃ idhāgamanāya. Nisīdatu, bhante, bhagavā, idaṃ āsanaṃ paññatta’’nti. Nisīdi bhagavā paññatte āsane. Poṭṭhapādopi kho paribbājako aññataraṃ nīcaṃ āsanaṃ gahetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho poṭṭhapādaṃ paribbājakaṃ bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘kāya nuttha kāya nottha (syā. ka.), poṭṭhapāda, etarahi kathāya sannisinnā, kā ca pana vo antarākathā vippakatā’’ti?
Then the Blessed One went to the wanderer Poṭṭhapāda.

Then the wanderer Poṭṭhapāda said to the Blessed One: “Come, Blessed One. Welcome, Blessed One! It’s been a long time, venerable sir, since the Blessed One has gone out of his way to come here. Please, be seated, Blessed One; this seat has been prepared.”

The Blessed One sat down on the prepared seat.

And Poṭṭhapāda, taking a lower seat, sat down at one side.

As he was sitting there, the Blessed One said to the wanderer Poṭṭhapāda, "For what topic of conversation are you gathered together here now, Poṭṭhapāda? And what was your discussion that was interrupted?"
410.Svāgataṃ, bhanteti suṭṭhu āgamanaṃ, bhante, bhagavato; bhagavati hi no āgate ānando hoti, gate sokoti dīpeti. Cirassaṃ kho, bhanteti kasmā āha? Kiṃ bhagavā pubbepi tattha gatapubboti, na gatapubbo. Manussānaṃ pana – ‘‘kuhiṃ gacchantā, kuto āgatattha, kiṃ maggamūḷhattha, cirassaṃ āgatatthā’’ti evamādayo piyasamudācārā honti, tasmā evamāha. Evañca pana vatvā na mānathaddho hutvā nisīdi, uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavato paccuggamanamakāsi. Bhagavantañhi upagataṃ disvā āsanena animantento vā apacitiṃ akaronto vā dullabho. Kasmā? Uccākulīnatāya. Ayampi paribbājako attano nisinnāsanaṃ papphoṭetvā bhagavantaṃ āsanena nimantento – ‘‘nisīdatu, bhante, bhagavā idamāsanaṃ paññatta’’nti āha. Antarākathā vippakatāti nisinnānaṃ vo ādito paṭṭhāya yāva mamāgamanaṃ, etasmiṃ antare kā nāma kathā vippakatā, mamāgamanapaccayā katamā kathā pariyantaṃ na gatā, vadatha, yāva naṃ pariyantaṃ netvā demīti sabbaññupavāraṇaṃ pavāresi. Atha paribbājako – ‘‘niratthakakathā esā nissārā vaṭṭasannissitā, na tumhākaṃ purato vattabbataṃ arahatī’’ti dīpento ‘‘tiṭṭhatesā , bhante’’tiādimāha.
410.
Welcome, venerable one; it is a good arrival, venerable one, of the Blessed One; for with the Blessed One having arrived, there is joy for us, and with [him] having departed, there is sorrow," he indicates.

Why does he say, "After a long time, venerable one?" [It is because] the Blessed One had never been there before.

But for humans – "Where are you going? From where have you come? Are you lost on the road? Have you come after a long time?" – such are the expressions of affection that exist; therefore, he spoke thus.

And having said this, he sat down without being haughty, and rising from his seat, he went to greet the Blessed One.

For one who, having seen the Blessed One approaching, does not invite [him] with a seat or does not pay respects, is rare.

Why? Because of high birth.

This wanderer too, having clapped his own sitting seat, inviting the Blessed One with a seat – "Sit down, venerable one, the Blessed One; this seat is prepared" – he said.

"The conversation has been interrupted," thus, for you who are seated, from the beginning until my arrival, in this interval, what conversation has been interrupted? Because of my arrival, what conversation has not reached its conclusion? Speak, so that I may lead it to its conclusion," he announced the invitation to omniscience.

Then the wanderer – indicating, "This is a meaningless conversation, insubstantial, dependent on the cycle [of rebirths], it is not worthy to be spoken in front of you" – said, "Let it stay there, venerable one," and so on.

Having gone through the middle of the father's house, in the rear portion there is the son's house; then, a young thera, having taken fine food and having come to the alms hall, asked, "From where was this delightful alms-food brought?"

"Not the beginning of the holy life (brahmacariya)," [means] it is not even the mere beginning of the holy life (brahmacariyakassa) of the Teaching (sāsana), which is reckoned as the threefold training (sikkhattayasaṅkhātassa), nor even just the training in higher morality (adhisīlasikkhāmattampi).

"Not for dispassion (virāgāya)," [means] it does not lead (na saṃvattati) to the purpose of dispassion (virāgatthāya) from the round (vaṭṭa).

"Not for cessation (nirodhāya)," [means] it does not lead (na saṃvattati) to the purpose of making cessation (nirodhakaraṇatthāya) of the round (vaṭṭassa).

"Not for appeasement (upasamāyāti)," [means] it does not lead (na saṃvattati) to the purpose of the appeasement (vūpasamanatthāya) of the round (vaṭṭassa).

"Not for direct knowledge (abhiññāya)," [means] it does not lead (na saṃvattati) to direct realization (paccakkhakiriyāya) through the direct knowing (abhijānanāya) of the round (vaṭṭa).

"Not for enlightenment (sambodhāya)," [means] it does not lead (na saṃvattati) to the purpose of awakening (sambujjhanatthāya) from the round (vaṭṭa).

"Not for nirvāṇa (nibbānāya)," [means] it does not lead (na saṃvattati) to the direct realization (paccakkhakiriyāya) of the deathless great nirvāṇa (amatamahānibbānassa).

In "This is suffering" (idaṃ dukkhanti) etc., having established craving (taṇhaṃ), the five aggregates (pañcakkhandhā) that are of the triple world (tebhūmakā) are suffering (dukkhanti), from the arising (pabhāvanato) of that very suffering (dukkhassa), craving (taṇhā) with its conditions (sappaccayā) is the origin of suffering (dukkhassa samudayoti).

This is the meaning (attho) as declared (byākatanti) by me (mayā).

Therefore, it is difficult for you, with such a different view, different patience, different liking, effort in a different direction, and teaching in a different place, to understand this.

Then the wandering ascetic said, "Let perception be the self of a person, or a different perception, I will ask him about that eternal state," and again he said, "What then, venerable sir?"

There, "world" refers to speaking with reference to self.

"It is not connected with meaning, Poṭṭhapāda," means, Poṭṭhapāda, this is based on view, not based on the benefit of this world and the next world, not based on one's own benefit and the benefit of others.

"It is not connected with the Dhamma," means, it is not based on the nine supramundane Dhammas.

"It is not the beginning of the holy life," means, it is not even the mere beginning of the holy life consisting of the three trainings, it is not even the mere training in higher morality.

"It does not lead to disenchantment," means, it does not lead to disenchantment with the round of rebirth.

"It does not lead to dispassion," means, it does not lead to dispassion for the round.

"It does not lead to cessation," means, it does not lead to making an end of the round.

"It does not lead to peace," means, it does not lead to the stilling of the round.

"It does not lead to direct knowledge," means, it does not lead to the direct realization of the round.

"It does not lead to enlightenment," means, it does not lead to the perfect awakening from the round.

"It does not lead to nirvāṇa," means, it does not lead to the direct realization of the deathless great nirvāṇa.

"This is suffering," etc., setting aside craving, the five aggregates of the three realms are suffering, and craving, with its conditions, is the origin of suffering, because of the manifestation of that very suffering.

The non-occurrence of both is the cessation of suffering, the noble eightfold path is the practice leading to the cessation of suffering, this is the meaning as declared by me.

And so, having said this, the Bhagavān, thinking, "For this wandering ascetic, there is neither the appearance of the path nor the realization of the fruit, and it is time for my alms round," became silent.

Pāmujjanti is youthful joy (taruṇapīti).

Joy (pīti) is strong satisfaction (balavatuṭṭhi).

What does it mean? Thus in the fourth.

In the Pāsādika Sutta, insight (vipassanā) is spoken of together with the four paths (catūhi maggehi saddhiṃ).

In the Dasuttara Sutta, the attainment of the fruit of the fourth jhāna (catutthajjhānikaphalasamāpatti) is spoken of.

But for the Buddhas, there are two kinds of speech: conventional speech (saṃmutikathā) and ultimate speech (paramatthakathā).

There, that which begins with "a being (sattva), a person (posa), a god (deva), Brahmā" is called conventional speech (saṃmutikathā).

That which begins with "impermanent (anitya), suffering (duḥkha), without self (anātmā), aggregates (skandha), elements (dhātu), sense bases (āyatana), mindfulness (smṛti), perfect exertion (samyakpradhāna)" is called ultimate speech (paramārtthakathā).

There, the Lord speaks first of "a being (sattva)," "a person (posa)," "a god (deva)," or "Brahmā" to someone who, when it is said in conventional teaching "a being (sattva)," or "a person (posa)," or "a god (deva)," or "Brahmā," is able to understand, penetrate, and proceed to take hold of the victory over the stronghold of arhatship; to someone who, having heard from the ultimate teaching "impermanent (anitya)," or "suffering (duḥkha)," and so forth, is able to understand, penetrate, and proceed to take hold of the victory over the stronghold of arhatship, the Lord speaks only of "impermanent (anitya)" or "suffering (duḥkha)," and so forth.

Likewise, to a being who is able to understand conventional speech (sammutikathā), he does not speak of ultimate speech (paramatthakathā) first.

But having enlightened [him] with conventional speech (sammutikathā), he speaks of ultimate speech (paramatthakathā) afterwards.

To a being who is able to understand ultimate speech (paramatthakathā), he does not speak of conventional speech (sammutikathā) first.

But having enlightened [him] with ultimate speech (paramatthakathā), he speaks of conventional speech (sammutikathā) afterwards.

For someone who, by nature, speaks of ultimate speech (paramatthakathā) first, the teaching is harsh in nature; therefore, the Buddhas, having spoken of conventional speech (sammutikathā) first, speak of ultimate speech (paramatthakathā) afterwards.

Even while speaking of conventional speech (sammutikathā), they speak only of truth, only of reality, not falsely.

Even while speaking of ultimate speech (paramārtthakathā), they speak only of truth, only of reality, not falsely.

"The tathāgata speaks by means of whatever he speaks; he does not cling; the tathāgata, without clinging because of the absence of clinging to craving (tṛṣṇā), pride (māna), views (dṛṣṭi)," having turned the teaching away from "the worldly designations, the worldly terms," he brought it to completion with the subtlety of arhatship.

The rest is clear in every respect.

Then, the Blessed One, in order to compare his own explanation with that explanation, said, "It is just so, Citta," and so on. Again, while establishing that meaning through simile, he said, "Just as, Citta, from a cow comes milk," and so on.

Here, this is the concise meaning: just as from a cow comes milk (khīra), and from milk etc. come curds (dadhi) etc., there, at whatever time there is milk, at that time there is not curds—or any other such as fresh butter (navanīta)—it goes into reckoning (saṅkhyaṃ), expression (niruttiṃ), name (nāmaṃ), and usage (vohāraṃ).

Why? Because of the absence of those dharmas taking hold of which the usages "curds" etc. exist.

But then, at that time, just milk itself goes into reckoning.

Why? Because of the existence of those dharmas taking hold of which the reckoning (saṅkhyā), expression (nirutti), name (nāmaṃ), and usage (vohāra), is "milk."

This method applies everywhere.

These, Citta, are gross attainment of self (oḷāriko attapaṭilābho)—and mental attainment of self (manomayo attapaṭilābho)—and formless attainment of self (arūpo attapaṭilābho). These, Citta, are worldly designations (lokasamaññā), worldly conventionalities (samaññāmattakāni), worldly understandings (samanujānanamattakāni).

Likewise, these are worldly expressions (lokaniruttimattakāni), pathways of speech (vacanapathamattakāni), conventional usages (vohāramattakāni), designations of names (nāmapaṇṇattimattakāni).

Thus, the Blessed One, having described below the three attainments of self, now says that all of this is merely usage (vohāramattakaṃ).

Why? Because, in ultimate truth (paramatthato), there is no being (satto) by the name, this world is empty (suñño), void (tuccho).

For Buddhas, however, there are two kinds of talk (kathā): conventional talk (sammutikathā) and ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathā).

There, “A being (satto), a person (poso), a god (devo), Brahmā (brahmā)" etc. are called "conventional talk (sammutikathā)."

"Impermanent (aniccaṃ), suffering (dukkham), without self (anattā), aggregates (khandhā), elements (dhātuyo), sense-bases (āyatanāni), mindfulness (satipaṭṭhānā), right efforts (sammappadhānā)" etc. are called "ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathā)."

There, the Blessed One initially speaks of "a being (satto)," "a person (poso)," "a god (devo)," or "Brahmā (brahmā)" etc. to someone who, having heard in conventional teaching (sammutidesanāya) "a being (satto)," or "a person (poso)," or "a god (devo)," or "Brahmā (brahmā)," is able to understand (vijānituṃ), penetrate (paṭivijjhituṃ), go forth (niyyātuṃ), and take hold of the grasp of arhat-hood (arahattajayaggāhaṃ gahetuṃ). To someone who, having heard in ultimate-meaning teaching (paramatthadesanāya) "impermanent (anicca)" or "suffering (dukkha)" etc., is able to understand (vijānituṃ), penetrate (paṭivijjhituṃ), go forth (niyyātuṃ), and take hold of the grasp of arhat-hood (arahattajayaggāhaṃ gahetuṃ), he speaks only of "impermanent (anicca)" or "suffering (dukkha)" etc.

Likewise, to a being (sattassa) capable of understanding (bujjhanaka) conventional talk (sammutikathāya), one does not initially speak ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathaṃ).

But having awakened him with conventional talk (sammutikathāya), one speaks ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathaṃ) afterwards.

To a being (sattassa) capable of understanding (bujjhanaka) ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathāya), one does not initially speak conventional talk (sammutikathaṃ).

But having awakened him with ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathāya), one speaks conventional talk (sammutikathaṃ) afterwards.

However, for someone who, by nature (pakatiyā), initially speaks only ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathaṃ), the teaching (desanā) is harsh (lūkhākārā). Therefore, Buddhas, having initially spoken conventional talk (sammutikathaṃ), speak ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathaṃ) afterwards.

Even while speaking conventional talk (sammutikathaṃ), they speak only the truth (saccameva), only reality (sabhāvaṃeva), [and] not falsely (amusāva).

Even while speaking ultimate-meaning talk (paramatthakathaṃ), they speak only the truth (saccameva), only reality (sabhāvaṃeva), [and] not falsely (amusāva).

With whatever [designations] the Tathāgata speaks, without clinging (aparāmasanti), with whatever worldly designations (lokasamaññāhi) and worldly expressions (lokaniruttīhi) the Tathāgata, because of the absence of clinging to craving, pride, and views (taṇhāmānadiṭṭhiparāmāsānaṃ abhāvā), speaks without clinging (aparāmasanto voharatīti)—having turned the teaching (desanaṃ vinivaṭṭetvā) with the subtlety of arhat-hood (arahattanikūṭena), he concluded (niṭṭhāpesi).
