396. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā aṅguttarāpesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ aḍḍhateḷasehi bhikkhusatehi yena āpaṇaṃ nāma aṅguttarāpānaṃ nigamo tadavasari. Assosi kho keṇiyo jaṭilo – ‘‘samaṇo khalu, bho, gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito aṅguttarāpesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṃ aḍḍhateḷasehi bhikkhusatehi āpaṇaṃ anuppatto. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavāti. So imaṃ lokaṃ sadevakaṃ samārakaṃ sabrahmakaṃ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ pajaṃ sadevamanussaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti. So dhammaṃ deseti ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’’ti. Atha kho keṇiyo jaṭilo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho keṇiyaṃ jaṭilaṃ bhagavā dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi . Atha kho keṇiyo jaṭilo bhagavatā dhammiyā kathāya sandassito samādapito samuttejito sampahaṃsito bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘adhivāsetu me bhavaṃ gotamo svātanāya bhattaṃ saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṅghenā’’ti. Evaṃ vutte, bhagavā keṇiyaṃ jaṭilaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘mahā kho, keṇiya, bhikkhusaṅgho aḍḍhateḷasāni bhikkhusatāni, tvañca brāhmaṇesu abhippasanno’’ti. Dutiyampi kho keṇiyo jaṭilo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kiñcāpi kho, bho gotama, mahā bhikkhusaṅgho aḍḍhateḷasāni bhikkhusatāni, ahañca brāhmaṇesu abhippasanno; adhivāsetu me bhavaṃ gotamo svātanāya bhattaṃ saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṅghenā’’ti. Dutiyampi kho bhagavā keṇiyaṃ jaṭilaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘mahā kho, keṇiya, bhikkhusaṅgho aḍḍhateḷasāni bhikkhusatāni, tvañca brāhmaṇesu abhippasanno’’ti. Tatiyampi kho keṇiyo jaṭilo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kiñcāpi kho, bho gotama, mahā bhikkhusaṅgho aḍḍhateḷasāni bhikkhusatāni, ahañca brāhmaṇesu abhippasanno; adhivāsetu me bhavaṃ gotamo svātanāya bhattaṃ saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṅghenā’’ti . Adhivāsesi bhagavā tuṇhībhāvena. Atha kho keṇiyo jaṭilo bhagavato adhivāsanaṃ viditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā yena sako assamo tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā mittāmacce ñātisālohite āmantesi – ‘‘suṇantu me bhonto, mittāmaccā ñātisālohitā; samaṇo me gotamo nimantito svātanāya bhattaṃ saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṅghena. Yena me kāyaveyyāvaṭikaṃ kāyaveyāvaṭṭikaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ.), kāyaveyyāvatikaṃ (ka.) kareyyāthā’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bho’’ti kho keṇiyassa jaṭilassa mittāmaccā ñātisālohitā keṇiyassa jaṭilassa paṭissutvā appekacce uddhanāni khaṇanti, appekacce kaṭṭhāni phālenti, appekacce bhājanāni dhovanti, appekacce udakamaṇikaṃ patiṭṭhāpenti, appekacce āsanāni paññapenti. Keṇiyo pana jaṭilo sāmaṃyeva maṇḍalamālaṃ paṭiyādeti. kāyaveyāvaṭṭikaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ.), kāyaveyyāvatikaṃ (ka.)
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the country of the Aṅguttarāpas with a large saṅgha of bhikkhus, with twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, and eventually he arrived at a town of the Aṅguttarāpas named Āpaṇa.

The matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya heard: “The recluse Gotama, the son of the Sakyans who went forth from a Sakyan clan, has been wandering in the country of the Aṅguttarāpas with a large saṅgha of bhikkhus, with twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, and he has come to Āpaṇa.

Now a good report of Master Gotama has been spread to this effect: ‘The Blessed One is an arahaṃ, a sammāsambuddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), sublime (sugato), knower of the world (lokavidū), incomparable (anuttaro), leader of persons to be tamed (purisadammasārathi), teacher of gods and humans (satthā devamanussānaṃ), a Buddha (buddho), blessed (bhagavā)’.

He, having realized it by his own super-knowledge, proclaims this world with its gods (devas), Māras, and Brahmās, this generation with its ascetics (ssamaṇas) and brāhmaṇas, its princes and its people, which he has himself realised by direct knowledge (abhiññā sacchikatvā).

He teaches the Dhamma, which is lovely in its beginning (ādikalyāṇaṃ), lovely in its middle (majjhekalyāṇaṃ), lovely in its ending (pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ), in the spirit (sātthaṃ) and in the letter (sabyañjanaṃ), and displays the fully-perfected and purified holy life (brahmacariyaṃ).

“It is good to see such a worthy one.”

Then the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya went to where the Blessed One was, and exchanged greetings with him.

After exchanging courteous greetings and polite conversation, he sat down at a distance.

As he was sitting there, the Blessed One instructed, urged, roused, & encouraged Keṇiya the coiled-hair ascetic with a Dhamma talk.

Then, having been instructed, urged, roused, and encouraged by the Blessed One with a talk on the Dhamma, the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya said to the Blessed One: “Let Master Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus consent to accept tomorrow’s meal from me.”

When this was said, the Blessed One told him: “The Saṅgha of bhikkhus is large, Keṇiya, consisting of twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, and you place full confidence in the brāhmaṇas.”

A second time the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya said to the Blessed One: “Although the Saṅgha of bhikkhus is large, Master Gotama, consisting of twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, and although I place full confidence in the brāhmaṇas, still let Master Gotama, together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus, consent to accept tomorrow’s meal from me.”

A second time the Blessed One told him: “The Saṅgha of bhikkhus is large, Keṇiya, consisting of twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, and you place full confidence in the brāhmaṇas.”

A third time the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya said to the Blessed One: “Although the Saṅgha is large, Master Gotama…still let Master Gotama together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus consent to accept tomorrow’s meal from me.”

The Lord signified his acceptance by silence (tuṇhībhāvena).

Then, knowing that the Blessed One had consented, the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya rose from his seat and went to his own hermitage where he addressed his friends and companions, his kinsmen and relatives thus: “Hear me, sirs, my friends and companions, my kinsmen and relatives. The recluse Gotama has been invited by me for tomorrow’s meal together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.

Therefore, you should help me.”

“Yes, sir,” they replied, and some dug out ovens, some chopped wood, some washed dishes, some set out water jugs, some prepared seats,

while the matted-hair ascetic Keṇiya himself set up a pavilion (maṇḍalamālaṃ).

396. Thus, "have I heard," is the Sela Sutta. There, "Aṅguttarāpesū," etc., is explained in detail in the Potaliya Sutta. "Aḍḍhateḷasehī" means half of thirteen, twelve hundred. And with fifty monks, it is said that there was faith. Those monks, gathered in the assembly of disciples, were all those who had gone forth by the "Come, monk" ordination, [and were] those who had destroyed the āsavas (influxes). "Keṇiyo" is his name, "Jaṭilo" is the ascetic. It is said that he was a great brahmin, who, for the purpose of protecting wealth, took the ascetic life, gave a gift to the king, took a share of the land, built a hermitage there, lived by engaging in trade with five hundred carts, and having become a refuge for a thousand families, a single palm tree in his hermitage would drop one golden palm fruit each day. He wears reddish-brown robes during the day, ties up his matted hair, and experiences the pleasures of the senses at night. "Dhammiyā Kathāyā" means in accordance with the Dhamma, connected with the benefits of the drink. This Keṇiyo, ashamed to go empty-handed to the Blessed One, thinking, "Drink is suitable even for those who are abstaining from untimely meals," had a well-prepared badara drink obtained with five hundred kārṣāpaṇas (a type of coin) and went. Thus was his state of being – "Then it occurred to Keṇiyo the ascetic, 'What should I offer to Gotama the Samaṇa (recluse)?'" – in the Bhesajjakkhandhaka (Mahāvagga. 300) it is clearly stated in the Pāli.

Again, why did the Blessed One repeatedly reject [his invitation]? Because of the Titthiyas' inclination to reject [him]. This is not a reason; there is no such ignorance among the Buddhas as to have a reason for their belief. But this one, having seen the thirteen and a half hundred monks, will provide alms for only so many. Sela himself will go forth with three hundred men. It is not fitting to send the new ones elsewhere and go with these, or to send these elsewhere and go with the new ones. But if I take all of them, there will not be enough alms food. Then people will criticize the monks who are wandering for alms – "Even after a long time, Keṇiyo, having invited Gotama the Samaṇa, was not able to give even enough for sustenance," and he himself will be remorseful. But when [his invitation] has been rejected, thinking, "Gotama the Samaṇa repeatedly takes the name of the Brahmins and is attached to the Brahmins," he will want to invite the Brahmins as well. Then he will invite each of the Brahmins separately, and those monks invited by him will eat. Thus, his faith will be protected, so he repeatedly rejected [his invitation]. "Kiñcāpi kho, bho" (Even though, sir) indicates this, – "Sir Gotama, what has happened if I am attached to the Brahmins? May Gotama accept [my invitation], I am able to give to the Brahmins as well as to you."

397. At that time, Sela the brāhmaṇa was living in Apana. He had mastered the three Vedas, with their nighaṇḍu (glossaries), keṭubha (commentaries), and systems of phonology, as well as the itihāsa (tradition) as a fifth subject. He was skilled in padaka (grammar) and veyyākaraṇa (etymology), and was not deficient concerning the characteristics of the lokāyata and the great man. He had three hundred students recite mantras.

At that time, Keniya the ascetic had full confidence in Sela the brāhmaṇa.

Then Sela the brāhmaṇa, while walking and wandering for exercise attended by his three hundred brahmin students, came to the hermitage of Keniya the ascetic.

Sela the brāhmaṇa saw at the hermitage of Keniya the ascetic some men digging out ovens, some chopping wood, some washing dishes, some setting out water jugs (udakamaṇikaṃ), some preparing seats, while the ascetic Keniya himself was setting up a pavilion (maṇḍalamālaṃ).

Seeing Keniya the ascetic, he said this: “Friend Keniya, are you holding a marriage (āvāho)? Or a wedding (vivāho)? Or is a great sacrifice (mahāyañño) being prepared? Or has King Seniya Bimbisara of Magadha been invited for tomorrow’s meal together with a large retinue (balakāyena)?”

“No, Master Sela, I am not holding a marriage, nor a wedding, nor has King Seniya Bimbisara of Magadha been invited for tomorrow’s meal together with a large retinue; but a great sacrifice is being prepared for me."

“Sir, the samaṇa Gotama, the son of the Sakyans who went forth from a Sakyan clan, has been wandering in the country of the Anguttarapas with a large saṅgha of bhikkhus, with twelve hundred and fifty bhikkhus, and has come to Apana.”

And of that master Gotama this fine reputation has spread: ‘He is indeed a Blessed One (bhagavā), a Worthy One (arahaṃ), a Fully Enlightened One (sammāsambuddho), consummate in clear-knowing and conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), Well-Gone One (sugato), an expert with regard to the cosmos (lokavidū), unexcelled trainer of people fit to be tamed (anuttaro purisadammasārathi), teacher of gods and human beings (satthā devamanussānaṃ), awakened (buddho), blessed (bhagavā)’."

"He has been invited by me for tomorrow’s meal together with the saṅgha of bhikkhus.”

“Do you say, ‘Buddha’—friend Keniya?”

“‘Buddha’—friend Sela, I say.”

“Do you say, ‘Buddha’—friend Keniya?”

“‘Buddha’—friend Sela, I say.”

Sujāto means being well-born, having excellent height, circumference and a well-formed constitution.

Cārudassano means having a pleasing appearance, never satiating those who gaze upon him, and possessing an enchanting form.

Suvaṇṇavaṇṇo means having a complexion like that of gold.

Susukkadāṭho means having very white teeth.

Mahāpurisalakkhaṇā means the previously mentioned characteristics of a great man, summarized in different words.

Now, having taken those characteristics that are pleasing to his own mind from among those marks, he praises [the Buddha], saying, "pasannanetto," and so forth.

Indeed, the Blessed One, through the perfection of the fivefold radiance of purity, is pasannanetto (clear-eyed); through having a face like a full moon, he is sumukho (having a beautiful face); through the perfection of height and circumference, he is brahā (great); through having the uprightness of Brahmā, he is uju (upright); and through possessing splendor, he is patāpavā (powerful).

And that which was previously said here, that same thing is said again by way of praise, with this expression: "In the midst of the community of śramaṇas."

Such a one shines forth in this way.

The same method [applies] also in the subsequent verse.
