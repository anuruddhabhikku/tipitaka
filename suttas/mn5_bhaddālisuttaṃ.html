Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was staying near Sāvatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park (jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

There the Blessed One addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: “Monks (bhikkhavo)!”

“Venerable sir (bhadante),” those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: “Monks (bhikkhave), I eat at a single session (ekāsanabhojanaṃ). By eating at a single session (ekāsanabhojanaṃ), monks (bhikkhave), I experience freedom from illness (appābādhatañca), freedom from affliction (appātaṅkatañca), lightness (lahuṭṭhānañca), strength (balañca), and a comfortable abiding (phāsuvihārañca).

Come, monks (bhikkhave), eat at a single session (ekāsanabhojanaṃ). By eating at a single session (ekāsanabhojanaṃ), monks (bhikkhave), you too will experience freedom from illness (appābādhatañca), freedom from affliction (appātaṅkatañca), lightness (lahuṭṭhānañca), strength (balañca), and a comfortable abiding (phāsuvihārañcā).”

When this was said, the venerable Bhaddāli told the Blessed One: “Venerable sir (bhante), I am not willing to eat at a single session (ekāsanabhojanaṃ); for if I were to eat at a single session (ekāsanabhojanaṃ), venerable sir (bhante), I might have worry (kukkuccaṃ) and anxiety (vippaṭisāro) about it.”

“Then, Bhaddāli, eat one part there where you are invited and bring away one part to eat.

Even then, Bhaddāli, while eating, you should maintain the practice of eating one meal a day."

“Even in that way, venerable sir (bhante), I am not willing to eat; for if I were to eat in that way, venerable sir (bhante), I might also have worry (kukkuccaṃ) and anxiety (vippaṭisāro) about it.”

Then, when this training precept (sikkhāpade) was being made known by the Blessed One, the venerable Bhaddāli publicly declared in the Sangha (bhikkhusaṅghe) of monks his unwillingness (anussāhaṃ) to undertake the training (sikkhaṃ samādiyamāne).

Then the venerable Bhaddāli did not present himself to the Blessed One for the whole of that three-month period of the Rains, as he did not fulfil the training in the Teacher’s Dispensation (satthusāsane).

134. Thus have I heard: the Bhaddālisutta.

There, ekāsanabhojana means eating a meal at one sitting before noon; the meaning is food that should be eaten.

Appābādhata, etc., are explained through the simile of a saw.

Na ussahāmī means I am not able.

"Siyā kukkuccaṃ siyā vippaṭisāro" means, thinking in this way while eating, "Will I be able to practice the brahmacariya for as long as I live? Or will I not?" the meaning is that regret and scruples may arise in me.

Ekadesaṃ bhuñjitvā means the elder monks of old, having poured food into their bowl and having been given ghee, having eaten only a little while it was still hot with the ghee, having washed their hands, having taken the remainder outside, they would sit down in a place comfortable with shade and water and eat.

With this in mind, the Teacher said:

Bhaddāli, consider this: "If, having filled the bowl once, having eaten the food that was given, having washed the bowl again, having filled it with rice, and having obtained more, he should take it outside, and should eat in a place comfortable with shade and water, in this way it would be proper, otherwise who would be able to do it?"

Therefore, "Even in this way, venerable sir, I am not able," he said.

It is said that in the past, in an uninterrupted birth, he was born in the crow-womb (kākayoniyaṃ).

And crows are very greedy.

Therefore, he was named Chātakathera.

While he was crying in this way, the Blessed One, having touched him and covered him, laid down the rule of training: "Whoever, monks, should eat or consume any khādanīyaṃ or bhojanīyaṃ at the wrong time, it is an offense entailing confession" (pācittiya).

Therefore it was said: "Then, venerable Bhaddāli...he declared his lack of enthusiasm".

Just as another, though not fulfilling the training, dwelling in one monastery, would not give face to face with the Teacher, just so he did not give. This is the meaning.

He did not go to the Blessed One's presence, nor to the place of teaching the Dharma, nor to the debating hall, nor did he follow a single alms-round path.

In whatever family the Blessed One would sit down, he did not stand even at the door.

When the Blessed One would go to his dwelling place, he, knowing it beforehand, would go elsewhere.

It is said that this son of a good family went forth out of faith, with pure morality.

Therefore, no other thought arose in him: "It is I who, for the sake of my belly, have rejected the Blessed One's laying down of a training rule; it is not fitting that this has been done by me." This very thought arose in him.

Therefore, even dwelling in one monastery, out of shame he did not give face to face with the Teacher.

Here, Bhaddali, a bhikkhu possesses the right view of one beyond training (asekhāya sammādiṭṭhiyā), the right intention of one beyond training (asekhena sammāsaṅkappena), the right speech of one beyond training (asekhāya sammāvācāya), the right action of one beyond training (asekhena sammākammantena), the right livelihood of one beyond training (asekhena sammāājīvena), the right effort of one beyond training (asekhena sammāvāyāmena), the right mindfulness of one beyond training (asekhāya sammāsatiyā), the right concentration of one beyond training (asekhena sammāsamādhinā), the right knowledge of one beyond training (asekhena sammāñāṇena), and the right deliverance of one beyond training (asekhāya sammāvimuttiyā). When a bhikkhu possesses these ten qualities, he is worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, an unsurpassed field of merit for the world.”

Thus spoke the Lord.

The venerable Bhaddali was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Bhaddāli Sutta, the fifth, is finished.

"Visūkāyitānī": namely, he related the visevana conduct, and so on.
All these reasons are synonymous with each other.
"Tasmiṃ ṭhāne": namely, in that visevana conduct.
"Parinibbāyatī": namely, one becomes without visevana; that is, abandoning that visevana.
"Yugādhāne": namely, in placing the yoke, for the proper grasping of the yoke.

Following the sequence, with a single strike, all four feet are raised and lowered.
Indeed, for the enemy army, standing in a pit, taking a sword, they cut the feet of the approaching horse.
At that time, this one, with a single strike, will lift all four feet; thus, with the method of binding the rope, they perform this cause.
"Maṇḍale": namely, just as the horse, remaining seated, is able to take the weapon that has fallen on the ground; thus, to do so, he makes the cause in the circle.
"Khurakāse": namely, the earth movement with the tips of the hooves.
At night, indeed, when the sound of footsteps is not heard in the okkanta practice, therefore, giving a signal in one place, they teach movement only with the tips of the hooves.
This was said with reference to that.
"Jave": namely, in swift vehicles.
"It should run": this is also a reading.
When there is defeat for oneself, for the purpose of fleeing, and having pursued the fleeing enemy, for the purpose of capturing him, he makes this cause.
"Davatte": namely, for the sake of davatta; indeed, in the time of war, when elephants are making the sound of koñca, or when horses are neighing, or when chariots are rumbling, or when warriors are shouting, not being afraid of that sound, this reason is made for the purpose of entering the enemy's army.

Following the sequence, in the circle, the hoof-mark, in speed, in davatta.
"Rājaguṇe": namely, qualities to be known by the king.
It is said that there was a horse named Guḷavaṇṇa belonging to the king Kūṭakaṇṇa.
The king, having departed through the eastern gate, having reached the bank of the Kalambanadī River, [thought] "I will go to Cetiyapabbata."
The horse, standing on the bank, does not wish to cross the water; the king, having called the horse-trainer, said: "Alas, this horse you have trained does not wish to cross the water."
The trainer said: "O King, the horse has been well trained; indeed, this is its thought: 'If I cross the water, the tail will get wet, and when the tail is wet, water will fall on the king's limbs.' Thus, it does not cross out of fear of water falling on your body. Let it grasp the tail!"
The king did so.
The horse, having crossed swiftly, went to the other side.
For this purpose, this cause is made.
"Rājavaṃse": namely, in the lineage of royal horses.
That lineage certainly causes the royal horse, even with a body torn and broken by a strike of that form, without making the horseman fall into the enemy's army, to lead him out.
For this purpose, he makes this reason.

In the king's qualities, in speed, "Uttame jave": namely, in the attainment of speed, just as there is supreme speed, so he makes that cause.
"Uttame haye": namely, in the state of being an excellent horse, just as there is an excellent horse, so he makes that reason.
There, by nature, only an excellent horse deserves the cause of an excellent horse, not another.
And indeed, it is for the sake of an excellent horse that a horse acquires excellent speed, not another.
