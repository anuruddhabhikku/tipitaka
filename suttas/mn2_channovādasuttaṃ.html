389. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe. Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā ca sāriputto āyasmā ca mahācundo āyasmā ca channo gijjhakūṭe pabbate viharanti. Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā channo ābādhiko hoti dukkhito bāḷhagilāno. Atha kho āyasmā sāriputto sāyanhasamayaṃ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yenāyasmā mahācundo tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ mahācundaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘āyāmāvuso cunda, yenāyasmā channo tenupasaṅkamissāma gilānapucchakā’’ti. ‘‘Evamāvuso’’ti kho āyasmā mahācundo āyasmato sāriputtassa paccassosi. Atha kho āyasmā ca sāriputto āyasmā ca mahācundo yenāyasmā channo tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā channena saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā sāriputto āyasmantaṃ channaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kacci te, āvuso channa, khamanīyaṃ, kacci yāpanīyaṃ? Kacci te dukkhā vedanā paṭikkamanti, no abhikkamanti; paṭikkamosānaṃ paññāyati, no abhikkamo’’ti? ‘‘Na me, āvuso sāriputta, khamanīyaṃ na yāpanīyaṃ. Bāḷhā me dukkhā vedanā abhikkamanti, no paṭikkamanti; abhikkamosānaṃ paññāyati, no paṭikkamo. Seyyathāpi , āvuso sāriputta, balavā puriso tiṇhena sikharena muddhani abhimattheyya; evameva kho me, āvuso sāriputta, adhimattā vātā muddhani ūhananti. Na me, āvuso sāriputta, khamanīyaṃ na yāpanīyaṃ. Bāḷhā me dukkhā vedanā abhikkamanti, no paṭikkamanti; abhikkamosānaṃ paññāyati no paṭikkamo. Seyyathāpi, āvuso sāriputta, balavā puriso daḷhena varattakkhaṇḍena sīse sīsaveṭhaṃ dadeyya; evameva kho me, āvuso sāriputta, adhimattā sīse sīsavedanā. Na me, āvuso sāriputta, khamanīyaṃ na yāpanīyaṃ. Bāḷhā me dukkhā vedanā abhikkamanti, no paṭikkamanti; abhikkamosānaṃ paññāyati, no paṭikkamo. Seyyathāpi, āvuso sāriputta, dakkho goghātako vā goghātakantevāsī vā tiṇhena govikantanena kucchiṃ parikanteyya; evameva kho me, āvuso sāriputta, adhimattā vātā kucchiṃ parikantanti. Na me, āvuso sāriputta, khamanīyaṃ na yāpanīyaṃ. Bāḷhā me dukkhā vedanā abhikkamanti, no paṭikkamanti; abhikkamosānaṃ paññāyati, no paṭikkamo. Seyyathāpi, āvuso sāriputta, dve balavanto purisā dubbalataraṃ purisaṃ nānābāhāsu gahetvā aṅgārakāsuyā santāpeyyuṃ samparitāpeyyuṃ; evameva kho me, āvuso sāriputta, adhimatto kāyasmiṃ ḍāho. Na me, āvuso sāriputta, khamanīyaṃ na yāpanīyaṃ. Bāḷhā me dukkhā vedanā abhikkamanti, no paṭikkamanti; abhikkamosānaṃ paññāyati, no paṭikkamo. Satthaṃ, āvuso sāriputta, āharissāmi, nāvakaṅkhāmi jīvita’’nti.
THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary.

Now on that occasion the venerable Sāriputta, the venerable Mahā Cunda, and the venerable Channa were living on the mountain Vulture Peak.

On that occasion the venerable Channa was afflicted, suffering, and gravely ill.

Then, when it was evening, the venerable Sāriputta rose from meditation, went to the venerable Mahā Cunda, and said to him: “Friend Cunda, let us go to the venerable Channa and ask about his illness.”

- “Yes, friend,” the venerable Maha Cunda replied.

Then the venerable Sāriputta and the venerable Mahā Cunda went to the venerable Channa and exchanged greetings with him. When they had concluded their greetings and cordial talk, they sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, the venerable Sāriputta said to the venerable Channa: “I trust, friend Channa, that things are bearable for you. I trust that things are comfortable for you. I trust that your painful feelings are subsiding, not increasing; that there are signs of their subsiding, and not of their increasing?”

“Friend Sāriputta, I am not getting well, I am not comfortable. My severe pains are increasing, not lessening. There are signs of their increasing, and not of their lessening. Just as if a strong man were crushing my head with the tip of a sharp sword, so too, violent winds cut through my head. I am not getting well, friend Sāriputta, I am not comfortable. My severe pains are increasing, not lessening. There are signs of their increasing, and not of their lessening. Just as if a strong man were tightening a tough leather strap around my head as a headband, so too, there are violent pains in my head. I am not getting well, friend Sāriputta, I am not comfortable. My severe pains are increasing, not lessening. There are signs of their increasing, and not of their lessening. Just as if a skilled butcher or his apprentice were to carve up an ox’s belly with a sharp butcher’s knife, so too, violent winds are carving up my belly. I am not getting well, friend Sāriputta, I am not comfortable. My severe pains are increasing, not lessening. There are signs of their increasing, and not of their lessening. Just as if two strong men were to seize a weaker man by the arms and roast and broil him over a charcoal pit, so too, there is an excessive fever inside my body. I am not getting well, friend Sāriputta, I am not comfortable. My severe pains are increasing, not lessening. There are signs of their increasing, and not of their lessening. I shall use the knife, friend Sāriputta; I have no desire to live.”
389.Evaṃme sutanti channovādasuttaṃ. Tattha channoti evaṃnāmako thero, na abhinikkhamanaṃ nikkhantatthero. Paṭisallānāti phalasamāpattito. Gilānapucchakāti gilānupaṭṭhānaṃ nāma buddhavaṇṇitaṃ, tasmā evamāha. Satthanti jīvitahārakasatthaṃ. Nāvakaṅkhāmīti icchāmi.
389.
Thus, "me suta" is the Channovādasutta.

There, "channo" means a thera of such a name, not an abhinikkhamana nikkhantatthera.

"Paṭisallānā" means from the attainment of fruition (phalasamāpatti).

"Gilānapucchakā" means tending to the sick (gilānupaṭṭhāna), which is praised by the Buddha, therefore he spoke thus.

"Sattha" means a weapon that takes life (jīvitahārakasatthaṃ).

"Nāvakaṅkhāmī" means I desire (icchāmi).

391. ‘‘Cakkhuṃ, āvuso channa, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ cakkhuviññāṇaviññātabbe dhamme ‘etaṃ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā’ti samanupassasi? Sotaṃ, āvuso channa, sotaviññāṇaṃ…pe… ghānaṃ, āvuso channa, ghānaviññāṇaṃ… jivhaṃ, āvuso channa, jivhāviññāṇaṃ … kāyaṃ, āvuso channa, kāyaviññāṇaṃ… manaṃ, āvuso channa, manoviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaviññātabbe dhamme ‘etaṃ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā’ti samanupassasī’’ti? ‘‘Cakkhuṃ, āvuso sāriputta, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ cakkhuviññāṇaviññātabbe dhamme ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti samanupassāmi. Sotaṃ, āvuso sāriputta…pe… ghānaṃ, āvuso sāriputta… jivhaṃ, āvuso sāriputta… kāyaṃ, āvuso sāriputta… manaṃ, āvuso sāriputta, manoviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaviññātabbe dhamme ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti samanupassāmī’’ti.
"Friend Channa, do you regard the eye, eye-consciousness, and things cognizable by eye-consciousness thus: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”
Friend Channa, the ear, ear-consciousness…the nose, nose-consciousness…the tongue, tongue-consciousness…the body, body-consciousness…the mind, mind-consciousness, and things cognizable by the mind through mind-consciousness: do you regard them thus: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“Friend Sariputta, I regard the eye, eye-consciousness, and things cognizable by eye-consciousness thus: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’
Friend Sariputta, the ear…the nose…the tongue…the body…the mind, mind-consciousness, and things cognizable by the mind through mind-consciousness: I regard them thus: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’“
391.Etaṃ mamātiādīni taṇhāmānadiṭṭhigāhavasena vuttāni. Nirodhaṃ disvāti khayavayaṃ ñatvā. Netaṃ mama nesohamasmi na meso attāti samanupassāmīti aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ anattāti samanupassāmi.
This, and so forth, beginning with "This is mine," are spoken due to craving, pride, views, and grasping.

"Having seen cessation" means having known destruction and disappearance.

"This is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self," thus contemplating impermanence (anitya), suffering (duḥkha), and selflessness (anātman), I contemplate.

392. ‘‘Cakkhusmiṃ, āvuso channa, cakkhuviññāṇe cakkhuviññāṇaviññātabbesu dhammesu kiṃ disvā kiṃ abhiññāya cakkhuṃ cakkhuviññāṇaṃ cakkhuviññāṇaviññātabbe dhamme ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti samanupassasi? Sotasmiṃ, āvuso channa, sotaviññāṇe … ghānasmiṃ, āvuso channa, ghānaviññāṇe… jivhāya, āvuso channa, jivhāviññāṇe… kāyasmiṃ, āvuso channa, kāyaviññāṇe… manasmiṃ, āvuso channa, manoviññāṇe manoviññāṇaviññātabbesu dhammesu kiṃ disvā kiṃ abhiññāya manaṃ manoviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaviññātabbe dhamme ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti samanupassasī’’ti? ‘‘Cakkhusmiṃ , āvuso sāriputta, cakkhuviññāṇe cakkhuviññāṇaviññātabbesu dhammesu nirodhaṃ disvā nirodhaṃ abhiññāya cakkhuṃ cakkhuviññāṇaṃ cakkhuviññāṇaviññātabbe dhamme ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti samanupassāmi. Sotasmiṃ, āvuso sāriputta, sotaviññāṇe… ghānasmiṃ, āvuso sāriputta, ghānaviññāṇe… jivhāya, āvuso sāriputta, jivhāviññāṇe… kāyasmiṃ, āvuso sāriputta, kāyaviññāṇe… manasmiṃ, āvuso sāriputta, manoviññāṇe manoviññāṇaviññātabbesu dhammesu nirodhaṃ disvā nirodhaṃ abhiññā manaṃ manoviññāṇaṃ manoviññāṇaviññātabbe dhamme ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti samanupassāmī’’ti.
“Friend Channa, what have you seen, what have you directly known in the eye, in eye-consciousness, and in things cognizable by the mind through eye-consciousness, that you regard them thus: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self’? In the ear…in the nose…in the tongue…in the body…in the mind, in mind-consciousness, and in things cognizable by the mind through mind-consciousness, what have you seen, what have you directly known, that you regard them thus: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self’?”

“Friend Sariputta, it is through seeing cessation, through directly knowing cessation in the eye, in eye-consciousness, and in things cognizable by the mind through eye-consciousness, that I regard them thus: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ In the ear…in the nose…in the tongue…in the body…in the mind, in mind-consciousness, and in things cognizable by the mind through mind-consciousness, it is through seeing cessation, through directly knowing cessation, that I regard them thus: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’”

Evaṃ vutte, āyasmā mahācundo āyasmantaṃ channaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘tasmātiha, āvuso channa, idampi tassa bhagavato sāsanaṃ vacanaṃ (sī.), niccakappaṃ manasi kātabbaṃ – ‘nissitassa calitaṃ, anissitassa calitaṃ natthi. Calite asati passaddhi, passaddhiyā sati nati na hoti. Natiyā asati āgatigati na hoti. Āgatigatiyā asati cutūpapāto na hoti. Cutūpapāte asati nevidha na huraṃ na ubhayamantarena. Esevanto dukkhassā’’’ti. Atha kho āyasmā ca sāriputto āyasmā ca mahācundo āyasmantaṃ channaṃ iminā ovādena ovaditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkamiṃsu.
When this was said, the venerable Mahācunda said to the venerable Channa: "Therefore, friend Channa, this instruction (śāsana) of the Blessed One is to be constantly given attention: ‘There is wavering in one who is dependent (niśrita), there is no wavering in one who is independent (aniśrita); When there is no wavering (calita), there is tranquility (prasrabdhi); when there is tranquility (prasrabdhi), there is no bias (nati); when there is no bias (nati), there is no coming and going (āgatigati); when there is no coming and going (āgatigati), there is no decease and reappearance (cyutyupapāta); when there is no decease and reappearance (cyutyupapāta), there is no here (nevidha) or beyond (na huraṃ) or in-between (na ubhayamantarena). This, just this, is the end of suffering (duḥkha).’”

Then the venerable Śāriputra and the venerable Mahācunda, having advised the venerable Channa with this instruction (ovāda), rose from their seats and departed.
393.Tasmāti yasmā māraṇantikavedanaṃ adhivāsetuṃ asakkonto satthaṃ āharāmīti vadati, tasmā. Puthujjano āyasmā, tena idampi manasi karohīti dīpeti. Niccakappanti niccakālaṃ. Nissitassāti taṇhādiṭṭhīhi nissitassa. Calitanti vipphanditaṃ hoti. Passaddhīti kāyacittapassaddhi, kilesapassaddhi nāma hotīti attho. Natīti taṇhānati. Natiyā asatīti bhavatthāya ālayanikantipariyuṭṭhānesu asati. Āgatigati na hotīti paṭisandhivasena āgati nāma na hoti, cutivasena gamanaṃ nāma na hoti. Cutūpapātoti cavanavasena cuti, upapajjanavasena upapāto. Nevidha na huraṃ na ubhayamantarenāti nayidha loke, na paraloke, na ubhayattha hoti. Esevanto dukkhassāti vaṭṭadukkhakilesadukkhassa ayameva anto ayaṃ paricchedo parivaṭumabhāvo hoti. Ayameva hi ettha attho. Ye pana ‘‘na ubhayamantarenā’’ti vacanaṃ gahetvā antarābhavaṃ icchanti, tesaṃ uttaraṃ heṭṭhā vuttameva.
393. "Therefore (tasmā)" [means:] Because one is unable to endure (adhivāsetuṃ asakkonto) agonizing pain (māraṇantikavedanaṃ), one says, "I will fetch (āharāmi) a weapon (satthaṃ)," therefore (tasmā).

The worthy (āyasmā) ordinary person (puthujjano), makes (dīpeti) this clear: "You should also bear this in mind (tena idampi manasi karohīti)."

"Constant appearance (niccakappa)" [means:] constant time (niccakālaṃ).

"Relying on (nissitassā)" [means:] relying on (nissitassa) craving (taṇhā) and views (diṭṭhīhi).

"Shaken (calita)" [means:] agitated (vipphanditaṃ) it is (hoti).

"Tranquility (passaddhī)" [means:] tranquility of body and mind (kāyacittapassaddhi); tranquility of defilements (kilesapassaddhi), this is called (nāma hoti), is the meaning (attho).

"Inclination (natī)" [means:] craving-inclination (taṇhānati).

"When inclination is not (natiyā asatī)" [means:] when there is no (asati) attachment (ālayanikantipariyuṭṭhānesu) to existence (bhavatthāya).

"There is no coming and going (āgatigati na hotī)" [means:] through the force (vasena) of rebirth (paṭisandhi), there is no (na hoti) such thing as coming (āgati nāma), and through the force (vasena) of decease (cutivasena), there is no (na hoti) such thing as going (gamanaṃ nāma).

"Decease and rebirth (cutūpapāto)" [means:] through the force (vasena) of dying (cavanavasena) there is decease (cuti), and through the force (vasena) of arising (upapajjanavasena), there is rebirth (upapāto).

"Neither here nor beyond nor in between both (nevidha na huraṃ na ubhayamantarenā)" [means:] not in this (nayidha) world (loke), not in the next world (na paraloke), not in either place (na ubhayattha) it is (hoti).

"This is the end of suffering (esevanto dukkhassā)" [means:] this itself (ayameva) is the end (anto), this (ayaṃ) is the limit (paricchedo) of the suffering of the cycle (vaṭṭadukkha), the suffering of defilements (kilesadukkhassa), there is no possibility of circumvention (parivaṭumabhāvo hoti).

This itself (ayameva hi) is the meaning (attho) here (ettha).

But (pana) those who, taking (gahetvā) the statement (vacanaṃ) "neither in between both (na ubhayamantarenā)," desire (icchanti) an intermediate existence (antarābhavaṃ), the answer (uttaraṃ) to them (tesaṃ) has already been stated (heṭṭhā vuttameva).

394. Then, soon after the venerable Sāriputta and the venerable Mahācunda had departed, the venerable Channa used the knife (satthaṃ āharesi).

Then the venerable Sāriputta went to the Blessed One (bhagavā); having approached, paid homage to the Blessed One, and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, the venerable Sāriputta said this to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir (bhante), Channa has used the knife (satthaṃ āharitaṃ)."

"What is his destiny (gati)? What is his future state (abhisamparāyo)?"

"Sāriputta, was not blamelessness (anupavajjatā) declared by the monk (bhikkhunā) Channa himself in your presence (sammukhāyeva)?"

"Venerable sir (bhante), there is a Vajjian village (vajjigāmo) named Pubbajira.

There, the venerable Channa had families that were his friends (mittakulāni), families that were his intimates (suhajjakulāni), families that were blameworthy (upavajjakulāni)."

"There are, Sāriputta, families that were friends (mittakulāni) of the monk (bhikkhuno) Channa, families that were his intimates (suhajjakulāni), families that were blameworthy (upavajjakulāni).

But I do not say, Sāriputta, that by this alone one is 'blameworthy' (saupavajjo).

Sāriputta, whoever lays down this body (kāyaṃ nikkhipati) and takes up another body (kāyaṃ upādiyati), him I call 'blameworthy' (saupavajjo)."

That was not the case with the monk (bhikkhuno) Channa.

'The monk Channa used the knife blamelessly (anupavajjo channo bhikkhu satthaṃ āharesī),' thus, Sāriputta, you should remember (dhārehīti)."

The Blessed One (bhagavā) spoke these words.

The venerable Sāriputta was pleased (attamano) and delighted (abhinandīti) in the Blessed One's words (bhāsitaṃ).

The Channovādasutta, the second, is finished.
394.Satthaṃ āharesīti jīvitahārakaṃ satthaṃ āhari, kaṇṭhanāḷiṃ chindi. Athassa tasmiṃ khaṇe maraṇabhayaṃ okkami, gatinimittaṃ upaṭṭhāsi. So attano puthujjanabhāvaṃ ñatvā saṃviggo vipassanaṃ paṭṭhapetvā saṅkhāre pariggaṇhanto arahattaṃ patvā samasīsī hutvā parinibbāyi. Sammukhāyeva anupavajjatā byākatāti kiñcāpi idaṃ therassa puthujjanakāle byākaraṇaṃ hoti, etena pana byākaraṇena anantarāyamassa parinibbānaṃ ahosi. Tasmā bhagavā tameva byākaraṇaṃ gahetvā kathesi. Upavajjakulānīti upasaṅkamitabbakulāni. Iminā thero, – ‘‘bhante, evaṃ upaṭṭhākesu ca upaṭṭhāyikāsu ca vijjamānāsu so bhikkhu tumhākaṃ sāsane parinibbāyissatī’’ti pucchati. Athassa bhagavā kulesu saṃsaggābhāvaṃ dīpento honti hete sāriputtātiādimāha. Imasmiṃ kira ṭhāne therassa kulesu asaṃsaṭṭhabhāvo pākaṭo ahosi. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Channovādasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
394. "He would bring a knife (satthaṃ āharesī)," meaning he brought a knife that takes away life (jīvitahārakaṃ satthaṃ āhari), he cut the windpipe (kaṇṭhanāḷiṃ chindi).

Then at that moment (tasmiṃ khaṇe), fear of death (maraṇabhayaṃ) overcame him, and the sign of his (future) destiny (gatinimittaṃ) appeared.

Having known his state as an ordinary person (attano puthujjanabhāvaṃ ñatvā), he became agitated (saṃviggo), established insight (vipassanaṃ paṭṭhapetvā), apprehended the mental formations (saṅkhāre pariggaṇhanto), attained arahantship (arahattaṃ patvā), became one whose head is level (samasīsī hutvā), and attained final nirvāṇa (parinibbāyi).

"Perfect purity is declared face to face (sammukhāyeva anupavajjatā byākatā)," although this declaration (byākaraṇaṃ) of the Elder (therassa) occurred at the time of (his) ordinary person-hood (puthujjanakāle), by this declaration (byākaraṇena) his (assa) final nirvāṇa (parinibbānaṃ) was unobstructed (anantarāyaṃ ahosi).

Therefore (tasmā), the Blessed One (bhagavā), having taken that very declaration (tameva byākaraṇaṃ gahetvā), spoke (kathesi).

"Families subject to censure (upavajjakulānī)" means families that should be avoided (upasaṅkamitabbakulāni).

With this (iminā) the Elder (thero) asks, "Venerable sir (bhante), thus (evaṃ), while supporters (upaṭṭhākesu) and female supporters (upaṭṭhāyikāsu) are present (vijjamānāsu), will that bhikkhu (so bhikkhu) attain final nirvāṇa (parinibbāyissati) in your (tumhākaṃ) dispensation (sāsane)?" (pucchati).

Then (atha), the Blessed One (bhagavā), revealing the absence of association (saṃsaggābhāvaṃ dīpento) with families (kulesu), said beginning with "These are (honti hete) Sāriputtas (sāriputtā)" (itiādimāha).

It is said (kira) that in this place (imasmiṃ ṭhāne), the Elder's (therassa) state of non-association (asaṃsaṭṭhabhāvo) with families (kulesu) was evident (pākaṭo ahosi).

The rest (sesaṃ) is clear (uttānam) throughout (sabbattha).

The Channovādasutta Commentary (channovādasuttavaṇṇanā) is finished (niṭṭhitā) in the Papañcasūdanī, the commentary to the Majjhimanikāya (papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya).
