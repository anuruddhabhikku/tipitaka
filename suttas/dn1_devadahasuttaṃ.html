Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘santi, bhikkhave, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘yaṃ kiñcāyaṃ purisapuggalo paṭisaṃvedeti sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā, sabbaṃ taṃ pubbekatahetu. Iti purāṇānaṃ kammānaṃ tapasā byantībhāvā, navānaṃ kammānaṃ akaraṇā, āyatiṃ anavassavo; āyatiṃ anavassavā kammakkhayo; kammakkhayā <pb ed="V" n="3.0002"/> dukkhakkhayo; dukkhakkhayā vedanākkhayo; vedanākkhayā sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī’ti. Evaṃvādino, bhikkhave, nigaṇṭhā.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Evaṃvādāhaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0002"/>, bhikkhave, nigaṇṭhe upasaṅkamitvā evaṃ vadāmi – ‘saccaṃ kira tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – yaṃ kiñcāyaṃ purisapuggalo paṭisaṃvedeti sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā, sabbaṃ taṃ pubbekatahetu. Iti purāṇānaṃ kammānaṃ tapasā byantībhāvā, navānaṃ kammānaṃ akaraṇā, āyatiṃ anavassavo; āyatiṃ anavassavā kammakkhayo; kammakkhayā dukkhakkhayo; dukkhakkhayā vedanākkhayo; vedanākkhayā sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī’ti? Te ca me, bhikkhave, nigaṇṭhā evaṃ puṭṭhā ‘āmā’ti paṭijānanti.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0002"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Tyāhaṃ <pb ed="M" n="3.0002"/> evaṃ vadāmi – ‘kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – ahuvamheva mayaṃ pubbe, na nāhuvamhā’ti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’.

<pb ed="M" n="3.0002"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘‘Kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – akaramheva mayaṃ pubbe pāpakammaṃ, na nākaramhā’ti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘‘Kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – evarūpaṃ vā evarūpaṃ vā pāpakammaṃ akaramhā’ti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘‘Kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – ettakaṃ vā dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ, ettakaṃ vā dukkhaṃ nijjīretabbaṃ, ettakamhi vā dukkhe nijjiṇṇe sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī’ti? ‘No <pb ed="P" n="2.0215"/> hidaṃ, āvuso’.

<pb ed="P" n="2.0215"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘‘Kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – diṭṭheva dhamme akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānaṃ, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ upasampada’nti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling among the Sakyans where there was a town of the Sakyans named Devadaha.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks (saying): "Monks."

"Venerable, sir," they replied.

The Blessed One said this: "Bhikkhus, there are some recluses and brāhmaṇas who hold such a doctrine and view as this: 'Whatever this person feels, whether pleasure or pain or neither-pain-nor-pleasure, all that is caused by what was done in the past ( pubbekatahetu).

So by annihilating with asceticism past actions (purāṇānaṃ kammānaṃ tapasā byantībhāvā) and by doing no fresh actions (navānaṃ kammānaṃ akaraṇā), there will be no consequence in the future (āyatiṃ anavassavo); with no consequence in the future (āyatiṃ anavassavā), there is the destruction of action (kammakkhayo); with the destruction of action (kammakkhayā), there is the destruction of suffering ( dukkhakkhayo); with the destruction of suffering ( dukkhakkhayā), there is the destruction of feeling (vedanākkhayo); with the destruction of feeling (vedanākkhayā), all suffering will be exhausted (sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī).' So speak the Niganthas (nigaṇṭhā), bhikkhus.

"I go to the Niganthas (nigaṇṭhe) who speak thus and I say: 'Friend Niganthas (āvuso nigaṇṭhā), is it true that you hold such a doctrine and view as this: “Whatever this person feels, whether pleasure or pain or neither-pain-nor-pleasure, all that is caused by what was done in the past (pubbekatahetu). So by annihilating with asceticism past actions (purāṇānaṃ kammānaṃ tapasā byantībhāvā) and by doing no fresh actions (navānaṃ kammānaṃ akaraṇā), there will be no consequence in the future (āyatiṃ anavassavo); with no consequence in the future (āyatiṃ anavassavā), there is the destruction of action (kammakkhayo); with the destruction of action (kammakkhayā), there is the destruction of suffering ( dukkhakkhayo); with the destruction of suffering ( dukkhakkhayā), there is the destruction of feeling (vedanākkhayo); with the destruction of feeling (vedanākkhayā), all suffering will be exhausted (sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī)'?” And when I ask the Niganthas (nigaṇṭhā) this, they acknowledge it to be so (āmā’ti paṭijānanti).

“Then I say this: ‘But, friends Niganthas (āvuso nigaṇṭhā), do you know that you existed in the past, and that it is not the case that you did not exist (ahuvamheva mayaṃ pubbe, na nāhuvamhā’ti)?’ ‘No, friend (no hidaṃ, āvuso).’

“‘But, friends Niganthas (āvuso nigaṇṭhā), do you know that you did evil actions in the past and did not abstain from them (akaramheva mayaṃ pubbe pāpakammaṃ, na nākaramhā’ti)?’ ‘No, friend (no hidaṃ, āvuso).’

“‘But, friends Niganthas (āvuso nigaṇṭhā), do you know that you did such and such evil actions (evarūpaṃ vā evarūpaṃ vā pāpakammaṃ akaramhā’ti)?’ ‘No, friend (no hidaṃ, āvuso).’

“‘But, friends Niganthas (āvuso nigaṇṭhā), do you know that so much suffering has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering has been exhausted all suffering will have been exhausted (ettakaṃ vā dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ, ettakaṃ vā dukkhaṃ nijjīretabbaṃ, ettakamhi vā dukkhe nijjiṇṇe sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī’ti)?’ ‘No, friend (no hidaṃ, āvuso).’

“‘But, friends Niganthas (āvuso nigaṇṭhā), do you know that in this very life there is the abandoning of unwholesome states and the attainment of wholesome states (diṭṭheva dhamme akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānaṃ, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ upasampada’nti)?’ ‘No, friend (no hidaṃ, āvuso).’”

2. “So, friends, it seems that you do not know that you existed in the past and that it is not the case that you did not exist; or that you did evil actions (pāpakamma) in the past and did not abstain from them; or that you did such and such evil actions; or that so much suffering (dukkha) has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering (dukkha) has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering (dukkha) has been exhausted all suffering (dukkha) will have been exhausted; or what the abandoning of unwholesome states is and what the cultivation of wholesome states is; that being so, it is not fitting for the venerable Niganthas to declare: “Whatever this person feels, whether pleasure (sukha) or pain (dukkha) or neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukha), all that is caused by what was done in the past (pūrbekatahetu)."

With no consequence in the future, there is the destruction of action (karmakkhayo). With the destruction of action (karmakkhayo), there is the destruction of suffering (dukkhakkhayo). With the destruction of suffering (dukkhakkhayo), there is the destruction of feeling (vedanākhayo). With the destruction of feeling (vedanākhayo), all suffering (dukkha) will be exhausted.”

3. “Just as, friend Nigaṇṭhas, suppose a man were wounded by an arrow thickly smeared with poison; because of being pierced by the arrow, he would feel painful, racking, bitter sensations. His friends and companions, his kinsmen and relatives, would bring a physician, a surgeon, to treat him. The surgeon would cut around the opening of the wound with a knife; because of cutting around the opening of the wound with the knife, he would feel painful, racking, bitter sensations. The surgeon would probe for the arrow with a probe; because of probing for the arrow with the probe, he would feel painful, racking, bitter sensations.
The surgeon would extract the arrow; because of extracting the arrow, he would feel painful, racking, bitter sensations. The surgeon would apply a medicinal cauterizer to the opening of the wound; because of applying the medicinal cauterizer to the opening of the wound, he would feel painful, racking, bitter sensations. Then on a later occasion, when the wound was healed and covered with skin, the man would be well and happy, independent, master of himself, able to go where he likes. He might think: “Formerly I was pierced by an arrow thickly smeared with poison. And because of being pierced by the arrow, I felt painful, racking, bitter sensations. Then my friends and companions, kinsmen and relatives, brought a physician, a surgeon, to treat me. The surgeon cut around the opening of the wound with a knife; because of cutting around the opening of the wound with the knife, I felt painful, racking, bitter sensations. The surgeon probed for the arrow with a probe; because of probing for the arrow with the probe, I felt painful, racking, bitter sensations. The surgeon extracted the arrow; because of extracting the arrow, I felt painful, racking, bitter sensations. The surgeon applied a medicinal cauterizer to the opening of the wound; because of applying the medicinal cauterizer to the opening of the wound, I felt painful, racking, bitter sensations. But now I am healed, the wound closed with skin, healthy, happy, independent, master of myself, able to go where I like.”

“In the same way, friend Nigaṇṭhas, if you knew that you existed in the past and that it is not the case that you did not exist, or that you did evil actions in the past and did not abstain from them, or that you did such and such evil actions, or that so much suffering has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering has been exhausted all suffering will have been exhausted, or what the abandoning of unwholesome states is and what the cultivation of wholesome states is here and now; that being so, it would be fitting for the venerable Nigaṇṭhas to declare: “Whatever this person feels, whether pleasant or painful or neither-painful-nor-pleasant, all that is caused by what was done in the past.” So by annihilating with asceticism past actions and by doing no fresh actions, there will be no consequence in the future. With no consequence in the future, there is the destruction of action. With the destruction of action, there is the destruction of suffering. With the destruction of suffering, there is the destruction of feeling (vedanā). With the destruction of feeling, all suffering will be exhausted."
“But since you, friends, the Niganthas, do not know that you existed in the past and that it is not the case that you did not exist; or that you did evil actions in the past and did not abstain from them; or that you did such and such evil actions; or that so much suffering (duḥkha) has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering has been exhausted all suffering will be exhausted; or what the abandoning of unwholesome dharmas is and what the attainment (upasampadā) of wholesome dharmas is here and now, it is not fitting for the venerable Niganthas to declare: “Whatever this person feels, whether it is pleasure (sukha) or suffering (duḥkha) or neither-pleasure-nor-suffering (adukkhamasukha), all that is due to previous actions.”

Thus, by annihilating past actions (karma) through asceticism (tapas), and by not doing new actions (karma), there will be no influx in the future; with no influx in the future, there is the destruction of action (karmakkhaya); with the destruction of action, there is the destruction of suffering (duḥkhakkhaya); with the destruction of suffering, there is the destruction of feeling (vedanā); with the destruction of feeling, all suffering (duḥkha) will be exhausted."

10. Now, how, monks, is effort fruitful, and striving fruitful? Here, monks, a monk does not overwhelm himself with suffering (duḥkha), nor overwhelm himself by means of suffering; he does not abandon the pleasure that accords with the Dhamma (dharmika), and he is not infatuated with that pleasure.

He strives with determination in regard to that particular source of suffering which fades away in him because of that determined striving; he strives with determination there.

and he develops equanimity in regard to that particular source of suffering which fades away in him while he is developing equanimity.

When he strives with determination, such and such a source of suffering fades away in him because of that determined striving; thus that suffering is exhausted in him.

When he looks oh with equanimity, such and such a source of suffering fades away in him while he develops equanimity; thus that suffering is exhausted in him.

Evaṃvādī, bhikkhave, tathāgatā. Evaṃvādīnaṃ, bhikkhave, tathāgatānaṃ <note>tathāgato, evaṃvādiṃ bhikkhave tathāgataṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note> dasa sahadhammikā pāsaṃsaṭṭhānā āgacchanti.

“When his mind is thus concentrated ( samāhite citte ), purified ( parisuddhe ), and bright ( pariyodāte ), unblemished ( anaṅgaṇe ), free from defects ( vigatūpakkilese ), pliant ( mudubhūte ), malleable ( kammaniye ), steady ( ṭhite ), and attained to imperturbability ( āneñjappatte ), the monk directs and inclines it to the knowledge of the ending of the āsavas.

He understands as it actually is: ‘This is suffering ( idaṃ dukkha )’……He understands as it actually is: ‘This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering ( dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā )’...He understands as it actually is: ‘These are the taints ( āsavas )’…He understands as it actually is: ‘This is the way leading to the cessation of the taints ( āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā ).’

When he knows and sees thus, his mind is liberated from the taint of sensual desire ( kāmāsava ), from the taint of being ( bhavāsava ), and from taint of ignorance ( avijjāsava ).

When it is liberated, there comes the knowledge: ‘It’s liberated ( vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti ).’

He understands: ‘Destroyed is birth ( khīṇā jāti ), the spiritual life has been lived ( vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ ), what had to be done has been done ( kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ ), there is no more coming back to any state of being ( nāparaṃ itthattāya ).’

Thus, bhikkhus, the exertion (upakkamo) is fruitful ( saphala ), the striving ( padhānaṃ ) is fruitful ( saphalaṃ ).

“So the Tathāgata speaks, bhikkhus.

"If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of what was done in the past, then surely, monks, the tathāgata must have done good deeds in the past, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of the creative act of a Supreme God (īśvara), then surely, monks, the tathāgata must have been created by a good Supreme God, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of circumstance and nature, then the tathāgata surely must have good luck, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of class among the six classes of birth, then the tathāgata surely must belong to a good class, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of exertion here and now, then the tathāgata surely must strive well here and now, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings.

"If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of what was done in the past, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if beings do not feel pleasure and pain because of what was done in the past, then the tathāgata is still to be praised. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of the creative act of a Supreme God, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if beings do not feel pleasure and pain because of the creative act of a Supreme God, then the tathāgata is still to be praised. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of circumstance and nature, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if beings do not feel pleasure and pain because of circumstance and nature, then the tathāgata is still to be praised. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of class, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if beings do not feel pleasure and pain because of class, then the tathāgata is still to be praised. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain because of exertion here and now, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if beings do not feel pleasure and pain because of exertion here and now, then the tathāgata is still to be praised.”

“So the tathāgatas speak, monks. And because the tathāgatas speak thus, there are these ten legitimate grounds for praising them.”

Thus spoke the Lord. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.
