<p n="197" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">197</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0246"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0161"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho sambahulānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkantānaṃ upaṭṭhānasālāyaṃ sannisinnānaṃ sannipatitānaṃ ayamantarākathā udapādi – ‘‘acchariyaṃ, āvuso, abbhutaṃ, āvuso, tathāgatassa mahiddhikatā mahānubhāvatā, yatra hi nāma tathāgato <pb ed="M" n="3.0160"/> atīte buddhe parinibbute chinnapapañce chinnavaṭume pariyādinnavaṭṭe sabbadukkhavītivatte jānissati <note>anussarissati jānissati (ka.)</note> – ‘evaṃjaccā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ’ itipi, ‘evaṃnāmā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ’ itipi, ‘evaṃgottā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ’ itipi, ‘evaṃsīlā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ’ itipi, ‘evaṃdhammā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ’ itipi, ‘evaṃpaññā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ’ itipi, ‘evaṃvihārī te bhagavanto ahesuṃ’ itipi, ‘evaṃvimuttā te bhagavanto ahesuṃ’ itipī’’ti! Evaṃ vutte, āyasmā ānando te bhikkhū etadavoca – ‘‘acchariyā ceva, āvuso, tathāgatā acchariyadhammasamannāgatā ca; abbhutā ceva, āvuso, tathāgatā abbhutadhammasamannāgatā cā’’ti. Ayañca <pb ed="P" n="3.0119"/> hidaṃ tesaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ antarākathā vippakatā hoti.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was staying at Sāvatthi in the Jetavana in Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park (ārāma).

Then, after their meal, on returning from their alms round, a number of monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) were sitting together, gathered in the service hall, and this discussion arose among them: “It is wonderful (acchariyaṃ), friends (āvuso), it is marvelous (abbhutaṃ), friends, how mighty is the Tathāgata (tathāgatassa), how great his power (mahānubhāvatā), that he can know of past Buddhas (buddhe), who have attained final Nirvāṇa (parinibbute), cut off delusion (chinnapapañce), destroyed the cycle, ended the round, and passed beyond all suffering (sabbadukkhavītivatte)—[he knows] that those Blessed Ones (bhagavanto) were of such a birth (evaṃjaccā), such a name (evaṃnāmā), such a lineage (evaṃgottā), such virtue (evaṃsīlā), such teaching (evaṃdhammā), such wisdom (evaṃpaññā), such a dwelling (evaṃvihārī), and such liberation (evaṃvimuttā)!” When this was said, venerable Ānanda (āyasmā ānando) said to the monks (bhikkhū): “The Tathāgatas (tathāgatā) are truly wonderful (acchariyā), friends, and endowed with wonderful qualities (acchariyadhammasamannāgatā); the Tathāgatas are truly marvelous (abhutā), friends, and endowed with marvelous qualities (abhutadhammasamannāgatā.”

And this conversation of those monks was interrupted (antarākathā vippakatā hoti).

Evaṃ vutte, bhante, āyasmā ānando amhe etadavoca – ‘acchariyā ceva, āvuso, tathāgatā acchariyadhammasamannāgatā ca, abbhutā <pb ed="V" n="3.0162"/> ceva, āvuso, tathāgatā abbhutadhammasamannāgatā cā’ti. Ayaṃ kho no, bhante, antarākathā vippakatā; atha bhagavā anuppatto’’ti.

Then the Blessed One, arising from seclusion in the evening, approached the service hall; having approached, he sat down on the appointed seat. Seated, the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: “With what discussion are you assembled here now, monks? And what was the discussion that was interrupted?”

“Here, venerable sir, after our meal, returning from alms round, gathering together in the service hall, this was the discussion that arose: ‘Amazing, friends, astonishing, friends, is the great power and great glory of the Tathāgata, in that the Tathāgata knows about the past Buddhas who have attained parinirvāṇa, cut off proliferation, broken the round, finished the cycle, and gone beyond all suffering—that those Blessed Ones were of such a birth, of such a name, of such a clan, of such virtue, of such a dharma, of such wisdom, of such a dwelling, and of such liberation!’ When this was said, venerable sir, the venerable Ānanda said this to us: ‘Amazing, friends, are the Tathāgatas, endowed with amazing qualities; astonishing, friends, are the Tathāgatas, endowed with astonishing qualities.’ This, venerable sir, was the discussion that was interrupted when the Blessed One arrived.”

"Bhante, I have heard this directly from the Blessed One, and received it directly: ‘When, Ānanda, the bodhisattva passed away from the Tusita heaven and descended into his mother’s womb, then in the world with its gods (sadevake loke), its Māras (samārake), and its Brahmās (sabrahmake), in this generation with its ascetics (sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā) and Brahmins, with its princes and its people (sadevamanussāya), a great immeasurable light (appamāṇo uḷāro obhāso) surpassing the splendour of the gods appeared in the world (loke pātubhavati atikkammeva devānaṃ devānubhāvaṃ).

And even in those abysmal world interspaces (lokantarikā) of vacancy (aghā), gloom (asaṃvutā), and utter darkness (andhakārā andhakāratimisā), where even the moon and the sun (candimasūriyā), so mighty and splendid (evaṃmahiddhikā evaṃmahānubhāvā), do not shine with their light (ābhāya nānubhonti)—there too a great immeasurable light (appamāṇo uḷāro obhāso) surpassing the splendour of the gods appeared in the world (loke pātubhavati atikkammeva devānaṃ devānubhāvaṃ).

And those beings that have been reborn there (ye tattha sattā upapannā) recognise each other by this light (tepi tenobhāsena aññamaññaṃ sañjānanti) and know: "Other beings, too, have been born here!" (aññepi kira, bho, santi sattā idhūpapannāti).

And this ten-thousandfold world system (dasa-sahassī lokadhātu) shook and quaked and trembled (saṅkampati sampakampati sampavedhati), and there too a great immeasurable light (appamāṇo ca uḷāro obhāso) surpassing the splendour of the gods appeared in the world (loke pātubhavati atikkammeva devānaṃ devānubhāva’nti).

And also, bhante, I remember this as a wonderful and marvellous quality (acchariyaṃ abbhutadhammaṃ dhāremi) of the Blessed One (bhagavato)."

203. “I heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s own lips: ‘When the Bodhisattva had descended into his mother’s womb, she became intrinsically virtuous, refraining from killing living beings (prāṇātipātā), from taking what is not given (adinnādānā), from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā), from false speech (musāvādā), and from wines, liquors, and intoxicants, which are the basis of negligence (surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā).’ This too, reverend sir, I remember as a wonderful and marvellous quality of the Blessed One.”

204. “Venerable, I have heard and learned this from the Blessed One's own lips: ‘When, Ānanda, a bodhisattva has descended into his mother's womb (mātukucchiṃ okkanto hoti), no kind of affliction arises in the bodhisattva's mother; the bodhisattva's mother is blissful and free from bodily fatigue. The bodhisattva's mother sees the bodhisattva within her womb with all his limbs, lacking no faculty (sabbaṅgapaccaṅgaṃ ahīnindriyaṃ).

Suppose, Ānanda, there were a beautiful beryl gem (maṇi veḷuriyo subho) of purest water, eight-faceted, well cut (aṭṭhaṃso suparikammakato). And through it there would run a blue, yellow, red, white or brown thread (nīlaṃ vā pītaṃ vā lohitaṃ vā odātaṃ vā paṇḍusuttaṃ vā). Then a man with good sight, taking it in his hand, might review it thus: “This is a beautiful beryl gem of purest water, eight-faceted, well cut, and through it is strung a blue, yellow, red, white, or brown thread."

Even so, Ānanda, when a bodhisattva has descended into his mother's womb, no kind of affliction arises in the bodhisattva's mother; the bodhisattva's mother is blissful and free from bodily fatigue; the bodhisattva's mother sees the bodhisattva within her womb with all his limbs, lacking no faculty.’

That when, venerable... and so on... venerable, I hold this as a wonderful and marvelous quality of the Blessed One (acchariyaṃ abbhutadhammaṃ dhāremi).

205. “I heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s own lips: ‘Seven days after the birth of the Bodhisattva, his mother died and reappeared in the Tuṣita heaven.’ That when the Bodhisattva came forth from his mother’s womb, then a great immeasurable light surpassing the splendour of the gods appeared…this too I remember as a wonderful and marvellous quality of the Blessed One.”

“I heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s own lips: ‘Other women give birth after carrying the child in the womb for nine or ten months, but not so the Bodhisattva's mother. The Bodhisattva's mother gave birth after carrying him in her womb for exactly ten months.’ That when the Bodhisattva came forth from his mother’s womb, then a great immeasurable light surpassing the splendour of the gods appeared…this too I remember as a wonderful and marvellous quality of the Blessed One.”

“I heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s own lips: ‘Other women give birth seated or lying down, but not so the Bodhisattva's mother. The Bodhisattva's mother gave birth to him standing up.’ That when the Bodhisattva came forth from his mother’s womb, then a great immeasurable light surpassing the splendour of the gods appeared…this too I remember as a wonderful and marvellous quality of the Blessed One.”

“I heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s own lips: ‘When the Bodhisattva came forth from his mother’s womb, first gods received him, then human beings.’ That when the Bodhisattva came forth from his mother’s womb, then a great immeasurable light surpassing the splendour of the gods appeared…this too I remember as a wonderful and marvellous quality of the Blessed One.”

“Bhante, I have heard this from the Blessed One's own lips, I have received it directly: ‘When, Ānanda, a Bodhisattva emerges from his mother's womb, before he even touches the earth, the four young gods receive him and place him before his mother, saying, "Rejoice, O queen! “Bhante, I have heard this from the Blessed One's own lips, I have received it directly: ‘When, Ānanda, a Bodhisattva emerges from his mother's womb, he emerges pure, unsullied by water, unsullied by mucus, unsullied by blood, unsullied by any impurity, clean and pure.’
Suppose, Ānanda, a jewel is placed on Kāśī cloth; neither does the jewel stain the Kāśī cloth, nor does the Kāśī cloth stain the jewel.
What is the reason for that? Because both are pure.
In the same way, Ānanda, when a Bodhisattva emerges from his mother's womb, he emerges pure, unsullied by water, unsullied by mucus, unsullied by blood, unsullied by any impurity, clean and pure.”
Also, Bhante…pe… this I, Bhante, remember as a wonderful and marvelous quality of the Blessed One.

“Bhante, I have heard this from the Blessed One's own lips, I have received it directly: ‘When, Ānanda, a Bodhisattva emerges from his mother's womb, two streams of water appear from the sky—one cool, one warm—with which they perform the water rituals for the Bodhisattva and his mother.’”
Also, Bhante…pe… this I, Bhante, remember as a wonderful and marvelous quality of the Blessed One.

207. “Bhante, I have heard and learned this directly from the Blessed One's own lips: 'As soon as the bodhisattva was born, Ānanda, he stood firmly with both feet on the ground; then he took seven steps north, and with a white parasol held over him, he surveyed all directions, uttering the voice of a leader: “I am the highest in the world; I am the best in the world; I am the foremost in the world.”

This is my last birth; now there is no continuation of existence ( नत्थि दानि पुनब्भवोऽति ).’

Bhante, I remember this as a wonderful and marvelous quality of the Blessed One.

“Bhante, I have heard and learned this directly from the Blessed One's own lips: 'When the bodhisattva emerged from his mother’s womb ( mātukucchimhā nikkhamati ), then an immeasurable, splendid light appeared in the world with its gods, its Māras, and its Brahmās, in this generation with its ascetics and brāhmaṇas, with its princes and its people, surpassing the divine majesty of the gods ( devānaṃ devānubhāvaṃ ).

And even in those abysmal world interspaces of vacancy, gloom, and utter darkness, where the moon and the sun, mighty and powerful as they are, cannot make their light prevail - there too an immeasurable, splendid light appeared in the world surpassing the divine majesty of the gods ( devānaṃ devānubhāvaṃ ).

And those beings who have been reborn there recognise each other by this light and know: “Other beings, too, have been born here!”

And this ten-thousandfold world system shook and quaked and trembled ( ayañca dasasahassī lokadhātu saṅkampati sampakampati sampavedhati ), and an immeasurable, splendid light appeared in the world surpassing the divine majesty of the gods ( devānaṃ devānubhāvaṃ ).’

Bhante, I remember this as a wonderful and marvelous quality of the Blessed One."

208. “Therefore, Ānanda, remember this too as a wonderful and marvellous quality of the Tathāgata:

Here, Ānanda, for the Tathāgata feelings (vedanā) are known as they arise, known as they are present, known as they disappear; perceptions (saṃjñā) are known as they arise, known as they are present, known as they disappear; thoughts (vitarka) are known as they arise, known as they are present, known as they disappear.

Remember this too, Ānanda, as a wonderful and marvellous quality of the Tathāgata.”

“Venerable sir, since for the Blessed One feelings (vedanā) are known as they arise, as they are present, as they disappear; perceptions (saṃjñā)… thoughts (vitarka) are known as they arise, as they are present, as they disappear.

this too I remember as a wonderful and marvellous quality of the Blessed One.”

This is what Āyasmā Ānanda said.

The Teacher was in agreement; and those monks, being delighted, rejoiced at the words of Āyasmato Ānanda.

The Wonderful and Marvellous Discourse is concluded, the third.
