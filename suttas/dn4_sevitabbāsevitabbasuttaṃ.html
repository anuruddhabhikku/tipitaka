Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Anāthapindika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Sāvatthi.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks (saying): “Monks!”

“Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Fortunate One.

The Fortunate One said this: “Bhikkhus, I shall teach you a discourse on what should be cultivated and what should not be cultivated.

Listen & pay close attention. I will speak.”

‘Yes, Lord’, said the monks,

The Blessed One said this:

“Monks, I say that bodily conduct is of two kinds: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated; and bodily conduct is either the one or the other.

Monks, I say that verbal conduct is of two kinds: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated; and verbal conduct is either the one or the other.

Monks, I say that mental conduct is of two kinds: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated; and mental conduct is either the one or the other.

Monks, I say that inclination of mind is of two kinds: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated; and inclination of mind is either the one or the other.

Monks, I say that the acquisition of perception (saṃjñāpratilambha) is of two kinds: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated; and the acquisition of perception is either the one or the other.

Monks, I say that the acquisition of view is of two kinds: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated. And the acquisition of view is either the one or the other.

Monks, I say that the acquisition of individuality is of two kinds: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated. And the acquisition of individuality is either the one or the other.”

When this was said, the venerable Sāriputta said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, I understand the detailed meaning of the Blessed One’s utterance, which he has spoken in brief without expounding the detailed meaning, to be thus:

111. “And what kind of bodily conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to increase and wholesome states to diminish in one who cultivates it? Here, venerable sir, someone kills living beings; he is murderous, bloody-handed, given to blows and violence, merciless to living beings. He takes what is not given; he takes by way of theft the wealth and property of others in the village or in the forest. He misconducts himself in sensual pleasures; he has intercourse with such women as are protected by their mother, father, mother and father, brother, sister, or relatives, who have a husband, who are protected by law, and even with those who are garlanded – such bodily conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to increase and wholesome states to diminish in one who cultivates it.

“And what kind of bodily conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to diminish and wholesome states to increase in one who cultivates it? Here, venerable sir, someone, abandoning the taking of life (prāṇātipāta), refrains from taking life. He has laid down the stick and the sword, is conscientious, kind, and dwells full of compassion for all living beings. Abandoning the taking of what is not given (adinnādāna), he refrains from taking what is not given, that is, the wealth and property of others in the village or in the forest. Abandoning sensual misconduct (kāmesumicchācāra), he abstains from sensual misconduct. He does not get sexually involved with those who are protected by their mothers, their fathers, their brothers, their sisters, their relatives, or their Dhamma; those with husbands, those who entail punishments, or even those crowned with flowers by another man - such bodily conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to diminish and wholesome states to increase in one who cultivates it.

“Bhikkhus, bodily conduct (kāyasamācāra) is of two kinds, I say: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated. And bodily conduct is either the one or the other.’ So it was with reference to this that it was said by the Blessed One.

112. “Venerable sir, what kind of verbal conduct (vacīsamācāra) causes unwholesome states (akusalā dhammā) to increase and wholesome states (kusalā dhammā) to diminish? Here, venerable sir, a certain person is a liar. When summoned to a court (sabhāgato) or to a meeting (parisāgato) or to his relatives’ presence (ñātimajjhagato) or to his guild (pūgamajjhagato) or to the royal family’s presence (rājakulamajjhagato), and questioned as a witness thus: ‘So, good man (purisa), tell what you know,’ not knowing, he says, ‘I know,’ or knowing, he says, ‘I do not know’; not seeing, he says, ‘I see,’ or seeing, he says, ‘I do not see’; he knowingly speaks falsehood for his own ends (attahetu) or for another’s ends (parahetu) or for some trifling worldly end (āmisakiñcikkhahetu). He speaks maliciously (pisuṇavāco); having heard something here, he repeats it elsewhere in order to divide those people from these; or having heard something there, he repeats it to these people in order to divide these people from those; thus he is one who rejoices in discord, delights in discord, is attached to discord and speaks words that create discord, or one who breaks up those who are united, or does not promote friendship between those who are divided. He speaks harshly (pharusavāco); his speech is prickly (kaṇḍakā), rough (kakkasā), harsh (pharusā), bitter (parakaṭukā), offensive (parābhisajjanī), connected with anger (kodhasāmantā), and unconducive to concentration (asamādhisaṃvattanikā); he speaks such speech. He is a gossip (samphappalāpī), speaking out of season (akālavādī), speaking what is untrue (abhūtavādī), speaking what is unbeneficial (anatthavādī), speaking what is against the Dharma (adhammavādī), speaking what is against the Discipline (avinayavādī), he speaks speech that is without a foundation (anidhānavatiṃ vācaṃ), out of season (akālena), without a valid reason (anapadesaṃ), unbounded (apariyantavatiṃ), and connected with what is unbeneficial (anatthasaṃhitaṃ). This kind of verbal conduct (vacīsamācāraṃ) causes unwholesome states (akusalā dhammā) to increase and wholesome states (kusalā dhammā) to diminish.”

“And what kind of verbal conduct (vacīsamācāraṃ) causes unwholesome states (akusalā dhammā) to diminish and wholesome states (kusalā dhammā) to increase? Here, venerable sir, a certain person, abandoning false speech (musāvādaṃ), abstains from false speech (musāvādā paṭivirato hoti); when summoned to a court (sabhāgato) or to a meeting (parisāgato) or to his relatives’ presence (ñātimajjhagato) or to his guild (pūgamajjhagato) or to the royal family’s presence (rājakulamajjhagato), and questioned as a witness thus: ‘So, good man (purisa), tell what you know,’ not knowing, he says, ‘I do not know,’ or knowing, he says, ‘I know’; not seeing, he says, ‘I do not see,’ or seeing, he says, ‘I see’; he does not knowingly speak falsehood for his own ends (attahetu) or for another’s ends (parahetu) or for some trifling worldly end (āmisakiñcikkhahetu). Abandoning malicious speech (pisuṇaṃ vācaṃ pahāya), he abstains from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti); having heard something here, he does not repeat it elsewhere in order to divide those people from these; or having heard something there, he does not repeat it to these people in order to divide these people from those; thus he is one who reunites those who are divided, a promoter of friendships between those who are united, who enjoys concord (samaggārāmo), rejoices in concord (samaggarato), delights in concord (samagganandī), and speaks words that promote concord (samaggakaraṇiṃ vācaṃ bhāsitā hoti). Abandoning harsh speech (pharusaṃ vācaṃ pahāya), he abstains from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti); he speaks speech that is gentle (nelā), pleasing to the ear (kaṇṇasukhā), lovable (pemanīyā), touching the heart (hadayaṅgamā), refined (porī), pleasing to many people (bahujanakantā), and liked by many people (bahujanamanāpā); he speaks such speech. Abandoning gossip (samphappalāpaṃ pahāya), he abstains from gossip (samphappalāpā paṭivirato hoti), he speaks in season (kālavādī), speaks what is true (bhūtavādī), speaks what is beneficial (atthavādī), speaks what is in accordance with the Dharma (dhammavādī), speaks what is in accordance with the Discipline (vinayavādī), he speaks speech that has a foundation (nidhānavatiṃ vācaṃ), in season (kālena), with a valid reason (sāpadesaṃ), bounded (pariyantavatiṃ), and connected with what is beneficial (atthasaṃhitaṃ). This kind of verbal conduct (vacīsamācāraṃ) causes unwholesome states (akusalā dhammā) to diminish and wholesome states (kusalā dhammā) to increase.”

“‘I describe verbal conduct (vacīsamācāraṃ), monks (bhikkhave), as twofold (duvidhena): to be cultivated (sevitabbampi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbampi); and verbal conduct (vacīsamācāraṃ) is either the one or the other’—it is with reference to this that this was said by the Blessed One (bhagavatā).

And with reference to what was this said? Such mental conduct (manosamācāraṃ) as causes unwholesome states (akusalā dhammā) to increase and wholesome states (kusalā dhammā) to diminish should not be cultivated (na sevitabbo). But such mental conduct (manosamācāraṃ) as causes unwholesome states (akusalā dhammā) to diminish and wholesome states (kusalā dhammā) to increase should be cultivated (sevitabbo).”

121. “Sāriputta, robes (cīvara) are of two kinds, I say: to be cultivated (sevitabba) and not to be cultivated (asevitabba)… Almsfood (piṇḍapāta) is of two kinds, Sāriputta… Resting places (senāsana) are of two kinds, Sāriputta… Villages (gāma) are of two kinds, Sāriputta… Towns (nigama) are of two kinds, Sāriputta… Cities (nagara) are of two kinds, Sāriputta… Districts (janapada) are of two kinds, Sāriputta… Persons (puggala) are of two kinds, I say: to be cultivated (sevitabba) and not to be cultivated (asevitabba).”

When this was said, the venerable Sāriputta said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir (bhante), I understand the detailed meaning of the Blessed One’s utterance, which he has spoken in brief without expounding the detailed meaning, to be thus:

‘Sāriputta, robes (cīvara) are of two kinds, I say: to be cultivated (sevitabba) and not to be cultivated (asevitabba).’ So it was said by the Blessed One.

What was said with reference to this? Venerable sir (bhante), such robes (cīvara) as cause unwholesome states (akusala dhamma) to increase and wholesome states (kusala dhamma) to diminish in one who cultivates them should not be cultivated (sevitabba). But such robes (cīvara) as cause unwholesome states (akusala dhamma) to diminish and wholesome states (kusala dhamma) to increase in one who cultivates them should be cultivated (sevitabba).

‘Sāriputta, robes (cīvara) are of two kinds, I say: to be cultivated (sevitabba) and not to be cultivated (asevitabba).’ So it was with reference to this that it was said by the Blessed One.

“Almsfood (piṇḍapāta) is of two kinds, Sāriputta… Such almsfood (piṇḍapāta) should not be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such almsfood (piṇḍapāta) should be cultivated (sevitabba)… Resting places (senāsana) are of two kinds, Sāriputta… Such a resting place (senāsana) should not be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such a resting place (senāsana) should be cultivated (sevitabba)… Villages (gāma) are of two kinds, Sāriputta… Such a village (gāma) should not be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such a village (gāma) should be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such a town (nigama) should not be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such a town (nigama) should be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such a city (nagara) should not be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such a city (nagara) should be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such a district (janapada) should not be cultivated (sevitabba)… Such a district (janapada) should be cultivated (sevitabba).

“‘Sāriputta, persons (puggala) are of two kinds, I say: to be cultivated (sevitabba) and not to be cultivated (asevitabba).’ So it was said by the Blessed One.

What was said with reference to this? Venerable sir (bhante), association with such persons (puggala) as causes unwholesome states (akusala dhamma) to increase and wholesome states (kusala dhamma) to diminish in one who cultivates it should not be cultivated (sevitabba). But association with such persons (puggala) as causes unwholesome states (akusala dhamma) to diminish and wholesome states (kusala dhamma) to increase in one who cultivates it should be cultivated (sevitabba).

‘Sāriputta, persons (puggala) are of two kinds, I say: to be cultivated (sevitabba) and not to be cultivated (asevitabba).’ So it was with reference to this that it was said by the Blessed One.

Venerable sir (bhante), I understand the detailed meaning of the Blessed One’s utterance, which he has spoken in brief without expounding the detailed meaning, to be thus.”
