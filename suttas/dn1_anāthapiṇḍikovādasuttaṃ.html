<pb ed="T" n="3.0465"/> <pb ed="V" n="3.0310"/> <pb ed="M" n="3.0302"/>
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park.

Now at that time, Anāthapindika the householder was ill, suffering, gravely ill.

Then Anāthapindika the householder addressed a certain man: “Come, good man, go to the Blessed One; having gone, pay homage to the Blessed One’s feet with your head in my name, and say: ‘Venerable sir, Anāthapindika the householder is ill, suffering, gravely ill. He pays homage to the Blessed One’s feet with his head.’ Go to the venerable Sāriputta; having gone, pay homage to the venerable Sāriputta’s feet with your head in my name, and say: ‘Venerable sir, Anāthapindika the householder is ill, suffering, gravely ill. He pays homage to the venerable Sāriputta’s feet with his head.’ And say this: ‘It would be good, venerable sir, if the venerable Sāriputta would visit Anāthapindika’s house, out of compassion.’”

“Even so, venerable sir,” that man replied to Anāthapindika the householder, and he went to the Blessed One; having gone, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. As he was sitting to one side, that man said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, Anāthapindika the householder is ill, suffering, gravely ill. He pays homage to the Blessed One’s feet with his head.”

He went to the venerable Sāriputta; having gone, he paid homage to the venerable Sāriputta and sat down to one side. As he was sitting to one side, that man said to the venerable Sāriputta: “Venerable sir, Anāthapindika the householder is ill, suffering, gravely ill. He pays homage to the venerable Sāriputta’s feet with his head; and he says this: ‘It would be good, venerable sir, if the venerable Sāriputta would visit Anāthapindika’s house, out of compassion.’”

The venerable Sāriputta consented in silence.

Then venerable Sāriputta, having dressed, taking his bowl and outer robe, went to the residence of Anāthapiṇḍika, the householder, with venerable Ānanda as his attendant monk; having arrived, he sat down on the prepared seat.

Having sat down, venerable Sāriputta said this to Anāthapiṇḍika, the householder: "Householder, I hope you are bearing up; I hope you are comfortable. I hope your painful feelings are subsiding and not increasing; that a subsiding is apparent, and not an increase?"

"Venerable Sāriputta, I am not bearing up, I am not comfortable. My severe painful feelings are increasing, not subsiding; an increase is apparent, and not a subsiding. Just as if, venerable Sāriputta, a strong man were to crush my head with a sharp weapon; just so, venerable Sāriputta, are the excessive winds assailing my head. Venerable Sāriputta, I am not bearing up, I am not comfortable. My severe painful feelings are increasing, not subsiding; an increase is apparent, and not a subsiding. Venerable Sāriputta, I am not bearing up, I am not comfortable. My severe painful feelings are increasing, not subsiding; an increase is apparent, and not a subsiding. Just as if, venerable Sāriputta, a skilled butcher or his apprentice were cutting open my belly with a sharp butcher’s knife; just so, venerable Sāriputta, are the excessive winds cutting up my belly. Venerable Sāriputta, I am not bearing up, I am not comfortable. My severe painful feelings are increasing, not subsiding; an increase is apparent, and not a subsiding. Just as if, venerable Sāriputta, two strong men, seizing a weaker man by his arms, were to roast and broil him over a pit of hot embers; just so, venerable Sāriputta, is the excessive burning in my body. Venerable Sāriputta, I am not bearing up, I am not comfortable. My severe painful feelings are increasing, not subsiding; an increase is apparent, and not a subsiding."

386. “Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to the earth property (pathavīdhātu); my consciousness will not be dependent on the earth property.’
That’s how you should train yourself.
Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to the water property (āpodhātu)… the fire property (tejodhātu)… the air property (vāyodhātu)… the space property (ākāsadhātu)… the consciousness property (viññāṇadhātu); my consciousness will not be dependent on the consciousness property.’
That’s how you should train yourself.

“Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to forms (rūpa); my consciousness will not be dependent on forms.’
That’s how you should train yourself.
Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to feeling (vedanā)… perception (sañña)… thought-fabrications (saṅkhāre)… consciousness (viññāṇa); my consciousness will not be dependent on consciousness.’
That’s how you should train yourself.

“Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to the dimension of the infinitude of space (ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ); my consciousness will not be dependent on the dimension of the infinitude of space.’
That’s how you should train yourself.
Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to the dimension of the infinitude of consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ)… the dimension of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ)… the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ); my consciousness will not be dependent on the sphere of neither perception nor non-perception.’
That’s how you should train yourself.

“Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to this world (idhalokaṃ); my consciousness will not be dependent on this world.’
That’s how you should train yourself.
Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to the world beyond (paralokaṃ); my consciousness will not be dependent on the world beyond.’
That’s how you should train yourself.
Then, householder, you should train yourself in this way: ‘I won’t cling to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, pondered by the intellect; my consciousness will not be dependent on that.’
That’s how you should train yourself.”

<p n="387" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">387</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ vutte, anāthapiṇḍiko gahapati parodi, assūni pavattesi. Atha kho āyasmā ānando anāthapiṇḍikaṃ gahapatiṃ etadavoca – ‘‘olīyasi kho tvaṃ, gahapati, saṃsīdasi kho tvaṃ, gahapatī’’ti? ‘‘Nāhaṃ, bhante ānanda, olīyāmi, napi saṃsīdāmi; api ca me dīgharattaṃ satthā payirupāsito manobhāvanīyā ca bhikkhū; na ca me evarūpī dhammī <pb ed="T" n="3.0471"/> kathā sutapubbā’’ti. ‘‘Na kho, gahapati, gihīnaṃ odātavasanānaṃ evarūpī dhammī kathā paṭibhāti; pabbajitānaṃ kho, gahapati, evarūpī dhammī kathā paṭibhātī’’ti. ‘‘Tena hi, bhante sāriputta, gihīnampi odātavasanānaṃ evarūpī dhammī kathā paṭibhātu. Santi hi, bhante, kulaputtā apparajakkhajātikā, assavanatā dhammassa parihāyanti; bhavissanti dhammassa aññātāro’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Atha kho āyasmā ca sāriputto āyasmā ca ānando anāthapiṇḍikaṃ gahapatiṃ iminā ovādena ovaditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkamiṃsu. Atha kho anāthapiṇḍiko gahapati, acirapakkante āyasmante ca sāriputte āyasmante ca ānande <pb ed="P" n="3.0262"/>, kālamakāsi tusitaṃ kāyaṃ upapajji. Atha kho anāthapiṇḍiko devaputto abhikkantāya rattiyā abhikkantavaṇṇo kevalakappaṃ <pb ed="V" n="3.0314"/> jetavanaṃ obhāsetvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ <pb ed="M" n="3.0306"/> ṭhito kho anāthapiṇḍiko devaputto bhagavantaṃ gāthāhi ajjhabhāsi –

<pb ed="P" n="3.0262"/> <pb ed="V" n="3.0314"/> <pb ed="M" n="3.0306"/> <p rend="gatha1">‘‘Idañhi taṃ jetavanaṃ, isisaṅghanisevitaṃ;

<p rend="gathalast">Āvutthaṃ dhammarājena, pītisañjananaṃ mama.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Kammaṃ vijjā ca dhammo ca, sīlaṃ jīvitamuttamaṃ;

<p rend="gathalast">Etena maccā sujjhanti, na gottena dhanena vā.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Tasmā hi paṇḍito poso, sampassaṃ atthamattano;

<p rend="gathalast">Yoniso vicine dhammaṃ, evaṃ tattha visujjhati.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Sāriputtova <pb ed="T" n="3.0472"/> paññāya, sīlena upasamena;

<pb ed="T" n="3.0472"/> <p rend="gathalast">Yopi pāraṅgato bhikkhu, etāvaparamo siyā’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca anāthapiṇḍiko devaputto. Samanuñño satthā ahosi. Atha kho anāthapiṇḍiko devaputto – ‘‘samanuñño me satthā’’ti bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā tatthevantaradhāyi.

387. When this was said, Anāthapiṇḍika the householder wept and shed tears.

Then the venerable Ānanda said this to Anāthapiṇḍika the householder: “You are drooping, householder; you are sinking, householder?”

“I am not drooping, venerable Ānanda, nor am I sinking; but for a long time the Teacher has been attended to by me, and also the bhikkhus who are pleasing to the mind; and such a talk on the Dhamma has never been heard by me before.”

“This sort of talk on the Dhamma, householder, is not given to lay people clad in white. This sort of talk on the Dhamma is given to those gone forth.”

“In that case, Ven. Sāriputta, please let this sort of talk on the Dhamma be given to lay people clad in white.

There are clansmen with little dust in their eyes who are wasting away through not hearing this Dhamma. There will be those who will understand it.”

Then Ven. Sāriputta and Ven. Ānanda, having given this instruction to Anāthapiṇḍika the householder, got up from their seats and left.

Then Anāthapiṇḍika the householder, not long after the venerable Sāriputta and the venerable Ānanda had left, passed away and arose in the Tusita heaven.

Then Anāthapiṇḍika the deva’s son, in the far extreme of the night, his extreme radiance lighting up the entirety of Jeta’s Grove, went to the Blessed One and, on arrival, bowed down to him and stood to one side.

Standing to one side, Anāthapiṇḍika the deva’s son addressed the Blessed One in stanzas:

“This is Jeta’s Grove, frequented by the communities of seers;

populated by the King of the Dhamma, a producer of joy for me.

“Deed, knowledge, and the Dhamma, virtue, and the most excellent life:

by this are mortals purified, not by lineage or wealth.

“Therefore, the wise person, seeing his own benefit,

should investigate the Dhamma properly; in that way one is purified there.

“Sāriputta is supreme in wisdom, in virtue, and in serenity;

any monk who has gone beyond, at best can only equal him.”

That is what Anāthapiṇḍika the deva’s son said.

The Teacher approved.

Then Anāthapiṇḍika the deva’s son, knowing, “The Teacher has approved of me,” bowed down to him, circled him three times, keeping him to his right, and then disappeared right there.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Kammaṃ vijjā ca dhammo ca, sīlaṃ jīvitamuttamaṃ;

<p rend="gathalast">Etena maccā sujjhanti, na gottena dhanena vā.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Tasmā <pb ed="V" n="3.0315"/> hi paṇḍito poso, sampassaṃ atthamattano;

<pb ed="V" n="3.0315"/> <p rend="gathalast">Yoniso vicine dhammaṃ, evaṃ tattha visujjhati.

<p rend="gatha1">‘‘Sāriputtova paññāya, sīlena upasamena;

<p rend="gathalast">Yopi pāraṅgato bhikkhu, etāvaparamo siyā’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Idamavoca <pb ed="P" n="3.0263"/>, bhikkhave, so devaputto. ‘Samanuñño me satthā’ti maṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā tatthevantaradhāyī’’ti.

<pb ed="P" n="3.0263"/> <p rend="bodytext">Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0473"/><pb ed="M" n="3.0307"/> vutte, āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘so hi nūna so, bhante, anāthapiṇḍiko devaputto bhavissati. Anāthapiṇḍiko, bhante, gahapati āyasmante sāriputte abhippasanno ahosī’’ti. ‘‘Sādhu, sādhu, ānanda! Yāvatakaṃ kho, ānanda, takkāya pattabbaṃ, anuppattaṃ taṃ tayā. Anāthapiṇḍiko so, ānanda, devaputto’’ti.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0473"/> <pb ed="M" n="3.0307"/> <p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā ānando bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti.

<p rend="centre">Anāthapiṇḍikovādasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.

Then, when the night had passed, the Blessed One addressed the monks: “Last night, monks, a certain deva's son ( devaputto), at the far extreme of the night, his extreme radiance lighting up the entirety of Jeta's Grove ( Jetavana), came to me and, on arrival, bowed down to me and stood to one side.

Standing to one side, that deva's son ( devaputto) addressed me in these verses:

“Indeed, this is that Jetavana, frequented by the communities of seers;

Abode of the King of Dharma ( dharmarājena), a generator of joy for me.

“Action, knowledge, and dharma, and morality—the highest life;

By these do mortals become pure, not by lineage or wealth.

“Therefore, a wise person, seeing his own benefit,

Should investigate the dharma thoroughly; thus is one purified there.

“Like Sāriputta ( Sāriputtova) in wisdom, morality, and tranquility,

Any bhikkhu ( bhikkhu) who has gone to the far shore might be unsurpassed.”

“That is what the deva's son ( devaputto) said, monks. Thinking, ‘The Teacher has approved of me,’ he bowed down to me, circled me, and then disappeared right there.”

When this was said, the venerable Ānanda ( Ānando) said to the Blessed One: “Surely, venerable sir, that deva's son ( devaputto) must have been Anāthapiṇḍika ( Anāthapiṇḍiko).

Anāthapiṇḍika ( Anāthapiṇḍiko), venerable sir, the householder ( gahapati) had supreme confidence in the venerable Sāriputta ( Sāriputte).”

“Good, good, Ānanda ( Ānanda)! As far as it could be reached by reasoning, Ānanda ( Ānanda), you have reached it.

That deva's son ( devaputto), Ānanda ( Ānanda), was Anāthapiṇḍika ( Anāthapiṇḍiko).”

This is what the Blessed One said. The venerable Ānanda ( Ānando), being pleased, rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words.

The Anāthapiṇḍikovāda Sutta is finished, the first.
