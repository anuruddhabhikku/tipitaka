428. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘mahāsaḷāyatanikaṃ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –
Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Anathapindika’s monastery (anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme), in the Jeta Grove (jetavane), near Savatthi (sāvatthiyaṃ).

There the Fortunate One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: “Bhikkhus (bhikkhavo)!”

“Venerable sir (bhadante),” those bhikkhus (bhikkhū) replied to the Fortunate One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this: “Bhikkhus (bhikkhave), I shall teach you a discourse on the great sixfold base (mahāsaḷāyatanikaṃ).

Listen to that (taṃ suṇātha) and attend closely, I will speak (bhāsissāmī).”

“As you say, lord (evaṃ bhante),” the monks (bhikkhū) responded to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said,
428.Evaṃme sutanti mahāsaḷāyatanikasuttaṃ. Tattha mahāsaḷāyatanikanti mahantānaṃ channaṃ āyatanānaṃ jotakaṃ dhammapariyāyaṃ.
Thus have I heard. The Mahāsaḷāyatanikasuttaṃ.

Therein, mahāsaḷāyatanika means a teaching discourse (dhamma-pariyāyaṃ) that illuminates the six great sense bases (mahantānaṃ channaṃ āyatanānaṃ jotakaṃ).

429. “Bhikkhus, when one does not know and see the eye (cakkhu) as it actually is, when one does not know and see forms (rūpe) as they actually are, when one does not know and see eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) as it actually is, when one does not know and see eye-contact (cakkhusamphassaṃ) as it actually is, when one does not know and see as it actually is the feeling (vedayitaṃ) felt as pleasant (sukhaṃ) or painful (dukkhaṃ) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukhaṃ) that arises with eye-contact as condition, then one is inflamed by lust (sārajjati) for the eye, for forms, for eye-consciousness, for eye-contact, and for whatever feeling arises with eye-contact as condition, be it pleasant, painful, or neither-painful-nor-pleasant.

“When one abides inflamed by lust (sārattassa), fettered (saṃyuttassa), infatuated (sammūḷhassa), contemplating gratification (assādānupassino), then the five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhā) are built up for oneself in the future (āyatiṃ); and one’s craving (taṇhā) - which brings renewal of being (ponobbhavikā), is accompanied by delight and lust (nandīrāgasahagatā), and delights in this and that (tatratatrābhinandinī) - increases (pavaḍḍhati).

One's bodily (kāyikāpi) troubles (darathā) increase (pavaḍḍhanti), and one's mental (cetasikāpi) troubles increase; one's bodily torments (santāpā) increase, and one's mental torments increase; one's bodily fevers (pariḷāhā) increase, and one's mental fevers increase.

One experiences bodily suffering (kāyadukkhampi) and mental suffering (cetodukkhampi) .

“Bhikkhus, when one does not know and see the ear (sotaṃ) as it actually is…pe… When one does not know and see the nose (ghānaṃ) as it actually is…pe… When one does not know and see the tongue (jivhaṃ) as it actually is…pe… When one does not know and see the body (kāyaṃ) as it actually is…pe… When one does not know and see the mind (manaṃ) as it actually is, when one does not know and see mental phenomena (dhamme) as it actually is, when one does not know and see mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇaṃ) as it actually is, when one does not know and see mind-contact (manosamphassaṃ) as it actually is, when one does not know and see as it actually is the feeling (vedayitaṃ) felt as pleasant (sukhaṃ) or painful (dukkhaṃ) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukhaṃ) that arises with mind-contact as condition, then one is inflamed by lust (sārajjati) for the mind, for mental phenomena, for mind-consciousness, for mind-contact, and for whatever feeling arises with mind-contact as condition, be it pleasant, painful, or neither-painful-nor-pleasant.

“When one abides inflamed by lust (sārattassa), fettered (saṃyuttassa), infatuated (sammūḷhassa), contemplating gratification (assādānupassino), then the five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhā) are built up for oneself in the future (āyatiṃ); and one’s craving (taṇhā) - which brings renewal of being (ponobbhavikā), is accompanied by delight and lust (nandīrāgasahagatā), and delights in this and that (tatratatrābhinandinī) - increases (pavaḍḍhati).

One's bodily (kāyikāpi) troubles (darathā) increase (pavaḍḍhanti), and one's mental (cetasikāpi) troubles increase; one's bodily torments (santāpā) increase, and one's mental torments increase; one's bodily fevers (pariḷāhā) increase, and one's mental fevers increase.

One experiences bodily suffering (kāyadukkhampi) and mental suffering (cetodukkhampi).
429.Ajānanti sahavipassanena maggena ajānanto. Upacayaṃ gacchantīti vuḍḍhiṃ gacchanti, vasibhāvaṃ gacchantīti attho. Kāyikāti pañcadvārikadarathā. Cetasikāti manodvārikadarathā. Santāpādīsupi eseva nayo.
429. They do not know (ajānanti), because of the path of insight (sahavipassanena maggena), they are ignorant (ajānanto).

"They go to accumulation (upacayaṃ gacchantī)" means they go to growth (vuḍḍhiṃ gacchanti), they go to mastery (vasibhāvaṃ gacchantīti); this is the meaning (attho).

"Physical (kāyikā)" means the five-sense-door chariot (pañcadvārikadarathā).

"Mental (cetasikā)" means the mind-door chariot (manodvārikadarathā).

This is also the method (eseva nayo) in afflictions such as suffering (santāpādīsupi).

430. ‘‘Cakkhuñca kho, bhikkhave, jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, rūpe jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, cakkhuviññāṇaṃ jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, cakkhusamphassaṃ jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, yamidaṃ cakkhusamphassapaccayā uppajjati vedayitaṃ sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā tampi jānaṃ passaṃ yathābhūtaṃ, cakkhusmiṃ na sārajjati, rūpesu na sārajjati, cakkhuviññāṇe na sārajjati, cakkhusamphasse na sārajjati, yamidaṃ cakkhusamphassapaccayā uppajjati vedayitaṃ sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā tasmimpi na sārajjati. ‘‘Tassa asārattassa asaṃyuttassa asammūḷhassa ādīnavānupassino viharato āyatiṃ pañcupādānakkhandhā apacayaṃ gacchanti. Taṇhā cassa ponobbhavikā nandīrāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī, sā cassa pahīyati. Tassa kāyikāpi darathā pahīyanti, cetasikāpi darathā pahīyanti; kāyikāpi santāpā pahīyanti, cetasikāpi santāpā pahīyanti; kāyikāpi pariḷāhā pahīyanti , cetasikāpi pariḷāhā pahīyanti. So kāyasukhampi cetosukhampi paṭisaṃvedeti.

“Bhikkhus, when one knows and sees the eye (cakkhu) as it actually is, when one knows and sees forms (rūpa) as they actually are, when one knows and sees eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇa) as it actually is, when one knows and sees eye-contact (cakkhusamphassa) as it actually is, when one knows and sees as it actually is the feeling felt as pleasant (sukha) or painful (dukkha) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukha) that arises with eye-contact as condition, then one is not inflamed by lust for the eye, for forms, for eye-consciousness, for eye-contact, for the feeling felt as pleasant or painful or neither-painful-nor-pleasant that arises with eye-contact as condition.

“When one abides uninflamed by lust, unfettered, uninfatuated, contemplating danger, then the five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhā) are diminished for oneself in the future; and one's craving (taṇhā) - which brings renewal of being, is accompanied by delight and lust, and delights in this or that - is abandoned.
One's bodily (kāyika) and mental (cetasika) troubles (daratha) are abandoned, one's bodily and mental torments (santāpa) are abandoned, one's bodily and mental fevers (pariḷāha) are abandoned.
And one experiences bodily (kāyasukha) and mental pleasure (cetosukha).
430.Kāyasukhanti pañcadvārikasukhaṃ. Cetosukhanti manodvārikasukhaṃ. Ettha ca pañcadvārikajavanena samāpajjanaṃ vā vuṭṭhānaṃ vā natthi, uppannamattakameva hoti. Manodvārikena sabbaṃ hoti. Ayañca maggavuṭṭhānassa paccayabhūtā balavavipassanā, sāpi manodvārikeneva hoti.
430.
Kāyasukha means pleasure through the five doors (pañcadvārika).
Cetosukha means pleasure through the mind-door (manodvārika).
Here, arising, attainment or emergence through the five-door impulsion (pañcadvārikajavana) does not exist; it only occurs momentarily.
Everything happens through the mind-door (manodvārikena).
Moreover, this strong insight (balavavipassanā), which is the causal condition for emergence from the path (maggavuṭṭhānassa), that also happens through the mind-door (manodvārikeneva).

"That vision of one who is thus, that is their right vision (samyagdṛṣṭi); that thought of one who is thus, that is their right thought (samyaksaṃkalpa); that effort of one who is thus, that is their right effort (samyagvyāyāma); that mindfulness of one who is thus, that is their right mindfulness (samyaksmṛti); that absorption of one who is thus, that is their right absorption (samyaksamādhi). But his bodily action, his verbal action, and his livelihood have already been well purified earlier. Thus, this Noble Eightfold Path comes to fulfilment in him by development.

"In him who thus develops this Noble Eightfold Path, the four applications of mindfulness also come to fulfilment in him by development; the four right kinds of striving also come to fulfilment in him by development; the four bases for spiritual power also come to fulfilment in him by development; the five faculties also come to fulfilment in him by development; the five powers also come to fulfilment in him by development; the seven enlightenment factors also come to fulfilment in him by development.

"These two things - serenity (śamatha) and insight (vipaśyanā) - occur in him yoked evenly together. He fully understands by direct knowledge (abhijñā) those things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge. He abandons by direct knowledge those things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge. He develops by direct knowledge those things that should be developed by direct knowledge. He realises by direct knowledge those things that should be realised by direct knowledge.

"And what, monks, are the things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge? It should be said, 'the five aggregates of clinging' (pañcupādānakkhandhā), that is to say: the aggregate of clinging to form (rūpupādānakkhandha), the aggregate of clinging to feeling (vedanupādānakkhandha), the aggregate of clinging to perception (saṃjñupādānakkhandha), the aggregate of clinging to formations (saṃskārupādānakkhandha), the aggregate of clinging to consciousness (vijñānupādānakkhandha). These are the things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge.

"And what, monks, are the things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge? Ignorance (avidyā) and craving for existence (bhavatṛṣṇā) - these are the things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge.

"And what, monks, are the things that should be developed by direct knowledge? Serenity (śamatha) and insight (vipaśyanā) - these are the things that should be developed by direct knowledge.

"And what, monks, are the things that should be realised by direct knowledge? True knowledge (vidyā) and deliverance (vimukti) - these are the things that should be realised by direct knowledge.
431.Tathābhūtassāti kusalacittasampayuttacetosukhasamaṅgībhūtassa. Pubbeva kho panassāti assa bhikkhuno vācākammantājīvā pubbasuddhikā nāma ādito paṭṭhāya parisuddhāva honti. Diṭṭhisaṅkappavāyāmasatisamādhisaṅkhātāni pana pañcaṅgāni sabbatthakakārāpakaṅgāni nāma. Evaṃ lokuttaramaggo aṭṭhaṅgiko vā sattaṅgiko vā hoti. Vitaṇḍavādī pana ‘‘yā yathābhūtassa diṭṭhī’’ti imameva suttapadesaṃ gahetvā ‘‘lokuttaramaggo pañcaṅgiko’’ti vadati. So – ‘‘evamassāyaṃ ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchatī’’ti iminā anantaravacaneneva paṭisedhitabbo. Uttari ca evaṃ saññāpetabbo – lokuttaramaggo pañcaṅgiko nāma natthi, imāni pana pañca sabbatthakakārāpakaṅgāni maggakkhaṇe virativasena pūrenti. ‘‘Yā catūhi vacīduccaritehi ārati viratī’’ti evaṃ vuttaviratīsu hi micchāvācaṃ pajahati, sammāvācaṃ bhāveti, evaṃ sammāvācaṃ bhāventassa imāni pañcaṅgikāni na vinā, saheva viratiyā pūrenti. Sammākammantājīvesupi eseva nayo. Iti vacīkammādīni ādito paṭṭhāya parisuddhāneva vaṭṭanti. Imāni pana pañca sabbatthakakārāpakaṅgāni virativasena paripūrentīti pañcaṅgiko maggo nāma natthi. Subhaddasuttepi (dī. ni. 2.214) cetaṃ vuttaṃ – ‘‘yasmiṃ kho, subhadda, dhammavinaye ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo’’ti. Aññesu ca anekesu suttasatesu aṭṭhaṅgikova maggo āgatoti.
431. tathābhūtassāti of one who has become endowed with the bliss of mind (cetasasukha) conjoined with wholesome thought (kusalacitta).

pubbeva kho panassāti namely, the speech, action, and livelihood of that bhikṣu are primarily purified from the outset.

Moreover, the five factors, namely, right view (dṛṣṭi), right thought (saṅkappa), right effort (vāyāma), right mindfulness (smṛti), and right concentration (samādhi), are called factors that accomplish everything in every respect.

Thus, the supramundane path (lokuttaramārga) is either eightfold (aṣṭāṅgika) or sevenfold (saptāṅgika).

A sophist (vitaṇḍavādī), however, taking this very passage of the sūtra, "whatever view is in accordance with reality (yathābhūta)," says, "the supramundane path is fivefold (pañcaṅgika)."

He should be refuted by this immediate statement: "Thus, this noble eightfold path (ārya aṣṭāṅgika mārga) goes to the complete fulfillment of cultivation (bhāvanāpāripūri)."

Moreover, it should be made known: There is no supramundane path that is fivefold (lokuttaramārga pañcaṅgika nāma natthi); rather, these five factors accomplish everything in every respect, fulfilling [the path] by way of cessation (virati) at the moment of the path (maggakkhaṇe).

"That which is abstaining and refraining from the four kinds of misconduct of speech (vacīduccarita)"—thus, in the case of those stated abstentions, one abandons wrong speech (mithyāvāc) and cultivates right speech (samyag-vāc); thus, for one cultivating right speech , these five factors are not separate, but together with abstinence, they fulfill [the path].

The same method (naya) applies to right action (samyak-karmānta) and right livelihood (samyag-ājīva).

Thus, speech-action and so forth are purified from the outset and continue as such.

These five factors, which accomplish everything in every respect, fulfill [the path] by way of cessation (virati), and therefore there is no fivefold path (pañcaṅgiko mārga nāma natthi).

In the Subhadda Sūtra (Dīgha Nikāya 2. 214) it was said: "In whatever Dharma and Discipline (dharma-vinaya), Subhadda, the noble eightfold path (ārya aṣṭāṅgika mārga) is [not found]."

And in many other hundreds of sūtras, it is the eightfold path (aṣṭāṅgika mārga) that is mentioned.

Taṇhā cassa ponobbhavikā nandīrāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī , sā cassa pahīyati. Tassa kāyikāpi darathā pahīyanti, cetasikāpi darathā pahīyanti; kāyikāpi santāpā pahīyanti, cetasikāpi santāpā pahīyanti; kāyikāpi pariḷāhā pahīyanti, cetasikāpi pariḷāhā pahīyanti. So kāyasukhampi cetosukhampi paṭisaṃvedeti.
432. “Bhikkhus, when one knows and sees the ear (sotaṃ) as it actually is... When one knows and sees the nose (ghānaṃ) as it actually is... When one knows and sees the tongue (jivhaṃ) as it actually is... When one knows and sees the body (kāyaṃ) as it actually is... When one knows and sees the mind (manaṃ) as it actually is, when one knows and sees phenomena (dhamme) as they actually are, when one knows and sees mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇaṃ) as it actually is, when one knows and sees mind-contact (manosamphassaṃ) as it actually is, when one knows and sees as it actually is the feeling felt as pleasant (sukhaṃ) or painful (dukkhaṃ) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukhaṃ) that arises with mind-contact as condition, then one is not inflamed by lust for the mind (manasmiṃ), for phenomena (dhammesu), for mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇe), for mind-contact (manosamphasse), for the feeling felt as pleasant or painful or neither-painful-nor-pleasant that arises with mind-contact as condition.

“When one abides uninflamed by lust (asārattassa), unfettered (asaṃyuttassa), uninfatuated (asammūḷhassa), contemplating danger (ādīnavānupassino), then the five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhā) are diminished (apacayaṃ gacchanti) for oneself in the future; and one’s craving (taṇhā) for renewed existence (ponobbhavikā), which is accompanied by delight and attachment (nandīrāgasahagatā), finding fresh delight now here, now there (tatratatrābhinandinī), that is abandoned (pahīyati).

One’s bodily (kāyikāpi) troubles (darathā) are abandoned (pahīyanti), one’s mental (cetasikāpi) troubles (darathā) are abandoned (pahīyanti); one’s bodily (kāyikāpi) torments (santāpā) are abandoned (pahīyanti), one’s mental (cetasikāpi) torments (santāpā) are abandoned (pahīyanti); one’s bodily (kāyikāpi) fevers (pariḷāhā) are abandoned (pahīyanti), one’s mental (cetasikāpi) fevers (pariḷāhā) are abandoned (pahīyanti).

And one experiences bodily (kāyasukhampi) and mental pleasure (cetosukhampi) (paṭisaṃvedeti).

433. ‘‘Yā tathābhūtassa diṭṭhi sāssa hoti sammādiṭṭhi; yo tathābhūtassa saṅkappo svāssa hoti sammāsaṅkappo; yo tathābhūtassa vāyāmo svāssa hoti sammāvāyāmo; yā tathābhūtassa sati sāssa hoti sammāsati; yo tathābhūtassa samādhi svāssa hoti sammāsamādhi. Pubbeva kho panassa kāyakammaṃ vacīkammaṃ ājīvo suparisuddho hoti. Evamassāyaṃ ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchati. ‘‘Tassa evaṃ imaṃ ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ bhāvayato cattāropi satipaṭṭhānā bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti, cattāropi sammappadhānā bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti, cattāropi iddhipādā bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti, pañcapi indriyāni bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti, pañcapi balāni bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti, sattapi bojjhaṅgā bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti. ‘‘Tassime dve dhammā yuganandhā vattanti – samatho ca vipassanā ca. So ye dhammā abhiññā pariññeyyā te dhamme abhiññā parijānāti. Ye dhammā abhiññā pahātabbā te dhamme abhiññā pajahati. Ye dhammā abhiññā bhāvetabbā te dhamme abhiññā bhāveti. Ye dhammā abhiññā sacchikātabbā te dhamme abhiññā sacchikaroti. ‘‘Katame ca, bhikkhave, dhammā abhiññā pariññeyyā? ‘Pañcupādānakkhandhā’ tissa vacanīyaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – rūpupādānakkhandho, vedanupādānakkhandho, saññupādānakkhandho, saṅkhārupādānakkhandho, viññāṇupādānakkhandho. Ime dhammā abhiññā pariññeyyā. ‘‘Katame ca, bhikkhave, dhammā abhiññā pahātabbā? Avijjā ca bhavataṇhā ca – ime dhammā abhiññā pahātabbā. ‘‘Katame ca, bhikkhave, dhammā abhiññā bhāvetabbā? Samatho ca vipassanā ca – ime dhammā abhiññā bhāvetabbā. ‘‘Katame ca, bhikkhave, dhammā abhiññā sacchikātabbā? Vijjā ca vimutti ca – ime dhammā abhiññā sacchikātabbā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Mahāsaḷāyatanikasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ sattamaṃ.
433. “The view of a person such as this is right view (sammādiṭṭhi); his intention is right intention (sammāsaṅkappo); his effort is right effort (sammāvāyāmo); his mindfulness is right mindfulness (sammāsati); his concentration is right concentration (sammāsamādhi). But his bodily action (kāyakammaṃ), his verbal action (vacīkammaṃ), and his livelihood (ājīvo) have already been well purified earlier. Thus, this Noble Eightfold Path (ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo) comes to fulfilment in him by development (bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchati).

“When he develops this Noble Eightfold Path (ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ bhāvayato), the four foundations of mindfulness (satipaṭṭhānā) also come to fulfilment in him by development (bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti); the four right kinds of striving (sammappadhānā) also come to fulfilment in him by development; the four bases for spiritual power (iddhipādā) also come to fulfilment in him by development; the five faculties (indriyāni) also come to fulfilment in him by development; the five powers (balāni) also come to fulfilment in him by development; the seven enlightenment factors (bojjhaṅgā) also come to fulfilment in him by development.

“These two things—serenity (samatho) and insight (vipassanā)—occur in him yoked evenly together. He fully understands by direct knowledge (abhiññā parijānāti) those things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge (abhiññā pariññeyyā). He abandons by direct knowledge (abhiññā pajahati) those things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge (abhiññā pahātabbā). He develops by direct knowledge (abhiññā bhāveti) those things that should be developed by direct knowledge (abhiññā bhāvetabbā). He realises by direct knowledge (abhiññā sacchikaroti) those things that should be realised by direct knowledge (abhiññā sacchikātabbā).

“And what, monks, are the things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā pariññeyyā)? ‘The five aggregates subject to grasping (pañcupādānakkhandhā)’ should be said, that is to say: the aggregate of material form subject to grasping (rūpupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of feeling subject to grasping (vedanupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of perception subject to grasping (saññupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of mental formations subject to grasping (saṅkhārupādānakkhandho), the aggregate of consciousness subject to grasping (viññāṇupādānakkhandho). These are the things that should be fully understood by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā pariññeyyā).

“And what, monks, are the things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā pahātabbā)? Ignorance (avijjā) and craving for existence (bhavataṇhā). These are the things that should be abandoned by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā pahātabbā).

“And what, monks, are the things that should be developed by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā bhāvetabbā)? Serenity (samatho) and insight (vipassanā). These are the things that should be developed by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā bhāvetabbā).

“And what, monks, are the things that should be realised by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā sacchikātabbā)? True knowledge (vijjā) and deliverance (vimutti). These are the things that should be realised by direct knowledge (dhammā abhiññā sacchikātabbā).”

The Blessed One said this. Gratified, the monks delighted in the Blessed One’s words (bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti).

The Mahāsaḷāyatanika Sutta, the seventh, is concluded.
433.Cattāropi satipaṭṭhānāti maggasampayuttāva cattāro satipaṭṭhānā. Sammappadhānādīsupi eseva nayo. Yuganandhāti ekakkhaṇikayuganandhā. Etehi aññasmiṃ khaṇe samāpatti, aññasmiṃ vipassanāti. Evaṃ nānākhaṇikāpi honti, ariyamagge pana ekakkhaṇikā. Vijjā ca vimutti cāti arahattamaggavijjā ca phalavimutti ca. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Vijjā ca vimutti cā Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Mahāsaḷāyatanikasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
433. "The four smṛtyupasthānas (foundations of mindfulness)" are the four smṛtyupasthānas that are connected with the path (mārga).

And similarly in the case of the right efforts (samyak-pradhāna), and so on.

"Yuganandhā (yoked in pairs)" means yoked in pairs in a single moment (eka-kṣaṇika).

By these, in one moment there is attainment (samāpatti), in another insight (vipaśyanā).

Thus, they can be of different moments (nānā-kṣaṇika), but on the ārya-mārga (noble path) they are of a single moment.

"Knowledge (vidyā) and liberation (vimukti)" are the knowledge of the path of arhatship (arhattva-mārga) and the liberation of the fruit (phala-vimukti).

All the rest is clear throughout.

Vidyā ca vimukti cā
