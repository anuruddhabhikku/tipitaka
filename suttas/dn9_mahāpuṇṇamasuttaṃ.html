<p n="85" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">85</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0101"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0064"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde. Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā tadahuposathe pannarase puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto abbhokāse nisinno hoti. Atha kho aññataro bhikkhu uṭṭhāyāsanā ekaṃsaṃ cīvaraṃ katvā yena bhagavā tenañjaliṃ paṇāmetvā bhagavantaṃ etadavoca –

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Puccheyyāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ kiñcideva desaṃ, sace me bhagavā okāsaṃ karoti pañhassa veyyākaraṇāyā’’ti. ‘‘Tena hi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, sake āsane nisīditvā puccha yadākaṅkhasī’’ti.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvattha (Sāvatthī) in the Eastern Park in Migāramātā’s mansion.

Now on that occasion, on the Uposatha day of the fifteenth, the full-moon night, the Blessed One was seated in the open surrounded by the Saṃgha (Saṅgha), the community of bhikkhus (bhikkhus).

Then a certain bhikkhu (bhikkhu) rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, raised his joined hands in reverential salutation (añjali) towards the Blessed One, and said to him:

“Venerable sir (bhante), I would ask the Blessed One about a certain point (desaṃ), if the Blessed One would grant me the favour of answering my question (pañhassa veyyākaraṇāyā)”.

“Well then, bhikkhu (bhikkhu), sit down in your own seat (āsane) and ask whatever you wish (yadākaṅkhasī)”.

<p n="86" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">86</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Atha kho so bhikkhu sake āsane nisīditvā bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ime nu kho, bhante, pañcupādānakkhandhā, seyyathidaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0016"/> – rūpupādānakkhandho, vedanupādānakkhandho, saññupādānakkhandho, saṅkhārupādānakkhandho, viññāṇupādānakkhandho’’ti? ‘‘Ime kho, bhikkhu, pañcupādānakkhandhā, seyyathidaṃ – rūpupādānakkhandho, vedanupādānakkhandho, saññupādānakkhandho, saṅkhārupādānakkhandho, viññāṇupādānakkhandho’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Sādhu, bhante’’ti kho so bhikkhu bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā bhagavantaṃ uttariṃ pañhaṃ pucchi – ‘‘ime pana, bhante, pañcupādānakkhandhā kiṃmūlakā’’ti? ‘‘Ime kho, bhikkhu, pañcupādānakkhandhā chandamūlakā’’ti. ‘‘Taṃyeva nu kho, bhante, upādānaṃ te pañcupādānakkhandhā, udāhu <pb ed="T" n="3.0102"/> aññatra pañcahupādānakkhandhehi upādāna’’nti? ‘‘Na kho, bhikkhu, taṃyeva upādānaṃ te pañcupādānakkhandhā, nāpi aññatra pañcahupādānakkhandhehi upādānaṃ. Yo kho, bhikkhu, pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu chandarāgo taṃ tattha upādāna’’nti.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0102"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Siyā pana, bhante, pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu chandarāgavemattatā’’ti? ‘‘Siyā bhikkhū’’ti bhagavā avoca ‘‘idha, bhikkhu, ekaccassa evaṃ hoti – ‘evaṃrūpo <pb ed="M" n="3.0067"/> siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānaṃ <pb ed="V" n="3.0065"/>, evaṃvedano siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānaṃ, evaṃsañño siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānaṃ, evaṃsaṅkhāro siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānaṃ, evaṃviññāṇo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhāna’nti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, siyā pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu chandarāgavemattatā’’ti.

<pb ed="M" n="3.0067"/> <pb ed="V" n="3.0065"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Kittāvatā pana, bhante, khandhānaṃ khandhādhivacanaṃ hotī’’ti? ‘‘Yaṃ kiñci, bhikkhu, rūpaṃ – atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā, oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā, hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā, yaṃ dūre santike vā – ayaṃ rūpakkhandho. Yā <pb ed="P" n="3.0017"/> kāci vedanā – atītānāgatapaccuppannā ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā, oḷārikā vā sukhumā vā, hīnā vā paṇītā vā, yā dūre santike vā – ayaṃ vedanākkhandho. Yā kāci saññā – atītānāgatapaccuppannā…pe… yā dūre santike vā – ayaṃ saññākkhandho. Ye keci saṅkhārā – atītānāgatapaccuppannā ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā, oḷārikā vā sukhumā vā, hīnā vā paṇītā vā, ye dūre santike vā – ayaṃ saṅkhārakkhandho. Yaṃ kiñci viññāṇaṃ – atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā, oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā, hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā, yaṃ dūre santike vā – ayaṃ viññāṇakkhandho. Ettāvatā kho, bhikkhu, khandhānaṃ khandhādhivacanaṃ hotī’’ti.

<pb ed="P" n="3.0017"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Ko nu kho, bhante, hetu ko paccayo rūpakkhandhassa paññāpanāya? Ko hetu ko paccayo vedanākkhandhassa paññāpanāya? Ko hetu ko paccayo saññākkhandhassa paññāpanāya? Ko <pb ed="T" n="3.0103"/> hetu ko paccayo saṅkhārakkhandhassa paññāpanāya? Ko hetu ko paccayo viññāṇakkhandhassa paññāpanāyā’’ti?

<pb ed="T" n="3.0103"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Cattāro kho, bhikkhu, mahābhūtā hetu, cattāro mahābhūtā paccayo rūpakkhandhassa paññāpanāya. Phasso hetu, phasso paccayo vedanākkhandhassa paññāpanāya. Phasso hetu, phasso paccayo saññākkhandhassa paññāpanāya. Phasso hetu, phasso paccayo saṅkhārakkhandhassa paññāpanāya. Nāmarūpaṃ kho, bhikkhu, hetu, nāmarūpaṃ paccayo viññāṇakkhandhassa paññāpanāyā’’ti.

86. Then that bhikkhu, after sitting on his seat, said this to the Blessed One: “Are these the five aggregates affected by clinging, venerable sir; that is, the form aggregate affected by clinging (rūpupādānakkhandha), the feeling aggregate affected by clinging (vedanupādānakkhandha), the perception aggregate affected by clinging (saññupādānakkhandha), the formations aggregate affected by clinging (saṅkhārupādānakkhandha), and the consciousness aggregate affected by clinging (viññāṇupādānakkhandha)?”

“These, bhikkhu, are the five aggregates affected by clinging; that is, the form aggregate affected by clinging, the feeling aggregate affected by clinging, the perception aggregate affected by clinging, the formations aggregate affected by clinging, and the consciousness aggregate affected by clinging.”

“Good, venerable sir,” that bhikkhu, delighting and rejoicing in the Blessed One’s words, asked the Blessed One a further question: “But, venerable sir, what is the root of these five aggregates affected by clinging?” “These five aggregates affected by clinging are rooted in desire (chandamūlakā), bhikkhu.”

“Is that very clinging the same as these five aggregates affected by clinging, venerable sir, or is clinging something apart from the five aggregates affected by clinging?” “That clinging is neither the same as these five aggregates affected by clinging, bhikkhu, nor is clinging something apart from the five aggregates affected by clinging. But rather, the desire and lust (chandarāgo) in regard to the five aggregates affected by clinging, that is the clinging (upādāna) there, bhikkhu.”

“But could there be diversity in the desire and lust regarding the five aggregates affected by clinging, venerable sir?” “There could be, bhikkhu,” the Blessed One said. “Here, bhikkhu, it occurs to someone: ‘May I have such form in the future; may I have such feeling in the future; may I have such perception in the future; may I have such formations in the future; may I have such consciousness in the future.’ Thus, there could be diversity in the desire and lust regarding these five aggregates affected by clinging, bhikkhu.”

“But to what extent does the term ‘aggregate’ apply to the aggregates, venerable sir?” “Any kind of form whatsoever, bhikkhu, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the form aggregate (rūpakkhandha). Any kind of feeling whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the feeling aggregate (vedanākkhandha). Any kind of perception whatsoever, whether past, future, or present…far or near – this is the perception aggregate (saññākkhandha). Any kind of formations whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the formations aggregate (saṅkhārakkhandha). Any kind of consciousness whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the consciousness aggregate (viññāṇakkhandha). It is to this extent, bhikkhu, that the term ‘aggregate’ applies to the aggregates.”

“What is the cause and condition, venerable sir, for the manifestation of the form aggregate? What is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the feeling aggregate? What is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the perception aggregate? What is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the formations aggregate? What is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the consciousness aggregate?”

“The four great elements (cattāro mahābhūtā), bhikkhu, are the cause and condition for the manifestation of the form aggregate. Contact (phasso) is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the feeling aggregate. Contact is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the perception aggregate. Contact is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the formations aggregate. Mentality-materiality (nāmarūpaṃ), bhikkhu, is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the consciousness aggregate.”

<p n="87" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">87</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Kathaṃ pana, bhante, sakkāyadiṭṭhi hotī’’ti? ‘‘Idha, bhikkhu, assutavā puthujjano ariyānaṃ adassāvī ariyadhammassa akovido ariyadhamme avinīto <pb ed="M" n="3.0068"/> sappurisānaṃ adassāvī <pb ed="V" n="3.0066"/> sappurisadhammassa akovido sappurisadhamme avinīto rūpaṃ attato samanupassati rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā rūpaṃ rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ; vedanaṃ attato samanupassati vedanāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā vedanaṃ vedanāya vā attānaṃ; saññaṃ attato samanupassati saññāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā saññaṃ saññāya vā attānaṃ; saṅkhāre attato samanupassati saṅkhāravantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā saṅkhāre saṅkhāresu vā attānaṃ; viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani <pb ed="P" n="3.0018"/> vā viññāṇaṃ viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ. Evaṃ kho <pb ed="T" n="3.0104"/>, bhikkhu, sakkāyadiṭṭhi hotī’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Kathaṃ pana, bhante, sakkāyadiṭṭhi na hotī’’ti? ‘‘Idha, bhikkhu, sutavā ariyasāvako ariyānaṃ dassāvī ariyadhammassa kovido ariyadhamme suvinīto sappurisānaṃ dassāvī sappurisadhammassa kovido sappurisadhamme suvinīto na rūpaṃ attato samanupassati na rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā rūpaṃ na rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ; na vedanaṃ attato samanupassati na vedanāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā vedanaṃ na vedanāya vā attānaṃ; na saññaṃ attato samanupassati na saññāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā saññaṃ na saññāya vā attānaṃ; na saṅkhāre attato samanupassati na saṅkhāravantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā saṅkhāre na saṅkhāresu vā attānaṃ; na viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati na viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā viññāṇaṃ na viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, sakkāyadiṭṭhi na hotī’’ti.

87. “But how, venerable sir, does identity view come to be?”

“Here, bhikkhu, an uninstructed ordinary person who has no regard for the noble ones, is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, who has no regard for true men and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, regards form (rūpa) as self (ātman), or self as possessed of form, or form as in self, or self as in form. He regards feeling (vedanā) as self…perception (saṃjñā) as self…formations (saṃskāra) as self…consciousness (vijñāna) as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness. That is how, bhikkhu, identity view (sakkāyadiṭṭhi) comes to be.”

“But how, venerable sir, does identity view not come to be?”

“Here, bhikkhu, a well-taught noble disciple, who has regard for noble ones and is skilled and disciplined in their Dhamma, who has regard for true men and is skilled and disciplined in their Dhamma, does not regard form as self, or self as possessed of form, or form as in self, or self as in form. He does not regard feeling as self…He does not regard perception as self…He does not regard formations as self…He does not regard consciousness as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness. That is how, bhikkhu, identity view does not come to be.”

Ko viññāṇe assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇa’’nti? ‘‘Yaṃ kho, bhikkhu, rūpaṃ paṭicca uppajjati sukhaṃ somanassaṃ, ayaṃ rūpe assādo. Yaṃ rūpaṃ aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0105"/> vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, ayaṃ rūpe ādīnavo. Yo rūpe chandarāgavinayo chandarāgappahānaṃ, idaṃ rūpe nissaraṇaṃ. Yaṃ kho <note>yañca (syā. kaṃ.)</note>, bhikkhu, vedanaṃ paṭicca… saññaṃ <pb ed="M" n="3.0069"/> paṭicca… saṅkhāre paṭicca… viññāṇaṃ paṭicca uppajjati sukhaṃ somanassaṃ, ayaṃ viññāṇe assādo. Yaṃ viññāṇaṃ aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, ayaṃ viññāṇe ādīnavo. Yo viññāṇe chandarāgavinayo chandarāgappahānaṃ, idaṃ viññāṇe nissaraṇa’’nti.

88. “But what, venerable sir, is the gratification (assāda), what is the danger (ādīnava), and what is the escape (nissaraṇa) in the case of form (rūpa)? What is the gratification, what is the danger, and what is the escape in the case of feeling (vedanā)? What is the gratification, what is the danger, and what is the escape in the case of perception (saṃjñā)? What is the gratification, what is the danger, and what is the escape in the case of volitional formations (saṅkhāra)? What is the gratification, what is the danger, and what is the escape in the case of consciousness (vijñāna)?”

“The pleasure and joy that arise in dependence on form, bhikkhu, this is the gratification in the case of form. That form is impermanent (anitya), suffering (duḥkha), and subject to change (vipariṇāmadhamma), this is the danger in the case of form. The removal of desire and lust, the abandonment of desire and lust for form, this is the escape in the case of form.

Furthermore, bhikkhu, the pleasure and joy that arise in dependence on feeling…in dependence on perception…in dependence on volitional formations…in dependence on consciousness, this is the gratification in the case of consciousness. That consciousness is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change, this is the danger in the case of consciousness. The removal of desire and lust, the abandonment of desire and lust for consciousness, this is the escape in the case of consciousness.”

Yā kāci vedanā… yā kāci saññā… ye keci saṅkhārā… yaṃ kiñci viññāṇaṃ – atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā yaṃ dūre santike vā – sabbaṃ viññāṇaṃ ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti – evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya passati. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, jānato evaṃ passato imasmiñca saviññāṇake kāye bahiddhā ca sabbanimittesu ahaṃkāramamaṃkāramānānusayā na hontī’’ti.

89. “But how, venerable sir, does I-making, mine-making, and the underlying tendency to conceit no longer occur in one who knows and sees thus in regard to this body with its consciousness and in regard to all external signs?”

“Bhikkhu, any kind of form (rūpa) whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal (ajjhattaṃ) or external (bahiddhā), gross (oḷārikaṃ) or subtle (sukhumaṃ), inferior (hīnaṃ) or superior (paṇītaṃ), far (dūre) or near (santike)—all form is to be seen with right wisdom (sammappaññāya) as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ): ‘This is not mine (netaṃ mama), this I am not (nesohamasmi), this is not my self (na meso attā).’

Any kind of feeling (vedanā) whatsoever… Any kind of perception (saññā) whatsoever… Any kind of volitional formations (saṅkhārā) whatsoever… Any kind of consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near—all consciousness is to be seen with right wisdom as it actually is: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’

“When one knows and sees thus, bhikkhu, then in regard to this body with consciousness and in regard to all external signs, I-making (ahaṃkāra), mine-making (mamaṃkāra), and the underlying tendency to conceit (mānānusayā) no longer occur within.”

Evaṃ passaṃ, bhikkhave, sutavā ariyasāvako rūpasmimpi nibbindati, vedanāyapi nibbindati, saññāyapi nibbindati, saṅkhāresupi nibbindati, viññāṇasmimpi nibbindati; nibbindaṃ virajjati <pb ed="T" n="3.0108"/>, virāgā vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānātī’’ti.

<pb ed="P" n="3.0020"/> <pb ed="M" n="3.0070"/> <pb ed="V" n="3.0068"/> <pb ed="T" n="3.0108"/> <p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Imasmiñca pana veyyākaraṇasmiṃ bhaññamāne saṭṭhimattānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ anupādāya āsavehi cittāni vimucciṃsūti.

<p rend="centre">Mahāpuṇṇamasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ navamaṃ.

90. Then, in the mind of a certain bhikkhu this thought arose: "So, it seems, material form (rūpa) is not self (anattā), feeling (vedanā) is not self (anattā), perception (saññā) is not self (anattā), formations (saṅkhārā) are not self (anattā), consciousness (viññāṇa) is not self (anattā). What self (attan), then, will actions (kamma) done by the not-self (anatta) affect?" Then the Blessed One, knowing in his mind the thought in the mind of that bhikkhu, addressed the bhikkhus thus: "It is possible, bhikkhus, that some misguided man here, obtuse and ignorant, with his mind dominated by craving, might think that he can outstrip the Teacher’s Dispensation thus: ‘So, it seems, material form (rūpa) is not self (anattā)…consciousness (viññāṇa) is not self (anattā). What self (attan), then, will actions (kamma) done by the not-self (anatta) affect?" You, bhikkhus, are well-guided by me in those teachings."

"What do you think, bhikkhus, is material form (rūpa) permanent (niccaṃ) or impermanent (aniccaṃ)?"
"Impermanent (aniccaṃ), venerable sir (bhante)."
"But that which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), is that unpleasant (dukkhaṃ) or pleasant (sukhaṃ)?"
"Unpleasant (dukkhaṃ), venerable sir (bhante)."
"That which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), unpleasant (dukkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ), is it suitable to be regarded thus: ‘This is mine (etaṃ mama), this I am (esohamasmi), this is my self (eso me attā)’?"
"No, venerable sir (bhante)."

"What do you think, bhikkhus, is feeling (vedanā)…perception (saññā)…formations (saṅkhārā)…consciousness (viññāṇa) permanent (niccaṃ) or impermanent (aniccaṃ)?"
"Impermanent (aniccaṃ), venerable sir (bhante)."
"But that which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), is that unpleasant (dukkhaṃ) or pleasant (sukhaṃ)?"
"Unpleasant (dukkhaṃ), venerable sir (bhante)."
"That which is impermanent (aniccaṃ), unpleasant (dukkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ), is it suitable to be regarded thus: ‘This is mine (etaṃ mama), this I am (esohamasmi), this is my self (eso me attā)’?"
"No, venerable sir (bhante)."

"Therefore, bhikkhus, any kind of material form (rūpa) whatever, whether past (atīta), future (anāgata), or present (paccuppanna), internal (ajjhattaṃ) or external (bahiddhā), gross (oḷārikaṃ) or subtle (sukhumaṃ), inferior (hīnaṃ) or superior (paṇītaṃ), far (dūre) or near (santike), all material form (rūpa) should be seen as it actually is with proper wisdom (sammappaññāya) thus: ‘This is not mine (netaṃ mama), this I am not (nesohamasmi), this is not my self (na meso attā)’. Any kind of feeling (vedanā) whatsoever… Any kind of perception (saññā) whatsoever… Any kind of volitional formations (saṅkhārā) whatsoever… Any kind of consciousness (viññāṇa) whatsoever, whether past (atīta), future (anāgata), or present (paccuppanna), internal (ajjhattaṃ) or external (bahiddhā), gross (oḷārikaṃ) or subtle (sukhumaṃ), inferior (hīnaṃ) or superior (paṇītaṃ), far (dūre) or near (santike) - one sees all consciousness (viññāṇa) as it really is with correct wisdom (sammappaññāya) thus: ‘This is not mine (netaṃ mama), this I am not (nesohamasmi), this is not my self (na meso attā)’.

Seeing thus, bhikkhus, the instructed noble disciple (ariyasāvako) experiences revulsion (nibbindati) towards form (rūpa), revulsion (nibbindati) towards feeling (vedanā), revulsion (nibbindati) towards perception (saññā), revulsion (nibbindati) towards volitional formations (saṅkhārā), revulsion (nibbindati) towards consciousness (viññāṇa). Experiencing revulsion (nibbindaṃ), he becomes dispassionate (virajjati). Through dispassion (virāgā), his mind is liberated (vimuccati). When it is liberated there comes the knowledge (ñāṇaṃ): ‘It is liberated (vimuttamiti)’. He understands: ‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the spiritual life (brahmacariyaṃ) has been lived, what had to be done (karaṇīyaṃ) has been done, there is no more for this state of being (itthattāyā)’."

This has been said by the Blessed One (bhagavā). Gratified, the bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s words. And while this explanation was being given, the minds of sixty bhikkhus, through no clinging, were fully released from fermentation/effluents (āsava).

The Great Full Moon Discourse (mahāpuṇṇamasuttaṃ), the ninth, is concluded.
