Disvāna āsanaṃ paññāpesi, udakañca pādānaṃ. Nisīdi bhagavā paññatte āsane. Nisajja pāde pakkhālesi. Āyasmāpi kho rāhulo bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi.
107. Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove (veḷuvana), the Squirrels' Sanctuary (kalandakanivāpa).

Now at that time, the venerable Rāhula was staying at Ambalaṭṭhika.

Then, when it was evening, the Blessed One rose from seclusion and went to Ambalaṭṭhika, to where the venerable Rāhula was.

The venerable Rāhula saw the Blessed One coming from afar.

Having seen him, he prepared a seat (āsanaṃ), and water for (washing) the feet (pādānaṃ).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) sat down on the prepared seat (āsane).

Having sat down, he washed his feet (pāde pakkhālesi).

And the venerable Rāhula, having paid homage to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā), sat down at one side.

107. Thus, I have heard: the Ambalaṭṭhikā-rāhulovāda Sutta.

There, ambalaṭṭhikāyaṃ viharatī means dwelling at Ambalaṭṭhikā. On the outskirts of the Veḷuvana monastery, in the abridged meditation house, he dwells, cultivating solitude in a palace with such a name as Ambalaṭṭhikā.

It is as if Kaṇṭaka was sharp from the time of his birth. Just so, this venerable one, too, cultivated solitude while just a novice of seven years, and dwelt there.

paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito means having arisen from fruition attainment.

āsana means there is a seat here, which is simply the conventional designation. Having clapped it, he placed it.

udakādhāne means in a water container.

There is also a reading: udakaṭṭhāne

Venerable Rāhula was addressed, meaning he was addressed for the purpose of giving instruction.

Indeed, the Blessed One gave many Dharma teachings to the Elder Rāhula.

The novice question was spoken by the Elder himself.

Thus, the Rāhula-saṃyutta, the Mahārāhulovāda Sutta, the Cūḷarāhulovāda Sutta—this is the Ambalaṭṭhikā-rāhulovāda Sutta.

Venerable Rāhula was addressed.

Indeed, when this venerable one was seven years old, having grabbed the Blessed One by the edge of his robe, he asked for his inheritance, saying, “Give me my inheritance, samaṇa!” The Blessed One had him ordained by the Dharma-commander, the Elder Sāriputta, and he went forth.

Then, the Blessed One, thinking, “How do these young boys speak what is fitting and unfitting? I will give them instruction,” addressed Rāhula the boy, saying, “It is not fitting for a novice, Rāhula, to speak animal talk. When you speak, you should speak in this way.” [He gave] the ten questions, inseparable from all Buddhas, with fifty-five explanations: “What is one? All beings are sustained by nutriment… What is ten? An arhat is said to be endowed with ten factors.” He spoke this novice question.

Again, he thought, “These young boys are fond of lying. Even though they have not seen, they say, ‘We have seen.’ Even though they have seen, they say, ‘We have not seen.’ I will give them instruction.” Having looked with his eyes, and also for the sake of easy comprehension, first the four water-container similes, then having shown two elephant similes and one mirror simile, he spoke this sutta.

Furthermore, after showing the development of craving in the four conditions, the abandonment of desire and passion in the five sensual qualities, and the greatness of reliance on a good friend, he spoke the Rāhula Sutta.

To show that desire and passion should not be made in the states of coming and going, he spoke the Rāhula-saṃyutta.

“I am beautiful, my sensory sphere of form is pleasing”—to show that desire and passion based on self-existence should not be made, he spoke the Mahārāhulovāda Sutta.

There, it should not be said that the Rāhula Sutta was spoken at such and such a time.

For that was spoken by way of frequent instruction.

The Rāhula-saṃyutta was spoken from the time of being a seven-year-old up to the time of being a homeless mendicant.

The Mahārāhulovāda Sutta was spoken in the eighteenth year of being a novice.
The Cūḷarāhulovāda Sutta was spoken when (Rāhula) was an Avassika Bhikkhu.

The Kumārakapañha and this Ambalaṭṭhikarāhulovāda Sutta were spoken when (Rāhula) was a novice of seven years.

Of these, the Rāhula Sutta is for frequent exhortation, the Rāhula Saṃyutta is for the elder to grasp the heart of insight, the Mahārāhulovāda is for removing desire and passion dependent on a house, the Cūḷarāhulovāda was spoken for the elder to attain arahantship at the time of the maturation of the fifteen liberations and the ripening of the Dhamma.

Furthermore, with reference to this, the Elder Rāhula, while recounting the qualities of the Tathāgata in the midst of the Saṅgha of monks, said this:

"As a hen would guard her eggs, or a yak its fine tail,
So the wise, virtuous, and mindful Tathāgata guarded me." (ApA. 1.2.83)

The Sāmaṇerapañha is for abandoning unsuitable speech, this Ambalaṭṭhikarāhulovāda Sutta was spoken for not intentionally speaking falsehood.

There, passasi no means passasi nu. paritta means little. sāmañña means the quality of a renunciate. nikkujjitvā means having made (it) face down. ukkujjitvā means having made (it) face up.

108. Then the Blessed One left a little water in the water vessel and asked the venerable Rāhula: “Rāhula, do you see this little water left in the water vessel?” “Yes, venerable sir.”

“Even so little, Rāhula, is the recluseship of those who are not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie.”

Then the Blessed One threw away the little water that was left and asked the venerable Rāhula: “Rāhula, do you see that little water that was thrown away?” “Yes, venerable sir.”

“Even so, Rāhula, those who are not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie have thrown away their recluseship.”

Then the Blessed One turned the water vessel upside down and asked the venerable Rāhula: “Rāhula, do you see this water vessel turned upside down?” “Yes, venerable sir.”

“Even so, Rāhula, those who are not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie have turned their recluseship upside down.”

Then the Blessed One turned the water vessel right way up again and asked the venerable Rāhula: “Rāhula, do you see this hollow, empty water vessel?” “Yes, venerable sir.”

“Even so hollow and empty, Rāhula, is the recluseship of those who are not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie.”

“Suppose, Rāhula, there were a royal tusker elephant with tusks as long as chariot poles, full-grown in stature, high-bred, and accustomed to battle. In battle he would perform his task with his forefeet and his hindfeet, with his forequarters and his hindquarters, with his head and his ears, with his tusks and his tail, yet he would keep back his trunk.

Then his rider would think: ‘This royal tusker elephant with tusks as long as chariot poles… performs his task in battle with his forefeet and his hindfeet… yet he keeps back his trunk. He has not yet given up his life.’

But when the royal tusker elephant… performs his task in battle with his forefeet and his hindfeet, with his forequarters and his hindquarters, with his head and his ears, with his tusks and his tail, and also with his trunk, then his rider would think: ‘This royal tusker elephant with tusks as long as chariot poles…performs his task in battle with his forefeet and his hindfeet… and also with his trunk. He has given up his life. Now there is nothing this royal tusker elephant would not do.’

So too, Rāhula, when one is not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie, there is no evil, I say, that one would not do.

Therefore, Rāhula, you should train thus: ‘I will not utter a falsehood even as a joke.’

108. Just as, Rāhula, a king's elephant: this simile is stated for the purpose of showing the attitude of one devoid of restraint in regard to intentional lying.

Therein, īsādanto means having teeth similar to the shafts of a chariot.

Uruḷhavā means well-developed, endowed with height.

Abhijāto means well-born, endowed with [good] birth.

Saṅgāmāvacaro means one who has previously entered battle.

Kammaṃ karotī means causing harm by repeatedly setting in motion what has come and gone.

Moreover, in regard to the eastern body, etc., with the eastern body alone, he fells palisades, storehouses, ramparts, walls, and so on against the opposing army; likewise with the western body.

"Action with the head" means having determined, "I will crush this region," and turning back, he looks around; with just this, even a hundred or a thousand are split in two.

"Action with the ears" means striking and felling with the ears at the sounds that come and go.

"Action with the teeth" means piercing the array of opposing elephants, horses, riders, and foot soldiers, and so on.

"Action with the tail" means cutting and splitting by binding the tail to a long stone pillar or with an iron pestle.

Rakkhateva soṇḍa means he protects by putting the trunk into his mouth.

There, tatthā means in that act of that elephant.

Apariccatta means unrestrained; he thinks, "I see the victory of others and our defeat."

Soṇḍāyapi kammaṃ karotī means he crushes the eighteen-cubit-wide place all around, taking either an iron hammer or a khadira wood pestle.

Pariccatta means restrained; now, among elephant warriors and so on, he fears no one; he thinks, "I see our victory and the defeat of others."

Nāhaṃ tassa kiñci pāpa means there is no evil that he would not do in transgressing offenses such as dukkaṭa, and in actions such as matricide.

Tasmā tiha te means since there is no evil that the intentional liar would not do, therefore you should train yourself, "I will not speak falsely even in jest or for the sake of pleasure."

Paccavekkhaṇattho means having the purpose of looking back, having the purpose of seeing; it has been said to have the purpose of seeing what fault there is in the mouth.

Paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā means having looked back, having looked back.

There, unrestrained, doing action even with the trunk, restrained, I [do] no evil to him, therefore to you, having the purpose of looking back, having looked back, having looked back.

“So too, Rāhula, an action with the body (kāyena karma) should be done after repeated reflection; an action by speech should be done after repeated reflection; an action by mind should be done after repeated reflection.

111.Aṭṭīyitabbanti aṭṭena pīḷitena bhavitabbaṃ. Harāyitabbanti lajjitabbaṃ. Jigucchitabbanti gūthaṃ disvā viya jigucchā uppādetabbā. Manokammassa pana adesanāvatthukattā idha desetabbanti na vuttaṃ. Kittake pana ṭhāne kāyakammavacīkammāni sodhetabbāni, kittake manokammanti. Kāyakammavacīkammāni tāva ekasmiṃ purebhatteyeva sodhetabbāni. Bhattakiccaṃ katvā divāṭṭhāne nisinnena hi paccavekkhitabbaṃ ‘‘aruṇuggamanato paṭṭhāya yāva imasmiṃ ṭhāne nisajjā atthi nu kho me imasmiṃ antare paresaṃ appiyaṃ kāyakammaṃ vā vacīkammaṃ vā’’ti. Sace atthīti jānāti, desanāyuttaṃ desetabbaṃ, āvikaraṇayuttaṃ āvikātabbaṃ. Sace natthi, teneva pītipāmojjena vihātabbaṃ. Manokammaṃ pana etasmiṃ piṇḍapātapariyesanaṭṭhāne sodhetabbaṃ. Kathaṃ? ‘‘Atthi nu kho me ajja piṇḍapātapariyesanaṭṭhāne rūpādīsu chando vā rāgo vā paṭighaṃ vā’’ti? Sace atthi, ‘‘puna na evaṃ karissāmī’’ti citteneva adhiṭṭhātabbaṃ. Sace natthi, teneva pītipāmojjena vihātabbaṃ.
111. Aṭṭīyitabba means one should be oppressed, afflicted by oppression. Harāyitabba means one should be ashamed. Jigucchitabba means disgust should be produced, as if seeing excrement. But because mental action (manokamma) is not the subject of confession, it is not said here that it should be confessed.

Now, in how many instances should bodily and verbal actions be purified, and in how many mental action? Bodily and verbal actions should be purified in a single forenoon. After performing the meal duties, one sitting in a place for the day should review: "From the rising of the dawn until sitting in this place, is there any bodily or verbal action that is displeasing to others?" If one knows that there is, one should confess what is fitting for confession, and reveal what is fitting for revelation. If there is not, one should abide in that same joy and delight.

But mental action should be purified in this place of seeking alms. How? "Is there today any desire, lust, or aversion in me towards forms, etc., in this place of seeking alms?" If there is, one should resolve in one's mind, "I will not do this again." If there is not, one should abide in that same joy and delight.

112. ‘‘Ye hi keci, rāhula, atītamaddhānaṃ samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā kāyakammaṃ parisodhesuṃ, vacīkammaṃ parisodhesuṃ, manokammaṃ parisodhesuṃ, sabbe te evamevaṃ paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā kāyakammaṃ parisodhesuṃ, paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā vacīkammaṃ parisodhesuṃ, paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā manokammaṃ parisodhesuṃ. Yepi hi keci, rāhula, anāgatamaddhānaṃ samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā kāyakammaṃ parisodhessanti, vacīkammaṃ parisodhessanti, manokammaṃ parisodhessanti, sabbe te evamevaṃ paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā kāyakammaṃ parisodhessanti, paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā vacīkammaṃ parisodhessanti , paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā manokammaṃ parisodhessanti. Yepi hi keci, rāhula, etarahi samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā kāyakammaṃ parisodhenti, vacīkammaṃ parisodhenti, manokammaṃ parisodhenti, sabbe te evamevaṃ paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā kāyakammaṃ parisodhenti, paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā vacīkammaṃ parisodhenti, paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā manokammaṃ parisodhenti. Tasmātiha, rāhula, ‘paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā kāyakammaṃ parisodhessāmi, paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā vacīkammaṃ parisodhessāmi, paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā manokammaṃ parisodhessāmī’ti – evañhi te, rāhula, sikkhitabba’’nti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā rāhulo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Ambalaṭṭhikarāhulovādasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.
112. “Rāhula, whatever recluses (śramaṇa) and brahmins (brāhmaṇa) in the past purified their bodily action (kāyakamma), their verbal action (vacīkamma), and their mental action (manokamma), all did so by repeatedly reflecting (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) thus. They repeatedly reflected (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purified their bodily action (kāyakamma), repeatedly reflected (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purified their verbal action (vacīkamma), and repeatedly reflected (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purified their mental action (manokamma).

“Rāhula, whatever recluses (śramaṇa) and brahmins (brāhmaṇa) in the future will purify their bodily action (kāyakamma), their verbal action (vacīkamma), and their mental action (manokamma), all will do so by repeatedly reflecting (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) thus. They will repeatedly reflect (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purify their bodily action (kāyakamma), repeatedly reflect (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purify their verbal action (vacīkamma), and repeatedly reflect (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purify their mental action (manokamma).

“Rāhula, whatever recluses (śramaṇa) and brahmins (brāhmaṇa) in the present are purifying their bodily action (kāyakamma), their verbal action (vacīkamma), and their mental action (manokamma), all are doing so by repeatedly reflecting (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) thus. They repeatedly reflect (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purify their bodily action (kāyakamma), repeatedly reflect (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purify their verbal action (vacīkamma), and repeatedly reflect (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) and purify their mental action (manokamma).

“Therefore, Rāhula, you should train thus: ‘We will purify our bodily action (kāyakamma), our verbal action (vacīkamma), and our mental action (manokamma) by repeatedly reflecting (paccavekkhitvā paccavekkhitvā) upon them.’“

The Buddha spoke thus. The venerable Rāhula was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Ambalaṭṭhikarāhulovādasuttaṃ, the first, is finished.
112.Samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vāti buddhā vā paccekabuddhā vā tathāgatasāvakā vā. Tasmātihāti yasmā atītepi evaṃ parisodhesuṃ, anāgatepi parisodhessanti, etarahipi parisodhenti, tasmā tumhehipi tesaṃ anusikkhantehi evaṃ sikkhitabbanti attho. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānameva. Imaṃ pana desanaṃ bhagavā yāva bhavaggā ussitassa ratanarāsino yojaniyamaṇikkhandhena kūṭaṃ gaṇhanto viya neyyapuggalavasena pariniṭṭhāpesīti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Ambalaṭṭhikarāhulovādasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
112. Śramaṇas or brāhmaṇas, that is, Buddhas, or pratyekabuddhas, or disciples of the Tathāgata.

Tasmātihā means: Because in the past they also investigated in this way, and in the future they will investigate, and now they are investigating, therefore you too should learn in this way, that is the meaning.

The rest is clear throughout.

Moreover, the Blessed One brought this teaching to completion in accordance with those persons who are to be guided, up to the summit of existence, as if he were seizing the peak with a measuring jewel-pillar of a heap of jewels that has been raised up.

The Commentary to the Papañcasūdanī of the Majjhimanikāya is completed.

The Commentary on the Ambalaṭṭhikarāhulovādasutta is finished.
