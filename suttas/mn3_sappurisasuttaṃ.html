Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jetavana at Anāthapiṇḍika’s park.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: “Monks.”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: “Bhikkhus, I shall teach you the character of a true man (satpuruṣadharma) and the character of an untrue man (asatpuruṣadharma).

Listen, pay close attention. I will speak.”

“As you say, lord,” the monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Bhikkhus, what is the character of an untrue man (asatpuruṣadharma)? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man (asatpuruṣa) has gone forth from an aristocratic family (uccākula).

He considers thus: ‘I have gone forth from an aristocratic family (uccākula); but these other bhikkhus have not gone forth from aristocratic families (uccākula).’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his aristocratic family (uccākulīnatā).

This, monks, is the character of an untrue man (asatpuruṣadharma).

But a true man (satpuruṣa) considers thus: ‘It is not because of one’s aristocratic family (uccākulīnatā) that states of greed ( lobhadharma), hatred (doṣadharma), or delusion (mohadharma) are destroyed.

Even though someone has not gone forth from an aristocratic family (uccākula); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the dharma, entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanna), and conducts himself according to the dharma (anudhammacārī), he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his aristocratic family (uccākulīnatā).

This, monks, is the character of a true man (satpuruṣadharma).”

“Again, bhikkhus, an untrue man (asatpuruṣa) has gone forth from a wealthy family (mahākula)…pe… As with the aristocratic family (uccākulavāra), so it should be explained in detail. He has gone forth from a family of great wealth (mahābhogakula)…pe… He has gone forth from an influential family (uḷārabhogakula).

He considers thus: ‘I have gone forth from an influential family (uḷārabhogakula); but these other bhikkhus have not gone forth from influential families (uḷārabhogakula).’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his influential family (uḷārabhogatā).

This too, monks, is the character of an untrue man (asatpuruṣadharma).

But a true man (satpuruṣa) considers thus: ‘It is not because of one’s influential family (uḷārabhogatā) that states of greed (lobhadharma) hatred (doṣadharma), or delusion (mohadharma) are destroyed.

Even though someone has not gone forth from an influential family (uḷārabhogakula); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the dharma, entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the dharma (anudhammacārī), he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his influential family (uḷārabhogatā).

This too, monks, is the character of a true man (satpuruṣadharma).

105. It is like this: sappurisadhammasuttaṃ.

There, sappurisadhamma means the dharma of the sappurisas.

asappurisadhamma means the dharma of the pāpapurisas.

Thus, having established the matrix, again, just as a person skilled in the path relinquishes the left and grasps the right.

First he tells what should be relinquished, thus, teaching first the dharma that should be abandoned, [saying]: "Which, O monks, is the asappurisadhamma?" and so forth.

There, uccākulā means khattiyakulā or brāhmaṇakulā.

Indeed, this pair of families is called "uccākula."

so tattha pujjo means that bhikkhu is worthy of honor among those bhikkhus.

antaraṃ karitvā means having made an interior.

mahākulā means khattiyakulā or brāhmaṇakulā or vessakulā.

Indeed, this triad of families is called "mahākula."

mahābhogakulā means families endowed with great bhogas.

uḷārabhogakulā means families endowed with excellent and refined bhogas.

In these two lines, even four families are obtained.

Wherever one is born, through the power of merit, one becomes mahābhogopi and uḷārabhogopi.

106. “Furthermore, bhikkhus, an untrue man is known and famous (ñāto yasassī).

He considers thus: ‘I am known and famous (ñāto yasassī); but these other bhikkhus are unknown and insignificant.’

On account of that recognition, he lauds himself and disparages others.

This, too, bhikkhus, is the characteristic of an untrue man (asappurisadhamma).

But a true man (sappurisa), bhikkhus, considers thus: ‘It is not because of being known that states of greed (lobhadhamma), hatred (dosadhamma), or delusion (mohadhamma) are destroyed.

Even though someone may not be known and famous (ñāto yasassī); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the dharma (dhammānudhammappaṭipanno), entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the dharma (anudhammacārī), he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of his recognition.

This, too, bhikkhus, is the characteristic of a true man (sappurisadhamma).

“Furthermore, bhikkhus, an untrue man gains robes (cīvara), almsfood (piṇḍapāta), resting places (senāsana), and requisites of medicine (gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāra).

He considers thus: ‘I gain robes (cīvara), almsfood (piṇḍapāta), resting places (senāsana), and requisites of medicine (gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāra); but these other bhikkhus do not gain these things.’

So he lauds himself and disparages others because of gain (lābha).

This, too, bhikkhus, is the characteristic of an untrue man (asappurisadhamma).

But a true man (sappurisa), bhikkhus, considers thus: ‘It is not because of gain (lābha) that states of greed (lobhadhamma), hatred (dosadhamma), or delusion (mohadhamma) are destroyed.

Even though someone has no gain (lābha), yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the dharma (dhammānudhammappaṭipanno), entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the dharma (anudhammacārī), he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of gain (lābha).

This, too, bhikkhus, is the characteristic of a true man (sappurisadhamma).

“Furthermore, bhikkhus, an untrue man is learned (bahussuto).

He considers thus: ‘I am learned (bahussuto); but these other bhikkhus are not learned (bahussutā).’

So, on account of that great learning (bāhusacca), he lauds himself and disparages others.

This, too, bhikkhus, is the characteristic of an untrue man (asappurisadhamma).

But a true man (sappurisa), bhikkhus, considers thus: ‘It is not because of great learning (bāhusacca) that states of greed (lobhadhamma), hatred (dosadhamma), or delusion (mohadhamma) are destroyed.

Even though someone may not be learned (bahussuto); yet if he has entered upon the way that accords with the dharma (dhammānudhammappaṭipanno), entered upon the proper way (sāmīcippaṭipanno), and conducts himself according to the dharma (anudhammacārī), he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.’

So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of that great learning (bāhusacca).

This, too, bhikkhus, is the characteristic of a true man (sappurisadhamma).

“Furthermore, bhikkhus, an untrue man is a master of the Vinaya (vinayadhara).
He considers thus: ‘I am an expert in the Discipline (vinayadhara); but these other monks are not experts in the Discipline.’
So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his being an expert in the Discipline.
This too, monks, is the characteristic of an untrue man.
But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘It is not because of being an expert in the Discipline that greed (lobha), hatred (dosa), or delusion (moha) diminish.’
Even though someone may not be an expert in the Discipline, if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dhamma, entered upon the proper way, and conducts himself according to the Dhamma, he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.
So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of being an expert in the Discipline.
This too, monks, is the characteristic of a true man.

Moreover, monks, an untrue man is a preacher of the Dhamma (dhammakathika).
He considers thus: ‘I am a preacher of the Dhamma; but these other monks are not preachers of the Dhamma.’
So he lauds himself and disparages others because of his being a preacher of the Dhamma.
This too, monks, is the characteristic of an untrue man.
But a true man, monks, considers thus: ‘It is not because of being a preacher of the Dhamma that greed, hatred, or delusion diminish.’
Even though someone may not be a preacher of the Dhamma, if he has entered upon the way that accords with the Dhamma, entered upon the proper way, and conducts himself according to the Dhamma, he should be honored for that, he should be praised for that.
So, putting the practice of the way first, he neither lauds himself nor disparages others because of being a preacher of the Dhamma.
This too, monks, is the characteristic of a true man.

“Moreover, monks, an untrue man is a forest dweller (āraññika). “Moreover, bhikkhus, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, an untrue man enters upon and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion.

108. “Atammayatā” means taṇhā is called craving, the meaning is without craving (nittaṇhā).
"Atammayataññeva antaraṃ karitvā" means having made absence of craving the cause, or having made it internal, the meaning is producing it in the mind (citte uppādetvāti attho).

In the chapter on cessation (nirodhavāre), since only non-returners and those with extinguished influxes attain that attainment (samāpattiṃ samāpajjanti), that does not exist for an ordinary person (puthujjanassa sā natthi), therefore the chapter on the bad person is deficient (asappurisavāro parihīno).
"Na kañci maññatī" means he does not consider any person with the three kinds of conceits (tīhi maññanāhi na maññati).
"Na kuhiñci maññatī" means he does not consider in any place (kismiñci okāse na maññati).
"Na kenaci maññatī" means he does not consider that person with anything (kenaci vatthunāpi taṃ puggalaṃ na maññati).
The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti).

Na kañci maññatī, na kuhiñci maññatī, na kenaci maññatī.
