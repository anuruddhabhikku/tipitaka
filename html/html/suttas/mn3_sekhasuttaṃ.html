22. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sakkesu viharati kapilavatthusmiṃ nigrodhārāme. Tena kho pana samayena kāpilavatthavānaṃ kapilavatthuvāsīnaṃ (ka.) sakyānaṃ navaṃ santhāgāraṃ acirakāritaṃ hoti anajjhāvuṭṭhaṃ anajjhāvutthaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) samaṇena vā brāhmaṇena vā kenaci vā manussabhūtena. Atha kho kāpilavatthavā sakyā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho kāpilavatthavā sakyā bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘idha, bhante, kāpilavatthavānaṃ sakyānaṃ navaṃ santhāgāraṃ acirakāritaṃ acirakāritaṃ hoti (syā. kaṃ. ka.) anajjhāvuṭṭhaṃ samaṇena vā brāhmaṇena vā kenaci vā manussabhūtena. Taṃ, bhante, bhagavā paṭhamaṃ paribhuñjatu. Bhagavatā paṭhamaṃ paribhuttaṃ pacchā kāpilavatthavā sakyā paribhuñjissanti. Tadassa kāpilavatthavānaṃ sakyānaṃ dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāyā’’ti . Adhivāsesi bhagavā tuṇhībhāvena. Atha kho kāpilavatthavā sakyā bhagavato adhivāsanaṃ viditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā yena navaṃ santhāgāraṃ tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā sabbasanthariṃ santhāgāraṃ sabbasanthariṃ santhataṃ (ka.) santharitvā āsanāni paññapetvā udakamaṇikaṃ upaṭṭhapetvā telappadīpaṃ āropetvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhaṃsu. Ekamantaṃ ṭhitā kho kāpilavatthavā sakyā bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘sabbasanthariṃ santhataṃ, bhante, santhāgāraṃ, āsanāni paññattāni, udakamaṇiko upaṭṭhāpito, telappadīpo āropito. Yassadāni, bhante , bhagavā kālaṃ maññatī’’ti. Atha kho bhagavā nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṅghena yena santhāgāraṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā pāde pakkhāletvā santhāgāraṃ pavisitvā majjhimaṃ thambhaṃ nissāya puratthābhimukho nisīdi. Bhikkhusaṅghopi kho pāde pakkhāletvā santhāgāraṃ pavisitvā pacchimaṃ bhittiṃ nissāya puratthābhimukho nisīdi, bhagavantaṃyeva purakkhatvā. Kāpilavatthavāpi kho sakyā pāde pakkhāletvā santhāgāraṃ pavisitvā puratthimaṃ bhittiṃ nissāya pacchimābhimukhā nisīdiṃsu, bhagavantaṃyeva purakkhatvā. Atha kho bhagavā kāpilavatthave sakye bahudeva rattiṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassetvā samādapetvā samuttejetvā sampahaṃsetvā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘paṭibhātu taṃ, ānanda, kāpilavatthavānaṃ sakyānaṃ sekho pāṭipado paṭipado (syā. kaṃ. ka.). Piṭṭhi me āgilāyati; tamahaṃ āyamissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato paccassosi. Atha kho bhagavā catugguṇaṃ saṅghāṭiṃ paññāpetvā dakkhiṇena passena sīhaseyyaṃ kappesi, pāde pādaṃ accādhāya, sato sampajāno, uṭṭhānasaññaṃ manasi karitvā.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the Sakyan country at Kapilavatthu in Nigrodha’s Park.

Now on that occasion a new assembly hall had recently been built for the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu, and it had not yet been inhabited by any recluse, brāhmaṇa, or human being. Then the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, they sat down at one side. Seated at one side, the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu said to the Blessed One: “Here, venerable sir, a new assembly hall has recently been built for the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu, and it has not yet been inhabited by any recluse, brāhmaṇa, or human being. May the Blessed One be the first to use it. When the Blessed One has used it first, the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu will use it afterwards. This will be for their benefit and happiness for a long time.” The Blessed One consented by remaining silent.

Then, when the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu understood that the Blessed One had consented, they got up from their seats, and after paying homage to him and keeping him on their right, they went to the new assembly hall. Having arrived, they covered the assembly hall completely with coverings and prepared seats, and they put out a water-pot and lit an oil lamp. Then they went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, they stood at one side. Standing at one side, the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, the assembly hall has been covered completely with coverings, seats have been arranged, a water-pot has been put out, and an oil lamp has been lit. May the Blessed One come at his convenience.”

Then the Blessed One dressed, and taking his bowl and robe, he went together with the bhikkhu-saṅgha to the assembly hall. Having arrived, he washed his feet, entered the assembly hall, and sat down facing east, leaning against the central pillar. The bhikkhu-saṅgha also washed their feet, entered the assembly hall, and sat down facing east, leaning against the western wall, with the Blessed One in front of them. The Sakyans of Kapilavatthu also washed their feet, entered the assembly hall, and sat down facing west, leaning against the eastern wall, with the Blessed One in front of them.

Then, after instructing, encouraging, rousing, and gladdening the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu far into the night with a talk on the dharma, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Ānanda, let the progressive practice be apparent to the Sakyans of Kapilavatthu. My back is aching; I will stretch it.”

“So be it, venerable sir,” the venerable Ānanda replied to the Blessed One.

Then the Blessed One folded his outer robe into four and lay down on his right side, in the lion's posture, with one foot on top of the other, mindful, clearly comprehending, having in mind the thought of rising.

24. “And how, Mahānāma, is a noble disciple possessed of virtue (śīlasampanno)? Here, Mahānāma, a noble disciple is virtuous (śīlavā), dwells restrained with the restraint of the Pāṭimokkha (pātimokkhasaṃvarasaṃvuto), is perfect in conduct and resort (ācāragocarasampanno), and seeing fear in the slightest fault, trains by undertaking the training precepts (sikkhāpadesu). This is how, Mahānāma, a noble disciple is possessed of virtue (śīlasampanno).

“And how, Mahānāma, does a noble disciple guard the doors of his senses (indriyesu guttadvāro)? Here, Mahānāma, a noble disciple, on seeing a form (rūpaṃ) with the eye (cakkhunā), does not grasp at its signs (na nimittaggāhī) and features (nānubyañjanaggāhī). Since, if he were to dwell without restraint over the faculty of the eye (cakkhundriyaṃ), evil unwholesome states such as covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassā) might assail him, he practises restraint (saṃvarāya paṭipajjati), guards the faculty of the eye (cakkhundriyaṃ), and achieves restraint over the faculty of the eye (cakkhundriye saṃvaraṃ āpajjati). On hearing a sound (saddaṃ) with the ear (sotena)…On smelling an odour (gandhaṃ) with the nose (ghānena)…On tasting a flavour (rasaṃ) with the tongue (jivhāya)…On touching a tangible object (phoṭṭhabbaṃ) with the body (kāyena)…On cognizing a mind-object (dhammaṃ) with the mind (manasā), he does not grasp at its major signs (nimittaggāhī) or secondary characteristics (nānubyañjanaggāhī). Since, if he were to dwell without restraint over the faculty of the intellect (manindriyaṃ), unskillful qualities such as greed (abhijjhā) or distress (domanassā) might assail him. He practices with restraint (saṃvarāya paṭipajjati). He guards the faculty of the intellect (manindriyaṃ). He achieves restraint with regard to the faculty of the intellect (manindriye saṃvaraṃ āpajjati). This is how, Mahānāma, a noble disciple guards the doors of his sense faculties (indriyesu guttadvāro).

“And how, Mahānāma, is a noble disciple moderate in eating (bhojane mattaññū)? Here, Mahānāma, a noble disciple, reflecting wisely (paṭisaṅkhā yoniso), partakes of food (āhāraṃ āhāreti) neither for amusement (neva davāya) nor for intoxication (na madāya) nor for the sake of physical beauty (na maṇḍanāya) and attractiveness (na vibhūsanāya), but only for the endurance (ṭhitiyā) and continuance (yāpanāya) of this body (kāyassa), for ending discomfort (vihiṃsūparatiyā), and for assisting the holy life (brahmacariyānuggahāya), considering: ‘Thus I shall terminate old feelings (purāṇañca vedanaṃ paṭihaṅkhāmi) without arousing new feelings (navañca vedanaṃ na uppādessāmi) and I shall be healthy (yātrā ca me bhavissati) and blameless (anavajjatā ca) and shall live in comfort (phāsuvihāro ca).’ This is how, Mahānāma, a noble disciple is moderate in eating (bhojane mattaññū).

“And how, Mahānāma, is a noble disciple devoted to wakefulness (jāgariyaṃ anuyutto)? Here, Mahānāma, a noble disciple during the day (divasaṃ) purifies his mind (cittaṃ parisodheti) of obstructive states (āvaraṇīyehi dhammehi) while walking back and forth (caṅkamena) and sitting (nisajjāya). In the first watch (paṭhamaṃ yāmaṃ) of the night (rattiyā), he purifies his mind (cittaṃ parisodheti) of obstructive states (āvaraṇīyehi dhammehi) while walking back and forth (caṅkamena) and sitting (nisajjāya). In the middle watch (majjhimaṃ yāmaṃ) of the night (rattiyā) he assumes the lion’s pose (sīhaseyyaṃ kappeti) on his right side (dakkhiṇena passena), placing one foot (pāde) over the other (pādaṃ accādhāya), mindful (sato) and fully aware (sampajāno), after noting in his mind (manasi karitvā) the time for rising (uṭṭhānasaññaṃ). In the last watch (pacchimaṃ yāmaṃ) of the night (rattiyā), after rising (paccuṭṭhāya), he purifies his mind (cittaṃ parisodheti) of obstructive states (āvaraṇīyehi dhammehi) while walking back and forth (caṅkamena) and sitting (nisajjāya). This is how, Mahānāma, a noble disciple is devoted to wakefulness (jāgariyaṃ anuyutto).

Vijjā nāma bahu anekavidhā, vipassanañāṇādīni aṭṭha ñāṇāni, tattha idampi ñāṇaṃ ekā vijjātipi attho. Padattho pana vinivijjhitvā etāya jānātīti vijjā. Esa nayo sabbattha. Vijjāsampanno itipīti tīhi vijjāhi vijjāsampanno itipi. Caraṇasampanno itipīti pañcadasahi dhammehi caraṇasampanno itipi. Tadubhayena pana vijjācaraṇasampanno itipīti. Idampissa hoti vijjāyā vijjā Vijjāsampanno itipī Caraṇasampanno itipī
29. “Idampissa hoti caraṇasmi” means: this is the sīla whose conduct is named for that monk.
"Caraṇaṃ" means much and various. Sīla, etc., are the fifteen qualities; and this, there, is one aspect of conduct.
Furthermore, padattho means: one conducts oneself, and by that one goes to a place never gone before; that is caraṇaṃ.
This method [applies] everywhere.

“Idampissa hoti vijjāyā” means: this is the knowledge of previous existences; vijjā is its name.
Vijjā means much and various. Vipassanāñāṇa, etc., are the eight ñāṇas; this ñāṇa is also one aspect of vidyā.
Furthermore, padattho means: having pierced through, one knows by this; that is vijjā.
This method [applies] everywhere.
"Vijjāsampanno itipī" means: one is endowed with vidyā by these three vidyās.
"Caraṇasampanno itipī" means: one is endowed with conduct by these fifteen qualities (dhammas).
Therefore, by both, one is endowed with both vidyā and caraṇa.

"Idampissa hoti vijjāyā, vijjā, vijjāsampanno itipī, caraṇasampanno itipī."

30. ‘‘Brahmunāpesā, mahānāma, sanaṅkumārena gāthā bhāsitā – ‘Khattiyo seṭṭho janetasmiṃ, ye gottapaṭisārino; Vijjācaraṇasampanno, so seṭṭho devamānuse’ti. ‘‘Sā kho panesā, mahānāma, brahmunā sanaṅkumārena gāthā sugītā no duggītā, subhāsitā no dubbhāsitā, atthasaṃhitā no anatthasaṃhitā, anumatā bhagavatā’’ti. Atha kho bhagavā uṭṭhahitvā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘sādhu sādhu, ānanda, sādhu kho tvaṃ, ānanda, kāpilavatthavānaṃ sakyānaṃ sekhaṃ pāṭipadaṃ abhāsī’’ti. Idamavocāyasmā ānando. Samanuñño satthā ahosi. Attamanā kāpilavatthavā sakyā āyasmato ānandassa bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Sekhasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ tatiyaṃ.
30. “Mahānāma, this verse was spoken by the Brahmā Sanankumāra:

‘The kṣatriya is the best among people who value lineage;
But he who is accomplished in knowledge and conduct is the best among gods and humans.’

“Now that stanza was well sung by the Brahmā Sanankumāra, not ill-sung; it was well spoken, not ill-spoken; it has a meaning, and is not meaningless; and it was approved by the Blessed One.”

Then the Blessed One rose and addressed the venerable Ānanda thus: “Good, good, Ānanda! It is good that you have spoken to the Śākyans of Kapilavatthu about the disciple in higher training who has entered upon the way.”

This is what the venerable Ānanda said. The Teacher approved of it, and the Śākyans of Kapilavatthu were satisfied and delighted in the venerable Ānanda’s words.

The Third Sekhasutta is finished.
30.Sanaṅkumārenāti porāṇakakumārena, cirakālato paṭṭhāya kumāroti paññātena. So kira manussapathe pañcacūḷakakumārakakāle jhānaṃ nibbattetvā aparihīnajjhāno brahmaloke nibbatti, tassa so attabhāvo piyo ahosi manāpo, tasmā tādiseneva attabhāvena carati, tena naṃ sanaṅkumāroti sañjānanti. Janetasminti janitasmiṃ, pajāyāti attho. Ye gottapaṭisārinoti ye janetasmiṃ gottaṃ paṭisaranti ‘‘ahaṃ gotamo, ahaṃ kassapo’’ti, tesu loke gottapaṭisārīsu khattiyo seṭṭho. Anumatā bhagavatāti mama pañhabyākaraṇena saddhiṃ saṃsanditvā desitāti ambaṭṭhasutte buddhena bhagavatā ‘‘ahampi, ambaṭṭha, evaṃ vadāmi – ‘Khattiyo seṭṭho janetasmiṃ, ye gottapaṭisārino; Vijjācaraṇasampanno, so seṭṭho devamānuse’ti’’. (dī. ni. 1.277) – Evaṃ bhāsantena anuññātā anumoditā. Sādhu sādhu ānandāti, bhagavā kira ādito paṭṭhāya niddaṃ anokkamantova imaṃ suttaṃ sutvā ānandena sekhapaṭipadāya kūṭaṃ gahitanti ñatvā uṭṭhāya pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā nisinno sādhukāraṃ adāsi. Ettāvatā ca pana idaṃ suttaṃ jinabhāsitaṃ nāma jātaṃ. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Sādhu sādhu ānandā Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Sekhasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
30. “Sanaṅkumārenā” means by the ancient youth (porāṇakakumāra), he is known as a youth since a long time (cirakālato paṭṭhāya kumāroti paññātena). It is said that he, having developed jhāna in the human realm (manussapathe) at the time of being a five-crested youth (pañcacūḷakakumārakakāle jhānaṃ nibbattetvā), and being of unfaded jhāna (aparihīnajjhāno), was reborn in the Brahma world (brahmaloke nibbatti). That state (attabhāvo) was dear and pleasing to him (piyo ahosi manāpo), therefore he lives with that same state (tasmā tādiseneva attabhāvena carati), therefore they recognize him as "Sanaṅkumāra" (tena naṃ sanaṅkumāroti sañjānanti).

"Janetasminti" means in the one who is born (janitasmiṃ), it means offspring (pajāyāti attho).

"Ye gottapaṭisārinoti" means those who, in the one who is born (ye janetasmiṃ), recall the lineage (gottaṃ paṭisaranti) "I am a Gotama, I am a Kassapa," among those who recall lineage in the world (tesu loke gottapaṭisārīsu), a kṣatriya is the best (khattiyo śreṣṭho).

"Anumatā bhagavatāti" means it was taught having agreed and discussed with my question and answer (mama pañhabyākaraṇena saddhiṃ saṃsanditvā desitāti) in the Ambaṭṭha Sutta (ambaṭṭhasutte) by the Buddha, the Blessed One (buddhena bhagavatā): "I too, Ambaṭṭha, say thus:

'A kṣatriya is the best in the one who is born (khattiyo śreṣṭho janetasmiṃ), those who recall lineage (ye gottapaṭisārino);

Endowed with knowledge and conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), he is the best among gods and humans (so śreṣṭho devamānuse'ti.'"

(Dīgha Nikāya 1.277) - Thus, it was approved and rejoiced over by the one who was speaking (evaṃ bhāsantena anuññātā anumoditā).

"Sādhu sādhu ānandāti," it is said that the Blessed One (bhagavā kira), from the beginning onwards (ādito paṭṭhāya), having heard this Sutta (imaṃ suttaṃ sutvā) while not yet emerging from sleep (niddaṃ anokkamantova), knowing that Ānanda had grasped the essence in the practice of a trainee (ānandena sekhapaṭipadāya kūṭaṃ gahitanti ñatvā), arose, arranged his seat, and sitting down (uṭṭhāya pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā nisinno), gave approval (sādhukāraṃ adāsi).

By this much, this Sutta became known as the speech of the Victor (ettāvatā ca pana idaṃ suttaṃ jinabhāsitaṃ nāma jātaṃ).

The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti).

Sādhu sādhu ānandā
