375. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā uruññāyaṃ ujuññāyaṃ (sī. pī.), udaññāyaṃ (syā. kaṃ.) viharati kaṇṇakatthale migadāye. Tena kho pana samayena rājā pasenadi kosalo uruññaṃ anuppatto hoti kenacideva karaṇīyena. Atha kho rājā pasenadi kosalo aññataraṃ purisaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘ehi tvaṃ, ambho purisa, yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkama; upasaṅkamitvā mama vacanena bhagavato pāde sirasā vandāhi, appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ puccha – ‘rājā, bhante, pasenadi kosalo bhagavato pāde sirasā vandati, appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ pucchatī’ti. Evañca vadehi – ‘ajja kira, bhante, rājā pasenadi kosalo pacchābhattaṃ bhuttapātarāso bhagavantaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissatī’’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, devā’’ti kho so puriso rañño pasenadissa kosalassa paṭissutvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho so puriso bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘rājā, bhante, pasenadi kosalo bhagavato pāde sirasā vandati, appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ pucchati; evañca vadeti – ‘ajja kira bhante, rājā pasenadi kosalo pacchābhattaṃ bhuttapātarāso bhagavantaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissatī’’’ti. Assosuṃ kho somā ca bhaginī sakulā ca bhaginī – ‘‘ajja kira rājā pasenadi kosalo pacchābhattaṃ bhuttapātarāso bhagavantaṃ dassanāya upasaṅkamissatī’’ti. Atha kho somā ca bhaginī sakulā ca bhaginī rājānaṃ pasenadiṃ kosalaṃ bhattābhihāre upasaṅkamitvā etadavocuṃ – ‘‘tena hi, mahārāja, amhākampi vacanena bhagavato pāde sirasā vandāhi, appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ puccha – ‘somā ca, bhante, bhaginī sakulā ca bhaginī bhagavato pāde sirasā vandati, appābādhaṃ appātaṅkaṃ lahuṭṭhānaṃ balaṃ phāsuvihāraṃ pucchatī’’’ti.
375. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Ujunna (Uruññā), in the Kannakatthala Deer Park.

Now on that occasion King Pasenadi of Kosala had arrived at Ujunna for some business or other.

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala addressed a certain man: “Come, good man, go to the Blessed One and pay homage in my name with your head at his feet, and ask whether he is free from illness and affliction, and is healthy, strong, and abiding in comfort, saying: ‘Venerable sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala pays homage with his head at the Blessed One’s feet, and he asks whether the Blessed One is free from illness…and abiding in comfort.’

And say this: ‘Venerable sir, today King Pasenadi of Kosala will come to see the Blessed One after he has had his breakfast.’”

“Yes, sire,” the man replied, and he went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, he sat down at one side.

Sitting at one side, the man said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, King Pasenadi of Kosala pays homage with his head at the Blessed One’s feet, and he asks whether he is free from illness and affliction, and is healthy, strong, and abiding in comfort; and he says this: ‘Venerable sir, today King Pasenadi of Kosala will come to see the Blessed One after he has had his breakfast.’”

The sisters Soma and Sakula heard: “Today King Pasenadi of Kosala will go to see the Blessed One after he has had his breakfast.”

Then the sisters Soma and Sakula went to King Pasenadi of Kosala while the meal was being served, and said to him: “Sire, pay homage in our name with your head at the Blessed One’s feet, and ask whether he is free from illness and affliction, and is healthy, strong, and abiding in comfort, saying: ‘Venerable sir, the sisters Soma and Sakula pay homage with their heads at the Blessed One’s feet, and they ask whether he is free from illness…and abiding in comfort.’”
375.Evaṃme sutanti kaṇṇakatthalasuttaṃ. Tattha uruññāyanti uruññāti tassa raṭṭhassapi nagarassapi etadeva nāmaṃ, bhagavā uruññānagaraṃ upanissāya viharati. Kaṇṇakatthale migadāyeti tassa nagarassa avidūre kaṇṇakatthalaṃ nāma eko ramaṇīyo bhūmibhāgo atthi, so migānaṃ abhayatthāya dinnattā migadāyoti vuccati, tasmiṃ kaṇṇakatthale migadāye. Kenacideva karaṇīyenāti na aññena, anantarasutte vuttakaraṇīyeneva. Somā ca bhaginī sakulā ca bhaginīti imā dve bhaginiyo rañño pajāpatiyo. Bhattābhihāreti bhattaṃ abhiharaṇaṭṭhāne. Rañño bhuñjanaṭṭhānañhi sabbāpi orodhā kaṭacchuādīni gahetvā rājānaṃ upaṭṭhātuṃ gacchanti, tāpi tatheva agamaṃsu.
375. Thus, "Thus I have heard" is the Kaṇṇakatthala Sutta.

Therein, "to Uruññā" means Uruññā is the name of both that district and city. The Blessed One dwelt near the city of Uruññā.

"At Kaṇṇakatthala in the Deer Park" means not far from that city, there is a delightful region called Kaṇṇakatthala, which is called Deer Park ( migadāya ) because it was given for the safety of the deer. [He dwelt] at that Kaṇṇakatthala in the Deer Park.

"By some duty" means not by another, but just by the duty stated in the immediately preceding sutta.

"Somā was a sister, and Sakulā was a sister" means these two sisters were the king's consorts (pajāpatiyo).

"Bhattābhihāre" means at the place of bringing food (bhattaṃ abhiharaṇaṭṭhāne).

Indeed, when the king is eating (rañño bhuñjanaṭṭhānañhi), all the harem women, taking spoons etc. (kaṭacchuādīni gahetvā), go to wait upon the king; they too went in that way (tāpi tatheva agamaṃsu).

376.Kiṃ pana, mahārājāti kasmā evamāha? Rañño garahaparimocanatthaṃ. Evañhi parisā cinteyya – ‘‘ayaṃ rājā āgacchamānova mātugāmānaṃ sāsanaṃ āroceti, mayaṃ attano dhammatāya bhagavantaṃ daṭṭhuṃ āgatoti maññāma, ayaṃ pana mātugāmānaṃ sāsanaṃ gahetvā āgato, mātugāmadāso maññe, esa pubbepi imināva kāraṇena āgacchatī’’ti. Pucchito pana so attano āgamanakāraṇaṃ kathessati, evamassa ayaṃ garahā na uppajjissatīti garahamocanatthaṃ evamāha.
“Why does he say, ‘What now, great king?’ (kiṃ pana, mahārājā)” It is to absolve the king of reproach.

For in this way, the assembly would think: “This king, as he comes, announces the message of women. We think that we have come to see the Blessed One by our own righteousness, but this one has come having taken the message of women. He seems to be a slave to women. He comes even formerly for this same reason.”

But if he is asked, he will tell the reason for his coming. In this way, this reproach will not arise for him. He says it to remove the reproach.

They misrepresent me with what is false and untrue."

378.Abbhudāhāsīti kathesi. Sakidevasabbaṃ ussati sabbaṃ dakkhitīti yo ekāvajjanena ekacittena atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ sabbaṃ ussati vā dakkhiti vā, so natthīti attho. Ekena hi cittena atītaṃ sabbaṃ jānissāmīti āvajjitvāpi atītaṃ sabbaṃ jānituṃ na sakkā, ekadesameva jānāti. Anāgatapaccuppannaṃ pana tena cittena sabbeneva sabbaṃ na jānātīti. Esa nayo itaresu. Evaṃ ekacittavasenāyaṃ pañho kathito. Heturūpanti hetusabhāvaṃ kāraṇajātikaṃ. Saheturūpanti sakāraṇajātikaṃ. Samparāyikāhaṃ, bhanteti samparāyaguṇaṃ ahaṃ, bhante, pucchāmi.

"Abbhudāhāsī" means he related (kathesi).
"Sakideva sabbaṃ ussati sabbaṃ dakkhitī" means the meaning (attho) is that there is no one (natthi) who, with a single attention (ekāvajjanena), with a single thought (ekacittena), can exert (ussati) or see (dakkhiti) everything (sabbaṃ) – past (atīta), future (anāgata), and present (paccuppanna) (sabbaṃ ussati vā dakkhiti vā).
Indeed (hi), with a single thought (ekena cittena), even having turned the mind (āvajjitvāpi) thinking, "I will know (jānissāmi) all the past (atītaṃ sabbaṃ)," it is not possible (na sakkā) to know (jānituṃ) all the past (atītaṃ sabbaṃ); one knows (jānāti) only a part (ekadesameva).
Moreover (pana), with that thought (tena cittena), one does not know (na jānāti) everything (sabbaṃ) entirely (sabbeneva) concerning the future and present (anāgatapaccuppannaṃ).
This (esa) is the method (nayo) for the rest (itaresu).
Thus (evaṃ) this question (pañho) has been discussed (kathito) based on a single thought (ekacittavasenāyaṃ).
"Heturūpa" means having the nature of a cause (hetusabhāvaṃ), being of the nature of a cause (kāraṇajātikaṃ).
"Saheturūpa" means being with a cause (sakāraṇajātikaṃ).
"Samparāyikāhaṃ, bhante" means, "Venerable Sir (bhante), I (ahaṃ) ask (pucchāmi) about the quality (guṇaṃ) related to the future (samparāya)."

What are the five? There are these four castes vaṇṇā, great king: the nobles khattiyā, the Brahmins brāhmaṇā, the merchants vessā, and the workers suddā.

"Here, great king, I speak of the inequality of striving.

Suppose, great king, there were two tamable elephants hatthidammā, or tamable horses assadammā, or tamable oxen godammā that were well tamed sudantā and well disciplined suvinītā, and two tamable elephants hatthidammā or tamable horses assadammā or tamable oxen godammā that were untamed adantā and undisciplined avinītā.

"Yes, venerable sir."

"Difference in striving" means difference in striving.

For the striving of an ordinary person (puthujjana) is of one kind, that of a stream-enterer (sotāpanna) is of another kind, that of a once-returner (sakadāgāmin) is of another kind, that of a non-returner (anāgāmin) is of another kind, that of an arhat is of another kind, that of the eighty great disciples is of another kind, that of the two chief disciples is of another kind, that of the pratyekabuddhas is of another kind, that of the all-knowing Buddhas is of another kind.

The striving of an ordinary person does not reach the striving of a stream-enterer...pe... the striving of a pratyekabuddha does not reach the striving of an all-knowing Buddha.

Referring to this meaning, he said, "I will speak of the difference in striving."

"One might go for a tooth cause" means that which is not a false cause, not an interruption, in the teeth a cause is seen, that cause one might approach, is the meaning.

"Tooth-ground" means a ground to be gone to with teeth.

In "[one who is] faithless" etc., ordinary persons, stream-enterers, once-returners, non-returners, all four are called faithless.

For an ordinary person, not having attained the faith of a stream-enterer, is faithless, a stream-enterer [not having attained the faith] of a once-returner, a once-returner [not having attained the faith] of a non-returner, a non-returner [not having attained the faith] of an arhat is faithless, an affliction arises even for an arhat, therefore all five are called "having many afflictions."

For a noble disciple, the name "deceitful, fraudulent" does not exist.

Therefore that Elder said, "The five factors of striving, the supramundane, have been spoken of. This is pleasing to me."

Rūpāvacare pana ekavimānasmiṃyeva tiṭṭhanti ca nisīdanti cāti cakkhuviññāṇadassanāya pahonti, etehi diṭṭhaṃ pana sallakkhitaṃ paṭividdhaṃ lakkhaṇaṃ daṭṭhuṃ sallakkhituṃ paṭivijjhituṃ na sakkontīti ñāṇacakkhunā dassanāya nappahonti, uparideve ca cakkhuviññāṇadassanenāpīti. Dassanāyapi nappahontī
381. pahotī means sakkoti, [he] is able. Just as a king, though [someone] is virtuous and endowed with gain and honor, if someone acts in such a way that no one approaches [the virtuous person], he is able to remove him from that position. And just as a king [removes] one who is unvirtuous, even though he begs for alms in the entire village, not obtaining even enough to subsist on, if [someone] becomes endowed with gain and honor, he is able to remove him from that position by acting in such a way. Even someone with monastic discipline, having connected [him] with women, causing the destruction of his virtue, or forcibly driving him out, he is able to remove him from that position. And even someone without monastic discipline, an official endowed with desirable qualities, having put [him] in a prison, not allowing [him] even to see the faces of women, he removes [him] from that position. But he banishes from the kingdom whomever he wishes.

dassanāyapi nappahontī, not even able to see: among the gods in the desire realm, the gods who are free from animosity are not even able to see with eye-consciousness the gods who are full of animosity. Why? Because of the absence of a place for an arhat there. But in the form realm, they both stand and sit in the same mansion; thus, they are able to see with eye-consciousness. However, they are not able to see, perceive, or penetrate with the eye of wisdom the characteristic that is seen, perceived, or penetrated by these [gods]; thus, they are not able to see [it] with the eye of wisdom, and also with eye-consciousness in the higher heavens.

Heturūpaṃ, bhante , āyasmā ānando āha; saheturūpaṃ, bhante, āyasmā ānando āha. Kiṃ pana, bhante, atthi brahmā’’ti? ‘‘Kiṃ pana tvaṃ, mahārāja, evaṃ vadesi – ‘kiṃ pana, bhante, atthi brahmā’’’ti? ‘‘Yadi vā so, bhante, brahmā āgantā itthattaṃ, yadi vā anāgantā itthatta’’nti? ‘‘Yo so, mahārāja, brahmā sabyābajjho so brahmā āgantā itthattaṃ, yo so brahmā abyābajjho so brahmā anāgantā itthatta’’nti. Atha kho aññataro puriso rājānaṃ pasenadiṃ kosalaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘sañjayo, mahārāja, brāhmaṇo ākāsagotto āgato’’ti. Atha kho rājā pasenadi kosalo sañjayaṃ brāhmaṇaṃ ākāsagottaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ko nu kho, brāhmaṇa, imaṃ kathāvatthuṃ rājantepure abbhudāhāsī’’ti? ‘‘Viṭaṭūbho, mahārāja, senāpatī’’ti. Viṭaṭūbho senāpati evamāha – ‘‘sañjayo, mahārāja, brāhmaṇo ākāsagotto’’ti. Atha kho aññataro puriso rājānaṃ pasenadiṃ kosalaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘yānakālo, mahārājā’’ti. Atha kho rājā pasenadi kosalo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘sabbaññutaṃ mayaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ apucchimhā, sabbaññutaṃ bhagavā byākāsi; tañca panamhākaṃ ruccati ceva khamati ca, tena camhā attamanā. Cātuvaṇṇisuddhiṃ mayaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ apucchimhā, cātuvaṇṇisuddhiṃ bhagavā byākāsi; tañca panamhākaṃ ruccati ceva khamati ca, tena camhā attamanā. Adhideve mayaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ apucchimhā, adhideve bhagavā byākāsi; tañca panamhākaṃ ruccati ceva khamati ca, tena camhā attamanā. Adhibrahmānaṃ mayaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ apucchimhā, adhibrahmānaṃ bhagavā byākāsi; tañca panamhākaṃ ruccati ceva khamati ca, tena camhā attamanā. Yaṃ yadeva ca mayaṃ bhagavantaṃ apucchimhā taṃ tadeva bhagavā byākāsi; tañca panamhākaṃ ruccati ceva khamati ca, tena camhā attamanā. Handa, ca dāni mayaṃ, bhante, gacchāma; bahukiccā mayaṃ bahukaraṇīyā’’ti. ‘‘Yassadāni tvaṃ, mahārāja, kālaṃ maññasī’’ti. Atha kho rājā pasenadi kosalo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā pakkāmīti. Kaṇṇakatthalasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ dasamaṃ. Rājavaggo niṭṭhito catuttho. Tassuddānaṃ – Ghaṭikāro raṭṭhapālo, maghadevo madhuriyaṃ; Bodhi aṅgulimālo ca, piyajātaṃ bāhitikaṃ; Dhammacetiyasuttañca, dasamaṃ kaṇṇakatthalaṃ.
Then King Pasenadi of Kosala said this to the Blessed One: “What is this bhikkhu’s name, venerable sir?” “Ānanda is his name, great king.” “Indeed, Ānanda, sir, is full of joy, full of Ānanda! What the venerable Ānanda has said appears reasonable, what he has said appears to be supported by reason.

“Is there, venerable sir, a Brahmā?” “Why do you ask that, great king? ‘Is there, venerable sir, a Brahmā?’” “If that Brahmā, venerable sir, is to come to this place, or is not to come to this place?” “That Brahmā, great king, who is subject to oppression, that Brahmā is to come to this place; that Brahmā who is not subject to oppression, that Brahmā is not to come to this place.”

Then a man announced to King Pasenadi of Kosala: “Sañjaya, great king, the brāhmaṇa of the Ākasa clan, has come.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala asked Sañjaya, the brāhmaṇa of the Ākasa clan: “Who introduced this story to the palace, brāhmaṇa?” “General Viḍaṭūbha, great king.” General Viḍaṭūbha said: “Sañjaya, great king, the brāhmaṇa of the Ākasa clan.”

Then a man announced to King Pasenadi of Kosala: “It is time to depart, great king.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala said this to the Blessed One: “We have asked the Blessed One about omniscience, venerable sir, and the Blessed One has answered about omniscience; we approve of and accept that answer, and so we are satisfied. We have asked the Blessed One about purification in the four castes, venerable sir, and the Blessed One has answered about purification in the four castes; we approve of and accept that answer, and so we are satisfied. We have asked the Blessed One about the gods, venerable sir, and the Blessed One has answered about the gods; we approve of and accept that answer, and so we are satisfied. We have asked the Blessed One about the Brahmās, venerable sir, and the Blessed One has answered about the Brahmās; we approve of and accept that answer, and so we are satisfied. Whatever we asked the Blessed One, that the Blessed One has answered; we approve of and accept those answers, and so we are satisfied.

“And now, venerable sir, we depart. We are busy and have much to do.” “Do whatever seems fit, great king.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala, having delighted and rejoiced in the Blessed One’s statement, rose from his seat, paid homage to him, and departed, keeping him on his right.

382.Ko nāmo ayaṃ, bhanteti rājā theraṃ jānantopi ajānanto viya pucchati. Kasmā? Pasaṃsitukāmatāya. Ānandarūpoti ānandasabhāvo. Brahmapucchāpi vuttanayeneva veditabbā. Atha kho aññataro purisoti sā kira kathā viṭaṭūbheneva kathitā, te ‘‘tayā kathitā, tayā kathitā’’ti kupitā aññamaññaṃ imasmiṃyeva ṭhāne attano attano balakāyaṃ uṭṭhāpetvā kalahampi kareyyunti nivāraṇatthaṃ so rājapuriso etadavoca. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānameva. Ayaṃ pana desanā neyyapuggalassa vasena niṭṭhitāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Kaṇṇakatthalasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā. Catutthavaggavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
382. "What is his name, venerable sir (bhante)?" Thus, the king asks the elder (thera), even though he knows him, as if he does not know.

Why? Because he wishes to praise him.

"Possessing the nature of bliss (ānandarūpo)": [this means] he has the nature of bliss (ānandasabhāvo).

brahmapucchāpi should be understood as stated in the previous method (vuttanayeneva veditabbā).

"Then, a certain man (atha kho aññataro puriso)": That story (kathā) was indeed told in a harsh manner (viṭaṭūbheneva kathitā), [and] being angered "you said it, you said it," they might raise their own army against each other in this very place, and even make a quarrel. In order to prevent that, that royal official (rājapuriso) said this (etadavoca).
