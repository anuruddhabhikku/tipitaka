98. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu aññaṃ byākaroti – ‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyāti pajānāmī’ti. Tassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno bhāsitaṃ neva abhinanditabbaṃ nappaṭikkositabbaṃ. Anabhinanditvā appaṭikkositvā pañho pucchitabbo – ‘cattārome, āvuso, vohārā tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena sammadakkhātā. Katame cattāro? Diṭṭhe diṭṭhavāditā, sute sutavāditā, mute mutavāditā, viññāte viññātavāditā – ime kho, āvuso, cattāro vohārā tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena sammadakkhātā. Kathaṃ jānato panāyasmato, kathaṃ passato imesu catūsu vohāresu anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimutta’nti? Khīṇāsavassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno vusitavato katakaraṇīyassa ohitabhārassa anuppattasadatthassa parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanassa sammadaññāvimuttassa ayamanudhammo hoti veyyākaraṇāya – ‘diṭṭhe kho ahaṃ , āvuso, anupāyo anapāyo anissito appaṭibaddho vippamutto visaṃyutto vimariyādīkatena cetasā viharāmi. Sute kho ahaṃ, āvuso…pe… mute kho ahaṃ, āvuso… viññāte kho ahaṃ, āvuso, anupāyo anapāyo anissito appaṭibaddho vippamutto visaṃyutto vimariyādīkatena cetasā viharāmi. Evaṃ kho me, āvuso, jānato evaṃ passato imesu catūsu vohāresu anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimutta’nti. Tassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno ‘sādhū’ti bhāsitaṃ abhinanditabbaṃ anumoditabbaṃ. ‘Sādhū’ti bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uttariṃ pañho pucchitabbo.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Anathapindika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Savatthi.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks (saying): “Monks.”

“Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied.

The Blessed One said this:

“Here, monks, a monk declares: ‘Destroyed is birth, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more for this state of being.’

Monks, the declaration of such a bhikkhu is neither to be received with approval nor with scorn.

Without approving or disapproving, a question should be put thus: ‘Friend, there are four kinds of expression rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

What are these four? Telling the seen as it is seen; telling the heard as it is heard; telling the sensed as it is sensed; telling the cognized as it is cognized – These, friend, are the four kinds of expression rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

How, in knowing, friend, how, in seeing, regarding these four kinds of expression, is the mind liberated from the taints through non-clinging?’ Bhikkhus, when a bhikkhu is one with taints destroyed’, who has lived the holy life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, reached the true goal, destroyed the fetters of being, and is completely liberated through final knowledge, this is the nature of his answer: ‘Friends, regarding the seen I abide unattracted, unrepelled, independent, detached, free, dissociated, with a mind rid of barriers.

Regarding the heard…Regarding the sensed…Regarding the cognized I abide unattracted, unrepelled, independent, detached, free, dissociated, with a mind rid of barriers.

It is by knowing thus, seeing thus, regarding these four kinds of expression, that through not clinging my mind is liberated from the taints.’

“Saying ‘good,’ monks, one may delight and rejoice in that bhikkhu’s words.

Saying ‘good,’ delighting and rejoicing in that statement, a further question should be asked.
98.Evaṃme sutanti chabbisodhanasuttaṃ. Tattha khīṇā jātītiādīsu ekenāpi padena aññā byākatāva hoti, dvīhipi. Idha pana catūhi padehi aññabyākaraṇaṃ āgataṃ. Diṭṭhe diṭṭhavāditātiādīsu yāya cetanāya diṭṭhe diṭṭhaṃ meti vadati, sā diṭṭhe diṭṭhavāditā nāma. Sesapadesupi eseva nayo. Ayamanudhammoti ayaṃ sabhāvo. Abhinanditabbanti na kevalaṃ abhinanditabbaṃ, parinibbutassa panassa sabbopi khīṇāsavassa sakkāro kātabbo. Uttariṃ pañhoti sace panassa veyyākaraṇena asantuṭṭhā hotha, uttarimpi ayaṃ pañho pucchitabboti dasseti. Ito paresupi tīsu vāresu ayameva nayo.
98. Thus, "me sutaṃ" is the Chabbisodhana Sutta.

There, in "khīṇā jātī" etc., even with one word, the meaning is explained; [and] with two.

Here, however, the explanation comes with four words.

In "diṭṭhe diṭṭhavāditā" etc., when one says, "With consciousness, in what is seen, I say 'it is seen'," that is called diṭṭhe diṭṭhavāditā.

The same method [of explanation] applies to the remaining terms as well.

"Ayamanudhammo" means this is its nature ( svabhāva).

"Abhinanditabbaṃ" means not only is it to be rejoiced at, but for one who has attained parinirvāṇa, all honor (satkāra) is to be done for such an arhat whose āsravas are destroyed (kṣīṇāsrava).

"Uttariṃ pañho" means if you are not satisfied with this explanation, he shows that this question can be asked again.

The same method applies in the three remaining instances.

99. ‘‘‘Pañcime, āvuso, upādānakkhandhā tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena sammadakkhātā. Katame pañca? Seyyathidaṃ – rūpupādānakkhandho, vedanupādānakkhandho, saññupādānakkhandho, saṅkhārupādānakkhandho, viññāṇupādānakkhandho – ime kho, āvuso, pañcupādānakkhandhā tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena sammadakkhātā. Kathaṃ jānato panāyasmato, kathaṃ passato imesu pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimutta’nti? Khīṇāsavassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno vusitavato katakaraṇīyassa ohitabhārassa anuppattasadatthassa parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanassa sammadaññāvimuttassa ayamanudhammo hoti veyyākaraṇāya – ‘rūpaṃ kho ahaṃ, āvuso, abalaṃ virāgunaṃ virāgaṃ (sī. pī.), virāgutaṃ (ṭīkā) anassāsikanti viditvā ye rūpe upāyūpādānā upayūpādānā (ka.) cetaso adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā tesaṃ khayā virāgā nirodhā cāgā paṭinissaggā vimuttaṃ me cittanti pajānāmi. Vedanaṃ kho ahaṃ, āvuso…pe… saññaṃ kho ahaṃ, āvuso… saṅkhāre kho ahaṃ, āvuso… viññāṇaṃ kho ahaṃ, āvuso, abalaṃ virāgunaṃ anassāsikanti viditvā ye viññāṇe upāyūpādānā cetaso adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā tesaṃ khayā virāgā nirodhā cāgā paṭinissaggā vimuttaṃ me cittanti pajānāmi. Evaṃ kho me, āvuso, jānato evaṃ passato imesu pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimutta’nti. Tassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno ‘sādhū’ti bhāsitaṃ abhinanditabbaṃ, anumoditabbaṃ. ‘Sādhū’ti bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uttariṃ pañho pucchitabbo.
99. “‘These five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhā), friends (āvuso), are rightly expounded by the Blessed One (bhagavatā)—the one who knows (jānatā), the one who sees (passatā), the Worthy One (arhatā), rightly self-awakened (sammāsambuddhena).

Which five? They are: the material form aggregate affected by clinging (rūpupādānakkhandho), the feeling aggregate affected by clinging (vedanupādānakkhandho), the perception aggregate affected by clinging (saññupādānakkhandho), the formations aggregate affected by clinging (saṅkhārupādānakkhandho), and the consciousness aggregate affected by clinging (viññāṇupādānakkhandho). These, friends, are the five aggregates affected by clinging, rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

How does the venerable one (panāyasmato) know, how does he see, regarding these five aggregates affected by clinging, so that through not clinging his mind (cittaṃ) is liberated (vimuttaṃ) from the taints (āsavehi)?’

“Bhikkhus, when a bhikkhu is one with taints destroyed (khīṇāsavassa)… and is completely liberated through final knowledge, this is the nature of his answer: ‘“Friends, having known material form (rūpaṃ) to be feeble (abalaṃ), fading away (virāgunaṃ), and comfortless (anassāsikanti), with the destruction (khayā), fading away (virāgā), cessation (nirodhā), giving up (cāgā), and relinquishing (paṭinissaggā) of attraction and clinging regarding material form, of mental standpoints (cetaso), adherences (adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā), and underlying tendencies regarding material form, I have understood that my mind is liberated (vimuttaṃ me cittanti pajānāmi).

‘Friends, having known feeling (vedanaṃ)…perception (saññaṃ)…formations (saṅkhāre)…consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) to be feeble (abalaṃ), fading away (virāgunaṃ), and comfortless (anassāsikanti), with the destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing of attraction and clinging regarding consciousness, of mental standpoints, adherences, and underlying tendencies regarding consciousness, I have understood that my mind is liberated (vimuttaṃ me cittanti pajānāmi).

‘“It is by knowing thus, seeing thus, regarding these five aggregates affected by clinging, that through not clinging my mind is liberated (vimuttaṃ) from the taints.’

“Saying ‘good (sādhū),’ bhikkhus, one may delight (abhinanditabbaṃ) and rejoice (anumoditabbaṃ) in that bhikkhu’s words.

Having delighted and rejoiced in that bhikkhu’s words saying ‘good (sādhū),’ a further question should be asked (pucchitabbo)."
99.Abalanti dubbalaṃ. Virāgunanti vigacchanasabhāvaṃ. Anassāsikanti assāsavirahitaṃ. Upāyūpādānāti taṇhādiṭṭhīnametaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Taṇhādiṭṭhiyo hi tebhūmakadhamme upentīti upāyā, upādiyantīti upādānā. Cetaso adiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayātipi tāsaṃyeva nāmaṃ. Cittañhi taṇhādiṭṭhīhi sakkāyadhammesu tiṭṭhati adhitiṭṭhatīti taṇhādiṭṭhiyo cetaso adhiṭṭhānā, tāhi taṃ abhinivisatīti abhinivesā, tāhiyeva taṃ anusetīti anusayāti vuccanti. Khayāvirāgātiādīsu khayena virāgenāti attho. Sabbāni cetāni aññamaññavevacanāneva.
99. Abala means weak.
Virāguna means the nature of vanishing.
Anassāsika means without exhalation.
Upāyūpādānā is another word for craving and views (taṇhādiṭṭhi).
Because craving and views approach the three planes of existence, they are upāyā; because they are clung to, they are upādānā.
Cetaso adiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā is also a name for them.
Because the mind abides and strongly abides in the sakkāyadhamma (things related to the existing body) through craving and views, craving and views are the cetaso adhiṭṭhānā; because the mind is intent on that, they are abhinivesā; because the mind habitually inclines towards that, they are called anusayā.
Khayā, virāgā, etc., means by destruction, by dispassion, this is the meaning.
All these are just synonyms for each other.

100. ‘‘‘Chayimā , āvuso, dhātuyo tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena sammadakkhātā. Katamā cha? Pathavīdhātu, āpodhātu, tejodhātu, vāyodhātu, ākāsadhātu, viññāṇadhātu – imā kho, āvuso, cha dhātuyo tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena sammadakkhātā. Kathaṃ jānato panāyasmato, kathaṃ passato imāsu chasu dhātūsu anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimutta’nti? Khīṇāsavassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno vusitavato katakaraṇīyassa ohitabhārassa anuppattasadatthassa parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanassa sammadaññāvimuttassa ayamanudhammo hoti veyyākaraṇāya – ‘pathavīdhātuṃ kho ahaṃ, āvuso, na attato upagacchiṃ, na ca pathavīdhātunissitaṃ attānaṃ. Ye ca pathavīdhātunissitā upāyūpādānā cetaso adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā tesaṃ khayā virāgā nirodhā cāgā paṭinissaggā vimuttaṃ me cittanti pajānāmi. Ye ca viññāṇadhātunissitā upāyūpādānā cetaso adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā tesaṃ khayā virāgā nirodhā cāgā paṭinissaggā vimuttaṃ me cittanti pajānāmi. Evaṃ kho me, āvuso, jānato, evaṃ passato imāsu chasu dhātūsu anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimutta’nti. Tassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno ‘sādhū’ti bhāsitaṃ abhinanditabbaṃ, anumoditabbaṃ. ‘Sādhū’ti bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uttariṃ pañho pucchitabbo.
100. “Friend, there are these six dhātus (elements) rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

What are the six? They are the earth element (pathavīdhātu), the water element (āpodhātu), the fire element (tejodhātu), the air element (vāyodhātu), the space element (ākāsadhātu), and the consciousness element (viññāṇadhātu). These, friend, are the six elements rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

How, in knowing, how, in seeing, is the mind liberated from the taints through not clinging to these six elements?” “Bhikkhus, when a bhikkhu (monk) is one with taints destroyed, one who has lived the life, done what was to be done, laid down the burden, attained his own benefit, with the fetters of being destroyed, and is completely liberated through final knowledge, this is the nature of his answer: ‘Friend, I have treated the earth element as not self, with no self based on the earth element.

And with the destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing of attraction and clinging, of mental standpoints, adherences, and underlying tendencies based on the earth element, I have understood that my mind is liberated.

‘Friend, I have treated the water element…the fire element…the air element…the space element…the consciousness element as not self, with no self based on the consciousness element.

And with the destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing of attraction and clinging based on the consciousness element, of mental standpoints, adherences, and underlying tendencies based on the consciousness element, I have understood that my mind is liberated.

It is by knowing thus, seeing thus, regarding these six elements, that through not clinging my mind is liberated from the taints.’

Bhikkhus, the speech of that monk should be rejoiced over and approved. Having rejoiced over and approved his speech, a further question should be asked.
100.Pathavīdhātūti patiṭṭhānadhātu. Āpodhātūti ābandhanadhātu. Tejodhātūti paripācanadhātu. Vāyodhātūti vitthambhanadhātu. Ākāsadhātūti asamphuṭṭhadhātu. Viññāṇadhātūti vijānanadhātu. Na anattato upagacchinti ahaṃ attāti attakoṭṭhāsena na upagamiṃ. Na ca pathavīdhātunissitanti pathavīdhātunissitā sesadhātuyo ca upādārūpañca arūpakkhandhā ca. Tepi hi nissitavatthurūpānaṃ pathavīdhātunissitattā ekena pariyāyena pathavīdhātunissitāva. Tasmā ‘‘na ca pathavīdhātunissita’’nti vadanto sesarūpārūpadhammepi attato na upagacchinti vadati. Ākāsadhātunissitapade pana avinibbhogavasena sabbampi bhūtupādārūpaṃ ākāsadhātunissitaṃ nāma , tathā taṃnissitarūpavatthukā arūpakkhandhā. Evaṃ idhāpi rūpārūpaṃ gahitameva hoti. Viññāṇadhātunissitapade pana sahajātā tayo khandhā cittasamuṭṭhānarūpañca viññāṇadhātunissitanti rūpārūpaṃ gahitameva hoti.
100. pathavīdhātu means patiṭṭhānadhātu.
āpodhātu means ābandhanadhātu.
tejodhātu means paripācanadhātu.
vāyodhātu means vitthambhanadhātu.
ākāsadhātu means asamphuṭṭhadhātu.
viññāṇadhātu means vijānanadhātu.
"na anattato upagacchi" means I did not approach with the idea of self, thinking "I am self" with a self-made idea.
"na ca pathavīdhātunissita" means earth element-dependent. The remaining elements, appropriated form, and formless aggregates are earth element-dependent.
Because they are dependent on the form of the object that is dependent, they are, in one sense, dependent only on the earth element.
Therefore, those who say "not dependent on the earth element" are saying that they do not approach the remaining form and formless phenomena as self.
However, in the phrase ākāsadhātunissita (dependent on the space element), all appropriated form that has not been separated is called ākāsadhātunissita, as well as the formless aggregates that have the form of an object dependent on it.
Thus, here also, form and formless are taken as grasped.
However, in the phrase viññāṇadhātunissita (dependent on the consciousness element), the three co-arisen aggregates and the form arising from the mind are viññāṇadhātunissita, thus form and formless are taken as grasped.

101. “But, friend, there are these six internal and external bases (ajjhattikabāhirāni āyatanāni) rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

What are the six? They are the eye (cakṣu) and forms (rūpā), the ear (sotañca) and sounds (saddā ca), the nose (ghānañca) and odours (gandhā ca), the tongue (jivhā) and flavours (rasā ca), the body (kāyo) and tangibles (phoṭṭhabbā ca), the mind (mano) and mind-objects (dhammā ca)—these, friend, are the six internal and external bases rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

How, friend, by knowing, how, by seeing, regarding these six internal and external bases, is the mind liberated through non-clinging from the taints? Bhikkhus, when a bhikkhu is one with taints destroyed, has lived the life, done what was to be done, laid down the burden, attained his own welfare, completely destroyed the fetters of being, and is completely liberated through final knowledge, this is the nature of his reply: ‘“Friend, regarding the eye (cakkhusmiṃ), form (rūpe), eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇe), things cognizable by the mind through eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaviññātabbesu dhammesu), whatever desire (chando), lust (rāgo), delight (nandī), craving (taṇhā), attraction and clinging (upāyūpādānā), mental standpoints, adherences, and underlying tendencies (adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā) based on the consciousness element, with their destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing, I have understood that my mind is liberated.

Regarding the ear (sotasmiṃ), sound (sadde), ear-consciousness (sotaviññāṇe)…pe… regarding the nose (ghānasmiṃ), odour (gandhe), nose-consciousness (ghānaviññāṇe)… regarding the tongue (jivhāya), flavour (rase), tongue-consciousness (jivhāviññāṇe)… regarding the body (kāyasmiṃ), tangible (phoṭṭhabbe), body-consciousness (kāyaviññāṇe)… regarding the mind (manasmiṃ), mind-object (dhamme), mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇe) things cognizable by the mind through mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇaviññātabbesu dhammesu), whatever desire (chando), lust (rāgo), delight (nandī), craving (taṇhā), attraction and clinging (upāyūpādānā), mental standpoints, adherences, and underlying tendencies (adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā) based on the consciousness element, with their destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing, I have understood that my mind is liberated.

102. Ahaṅkāramamaṅkāramānānusayā means here ahaṅkāra is pride (māna), mamaṅkāra is craving (taṇhā), and it is pride (mānānusaya) itself.

Why is this said, āsavānaṃ khayañāṇāyā, without mentioning the former dwelling and the divine eye? Monks do not ask about mundane things, they only ask about supramundane things, therefore he speaks thus, relating only to the question that was asked.

This is named the “sutra of one explanation” (ekavissajjitasuttaṃ), and it is also named the “sixfold purification” (chabbisodhanantipissa).

Here, indeed, the six sections are purified: the four expressions, the five aggregates (pañca khandhā), the six elements (cha dhātuyo), the six internal and external sense bases (cha ajjhattikabāhirāni āyatanāni), one's own body together with consciousness (attano saviññāṇakakāyo), and the body of others together with consciousness (paresāṃ saviññāṇakakāyo), therefore it is said "sixfold purification" (chabbisodhanīyanti).

However, the Elders dwelling beyond the sea (parasamuddavāsittherā) say that the six sections are the four kinds of nutriment (āhārehi) together with one's own body together with consciousness and the body of others together with consciousness made as one.

But these six sections should be examined by way of the exposition of the discipline, "What have you attained? How have you attained it? When have you attained it? Where have you attained it? What defilements have been abandoned? Of what qualities have you gained?"

Here, kiṃ te adhigataṃ is a question about attainment. What have you attained in the absorptions (jhāna), liberations (vimokkha), etc., or in the path of stream-entry (sotāpattimaggādīsu), etc.?

Kinti te adhigataṃ is a question about the means.

For here is the meaning in this: What have you attained by taking impermanence as your burden, or one of the characteristics of suffering and non-self, or what by clinging to absorption, or by way of insight (vipassanā), likewise, by clinging to form, or to the formless, or by clinging to what is internal, or to what is external?

Kadā te adhigataṃ is a question about time, it is said, “In which time, in the forenoon, at midday, etc.?”

Kattha te adhigataṃ is a question about place, it is said, “In which place, in the night dwelling, in the day dwelling, at the foot of a tree, in a pavilion, or in which monastery?”

Katame te kilesā pahīnā asks about abandoned defilements, it is said, “Which defilements are abandoned by you by way of which path?”

Katamesaṃ tvaṃ dhammānaṃ lābhī is a question about qualities attained, it is said, “Of which qualities have you gained, of the first path, etc.?”

Therefore, even now, if any monk should declare the attainment of a superhuman quality (uttarimanussadhammādhigamaṃ), he should not be respected merely by that much.

But to these six places should be spoken for the sake of examination, "What have you attained, what absorption or one of the liberations, etc.?" For the quality that has been attained by someone, that is evident to him.

If he says, "This is what I have attained," then he should be asked, "How have you attained it?"

By taking what as your burden among the characteristics of impermanence, etc., or in the thirty-eight objects, or in the qualities that are divided into form, formless, internal, external, etc., by which means have you clung? For the thing to which someone clings, that is evident to him.

But if he says, "This is my clinging, thus I have attained it," then he should be asked, "When have you attained it?", "In the forenoon, or in which time among midday, etc.?" For the time that has been attained by oneself is evident to all.

If he says, "I attained it at such and such a time," then he should be asked, "Where have you attained it?", "In the day dwelling, or in which place among the night dwelling, etc.?" For the place that has been attained by oneself is evident to all.

If he says, "I attained it in such and such a place," then he should be asked, "What defilements have been abandoned?", "What is to be abandoned by the first path, or what is to be abandoned by the second path, etc.?" For the defilements abandoned by the path that has been attained by oneself are evident to all.

If he says, "These defilements have been abandoned by me," then he should be asked, "Of what qualities have you gained?", "Of the path of stream-entry, or of which among the path of once-returning, etc.?" For the quality that has been attained by oneself is evident to all.

If he says, "I have gained these qualities," even by that much his statement should not be trusted.

For monks who are very learned, skilled in learning and questioning, are able to examine these six places.

The conduct of this monk's coming should be examined, if the conduct of his coming is not pure, he should be expelled, "By this conduct, supramundane qualities are not attained."

But if the conduct of his coming is pure, it is known, "For a long time he dwells, unattached to the four requisites, intent on wakefulness, devoted to the three trainings, with a mind like the palm of the hand in the sky," the statement of that monk accords and agrees with the practice.

These are the instances: Elder Dīghabhāṇaka Abhaya, unable to accept a single alms-bowl portion, gave a sign to a young boy.
He dived into the Kalyāṇī River at the river mouth while [the boy] was bathing and grabbed his feet.
The alms-food receiver made a loud noise with the thought "a crocodile!", then they recognized him as a worldling.
In the time of King Candamukhatissa, Elder Saṅgha, a khīṇāsava with weak eyes, dwelt in the Mahāvihāra.
The king, thinking, "I will favor the Elder," approached quietly when the bhikkhus had gone for alms, and grabbed his feet like a snake.
The Elder, being as motionless as a stone pillar, said, "Who is here?" I am Tissa, bhante.
"Do you smell a good fragrance, Tissa?" Thus, there is no fear for a khīṇāsava.

But even a worldling is very brave and fearless.
He should be favored with an attractive object.
King Vasabha, while favoring an Elder, had him sit in his house and sat near him, crushing badara fruits.
The Elder's saliva dripped, then the worldling-state of the Elder became apparent.
For a khīṇāsava, craving for flavors is completely abandoned; there is no craving even for divine flavors.
Therefore, having favored [him] by these means, if fear, terror, or hair-raising, or craving for flavors arises in one who is attached, he should be removed [saying], "You are not worthy."
But if he sits like a lion, fearless, unperturbed, and undismayed, he does not generate craving even for divine objects.
