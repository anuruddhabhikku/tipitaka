279. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati tapodārāme. Atha kho āyasmā samiddhi rattiyā paccūsasamayaṃ paccuṭṭhāya yena tapodo tapodā (sī.) tenupasaṅkami gattāni parisiñcituṃ. Tapode gattāni parisiñcitvā paccuttaritvā ekacīvaro aṭṭhāsi gattāni pubbāpayamāno sukkhāpayamāno (ka.). Atha kho aññatarā devatā abhikkantāya rattiyā abhikkantavaṇṇā kevalakappaṃ tapodaṃ obhāsetvā yenāyasmā samiddhi tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhitā kho sā devatā āyasmantaṃ samiddhiṃ etadavoca – ‘‘dhāresi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañcā’’ti? ‘‘Na kho ahaṃ, āvuso, dhāremi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Tvaṃ panāvuso, dhāresi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañcā’’ti? ‘‘Ahampi kho, bhikkhu, na dhāremi bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Dhāresi pana tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’’ti? ‘‘Na kho ahaṃ, āvuso, dhāremi bhaddekarattiyo gāthāti. Tvaṃ panāvuso, dhāresi bhaddekarattiyo gāthā’’ti? ‘‘Ahampi kho, bhikkhu na dhāremi bhaddekarattiyo gāthāti. Uggaṇhāhi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca; pariyāpuṇāhi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca; dhārehi tvaṃ, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddesañca vibhaṅgañca. Atthasaṃhito, bhikkhu, bhaddekarattassa uddeso ca vibhaṅgo ca ādibrahmacariyako’’ti. Idamavoca sā devatā; idaṃ vatvā tatthevantaradhāyi.
THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rajagaha in the Park of the Hot Springs (tapodārāme).

Then, when it was near dawn, the venerable Samiddhi went to the hot springs (tapoda) to bathe his limbs (gattāni parisiñcituṃ).

Having bathed his limbs (gattāni) at the hot springs (tapode), having come out and being clad in a single robe, he stood drying his limbs.

Then, when the night was well advanced, a certain deity (devatā) of beautiful appearance who illuminated the whole of the Hot Springs (tapodaṃ), approached the venerable Samiddhi; having approached, she stood at one side.

Standing at one side, the deity (devatā) said to the venerable Samiddhi: “Bhikkhu, do you remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattassa)?” “No, friend (āvuso), I do not remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattassa)."

"Then, friend (āvuso), do you remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattassa)?" "I, also, bhikkhu, do not remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattassa)."

“Then, bhikkhu, do you remember the verses (gāthā) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattiyo)?" “No, friend (āvuso), I do not remember the verses (gāthāti) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattiyo)."

“Then, friend (āvuso), do you remember the verses (gāthā) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattiyo)?” "I, also, bhikkhu, do not remember the verses (gāthāti) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattiyo)."

"Bhikkhu, learn the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattassa). Bhikkhu, master the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattassa). Bhikkhu, remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattassa)."

"The summary (uddeso) and the exposition (vibhaṅgo) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekarattassa) are connected with the meaning (atthasaṃhito), and it is the beginning of the holy life (ādibrahmacariyako)."

This is what the deity (devatā) said; having said this, she vanished right there (tatthevantaradhāyi).

279. Thus have I heard: the Mahākaccāna-bhaddekaratta Sūtra.

There, Tapodārāma means a garden (ārāma) named thus, on account of the lake of hot water (tattodakassa rahadassa vasena evaṃladdhanāme).

Below the VeBhāra Mountain, there is a Nāga-dwelling (nāgabhavanaṃ) five hundred yojanas in extent, similar to the world of the gods (devalokasadisaṃ), complete with jeweled surfaces (maṇimayena talena) and pleasure gardens (ārāmauyyānehi ca samannāgataṃ). There, in the playing place of the Nāgas, is a great water-lake (mahāudakarahado), from which flows the Tapodā river (tapodā nāma nadī), stinking with hot water (kuthitā uṇhodakā).

Why did this happen? The world of the great departed spirits (mahā petaloko) is said to surround Rājagaha. The Tapodā comes between two great iron-pot hells (dvinnam் mahālohakumbhinirayānam் antarena), therefore it flows stinking (kuthitā sandati).

Thus it was said: "Monks, this Tapodā flows, that lake has clear water (acchodako), cold water (sītodako), pleasant water (sātodako), white water (setodako), is a good bathing place (suppatittho), is delightful (ramaṇīyo), has abundant fish and turtles (pahūtamacchakacchapo), and lotus flowers (padumāni) bloom as large as chariot wheels (cakkamattāni)."

Moreover, monks, this Tapodā comes in the space between two great hells (dvinnam் mahānirayānam் antarikāya āgacchati), therefore this Tapodā flows stinking (kuthitā sandati)."

(Pārā. 231).

Furthermore, opposite this garden (ārāmassa abhisammukhaṭṭhāne), a great water-lake (mahāudakarahado) came into being, and because of its name, this monastery (vihāro) is called Tapodārāma.

280.Samiddhīti tassa kira therassa attabhāvo samiddho abhirūpo pāsādiko, tasmā samiddhitveva saṅkhaṃ gato. Ādibrahmacariyakoti maggabrahmacariyassa ādi pubbabhāgappaṭipattibhūto. Idaṃ vatvāna sugato uṭṭhāyāsanāti madhupiṇḍikasutte (ma. ni. 1.199 ādayo) vuttanayeneva vitthāretabbaṃ.
280. Samiddhī: It is said that the Elder's self ( attabhāvo) was radiant (samiddho), beautiful (abhirūpo), and pleasing (pāsādiko); therefore, he was reckoned as "radiant" (samiddhitveva saṅkhaṃ gato).

Ādibrahmacariyako: "[It] is the initial basis for practice which is the preliminary part (pubbabhāgappaṭipattibhūto) of the path of the holy life (maggabrahmacariyassa ādi)."

Idaṃ vatvāna sugato uṭṭhāyāsanā: As it says in the Madhupiṇḍika Sutta (Majjhima Nikāya 1.199 ādayo), [this] should be explained in detail in the manner that has been stated.

“Just as, friend, a man needing heartwood, seeking heartwood, wandering in search of heartwood, passing by the root, passing by the trunk, branches and leaves of a great tree standing possessed of heartwood, might think that heartwood should be sought in the branches and leaves. For knowing, the Blessed One knows; seeing, he sees; he is vision, he is knowledge, he is the Dhamma, he is the holy one; he is the sayer, the proclaimer, the elucidator of meaning, the giver of the Deathless, the lord of the Dhamma, the Tathāgata. The venerable Mahākaccāna explained the meaning to us with these terms, statements, and phrases.”

“Monks, Mahākaccāna is wise; monks, Mahākaccāna has great wisdom. If you were to ask me about the meaning of this, I would explain it to you in the same way that Mahākaccāna has explained it. Such is its meaning, and so you should remember it.”

The Blessed One spoke thus. Elated, those monks delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Mahākaccānabhaddekaratta Sutta is finished, the third.
