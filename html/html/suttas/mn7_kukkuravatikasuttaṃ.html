78. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā koliyesu viharati haliddavasanaṃ nāma koliyānaṃ nigamo. Atha kho puṇṇo ca koliyaputto govatiko acelo ca seniyo kukkuravatiko yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā puṇṇo koliyaputto govatiko bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Acelo pana seniyo kukkuravatiko bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā kukkurova palikujjitvā palikuṇṭhitvā (syā. kaṃ.), paliguṇṭhitvā (ka.) ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho puṇṇo koliyaputto govatiko bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ayaṃ , bhante, acelo seniyo kukkuravatiko dukkarakārako chamānikkhittaṃ bhojanaṃ bhuñjati. Tassa taṃ kukkuravataṃ dīgharattaṃ samattaṃ samādinnaṃ. Tassa kā gati, ko abhisamparāyo’’ti? ‘‘Alaṃ, puṇṇa, tiṭṭhatetaṃ; mā maṃ etaṃ pucchī’’ti. Dutiyampi kho puṇṇo koliyaputto govatiko…pe… tatiyampi kho puṇṇo koliyaputto govatiko bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ayaṃ, bhante, acelo seniyo kukkuravatiko dukkarakārako chamānikkhittaṃ bhojanaṃ bhuñjati. Tassa taṃ kukkuravataṃ dīgharattaṃ samattaṃ samādinnaṃ. Tassa kā gati, ko abhisamparāyo’’ti?
THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the Koliyan country at a town of the Koliyans named Haliddavasana.

Then Punna, son of the Koliyans, an ox-duty ascetic govatika, and also Seniya, a naked dog-duty ascetic kukkuravatika, went to the Blessed One. Having approached, Punna, the ox-duty ascetic, paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side.

while Seniya, the naked dog-duty ascetic, exchanged greetings with the Blessed One,

and when this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he too sat down at one side curled up like a dog.

While sat on one side Punna, the ox-duty ascetic, said this to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, this Seniya is a naked dog-duty ascetic who does what is hard to do: he eats his food when it is thrown to the ground.

He has long taken up and practised that dog-duty kukkuravata.

What is his destination? What is his future course?” “Enough, Punna, let that be; do not ask me this.”

A second time…And a third time Punna, the ox-duty ascetic, said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, this Seniya is a naked dog-duty ascetic who does what is hard to do: he eats his food when it is thrown to the ground.

He has long taken up and practised that dog-duty kukkuravata. What is his destination? What is his future course?”

Taṃ gocaragāmaṃ katvā viharatīti attho. Vihāro panettha kiñcāpi na niyāmito, tathāpi buddhānaṃ anucchavike senāsaneyeva vihāsīti veditabbo. 78. Thus, I have heard: the Kukkuravatika-sutta.

There, "in Koliyesu" means in a country of that name.

For even one Koliya prince, dwelling in Kolanagara, is spoken of in this way because of it being a dwelling place.

In that country of Koliyesu.

"Haliddavasana" means that at the time of the building of that market town, people dressed in yellow clothes played at festivals.

They, at the end of the festival game, naming the market-town, called the name "Haliddavasana".

That, having made it a pasture village, he dwells there, is the meaning.

The monastery here, although not fixed, nevertheless should be understood as dwelling in a suitable dwelling for Buddhas.

"Govatiko" means one who has undertaken the govata, placing horns on his head, tying on a tail, and behaving like one who eats grass together with cows, he wanders about.

"Acelo" means naked, without a cloth.

"Seniyo" means his name.

"Kukkuravatiko" means one who has undertaken the kukkuravata, he does every dog-like activity.

Both of these are friends, companions in playing with dust.

"Kukkurova palikujjitvā" means the dog, namely, sitting near its owner, scratching the ground with both feet, and cooing dog-like.

Cooing, he sits down, and this one, too, having agreed with the Blessed One, "I will do dog-like activity," having scratched the ground with both hands, shaking his head, making 'bhu bhu' sound, contracting his hands and feet, he sat down like a dog.

"Chamānikkhitta" means placed on the ground.

"Samattaṃ samādinna" means having made it complete, it is taken.

"Kā gatī" means what is the destiny?

"Ko abhisamparāyo" means where is the rebirth in the future life?

"Ala" means he rejects it as far as the third time, thinking it will be unpleasant for him.

"Kukkuravata" means the undertaking of the kukkuravata.

Kukkuravatiko, Kukkurova palikujjitvā, Chamānikkhitta, Samattaṃ samādinna, Kā gatī, Ko abhisamparāyo, Ala, Kukkuravata.

So kukkuravataṃ bhāvetvā paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, kukkurasīlaṃ bhāvetvā paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, kukkuracittaṃ bhāvetvā paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, kukkurākappaṃ bhāvetvā paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā kukkurānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjati. Sace kho panassa evaṃdiṭṭhi hoti – ‘imināhaṃ sīlena vā vatena vā tapena vā brahmacariyena vā devo vā bhavissāmi devaññataro vā’ti, sāssa sāyaṃ (ka.) hoti micchādiṭṭhi. Micchādiṭṭhissa micchādiṭṭhikassa (sī.) kho ahaṃ, puṇṇa, dvinnaṃ gatīnaṃ aññataraṃ gatiṃ vadāmi – nirayaṃ vā tiracchānayoniṃ vā. Iti kho, puṇṇa, sampajjamānaṃ kukkuravataṃ kukkurānaṃ sahabyataṃ upaneti, vipajjamānaṃ niraya’’nti. Evaṃ vutte, acelo seniyo kukkuravatiko parodi, assūni pavattesi. Atha kho bhagavā puṇṇaṃ koliyaputtaṃ govatikaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘etaṃ kho te ahaṃ, puṇṇa, nālatthaṃ. Alaṃ, puṇṇa, tiṭṭhatetaṃ; mā maṃ etaṃ pucchī’’ti. ‘‘Nāhaṃ, bhante, etaṃ rodāmi yaṃ maṃ bhagavā evamāha; api ca me idaṃ, bhante, kukkuravataṃ dīgharattaṃ samattaṃ samādinnaṃ. Ayaṃ, bhante, puṇṇo koliyaputto govatiko. Tassa taṃ govataṃ dīgharattaṃ samattaṃ samādinnaṃ. Tassa kā gati, ko abhisamparāyo’’ti? ‘‘Alaṃ, seniya, tiṭṭhatetaṃ; mā maṃ etaṃ pucchī’’ti. Dutiyampi kho acelo seniyo…pe… tatiyampi kho acelo seniyo kukkuravatiko bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ayaṃ, bhante, puṇṇo koliyaputto govatiko. Tassa taṃ govataṃ dīgharattaṃ samattaṃ samādinnaṃ. 79. “Well, Puṇṇa, since I certainly cannot persuade you.

Enough, Puṇṇa, let that be. Do not ask me that; I shall therefore answer you.

Here, Puṇṇa, someone develops the dog-duty (kukkuravataṃ) fully and uninterruptedly (paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ); he develops the dog-habit (kukkurasīlaṃ) fully and uninterruptedly (paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ); he develops the dog-mind (kukkuracittaṃ) fully and uninterruptedly (paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ), he develops dog-behaviour (kukkurākappaṃ) fully and uninterruptedly (paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ).

Having done so, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of dogs (kukkurānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjati).

But if he has such a view as this: ‘By this virtue (sīlena) or observance (vatena) or asceticism (tapena) or holy life (brahmacariyena) I shall become a great god (devo) or some lesser god.’ that is wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi) in his case.

Now there are two destinations for one with wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi), I say: hell (nirayaṃ) or the animal realm (tiracchānayoniṃ).

So, Puṇṇa, if his dog-duty (kukkuravataṃ) succeeds, it will lead him to the company of dogs (kukkurānaṃ sahabyataṃ upaneti); if it fails, it will lead him to hell (nirayaṃ)".

When this was said, Seniya the naked dog-duty ascetic cried out and burst into tears.

Then the Blessed One said this to Puṇṇa, son of the Koliyans, the ox-duty ascetic: “Puṇṇa, I was not able to persuade you regarding this.

Enough, Puṇṇa, let that be. Do not ask me that.”

“Venerable sir, I am not crying because the Blessed One has said this about me, but because I have long taken up and practised this dog-duty (kukkuravataṃ dīgharattaṃ samattaṃ samādinnaṃ).

Venerable sir, this Puṇṇa, son of the Koliyans, is an ox-duty ascetic.

He has long taken up and practised that ox-duty (govataṃ dīgharattaṃ samattaṃ samādinnaṃ).

What is his destination, what is his future state?” “Enough, Seniya, let that be. Do not ask me that.”

A second time…And a third time Seniya the naked dog-duty ascetic asked the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, this Puṇṇa, son of the Koliyans, is an ox-duty ascetic.

He has long taken up and practised that ox-duty (govataṃ dīgharattaṃ samattaṃ samādinnaṃ).

What is his destination, what is his future state?”

79. bhāvetī means to cultivate.
paripuṇṇa means not less than.
abbokiṇṇa means without interruption.
kukkurasīla means the conduct of a dog (kukkura).
kukkuracitta means "From today onward, I will do what should be done by dogs," thus, the mind has arisen.
kukkurākappa means the manner of dogs' gait exists, the manner of standing exists, the manner of sitting exists, the manner of lying down exists, the manner of defecating and urinating exists, seeing other dogs, the manner of going with teeth bared exists, this is called the kukkurākappa, that is to be cultivated, is the meaning.
imināhaṃ sīlenā etc. means, "I, by this conduct (ācāra) or observance of vows (vatasamādāna) or practice of difficult asceticism (dukkaratapacaraṇa) or celibacy abstaining from sexual intercourse (methunaviratibrahmacariya)," is the meaning.
devo means one of Śakra, Suyāma, etc.
devaññataro means a certain god (deva) among the second and third ranks.
micchādiṭṭhī means, taking what is the path going to a non-god realm (adevaloka) alone as the path going to a god realm (devaloka), because of the arisen nature, that is called wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi).
aññataraṃ gatiṃ vadāmī means, for him, another destination other than hell (niraya) or the animal realm (tiracchānayoni) does not exist; therefore, he speaks thus.
sampajjamāna means lying down, not mixed with views (diṭṭhi).

nāhaṃ, bhante, etaṃ rodāmi, yaṃ maṃ bhagavā evamāhā means, "I, venerable sir (bhante), do not weep for this that the Blessed One (bhagavā) has said to me," is the meaning.
Thus, the meaning here should be understood in terms of karmic agency, not merely by the shedding of tears.

80. “Well, Seniya, since I certainly cannot persuade you when I say:

‘Enough, Seniya, let that be. Do not ask me that.’ I shall therefore answer you.

“Here, Seniya, someone develops the ox-duty (govataṃ) fully and uninterruptedly; he develops the ox-habit (gosīlaṃ) fully and uninterruptedly; he develops the ox-mind (gocittaṃ) fully and uninterruptedly; he develops ox-behaviour (gavākappaṃ) fully and uninterruptedly.
Having done so, on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he reappears in the company of oxen (gunnaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjati).
If he has such a view: ‘By this moral discipline (sīlena), or vow (vatena), or asceticism (tapena), or living the holy life (brahmacariyena), may I become a god (devo) or one among the gods (devaññataro vā)’, that is wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi).
Now there are two destinations for one with wrong view (micchādiṭṭhissa), I say: hell (nirayaṃ) or the animal realm (tiracchānayoniṃ).
So, Seniya, if his ox-duty (govataṃ) succeeds (sampajjamānaṃ), it will lead him to the company of oxen (gunnaṃ sahabyataṃ upaneti); if it fails (vipajjamānaṃ), it will lead him to hell (nirayaṃ).”

When this was said, Puṇṇa, son of the Koliyas, the ox-duty ascetic cried out and burst into tears (assūni pavattesi).

Then the Blessed One (bhagavā) told Seniya the naked dog-duty ascetic (kukkuravatikaṃ): “Seniya, I could not persuade you when I said:

‘Enough, Seniya, let that be. Do not ask me that.’”

Then Puṇṇa the ox-duty ascetic said: “Venerable sir (bhante), I am not crying because the Blessed One (bhagavā) has said this about me, but because I have long taken up and practised this ox-duty (govataṃ).
Venerable sir (bhante), I have confidence in the Blessed One (bhagavati) thus: ‘The Blessed One (bhagavā) is capable of teaching me the Dhamma in such a way that I can abandon this ox-duty (govataṃ) and that this Seniya the naked dog-duty ascetic (kukkuravatiko) can abandon that dog-duty (kukkuravataṃ).’”

“Well then, Puṇṇa, listen (suṇāhi) and attend carefully to what I shall say (bhāsissāmī).”

“Yes, venerable sir (bhante),” he replied.
The Blessed One (bhagavā) said:
80.Govatantiādīni kukkuravatādīsu vuttanayeneva veditabbāni. Gavākappanti goākappaṃ. Sesaṃ kukkurākappe vuttasadisameva. Yathā pana tattha aññe kukkure disvā dante vivaritvā gamanākāro, evamidha aññe gāvo disvā kaṇṇe ukkhipitvā gamanākāro veditabbo. Sesaṃ tādisameva.
80.
Govatanti and the rest should be understood just by the method already stated in kukkuravatādi.
Gavākappanti [means the same as] goākappaṃ.
The remainder is just like what has been stated in the case of kukkurākappa.
Just as there, on seeing other dogs, the manner of going is [with] teeth bared, so here, on seeing other cows, the manner of going is to be understood [with] ears raised.
The rest is just the same as that.

81. “Puṇṇa, there are these four kinds of kamma proclaimed by me after realizing them for myself with direct knowledge.

What are the four? There is, Puṇṇa, dark action (kamma) with dark result; there is, Puṇṇa, bright action with bright result; there is, Puṇṇa, dark-and-bright action with dark-and-bright result; and there is, Puṇṇa, action that is neither dark nor bright with neither-dark-nor-bright result, action that leads to the destruction of action.

“And what, Puṇṇa, is dark action with dark result? Here, someone generates an afflictive (sabyābajjhaṃ) bodily formation (kāyasaṅkhāraṃ), an afflictive verbal formation (vacīsaṅkhāraṃ), an afflictive mental formation (manosaṅkhāraṃ). Having generated an afflictive bodily formation, an afflictive verbal formation, an afflictive mental formation, he reappears in an afflictive world. When he has reappeared in an afflictive world, afflictive contacts touch him. Being touched by afflictive contacts, he feels afflictive feelings, extremely painful, as in the case of the beings in hell. Thus a being’s reappearance is due to a being; one reappears through the actions one has performed. When one has reappeared, contacts touch one. Thus I say beings are the heirs to their actions. This is called dark action with dark result.

“And what, Puṇṇa, is bright action with bright result? Here, someone generates an unafflictive bodily formation (abyābajjhaṃ kāyasaṅkhāraṃ), an unafflictive verbal formation (abyābajjhaṃ vacīsaṅkhāraṃ), an unafflictive mental formation (abyābajjhaṃ manosaṅkhāraṃ). Having generated an unafflictive bodily formation, an unafflictive verbal formation, an unafflictive mental formation, he reappears in an unafflictive world. When he has reappeared in an unafflictive world, unafflictive contacts touch him. Being touched by unafflictive contacts, he feels unafflictive feelings, extremely pleasant, as in the case of the gods of Refulgent Glory (subhakiṇhā). Thus a being’s reappearance is due to a being; one reappears through the actions one has performed. When one has reappeared, contacts touch one. Thus I say beings are the heirs to their actions. This is called bright action with bright result.

“And what, Puṇṇa, is dark-and-bright action with dark-and-bright result? Here, someone generates a bodily formation that is both afflictive and unafflictive (sabyābajjhampi abyābajjhampi kāyasaṅkhāraṃ), a verbal formation that is both afflictive and unafflictive (sabyābajjhampi abyābajjhampi vacīsaṅkhāraṃ), a mental formation that is both afflictive and unafflictive (sabyābajjhampi abyābajjhampi manosaṅkhāraṃ). Having generated a bodily formation, a verbal formation, a mental formation that is both afflictive and unafflictive, he reappears in a world that is both afflictive and unafflictive. When he has reappeared in a world that is both afflictive and unafflictive, both afflictive and unafflictive contacts touch him. Being touched by both afflictive and unafflictive contacts, he feels both afflictive and unafflictive feelings, mingled pleasure and pain (vokiṇṇasukhadukkhaṃ), as in the case of human beings and some gods and some beings in the lower worlds (vinipātikā). Thus a being’s reappearance is due to a being; one reappears through the actions one has performed. When one has reappeared, contacts touch one. Thus I say beings are the heirs to their actions. This is called dark-and-bright action with dark-and-bright result.
What, Puṇṇa, is the karma that is neither dark nor bright with neither dark nor bright result, [but] leads to the destruction of karma (kammakkhayāya)? Therein, Puṇṇa, the intention (cetanā) in abandoning that karma which is dark with dark result (kṛṣṇa-kṛṣṇa-vipāka), the intention in abandoning that karma which is bright with bright result (śukla-śukla-vipāka), and the intention in abandoning that karma which is dark and bright with dark and bright result (kṛṣṇaśukla-kṛṣṇaśukla-vipāka) – this is called, Puṇṇa, karma that is neither dark nor bright with neither dark nor bright result, [but] leads to the destruction of karma.

These, Puṇṇa, are the four kinds of karma proclaimed by me after realizing them for myself with direct knowledge."

81. Why did the Blessed One begin this discourse, "[There are] these four karmas"? This discourse came about due to a certain act of karma, and having spoken on this set of four karmas, the action of these will become evident; [therefore,] he began this discourse. Moreover, if this very set of four karmas is taught, they will understand these, then one will go to refuge, one will renounce and attain arhatship; knowing that this itself is suitable for them, he also began this discourse.

There, kaṇha means black, the ten paths of unwholesome action. Kaṇhavipāka means black result, because it is produced in the states of loss. Sukka means white, the ten paths of wholesome action. Sukkavipāka means white result, because it is produced in heaven. Kaṇhasukka means mixed karma. Kaṇhasukkavipāka means result of pleasure and pain. Having performed mixed karma, produced by unwholesome [karma] in the animal womb in places such as the elephant stables, he experiences pleasure when wholesome [karma] prevails. Produced by wholesome [karma] even in a royal family, he experiences pain when unwholesome [karma] prevails. Akaṇhaṃ asukka means the karma of the four paths of consciousness that destroys karma is intended. If that were black, it would give a black result. If it were white, it would give a white result. However, since it does not give both results, it is called “akaṇhaṃ asukka” because of being akaṇhāsukkavipāka. This, for the time being, is the meaning in the summary.

Kaṇha, kaṇhavipāka, sukka, sukkavipāka, kaṇhasukka, kaṇhasukkavipāka, akaṇhaṃ asukka.

In the explanation, however, sabyābajjha means with suffering. Among the bodily fabrications, etc., the twelve unwholesome consciousnesses that are obtained in the body-door by way of grasping, etc., are called sabyābajjhakāyasaṅkhāro. The twelve that occur in the speech-door by way of the movement of the jaws, which are the cause of the breaking up of speech, are called vacīsaṅkhāro. The manosaṅkhāro is that which occurs in the mind-door of one who is thinking in private, not having obtained both kinds of movement. Thus, in all three doors, only unwholesome consciousnesses with the distinction of bodily misconduct, etc., are to be understood as saṅkhāra. In this sutta, consciousness is the chief thing; in the Upāli sutta, karma [is the chief thing]. Abhisaṅkharitvā means having tightened, having made a lump. Sabyābajjhaṃ loka means they are reborn in a world with suffering. Sabyābajjhā phassā phusantī means painful result-contacts touch. Ekantadukkha means uninterrupted suffering. Bhūtā is a word of certainty in the sense of a cause; the arising of a being is from past karma. This has been said: beings perform karma according to what has come to be; their rebirth is by way of the similarity of karma with karma that has come to be. Therefore, he said, "what one does, by that one is reborn." And here, tenā is said as if it were by karma; and rebirth is by result. However, since karma is the cause of result, therefore it is that it is produced by the original causal karma; this is the meaning here. Phassā phusantī means by whatever result of karma one is produced, those result-contacts of karma touch. Kammadāyādā means kammadāyajjā, I say that karma itself is their inheritance, their own property.

Sabyābajjha, sabyābajjhakāyasaṅkhāro, vacīsaṅkhāro, manosaṅkhāro, abhisaṅkharitvā, sabyābajjhaṃ loka, sabyābajjhā phassā phusantī, ekantadukkha, bhūtā, tenā, phassā phusantī, kammadāyādā.

I go to the Blessed One for refuge, and to the Dhamma and to the Saṅgha of monks. May I receive the going forth in the Blessed One’s presence? May I receive admission?”

“Seniya, one who formerly belonged to another sect and desires the going forth and the full admission in this Dhamma and Discipline lives on probation for four months. At the end of four months, if the bhikkhus are satisfied with him, they give him the going forth and the full admission to the bhikkhus’ state. But I recognise individual differences in this matter.”

“Venerable sir, if those who formerly belonged to another sect and desire the going forth and the full admission in this Dhamma and Discipline live on probation for four months, and if at the end of the four months the bhikkhus being satisfied with them give them the going forth and the full admission to the bhikkhus’ state, then I will live on probation for four years. At the end of the four years if the bhikkhus are satisfied with me, let them give me the going forth and the full admission to the bhikkhus’ state.”

Then Seniya the naked dog-duty ascetic received the going forth under the Blessed One, and he received the full admission. And soon, not long after his full admission, dwelling alone, withdrawn, diligent, ardent, and resolute, the venerable Seniya, by realizing for himself with direct knowledge, here and now entered upon and abided in that supreme goal of the holy life for the sake of which clansmen rightly go forth from the home life into homelessness. He directly knew: “Birth is destroyed, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of being.” And the venerable Seniya became one of the arahants.

The Kukuravatika Sutta, the seventh, is finished.

"I would receive it, venerable sir," thinking, "For a long time, indeed, I have exhausted myself by applying [my] self to what leads to no release, just like someone agitating at a pile of husks, or like someone pounding at husks, with no purpose accomplished by someone thinking, 'I will bathe at the bank of a dried-up river,' now I will apply myself to the practice," he said.

Then, in the section on types, the Blessed One established the probationary period for those formerly of other sects. Someone formerly of another sect, standing on the level of a novice, having undertaken it in the manner of "I, venerable sir, named thus-and-so, formerly of another sect, desire the higher ordination in this Doctrine and Discipline; I, venerable sir, ask the Saṅgha for the probationary period of four months," (Mahāva. 86) lives on probation. With reference to that, he said, "Whoever, Seniya, was formerly of another sect," and so on.

There, pabbajja (going forth) is mentioned merely by way of verbal embellishment. For he obtains the going forth without having lived on probation. But by one seeking the higher ordination, having fulfilled the eight duties such as not entering a village at an unseasonable time, the probationary period should be lived through. āraddhacittā means with mind pleased by the fulfillment of the eight duties. This is a brief explanation here. But this probationary period for those of other sects should be understood in detail in the same way as stated in the section on the going forth in the commentary on the Vinaya, the Samantapāsādikā (Mahāva. aṭṭha. 86). apica metthā means apica me ettha (moreover, to me here). puggalavemattatā viditā means puggalanānattaṃ viditaṃ (the distinction of individuals is known). It shows that "This individual is fit for the probationary period, this one is not fit for the probationary period; this is plain to me."

acirūpasampanno means having been ordained, not long after. vūpakaṭṭho means secluded by body and also by mind from sense-desires relating to the objects [of sense] and to defilements. appamatto means not abandoning mindfulness in the meditation subject. ātāpī means ardent with the heat of effort consisting of what is bodily and mental. pahitatto means with self sent forth, with essence of self dismissed, through not being attached to body and life. yassatthāyā means yassa atthāya (for the sake of which). kulaputtā means sons of good families in terms of conduct. sammadevā means just by reason, just by cause. tadanuttara means that unsurpassed. brahmacariyapariyosāna means the fruit of arahantship that is the culmination of the holy life of the path. Indeed, for that benefit young men of good families become monks. diṭṭheva dhamme means in this very existence. sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā means having directly experienced with his own wisdom, having known without reliance on another.
Having attained and accomplished, he dwelt.
Thus, as he lived, destroyed was birth...and he directly knew.

Newly ordained, secluded, diligent, ardent, with self-controlled, for which the sons of good family rightly go forth from home to homelessness, that unsurpassed culmination of the holy life, in this very life, having directly known by himself with higher knowledge, he dwells, attained. Destroyed is birth...and he directly knew.

Having shown the ground for reflection, in order to complete the teaching with the pinnacle of Arahatship, it was said, "A certain venerable Seniya was among the Arahats."

There, "a certain one" means one.

"Of the Arahats" means he was among the inner circle of the Arahats, disciples of the Blessed One; this is the meaning.

The rest is clear throughout.

A certain one, of the Arahats.
