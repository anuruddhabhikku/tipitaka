Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘vīmaṃsakena, bhikkhave, bhikkhunā parassa cetopariyāyaṃ ajānantena ājānantena (pī. ka.), ajānantena kinti (?) tathāgate samannesanā kātabbā ‘sammāsambuddho vā no vā’ iti viññāṇāyā’’ti. ‘‘Bhagavaṃmūlakā no, bhante, dhammā, bhagavaṃnettikā bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā; sādhu vata, bhante, bhagavantaṃyeva paṭibhātu etassa bhāsitassa attho; bhagavato sutvā bhikkhū dhāressantī’’ti. ‘‘Tena hi, bhikkhave, suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha, bhāsissāmī’’ti . ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –
Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was staying at Sāvatthī (sāvatthiyaṃ) in Jeta's Grove (jetavane), Anāthapindika's Park (anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

There the Blessed One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: "Monks (bhikkhavo)!"

"Venerable Sir (bhadante)," those monks (bhikkhū) replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this: "Monks (bhikkhave), a monk (bhikkhunā) who is an inquirer (vīmaṃsakena), not knowing how to gauge another’s mind (parassa cetopariyāyaṃ ajānantena), should make an investigation (samannesanā kātabbā) of the Tathāgata (tathāgate) in order to find out whether or not he is a sammāsambuddha (sammāsambuddho vā no vā) for the purpose of understanding (viññāṇāya)."

"Venerable Sir (bhante), our teachings (dhammā) are rooted in the Blessed One (bhagavaṃmūlakā no), guided by the Blessed One (bhagavaṃnettikā), have the Blessed One as their resort (bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā); it would be good (sādhu vata), Venerable Sir (bhante), if the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃyeva) would explain (paṭibhātu) the meaning (attho) of these words (etassa bhāsitassa); having heard (sutvā) it from the Blessed One (bhagavato), the monks (bhikkhū) will remember (dhāressantī)."

"Then listen (suṇātha), monks (bhikkhave), and attend carefully (sādhukaṃ manasi karotha). I shall speak (bhāsissāmī)."

"As you say, lord (evaṃ, bhante)," those monks (bhikkhū) replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said:
487.Evaṃme sutanti vīmaṃsakasuttaṃ. Tattha vīmaṃsakenāti tayo vīmaṃsakā – atthavīmaṃsako saṅkhāravīmaṃsako satthuvīmaṃsakoti. Tesu, ‘‘paṇḍitā hāvuso, manussā vīmaṃsakā’’ti (saṃ. ni. 3.2) ettha atthavīmaṃsako āgato. ‘‘Yato kho, ānanda, bhikkhu dhātukusalo ca hoti, āyatanakusalo ca hoti, paṭiccasamuppādakusalo ca hoti, ṭhānāṭṭhānakusalo ca hoti, ettāvatā kho, ānanda, paṇḍito bhikkhu vīmaṃsakoti alaṃ vacanāyā’’ti (ma. ni. 3.124) ettha saṅkhāravīmaṃsako āgato. Imasmiṃ pana sutte satthuvīmaṃsako adhippeto. Cetopariyāyanti cittavāraṃ cittaparicchedaṃ. Samannesanāti esanā pariyesanā upaparikkhā. Iti viññāṇāyāti evaṃ vijānanatthāya.
487. Thus have I heard, is the Vīmaṃsaka Sutta.

Therein, vīmaṃsakenā (by an investigator) refers to three types of investigators: an investigator of meaning (attha-vīmaṃsako), an investigator of formations (saṅkhāra-vīmaṃsako), and an investigator of the teacher (satthu-vīmaṃsako).

Among these, "Wise men, friends, are investigators" (SN 3.2) refers to the investigator of meaning.

"When, Ānanda, a bhikkhu is skilled in the elements (dhātukusalo), skilled in the bases (āyatanakusalo), skilled in dependent origination (paṭiccasamuppādakusalo), skilled in what is possible and what is impossible (ṭhānāṭṭhānakusalo), in that way he can be called a wise man and an investigator" (MN 3.124) refers to the investigator of formations.

But in this sutta, the investigator of the teacher is intended.

cetopariyāya (awareness of the minds of others) means an occasion of mind (cittavāraṃ), a delimitation of mind (cittaparicchedaṃ).

samannesanā (investigation) means searching (esanā), seeking (pariyesanā), examination (upaparikkhā).

iti viññāṇāya (for the sake of knowing) means thus for the sake of understanding (evaṃ vijānanatthāya).

488. ‘‘Vīmaṃsakena, bhikkhave, bhikkhunā parassa cetopariyāyaṃ ajānantena dvīsu dhammesu tathāgato samannesitabbo cakkhusotaviññeyyesu dhammesu – ‘ye saṃkiliṭṭhā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, saṃvijjanti vā te tathāgatassa no vā’ti? Tamenaṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘ye saṃkiliṭṭhā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, na te tathāgatassa saṃvijjantī’ti. ‘‘Yato naṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘ye saṃkiliṭṭhā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, na te tathāgatassa saṃvijjantī’ti, tato naṃ uttariṃ samannesati – ‘ye vītimissā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, saṃvijjanti vā te tathāgatassa no vā’ti? Tamenaṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘ye vītimissā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, na te tathāgatassa saṃvijjantī’ti. ‘‘Yato naṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘ye vītimissā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, na te tathāgatassa saṃvijjantī’ti, tato naṃ uttariṃ samannesati – ‘ye vodātā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, saṃvijjanti vā te tathāgatassa no vā’ti? Tamenaṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘ye vodātā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, saṃvijjanti te tathāgatassā’ti. ‘‘Yato naṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘ye vodātā cakkhusotaviññeyyā dhammā, saṃvijjanti te tathāgatassā’ti, tato naṃ uttariṃ samannesati – ‘dīgharattaṃ samāpanno ayamāyasmā imaṃ kusalaṃ dhammaṃ, udāhu ittarasamāpanno’ti? Tamenaṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘dīgharattaṃ samāpanno ayamāyasmā imaṃ kusalaṃ dhammaṃ, nāyamāyasmā ittarasamāpanno’ti. ‘‘Yato naṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘dīgharattaṃ samāpanno ayamāyasmā imaṃ kusalaṃ dhammaṃ, nāyamāyasmā ittarasamāpanno’ti, tato naṃ uttariṃ samannesati – ‘ñattajjhāpanno ayamāyasmā bhikkhu yasappatto, saṃvijjantassa idhekacce ādīnavā’ti? Na tāva, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno idhekacce ādīnavā saṃvijjanti yāva na ñattajjhāpanno hoti yasappatto. Yato ca kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ñattajjhāpanno hoti yasappatto , athassa idhekacce ādīnavā saṃvijjanti. Tamenaṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘ñattajjhāpanno ayamāyasmā bhikkhu yasappatto, nāssa idhekacce ādīnavā saṃvijjantī’ti. ‘‘Yato naṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘ñattajjhāpanno ayamāyasmā bhikkhu yasappatto, nāssa idhekacce ādīnavā saṃvijjantī’ti, tato naṃ uttariṃ samannesati – ‘abhayūparato ayamāyasmā, nāyamāyasmā bhayūparato; vītarāgattā kāme na sevati khayā rāgassā’ti? Tamenaṃ samannesamāno evaṃ jānāti – ‘abhayūparato ayamāyasmā, nāyamāyasmā bhayūparato; vītarāgattā kāme na sevati khayā rāgassā’ti. Tañce, bhikkhave, bhikkhuṃ pare evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘ke panāyasmato ākārā, ke anvayā, yenāyasmā evaṃ vadesi – abhayūparato ayamāyasmā, nāyamāyasmā bhayūparato; vītarāgattā kāme na sevati khayā rāgassā’ti. Sammā byākaramāno, bhikkhave, bhikkhu evaṃ byākareyya – ‘tathā hi pana ayamāyasmā saṅghe vā viharanto eko vā viharanto, ye ca tattha sugatā ye ca tattha duggatā, ye ca tattha gaṇamanusāsanti, ye ca idhekacce āmisesu saṃdissanti, ye ca idhekacce āmisena anupalittā, nāyamāyasmā taṃ tena avajānāti . Sammukhā kho pana metaṃ bhagavato sutaṃ sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ – abhayūparatohamasmi, nāhamasmi bhayūparato, vītarāgattā kāme na sevāmi khayā rāgassā’ti.

"Bhikkhus, a bhikkhu who is an inquirer, not knowing how to gauge another’s mind, should investigate the Tathāgata with respect to two kinds of states, states cognizable through the eye (cakṣu) and through the ear (śrotra) thus: ‘Are there found in the Tathāgata or not any defiled (saṃkiliṣṭhā) states cognizable through the eye or through the ear?’ When he investigates him, he comes to know: ‘No defiled (saṃkiliṣṭhā) states cognizable through the eye or through the ear are found in the Tathāgata.’

"When he comes to know this, he investigates him further thus: ‘Are there found in the Tathāgata or not any mixed (vītimissā) states cognizable through the eye or through the ear?’ When he investigates him, he comes to know: ‘No mixed (vītimissā) states cognizable through the eye or through the ear are found in the Tathāgata.’

"When he comes to know this, he investigates him further thus: ‘Are there found in the Tathāgata or not cleansed (vodātā) states cognizable through the eye or through the ear?’ When he investigates him, he comes to know: ‘Cleansed (vodātā) states cognizable through the eye or through the ear are found in the Tathāgata.’

"When he comes to know this, he investigates him further thus: ‘Has this venerable one attained this wholesome (kuśalaṃ) state over a long time (dīgharattaṃ) or did he attain it recently (ittarasamāpanno)?’ When he investigates him, he comes to know: ‘This venerable one has attained this wholesome (kuśalaṃ) state over a long time (dīgharattaṃ), he did not attain it recently (ittarasamāpanno).’

"When he comes to know this, he investigates him further thus: ‘This venerable one has acquired renown (ñattajjhāpanno) and attained fame (yasappatto), are there found in him any dangers (ādīnavā)?’ As long as a bhikkhu has not acquired renown (ñattajjhāpanno) and attained fame (yasappatto), those dangers (ādīnavā) are not found in him.
But when, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu has acquired renown (ñattajjhāpanno) and attained fame (yasappatto), then those dangers (ādīnavā) are found in him.
When he investigates him, he comes to know: ‘This venerable bhikkhu has acquired renown (ñattajjhāpanno) and attained fame (yasappatto), but the dangers (ādīnavā) are not found in him.’

"When he comes to know this, he investigates him further thus: ‘Is this venerable one restrained without fear (abhayūparato), not restrained by fear (bhayūparato), and does he avoid indulging in sensual pleasures (kāme) because he is without lust (vītarāgattā) through the destruction (khayā) of lust (rāgassa)?’ When he investigates him, he comes to know: ‘This venerable one is restrained without fear (abhayūparato), not restrained by fear (bhayūparato), and he avoids indulging in sensual pleasures (kāme) because he is without lust (vītarāgattā) through the destruction (khayā) of lust (rāgassa).’
“Now, bhikkhus, if others should ask that bhikkhu thus: ‘What are the venerable one’s reasons (ākārā) and what is his evidence (anvayā) whereby he says: “That venerable one is restrained without fear (abhayūparato), not restrained by fear (bhayūparato), and he avoids indulging in sensual pleasures (kāme) because he is without lust (vītarāgattā) through the destruction (khayā) of lust (rāgassa)”?’
- answering rightly, that bhikkhu would answer thus: ‘Whether that venerable one dwells in the Saṅgha or alone, while some there are well-behaved (sugatā) and some are ill-behaved (duggatā) and some there teach a group (gaṇamanusāsanti), while some here are seen concerned about material things (āmisesu saṃdissanti) and some are unsullied by material things (āmisena anupalittā), still that venerable one does not despise anyone because of that.
And I have heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s own lips: “I am restrained without fear (abhayūparato), not restrained by fear (bhayūparato), and I avoid indulging in sensual pleasures (kāme) because I am without lust (vītarāgattā) through the destruction (khayā) of lust (rāgassa).’“

Mūlajātāti sotāpattimaggavasena sañjātamūlā. Sotāpattimaggo hi saddhāya mūlaṃ nāma. Ākāravatīti kāraṇaṃ pariyesitvā gahitattā sakāraṇā. Dassanamūlikāti sotāpattimaggamūlikā. So hi dassananti vuccati. Daḷhāti thirā. Asaṃhāriyāti harituṃ na sakkā. Samaṇena vāti samitapāpasamaṇena vā. Brāhmaṇena vāti bāhitapāpabrāhmaṇena vā. Devena vāti upapattidevena vā. Mārena vāti vasavattimārena vā, sotāpannassa hi vasavattimārenāpi saddhā asaṃhāriyā hoti sūrambaṭṭhassa viya. So kira satthu dhammadesanaṃ sutvā sotāpanno hutvā gehaṃ āgato. Atha māro dvattiṃsavaralakkhaṇappaṭimaṇḍitaṃ buddharūpaṃ māpetvā tassa gharadvāre ṭhatvā – ‘‘satthā āgato’’ti sāsanaṃ pahiṇi. Sūro cintesi, ‘‘ahaṃ idāneva satthu santikā dhammaṃ sutvā āgato, kiṃ nu kho bhavissatī’’ti upasaṅkamitvā satthusaññāya vanditvā aṭṭhāsi. Māro āha – ‘‘yaṃ te mayā, sūrambaṭṭha, rūpaṃ aniccaṃ…pe… viññāṇaṃ aniccanti kathitaṃ, taṃ anupadhāretvāva sahasā mayā evaṃ vuttaṃ. Tasmā tvaṃ rūpaṃ niccaṃ…pe… viññāṇaṃ niccanti gaṇhāhī’’ti. Sūro cintesi – ‘‘aṭṭhānametaṃ, yaṃ buddhā anupadhāretvā apaccakkhaṃ katvā kiñci katheyyuṃ, addhā ayaṃ mayhaṃ vibādhanatthaṃ māro āgato’’ti. Tato naṃ tvaṃ māroti āha. So musāvādaṃ kātuṃ nāsakkhi, āma mārosmīti paṭijāni. Kasmā āgatosīti vutte tava saddhācālanatthanti āha. Kaṇha pāpima, tvaṃ tāva ekako tiṭṭha, tādisānaṃ mārānaṃ satampi sahassampi mama saddhaṃ cāletuṃ asamatthaṃ, maggena āgatā saddhā nāma silāpathaviyaṃ patiṭṭhitasineru viya acalā hoti, kiṃ tvaṃ etthāti accharaṃ pahari. So ṭhātuṃ asakkonto tatthevantaradhāyi. Brahmunā vāti brahmakāyikādīsu aññatarabrahmunā vā. Kenaci vā lokasminti ete samaṇādayo ṭhapetvā aññenapi kenaci vā lokasmiṃ harituṃ na sakkā. Dhammasamannesanāti sabhāvasamannesanā. Dhammatāsusamanniṭṭhoti dhammatāya susamanniṭṭho, sabhāveneva suṭṭhu samannesito hotīti attho. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Brahmunā vā Kenaci vā lokasmi Dhammasamannesanā Dhammatāsusamanniṭṭho Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Vīmaṃsakasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
490.
"Because of these characteristics ( imehi ākārehī )" means because of these reasons for investigating the Teacher.
"Because of these words ( imehi padehī )" means because of these words consisting of a collection of syllables.
"Because of these consonants ( imehi byañjanehī )" means because of these syllables stated here.
"Faith is fixed ( saddhā niviṭṭhā )" means conviction is established.
"Having a root ( mūlajātā )" means having a root that has arisen through the way of stream-entry ( sotāpattimagga ).
The way of stream-entry ( sotāpattimagga ) is called the root of faith ( saddhā ).
"Having characteristics ( ākāravatī )" means having a cause ( kāraṇa ) because of taking it after seeking a cause.
"Having the stream-entry way as its root ( dassanamūlikā )" means having the sotāpattimagga as its root.
That is called insight ( dassana ).
"Firm ( daḷhā )" means stable ( thirā ).
"Unshakeable ( asaṃhāriyā )" means it is not possible to take away ( harituṃ na sakkā ).
"By a śramaṇa ( samaṇena vā )" means by a śramaṇa whose evil has been calmed.
"By a brāhmaṇa ( brāhmaṇena vā )" means by a brāhmaṇa who has discarded evil.
"By a deva ( devena vā )" means by a deva who has arisen.
"By a māra ( mārena vā )" means by a māra who has power, or, for a stream-enterer ( sotāpanna ), even by a māra who has power, faith is unshakeable, like that of Sūrambaṭṭha.

Then he, having heard the Dharma teaching of the Teacher, having become a stream-enterer, came to his house.
Then Māra, having created a Buddha-image adorned with the thirty-two excellent marks, having stood at the door of his house, sent the message, "The Teacher has arrived."
Sūra thought, "I have just now heard the Dharma from the Teacher, what will happen?", having approached, having saluted with the perception of the Teacher, he stood aside.
Māra said, "That which was said by me to you, Sūrambaṭṭha, that form is impermanent...etc...consciousness is impermanent, that was said by me rashly, without considering it."
Therefore, you should grasp that form is permanent...etc...consciousness is permanent.
Sūra thought, "This is not a possibility, that Buddhas would say something without considering it, having made it non-manifest, truly this Māra has come to disturb me."
Then he said to him, "You are Māra."
He was not able to speak falsely, he acknowledged, "Yes, I am Māra."
When asked, "Why have you come?", he said, "To shake your faith."
"Kanhha, evil one, you just stand alone, even a hundred or a thousand of such Māras are unable to shake my faith, the faith that has come by the path is unmoving like Sineru established on the stone earth, what are you here?", he uttered a verse.
He, being unable to stand, disappeared right there.
"By a brahmā ( brahmunā vā )" means by some brahmā or other among those beginning with the brahmakāyikas.
"By someone in the world ( kenaci vā lokasmi )" means that these śramaṇas and the rest having been set aside, by someone else in the world it is not possible to take it away.
"Dhammasamannesanā" means investigation of intrinsic nature.
"Dhammatāsusamanniṭṭho" means well-established in the nature of phenomena, meaning he is well investigated by intrinsic nature itself.
The rest is clear everywhere.
