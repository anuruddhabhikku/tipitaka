Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā mahācundo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘yā imā, bhante, anekavihitā diṭṭhiyo loke uppajjanti – attavādapaṭisaṃyuttā vā lokavādapaṭisaṃyuttā vā – ādimeva nu kho, bhante, bhikkhuno manasikaroto evametāsaṃ diṭṭhīnaṃ pahānaṃ hoti, evametāsaṃ diṭṭhīnaṃ paṭinissaggo hotī’’ti?
81. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Savatthi in the Jeta Grove, Anathapindika’s Park.

Then, when it was evening, the venerable Mahācunda rose from meditation and went to the Blessed One. After paying homage to the Blessed One he sat down at one side.

Sitting at one side, the venerable Mahācunda said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, various views arise in the world associated either with doctrines of a self (ātma-vāda) or with doctrines about the world (loka-vāda). Now does the abandoning and relinquishing of those views come about in a bhikkhu who is attending only to the beginning of his meditative training?"
81.Evaṃme sutanti sallekhasuttaṃ. Tattha mahācundoti tassa therassa nāmaṃ. Sāyanhasamayanti sāyanhakāle. Paṭisallānā vuṭṭhitoti ettha paṭisallānanti tehi tehi sattasaṅkhārehi paṭinivattitvā sallānaṃ nilīyanaṃ, ekībhāvo pavivekoti vuttaṃ hoti. Yo tato vuṭṭhito, so paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito nāma hoti. Ayaṃ pana yasmā paṭisallānānaṃ uttamato phalasamāpattito vuṭṭhāsi, tasmā ‘‘paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito’’ti vutto. Bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvāti samadasanakhujjalavibhūsitena sirasā bhagavantaṃ sakkaccaṃ vanditvā, abhivādāpetvā vā ‘‘sukhī bhava, cundā’’ti evaṃ vacībhedaṃ kārāpetvā, bhagavā pana kira vandito samāno suvaṇṇadundubhisadisaṃ gīvaṃ paggayha kaṇṇasukhaṃ pemaniyaṃ amatābhisekasadisaṃ brahmaghosaṃ nicchārento ‘‘sukhī hohī’’ti tassa tassa nāmaṃ gahetvā vadati, etaṃ āciṇṇaṃ tathāgatānaṃ. Tatridaṃ sādhakasuttaṃ, ‘‘sakko, bhante, devānamindo sāmacco saparijano bhagavato pāde sirasā vandatīti, sukhī hotu pañcasikha sakko devānamindo sāmacco saparijano, sukhakāmā hi devā manussā asurā nāgā gandhabbā, ye caññe santi puthukāyā’’ti. Evañca pana tathāgatā evarūpe mahesakkhe yakkhe abhivadantīti. Yā imāti idāni vattabbābhimukhaṃ karonto viya āha. Anekavihitāti nānappakārā. Diṭṭhiyoti micchādiṭṭhiyo . Loke uppajjantīti sattesu pātubhavanti. Attavādappaṭisaṃyuttāti ‘‘rūpaṃ attato samanupassatī’’tiādinayappavattena attavādena paṭisaṃyuttā, tā vīsati bhavanti. Lokavādappaṭisaṃyuttāti ‘‘sassato attā ca loko cā’’tiādinayappavattena lokavādena paṭisaṃyuttā, tā aṭṭha honti sassato, asassato, sassato ca asassato ca, neva sassato nāsassato, antavā, anantavā, antavā ca anantavā ca, nevantavā nānantavā attā ca loko cāti evaṃ pavattattā. Yā imā Anekavihitā Diṭṭhiyo Loke uppajjantī Attavādappaṭisaṃyuttā Lokavādappaṭisaṃyuttā Ādimevātiādīsu ayamattho kiṃ nu kho bhante ādimeva manasikarontassa appatvāpi sotāpattimaggaṃ vipassanāmissakapaṭhamamanasikārameva manasikarontassa bhikkhuno evametāsaṃ ettakeneva upāyena etāsaṃ diṭṭhīnaṃ pahānañca paṭinissaggo ca hotīti. Idañca thero attanā anadhimānikopi samāno adhimānikānaṃ adhimānappahānatthaṃ adhimāniko viya hutvā pucchatīti veditabbo. Apare panāhu ‘‘therassa antevāsikā ādimanasikāreneva diṭṭhīnaṃ samucchedappahānaṃ hotīti evaṃsaññinopi, samāpattivihārā sallekhavihārāti evaṃsaññinopi atthi. 81. Thus have I heard, the Sallekha Sutta.

There, mahācundo means the name of that elder.

sāyanhasamaya means at evening time.

paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito means here, paṭisallāna means having turned back from those various clinging to existence (sattasaṅkhārehi), seclusion (sallānaṃ), retreat (nilīyanaṃ), unification, it is said to be solitude (pavivekoti vuttaṃ hoti).

He who has arisen from that, is said to have arisen from seclusion.

Moreover, since he arose from the highest attainment of fruition of the instances of seclusion, therefore he is called "arisen from seclusion."

bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā means having respectfully venerated the Blessed One with a head adorned with a joyful smile, and also having caused [someone] to salute, or having caused [him] to make a distinction of speech thus: "May you be happy, Cunda," the Blessed One, it is said, being venerated, raising his neck resembling a golden kettledrum, emanating a Brahmā-like voice that is pleasing to the ear, lovely, and like an anointment of immortality, taking the name of each of them, says, "May you be happy." This is the custom of the Tathāgatas.

There is this establishing sutra, "Sakka, Venerable Sir, devānamindo sāmacco saparijano bhagavato pāde sirasā vandatīti, may Sakka, king of the gods, his ministers and followers be happy, devas, humans, asuras, nāgas, gandhabbas, whatever other groups of beings there are, are happy."

And that is how the Tathāgatas greet such mighty yakṣas.

yā imā means now, as if making [someone] face what is to be said, he said.

anekavihitā means various kinds.

diṭṭhiyo means wrong views.

loke uppajjantī means they appear in beings.

attavādappaṭisaṃyuttā means "form, he perceives as self," connected with the doctrine of self that arises from the way beginning with this, they are twenty.

lokavādappaṭisaṃyuttā means "the self and the world are eternal," connected with the doctrine of the world that arises from the way beginning with this, they are eight: eternal (sassato), non-eternal (asassato), both eternal and non-eternal (sassato ca asassato ca), neither eternal nor non-eternal (neva sassato nāsassato), having an end (antavā), not having an end (anantavā), both having an end and not having an end (antavā ca anantavā ca), neither having an end nor not having an end (nevantavā nānantavā), the self and the world, are of such a nature.

yā imā anekavihitā diṭṭhiyo loke uppajjantī attavādappaṭisaṃyuttā lokavādappaṭisaṃyuttā

ādimevā etc., in these, what is the meaning? Venerable Sir, even if one does not attain the path of stream-entry (sotāpattimaggaṃ), through initially directing the mind (ādimeva manasikarontassa), the renunciation (pahānañca) and relinquishment (paṭinissaggo ca) of these views (diṭṭhīnaṃ) occur for a bhikkhu who initially directs the mind (manasikarontassa bhikkhuno), by this mere means (ettakeneva upāyena), in this way (evametāsaṃ) (through the initial mental application mixed with insight (vipassanāmissakapaṭhamamanasikārameva)).

And this elder, though not conceited himself, should be known as asking as if he were conceited, for the sake of abandoning the conceit of the conceited.

Others, however, say, "For the residents of the elder, the destruction and abandonment of views occur only through the initial direction of the mind (ādimanasikāreneva). Even for those who are so-minded, there are abodes of attainment and abodes of relinquishment."

He asks the Blessed One for their benefit.

82. ‘‘Yā imā, cunda, anekavihitā diṭṭhiyo loke uppajjanti – attavādapaṭisaṃyuttā vā lokavādapaṭisaṃyuttā vā – yattha cetā diṭṭhiyo uppajjanti yattha ca anusenti yattha ca samudācaranti taṃ ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na me so attā’ti – evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññā passato evametāsaṃ diṭṭhīnaṃ pahānaṃ hoti, evametāsaṃ diṭṭhīnaṃ paṭinissaggo hoti. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, cunda, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja vihareyya. Tassa evamassa – ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti. Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti. Diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārā ete ariyassa vinaye vuccanti. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, cunda, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco bhikkhu vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja vihareyya. Tassa evamassa – ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti. Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti. Diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārā ete ariyassa vinaye vuccanti. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, cunda, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco bhikkhu pītiyā ca virāgā upekkhako ca vihareyya, sato ca sampajāno sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṃvedeyya, yaṃ taṃ ariyā ācikkhanti – ‘upekkhako satimā sukhavihārī’ti tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja vihareyya. Tassa evamassa – ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti. Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti. Diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārā ete ariyassa vinaye vuccanti. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, cunda, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco bhikkhu sukhassa ca pahānā dukkhassa ca pahānā pubbeva somanassadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamā adukkhamasuṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja vihareyya. Tassa evamassa – ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti. Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti. Diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārā ete ariyassa vinaye vuccanti. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, cunda, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco bhikkhu sabbaso rūpasaññānaṃ samatikkamā, paṭighasaññānaṃ atthaṅgamā, nānattasaññānaṃ amanasikārā, ‘ananto ākāso’ti ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja vihareyya. Tassa evamassa – ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti. Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti. Santā ete vihārā ariyassa vinaye vuccanti. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, cunda, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco bhikkhu sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma ‘anantaṃ viññāṇa’nti viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja vihareyya. Tassa evamassa – ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti. Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti. Santā ete vihārā ariyassa vinaye vuccanti. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, cunda, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco bhikkhu sabbaso viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma ‘natthi kiñcī’ti ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja vihareyya. Tassa evamassa – ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti. Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti. Santā ete vihārā ariyassa vinaye vuccanti. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, cunda, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco bhikkhu sabbaso ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ samatikkamma nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja vihareyya. Tassa evamassa – ‘sallekhena viharāmī’ti . Na kho panete, cunda, ariyassa vinaye sallekhā vuccanti. Santā ete vihārā ariyassa vinaye vuccanti.

Cunda, as to those various views that arise in the world associated either with doctrines of a self or with doctrines about the world: if the object in relation to which those views arise, which they underlie, and which they are exercised upon is seen as it actually is with proper wisdom thus: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self,’ then the abandoning and relinquishing of those views comes about.

It is possible here that with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, some bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna: rapture & pleasure born of seclusion, accompanied by directed thought & evaluation.

He might think thus: ‘I am abiding in effacement ( sallekha ).’

But these attainments are not called ‘effacement’ in the Noble One’s Discipline: these are called ‘pleasant abidings here and now’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.

It is possible here that with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, some bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the second jhāna, which has self-confidence and singleness of mind without applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of concentration.

He might think thus: ‘I am abiding in effacement ( sallekha ).’

But these attainments are not called ‘effacement’ in the Noble One’s Discipline: these are called ‘pleasant abidings here and now’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.

It is possible here that with the fading away as well of rapture, some bhikkhu abides in equanimity, and mindful and fully aware, still feeling pleasure with the body, he enters upon and abides in the third jhāna, on account of which noble ones announce: ‘He has a pleasant abiding who has equanimity and is mindful.’

He might think thus: ‘I am abiding in effacement ( sallekha ).’

But these attainments are not called ‘effacement’ in the Noble One’s Discipline: these are called ‘pleasant abidings here and now’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.

It is possible here that with the abandoning of pleasure & pain—as with the earlier disappearance of elation & distress—some bhikkhu enters & remains in the fourth jhāna: purity of equanimity & mindfulness, neither pleasure nor pain.

He might think thus: ‘I am abiding in effacement ( sallekha ).’

But these attainments are not called ‘effacement’ in the Noble One’s Discipline: these are called ‘pleasant abidings here and now’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.

It is possible here that with the complete surmounting of perceptions of form, with the disappearance of perceptions of sensory impact, with non-attention to perceptions of diversity, aware that ‘space is infinite,’ some bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ).

He might think thus: ‘I am abiding in effacement ( sallekha ).’

But these attainments are not called ‘effacement’ in the Noble One’s Discipline: these are called ‘peaceful abidings’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.

It is possible here that by completely surmounting the base of infinite space, aware that ‘consciousness is infinite,’ some bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ).

He might think thus: ‘I am abiding in effacement ( sallekha ).’

But these attainments are not called ‘effacement’ in the Noble One’s Discipline: these are called ‘peaceful abidings’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.

It is possible here that by completely surmounting the base of infinite consciousness, aware that ‘there is nothing,’ some bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ).

He might think thus: ‘I am abiding in effacement ( sallekha ).’

But these attainments are not called ‘effacement’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.
These are called ‘peaceful abidings’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.

“It is possible here, Cunda, that by completely surmounting the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ), some bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ).
It might occur to him, ‘I am abiding in detachment (sallekhena viharāmī).’

But these, Cunda, are not called detachment (sallekhā) in the Noble One’s Discipline (ariyassa vinaye).
These are called ‘peaceful abidings’ in the Noble One’s Discipline.
82. Athassa bhagavā tāsaṃ diṭṭhīnaṃ pahānūpāyaṃ dassento yā imātiādimāha. Tattha yattha cetā diṭṭhiyo uppajjantītiādi pañcakkhandhe sandhāya vuttaṃ. Etesu hi etā diṭṭhiyo uppajjanti. Yathāha ‘‘rūpe kho, bhikkhave, sati rūpaṃ abhinivissa evaṃ diṭṭhi uppajjati, so attā so loko so pecca bhavissāmi nicco dhuvo sassato avipariṇāmadhammo’’ti (saṃ. ni. 3.152) vitthāro. Taṃ netaṃ mamātiādīsu taṃ pañcakkhandhappabhedaṃ ārammaṇametaṃ mayhaṃ na hoti, ahampi eso na asmi, eso me attāpi na hotīti evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya passatoti evaṃ tāva padattho veditabbo. 82. Then, in order to show the way to abandon those views, the Blessed One said, "These which..." etc.

There, "where these views arise" etc. was said referring to the five aggregates (pañcakkhandha). For in these, these views arise. As he said, "When there is form (rūpa), monks, by clinging to form, this view arises: 'This is the self, this is the world, after death I will be permanent, stable, eternal, not subject to change'" (Saṃyutta Nikāya 3.152) in detail.

However, having made it singular in terms of object (ārammaṇa), he said "and where," meaning, in which object they arise. And here, it should be understood that "arise, lie latent, manifest" have different meanings in this way. For, in terms of genus (jāti), those that are not yet arisen, but are arising, are called "arise (uppajjanti)." Those that are repeatedly cultivated, have become strong, unabandoned are called "lie latent (anusenti)." Those that have reached the body and speech doors are called "manifest (samudācaranti)," this is the difference between them.

In "This is not mine" etc., that which is the object (ārammaṇa), the variety of the five aggregates, this is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self either, thus one should understand the meaning of the words first, seeing this as it actually is with proper wisdom (sammappaññā).

But since here "This is mine" is grasping of craving (taṇhā), and while grasping that, one grasps the manifold proliferation of craving (taṇhāpapañca), with its eight-hundred variations. "I am this" is grasping of conceit (māna), and while grasping that, one grasps the ninefold proliferation of conceit (mānapapañca). "This is my self" is grasping of views (diṭṭhi), and while grasping that, one grasps the proliferation of views (diṭṭhipapañca) with its sixty-two varieties. Therefore, the Blessed One, saying "This is not mine," rejects the proliferation of craving (taṇhāpapañca) in its aforementioned variety. "I am not this" [rejects] the proliferation of conceit (mānapapañca). "This is not my self" [rejects] the proliferation of views (diṭṭhipapañca). Here, craving (taṇhā) and conceit (māna) should be understood as having the same basis as views (diṭṭhi).

"Thus" (evam) means, in this way, with "This is not mine" etc., this five aggregates (khandhapañcaka). "As it actually is" (yathābhūta) means as its own nature (sabhāva), as it is. For the five aggregates exist in just this way. But even when grasped as "mine" etc., the meaning is that it does not exist in that way. "Seeing with proper wisdom" (sammappaññāya passato) means for one who sees well with the wisdom of insight (vipassanāpaññā), ending in the wisdom of the path of stream-entry (sotāpattimagga). "In this way" (evametāsaṃ) means by this means, of these. "Abandoning (pahānaṃ), relinquishing (paṭinissaggo)" both of these are terms for abandoning by cutting off (samucchedappahāna).

This is not mine. I am not this. This is not my self. Thus. As it actually is. Seeing with proper wisdom. In this way. Abandoning. Relinquishing.

Thus, the Blessed One, having shown the abandonment of views (diṭṭhippahāna) by the path of stream-entry (sotāpattimagga), having been asked a question by venerable Mahācunda in terms of those with excessive conceit (adhimānikānaṃ), [asking] whether the abandonment of views occurs only with initial attention (ādimanasikārena), now dividing the jhāna of those with excessive conceit himself, says, "This is a case of..." etc.

There, "those with excessive conceit" (adhimānikā) are those in whom excessive conceit arises with the perception of having attained what is not yet attained. Now, this arising does not arise in foolish ordinary people (bālaputhujjanānaṃ) who follow the ways of the world (lokavaṭṭānusārīnaṃ), nor in noble disciples (ariyasāvakānaṃ). For excessive conceit does not arise in a stream-enterer (sotāpanna) [thinking] "I am a once-returner (sakadāgāmī)," nor in a once-returner [thinking] "I am a non-returner (anāgāmī)," nor in a non-returner [thinking] "I am an arahant." 83. Thus, because for a monk who is overly conceited, dwelling in jhāna is not conducive to insight (vipaśyanā), dwelling in effacement (sallekha) is not [possible]. For he, having attained jhāna, does not reflect on conditioned things (saṅkhāra) after arising from that. But for him there is a dwelling of happiness (sukhavihāra) in the visible dharma, making the mind one-pointed. Therefore, showing that meaning, and distinguishing the rūpa jhānas and arūpa jhānas, and now, where effacement should be practiced with forty-four aspects, and showing that thing and that effacement, [he] says, "Here, Cunda, effacement should be practiced by you," and so on.

Why is it said that "effacements (sallekha) are non-harming (avihiṃsā) and so on, with eight attainments (samāpatti)?" Because [they] are conducive to the supramundane (lokuttara). For those outside [the Buddhist teaching], the eight attainments are merely conducive to continued existence (vaṭṭa). In the teaching (sāsana), going for refuge (saraṇagamana) is also conducive to the supramundane, what to say of non-harming and so on. And by this very sutta it should be understood that "giving a gift (dinnadāna) by one who has attained the eight attainments and has the five superknowledges (pañcābhiñña), even for one gone for three refuges (tisaraṇagata) in the teaching (sāsana), is more greatly fruitful (mahapphalataraṃ)." For, referring to this, in the Dakkhiṇāvisuddhi Sutta, "having given a gift to one free from lust (vītarāga) for sense pleasures (kāmesu), a hundred thousand times a crore (koṭisatasahassaguṇā) may be expected. Having given a gift to one who has undertaken the realization (sacchikiriyāya) of the fruit (phala) of stream-entry (sotāpatti), immeasurable and incalculable merit (dakkhiṇā) may be expected, what to say of a stream-enterer (sotāpanne)," is said (Majjhima Nikāya 3.379). From going for refuge onwards, one who has undertaken the realization of the fruit of stream-entry is intended there, this is the explanation of the pāḷi here.

In the subsequent commentary, "Here" indicates the object (vatthu) of non-harming and so on. "Kho panā" is merely a particle (nipātamattaṃ). "Vo" is a possessive expression in the sense of instrumentality; this is, however, an abbreviated meaning: "We speak of the object of non-harming and so on by the method of 'others will be harmful (vihiṃsakā),' and so on." Here, Cunda, effacement should be practiced by you.

Here kho panā vo Having thus spoken in brief, and now elaborating, [he] says, "Others will be harmful (vihiṃsakā bhavissanti), we shall be non-harmful (avihiṃsakā bhavissāmāti) here': effacement (sallekho) should be practiced thus," and so on.

There, "others" are whoever is not engaged in this effacement (sallekha). "Will be harmful" means will be injurers (vihesakā) of beings (sattānaṃ) by hand or stick or sword, and so on. "We shall be non-harmful here" means we, however, in whatever object others will be thus harmful, just there we shall be non-harmful, having produced non-harming, we shall dwell. "Thus, effacement should be practiced" means thus effacement should be done by you.

And "effacement" here should be understood as just non-harming. For non-harming effaces harming, it cuts it off, therefore it is called "effacement." This method [applies] everywhere. This, however, is the distinction. "Others [are of] wrong view": here, having mixed wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi) which is the extreme of the paths of action (kammapathānaṃ), and wrongness (micchattānaṃ), which is the beginning of wrong view, view is said thus it should be understood. In this way, all the words should be connected.

Yathā cettha, evaṃ sabbapadesu. Ativitthārabhayena pana anupadayojanā na katāti.
87. Thus, having shown the attainability of the superior state of that sallekha, now, in order to show the capability of complete nirvāṇa, [he] says, "," and so forth.

There, "so" indicates the designation of a person of the aforementioned kind.

Having brought forth this statement of purpose, "yo," whoever is himself mired in the mire, "so vata, Cunda, will lift up another who is mired in the mire," thus, the connection should be understood in all the wordings.

"Palipapalipanno" means sunk in deep mud, it is said, but not in the discipline of the noble ones (ārya).

But in the discipline of the noble ones, "palipa" means the five sensual qualities, it is said.

"Palipanno" means a fool, an ordinary person, sunk there; therefore, thus the interpretation of the meaning here should be understood.

Just as, Cunda, a certain man sunk in deep mud up to the tip of his nose would lift up another sunk in that same mud by grasping his hand or head, that is not possible, there is no such possibility. For how could he lift him up and establish him on dry land? Just so, whoever is himself mired in the mire of the five sensual qualities, that one will lift up another who is likewise sunk in the mire, this is not possible.

There, it might be objected, "This is inappropriate. Even ordinary people, monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, having heard the teaching of the Dharma, are indeed able to comprehend the Dharma. Therefore, [it is said that] one mired in the mire lifts up [another]," but that is not how it should be viewed.

For the Blessed One (bhagavān) himself lifts [them] up there; however, the teachers of the Dharma only obtain mere praise, like a letter-reader sent by the king.

Just as, indeed, a letter sent by the king to a border district, the people there, not knowing how to read the letter, have it read by someone who knows how to read it, and having heard its meaning, they respectfully carry out the "āṇā" (command) of the king, but for them there is no "ayaṃ āṇā" (this is the command) of the letter-reader.

The letter-reader, however, obtains only mere praise, "vissaṭṭhāya vācāya vācesi anelagaḷāyā," (he caused [it] to be read with a clear voice, without stammering). Just so, although Sāriputta and other teachers of the Dharma teach the Dharma, they are like letter-readers.

But that teaching of the Dharma is like the command of the king.

And those who, having heard that, comprehend the Dharma, it should be understood that the Blessed One (bhagavān) himself lifts [them] up.

The teachers of the Dharma, however, obtain only mere praise, "vissaṭṭhāya vācāya dhammaṃ desenti anelagaḷāyā," (they teach the Dharma with a clear voice, without stammering).

Therefore, it is indeed appropriate.

The bright side should be understood in the opposite way.

Adanto avinīto aparinibbuto (untamed, undisciplined, unreleased). Here, adanto (untamed) [is said] because of not being tamed.

Avinīto (undisciplined) because of lack of training in discipline.

Aparinibbuto (unreleased) should be understood as because of not extinguishing the defilements (kleśa).

Such a one will tame another, will make [him] calm, will discipline, will train [him in] the three trainings (śikṣā), or will completely release [him], will extinguish his defilements—this is not possible.

The bright side should be understood in the opposite way.

Adanto avinīto aparinibbuto adanto avinīto aparinibbuto (untamed, undisciplined, unreleased; untamed, undisciplined, unreleased).

"Evameva kho, Cunda, vihiṃsakassa…pe… parinibbānāyā" (Just so, Cunda, for the violent…pe… for final nirvāṇa). Here, however, this meaning should be understood: Just as one who is himself un-mired will lift up another who is mired, one who is tamed will tame, one who is disciplined will discipline, one who is released will completely release—this is possible.

What, then, is the reason? It is the state of being un-mired, the state of being tamed, the state of being disciplined, and the state of being released. Just so, Cunda, for the violent person, there is non-violence for final nirvāṇa.

What is said? For the one who is himself non-violent, that non-violence, this that is violence towards another for the violent person, is for final nirvāṇa. For one who is himself non-violent will extinguish the intention of violence towards another—this is possible.

What, then, is the reason? It is simply the state of being non-violent.

For whatever one has attained by oneself, that one is able to encourage another for that purpose.

"Evameva kho, Cunda, vihiṃsakassa…pe… parinibbānāyā" (Just so, Cunda, for the violent…pe… for final nirvāṇa).

Or, just as one who is himself un-mired, tamed, disciplined, released will lift up another who is mired, untamed, undisciplined, unreleased; will tame, will discipline, will completely release—this is possible. Just so, for the violent person, cultivating the path for the abandonment of violence, the non-violence that arises is for final nirvāṇa.

For, like one who is completely released, the intention of non-violence is able to completely release the intention of violence.

Showing this meaning, [he] says, ""Evameva kho, Cunda,"" and so forth. Thus, the meaning here should be understood.

As here, so in all the wordings.

However, for fear of excessive elaboration, the analysis has not been done step by step.

88. ‘‘Iti kho, cunda, desito mayā sallekhapariyāyo, desito cittuppādapariyāyo, desito parikkamanapariyāyo, desito uparibhāgapariyāyo, desito parinibbānapariyāyo. Yaṃ kho, cunda, satthārā karaṇīyaṃ sāvakānaṃ hitesinā anukampakena anukampaṃ upādāya, kataṃ vo taṃ mayā. ‘Etāni, cunda, rukkhamūlāni, etāni suññāgārāni, jhāyatha, cunda, mā pamādattha, mā pacchāvippaṭisārino ahuvattha’ – ayaṃ kho amhākaṃ anusāsanī’’ti. 88. “Thus, Cunda, the discourse on effacement (sallekhapariyāya) has been taught by me, the discourse on the arising of the mind (cittuppādapariyāya) has been taught by me, the discourse on avoidance (parikkamanapariyāya) has been taught by me, the discourse on the higher portion (uparibhāgapariyāya) has been taught by me, and the discourse on complete extinguishing (parinibbānapariyāya) has been taught by me.

Whatever should be done by a teacher, seeking the welfare of his disciples, out of compassion, that I have done for you, Cunda.

‘These are the roots of trees, these are empty huts. Meditate, Cunda, do not delay, or you will regret it later.’ This is our instruction to you.”

Thus spoke the Blessed One.

The venerable Mahācunda was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

Forty-four verses were spoken, and five joining verses were taught;

The sūtra named "Effacement" is profound, like the ocean.

Esa nayo sabbattha ettha avihiṃsādayo eva vihiṃsādīnaṃ sallekhanato sallekhakāraṇaṃ . Tesaṃ vasena cittassa uppādetabbato cittupādakāraṇaṃ, vihiṃsādi, parikkamanassa hetuto parikkamanakāraṇaṃ, uparibhāganipphādanato uparibhāgakāraṇaṃ , vihiṃsādīnaṃ parinibbāpanato parinibbānakāraṇanti veditabbā. Hitesināti hitaṃ esantena. Anukampakenāti anukampamānena. Anukampaṃ upādāyāti anukampaṃ cittena pariggahetvā, pariccātipi vuttaṃ hoti. Kataṃ vo taṃ mayāti taṃ mayā ime pañca pariyāye dassentena tumhākaṃ kataṃ. Ettakameva hi anukampakassa satthu kiccaṃ, yadidaṃ aviparītadhammadesanā. Ito paraṃ pana paṭipatti nāma sāvakānaṃ kiccaṃ. Tenāha etāni, cunda, rukkhamūlāni…pe… amhākaṃ anusāsanīti. Tattha ca rukkhamūlānīti iminā rukkhamūlasenāsanaṃ dasseti. Suññāgārānīti iminā janavivittaṭṭhānaṃ. Mā pamādatthāti mā pamajjittha. Mā pacchā vippaṭisārino ahuvatthāti ye hi pubbe daharakāle, ārogyakāle, sattasappāyādisampattikāle, satthu sammukhībhāvakāle ca yonisomanasikāravirahitā rattindivaṃ maṅgulabhattā hutvā seyyasukhaṃ middhasukhamanubhontā pamajjanti, te pacchā jarākāle, rogakāle, maraṇakāle, vipattikāle, satthu parinibbutakāle ca taṃ pubbe pamādavihāraṃ anussarantā, sappaṭisandhikālakiriyañca bhāriyaṃ sampassamānā vippaṭisārino honti, tumhe pana tādisā mā ahuvatthāti etamatthaṃ dassento āha ‘‘mā pacchā vippaṭisārino ahuvatthā’’ti. Ayaṃ vo amhākaṃ anusāsanīti ayaṃ amhākaṃ santikā ‘‘jhāyatha mā pamādatthā’’ti tumhākaṃ anusāsanī, ovādoti vuttaṃ hoti. rukkhamūlānī Suññāgārānī Jhāyathā Mā pamādatthā Mā pacchā vippaṭisārino ahuvatthā Ayaṃ vo amhākaṃ anusāsanī Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Sallekhasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
88. Thus, having shown the capacity for his complete emancipation, now, in order to apply that teaching to the practice of Dharma, he said, "Thus, Cunda," and so on.

There, sallekhapariyāyo means the cause of refinement (sallekhakāraṇaṃ).

This is the method everywhere here: non-injury and so forth are indeed the cause of refinement because of the refinement of injury and so forth.

Because of the origination of the mind depending on them, it is the cause of the arising of the mind (cittupādakāraṇaṃ), [namely] injury and so forth; because of the reason for exertion, it is the cause of exertion (parikkamanakāraṇaṃ); because of the production of an upper portion, it is the cause of an upper portion (uparibhāgakāraṇaṃ), because of the complete emancipation of injury and so forth, it should be understood as the cause of complete emancipation (parinibbānakāraṇaṃ).

hitesinā means seeking benefit (hitaṃ esantena).

anukampakenā means being compassionate (anukampamānena).

anukampaṃ upādāyā means having taken up compassion with the mind (anukampaṃ cittena pariggahetvā), it is said to be also giving up (pariccātipi vuttaṃ hoti).

kataṃ vo taṃ mayā means that which was done by me, done by me showing these five methods (taṃ mayā ime pañca pariyāye dassentena tumhākaṃ kataṃ).

Indeed, the task of the compassionate teacher is only this: the teaching of the Dharma that is not reversed (aviparītadhammadesanā).

But from here on, practice (paṭipatti) is the task of the disciples (sāvakānaṃ kiccaṃ).

Therefore, he said, "These, Cunda, are the roots of trees...pe...our instruction (amhākaṃ anusāsanī)."

And there, rukkhamūlānī means he shows a dwelling at the root of a tree (rukkhamūlasenāsanaṃ dasseti).

suññāgārānī means a place secluded from people (janavivittaṭṭhānaṃ).

And by both, he indicates a dwelling suitable for yoga (yogānurūpasenāsanamācikkhati), he bestows an inheritance (dāyajjaṃ niyyāteti).

jhāyathā means meditate (ārammaṇūpanijjhānena aṭṭhatiṃsārammaṇāni), and by contemplating the characteristics (lakkhaṇūpanijjhānena), contemplate impermanence and so forth, the aggregates, the sense bases and so forth (aniccādito khandhāyatanādīni upanijjhāyatha), it is said that one develops serenity and insight (samathañca vipassanañca vaḍḍhethāti vuttaṃ hoti).

mā pamādatthā means do not be negligent (mā pamajjittha).

mā pacchā vippaṭisārino ahuvatthā means those who were previously negligent in their youth (ye hi pubbe daharakāle), in health (ārogyakāle), at the time of possessing suitable circumstances and so forth (sattasappāyādisampattikāle), at the time of facing the Teacher (satthu sammukhībhāvakāle ca), being without proper attention (yonisomanasikāravirahitā), having become gluttons day and night (rattindivaṃ maṅgulabhattā hutvā), enjoying the pleasure of lying down and the pleasure of sleep (seyyasukhaṃ middhasukhamanubhontā pamajjanti), they later, in old age (jarākāle), in sickness (rogakāle), at the time of death (maraṇakāle), at the time of misfortune (vipattikāle), and at the time of the Teacher's complete nirvāṇa (satthu parinibbutakāle ca) recalling that former conduct of negligence (taṃ pubbe pamādavihāraṃ anussarantā), and seeing the heavy burden of rebirth-linking activity (sappaṭisandhikālakiriyañca bhāriyaṃ sampassamānā), they are remorseful (vippaṭisārino honti), but do not be like that (tumhe pana tādisā mā ahuvatthāti), showing this meaning, he said, "Do not be remorseful later (mā pacchā vippaṭisārino ahuvatthā)."

ayaṃ vo amhākaṃ anusāsanī means this is our instruction to you from us (ayaṃ amhākaṃ santikā), "Meditate, do not be negligent (jhāyatha mā pamādatthā)," this is your instruction, it is said to be advice (ovādoti vuttaṃ hoti).

The roots of trees, empty dwellings, meditate, do not be negligent, do not be remorseful later, this is our instruction to you.
