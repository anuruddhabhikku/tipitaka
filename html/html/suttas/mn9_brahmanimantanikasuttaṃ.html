501. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Ekamidāhaṃ, bhikkhave, samayaṃ ukkaṭṭhāyaṃ viharāmi subhagavane sālarājamūle. Tena kho pana, bhikkhave, samayena bakassa brahmuno evarūpaṃ pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ uppannaṃ hoti – ‘idaṃ niccaṃ, idaṃ dhuvaṃ, idaṃ sassataṃ, idaṃ kevalaṃ, idaṃ acavanadhammaṃ, idañhi na jāyati na jīyati na mīyati na cavati na upapajjati, ito ca panaññaṃ uttari nissaraṇaṃ natthī’ti. Atha khvāhaṃ, bhikkhave, bakassa brahmuno cetasā cetoparivitakkamaññāya – seyyathāpi nāma balavā puriso samiñjitaṃ vā bāhaṃ pasāreyya, pasāritaṃ vā bāhaṃ samiñjeyya, evameva – ukkaṭṭhāyaṃ subhagavane sālarājamūle antarahito tasmiṃ brahmaloke pāturahosiṃ. Addasā kho maṃ, bhikkhave, bako brahmā dūratova āgacchantaṃ; disvāna maṃ etadavoca – ‘ehi kho, mārisa, svāgataṃ, mārisa! Cirassaṃ kho, mārisa, imaṃ pariyāyamakāsi yadidaṃ idhāgamanāya. Idañhi, mārisa, niccaṃ, idaṃ dhuvaṃ, idaṃ sassataṃ, idaṃ kevalaṃ, idaṃ acavanadhammaṃ, idañhi na jāyati na jīyati na mīyati na cavati na upapajjati. Ito ca panaññaṃ uttari nissaraṇaṃ natthī’’’ti. Evaṃ vutte, ahaṃ, bhikkhave, bakaṃ brahmānaṃ etadavocaṃ – ‘‘avijjāgato vata, bho, bako brahmā; avijjāgato vata, bho, bako brahmā; yatra hi nāma aniccaṃyeva samānaṃ niccanti vakkhati, addhuvaṃyeva samānaṃ dhuvanti vakkhati, asassataṃyeva samānaṃ sassatanti vakkhati, akevalaṃyeva samānaṃ kevalanti vakkhati, cavanadhammaṃyeva samānaṃ acavanadhammanti vakkhati; yattha ca pana jāyati jīyati mīyati cavati upapajjati tañca vakkhati – ‘idañhi na jāyati na jīyati na mīyati na cavati na upapajjatī’ti; santañca panaññaṃ uttari nissaraṇaṃ ‘natthaññaṃ uttari nissaraṇa’nti vakkhatī’’ti.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Sāvatthi.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: “Monks.”

“Venerable sir,” they replied.

The Blessed One said this:

“Once, monks, I was staying at Ukkaṭṭha in the Subhaga forest, at the root of the royal sal tree.

Now on that occasion an evil viewpoint had arisen to Baka Brahma: ‘This is constant (niccaṃ). This is permanent (dhuvaṃ). This is eternal (sassataṃ). This is total (kevalaṃ). This is not subject to falling away (acavanadhammaṃ)—for this does not take birth (na jāyati), does not age (na jīyati), does not die (na mīyati), does not fall away (na cavati), does not reappear (na upapajjati). And there is no other, higher escape (nissaraṇaṃ).’

“So I—having known with my awareness the train of thought in Baka Brahma’s awareness—as a strong man would extend his flexed arm or flex his extended arm, vanished into the root of the royal sala tree in the Subhaga forest in Ukkaṭṭha and appeared in that Brahma world (brahmaloke).

Baka Brahma saw me coming in the distance and, on seeing me, said, ‘Come, good sir (mārisa). You are welcome (svāgataṃ), good sir! It has been long, good sir, since you took the trouble to come here.

For here, good sir, is constant (niccaṃ). This is permanent (dhuvaṃ). This is eternal (sassataṃ). This is total (kevalaṃ). This is not subject to falling away (acavanadhammaṃ)—for here one does not take birth (na jāyati), does not age (na jīyati), does not die (na mīyati), does not fall away (na cavati), does not reappear (na upapajjati). And there is no other, higher escape (nissaraṇaṃ).’

“When this was said, I told Baka Brahma, ‘How immersed in ignorance (avijjāgato) is Baka Brahma! How immersed in ignorance (avijjāgato) is Baka Brahma!—in that what is actually inconstant (aniccaṃ) he calls “constant” (niccanti). What is actually impermanent (addhuvaṃ) he calls “permanent” (dhuvanti). What is actually non-eternal (asassataṃ) he calls “eternal” (sassatanti). What is actually partial (akevalaṃ) he calls “total” (kevalanti). What is actually subject to falling away (cavanadhammaṃ) he calls “not subject to falling away” (acavanadhammanti). Where one takes birth (jāyati), ages (jīyati), dies (mīyati), falls away (cavati), and reappears (upapajjati), he says, “For here one does not take birth (na jāyati), does not age (na jīyati), does not die (na mīyati), does not fall away (na cavati), does not reappear (na upapajjati).” Though there is another, higher escape (nissaraṇaṃ), he says, “There is no other, higher escape (natthaññaṃ uttari nissaraṇa).”’”

“Then, monks, Māra the Evil One, taking possession of an attendant of the Brahma assembly, said to me, ‘Monk! Monk! Don’t attack him! Don’t attack him! For this Brahmā, monk, is the Great Brahmā, the Conqueror, the Unconquered, the All-Seeing, All-Powerful, the Sovereign Lord, the Maker, Creator, Chief, Appointer and Ruler, Father of All That Have Been and Shall Be.

There were, monk, before your time, śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas in the world who found fault with earth and were disgusted with earth, who found fault with liquid and were disgusted with liquid, who found fault with fire and were disgusted with fire, who found fault with wind and were disgusted with wind, who found fault with beings and were disgusted with beings, who found fault with devas and were disgusted with devas, who found fault with Pajāpati and were disgusted with Pajāpati, who found fault with Brahmā and were disgusted with Brahmā—they, with the break-up of the body, after death, were established in an inferior body.

So I tell you, monk, “Please, good sir (mārisa), do only as Brahmā says. Don’t defy the word of Brahmā.”

If you defy the word of Brahmā, then—as a man, when the goddess of fortune approaches, chases her away with a stick, or as a man, falling into hell, loses hold of the earth with his hands and feet—that will be what you have accomplished.

Please, good sir (mārisa), do only as Brahmā says. Don’t defy the word of Brahmā.

“Surely, monk, you see the Brahma assembly gathered together?” In this way, monks, Māra the Evil One led me to the Brahma assembly.

When this was said, I told Māra the Evil One, ‘I know you, Evil One (pāpima). Don’t assume, “He doesn’t know me.”’

You are Māra, Evil One (pāpima).

And Brahmā, and Brahma’s assembly, and the attendants of Brahma’s assembly have all fallen into your hands. They have all fallen into your power.

And you think, “This one, too, has come into my hands, has come under my control.”

But, Evil One (pāpima), I have neither come into your hands nor have I come under your control.’

502. Then, monks, Māra the Evil One: How did Māra see the Blessed One? It seems that he, sitting in his own palace, would periodically turn his attention to the Teacher, thinking, “Today, in which village or town does the ascetic Gotama reside?”

However, while turning his attention at this time, having realized that "[he] dwells in Subhaga Grove, relying on Ukkaṭṭha," and looking to see "where has he gone?" he saw him going to the Brahma world. [Māra thought:] "The ascetic Gotama is going to the Brahma world. Before he can preach the Dharma there and lead the Brahma host beyond my domain, I will go and disrupt his Dharma teaching." Following closely behind the Teacher, he stood within the Brahma host with an invisible body.

Realizing that "the Teacher has displeased Baka Brahma," he stood as a support for Brahma.

Therefore it was said: "Then, monks, Māra the Evil One."

"Seṭṭho sajitā": He is the highest and the conqueror of the world. He shows that he is the one who assigns beings, saying, "You be a Khattiya, you be a Brāhmaṇa, a Vessa, a Sudda, a householder, a renunciant, even be a camel or an ox."

"Vasī pitā bhūtabhabyāna": He is a master by habitual mastery; he says that he is the father of beings that have come to be and will come to be.

There, beings born from eggs, wombs, and moisture are called "will come to be" while still inside the eggshell and inside the womb, but from the time they emerge, they are "have come to be".

Beings born spontaneously are "will come to be" at the first moment of consciousness, but from the second moment on, they "have come to be".

It should be understood that beings born through spontaneous appearance are "will come to be" at the first arising of movement, but from the second arising on, they "have come to be."

Because all of these are called his children, he says, "father of beings that have come to be and will come to be."

Highest and conqueror, master, father of beings that have come to be and will come to be, will come to be, have come to be.

What is this?" Reflecting and seeing the Nāga-king, [and thinking,] "While I am watching, may these beings not perish," he entered the meditation that is the basis of super-knowledge (abhijñāpādakajjhānaṃ samāpajjitvā), gave up his own body, created the color of a supaṇṇa, and showed it to the Nāga-king.

The Nāga-king, frightened, contracted his hood, and entered the water.

The great multitude attained a state of well-being (sotthibhāvaṃ pāpuṇi).

The Teacher, showing this as his former deed, [said]:

"On the Ganges river current, you released the seized boat,

[Seized] by the cruel Nāga, [endangering] humans;

You freed it by force, overpowering [him].

That ancient practice of virtuous conduct of yours,

Awakened from sleep, I recall."

(Jā. 1.7.73)

He spoke this verse.

At another time, he, having gone forth into the ascetic life, became an ascetic named Keśava.

At that time, our Bodhisattva, a young man named Kappa, became the constant attendant (baddhacaro) and pupil (antevāsiko) of Keśava, a doer of what the teacher requested (kiṃkārapaṭissāvī), a pleasant practitioner (manāpacārī), endowed with intelligence (buddhisampanno), and a practitioner of the goal (atthacaro).

Keśava was unable to live without him; he made his living dependent on him.

The Teacher, showing this as his former deed, [said]:

"Keśava, Kappa was your constant attendant,

You did not consider [anyone] as having equal intelligence.

That ancient practice of virtuous conduct of yours,

Awakened from sleep, I recall."
(jā. 1.7.74) –
This verse was spoken.

Thus, the Teacher (satthā) revealed the accomplished deed (katakammaṃ) in the various states (nānattabhāvesu) of Brahmā (brahmuno). As the Teacher (satthari) was speaking, Brahmā (brahmā) considered (sallakkhesi): "Just as when a thousand lamps (dīpasahasse) are lit, forms (rūpāni) become clear (pākaṭāni), so too are all his deeds (sabbakammānissa)."

So, with a pleased mind (pasannacitto), he spoke this verse:

"Indeed (addhā), you know (pajānāsi) my (mama) life (āyuṃ),
And you also know (jānāsi) another (aññampi), for (hi) you are the awakened one (buddho);
Thus (tathā hi), this (tāyaṃ) radiant power (jalitānubhāvo)
Illuminating (obhāsayaṃ) remains (tiṭṭhati) in the Brahma-world (brahmaloka)."

(jā. 1.7.75);
Then, the Blessed One (bhagavā), revealing further the unequaled equality (asamasamataṃ), spoke, beginning with: "O Brahmā, I am the earth (pathaviṃ kho ahaṃ brahme)."

There (tattha), "unanubūta by the earth through earthness (pathaviyā pathavattena ananubhūta)" means (nti): unreached (appattaṃ), unexperienced (ananubhūtaṃ) by the earth through the state of being earth (pathavisabhāvena).

What then (kiṃ pana) is that (tanti)? Nirvāṇa (nibbānaṃ).
Indeed (hi), because it (taṃ) is free (nissaṭattā) from all conditioned things (sabbasmā saṅkhatā), it is called (nāma) unreached (appattaṃ) through the state of being earth (pathavisabhāvena).

"Through its highest knowledge (tadabhiññāyā)" means (ti): having known (jānitvā) and realized (sacchikatvā) that Nirvāṇa (taṃ nibbānaṃ).

"He did not abandon the earth (pathaviṃ nāpahosi)" means (nti): he did not grasp (na gaṇhiṃ) the earth (pathaviṃ) with craving (taṇhā), views (diṭṭhi), and the conceit of self (mānagāhehi).

"Even for water, etc. (āpādīsupi)" the method (nayo) is the same (eseva).
But (pana) the detailed exposition (vitthāro) should be understood (veditabbo) by the method stated (vuttanayeneva) in the Root Discourse (mūlapariyāye).

O Brahmā, I am the earth (pathaviṃ kho ahaṃ brahme) unanubūta by the earth through earthness (pathaviyā pathavattena ananubhūta) Through its highest knowledge (tadabhiññāyā) He did not abandon the earth (pathaviṃ nāpahosi) Even for water, etc. (āpādīsupi)
"If indeed (sace kho), Mārisa, everything through allness (sabbassa sabbattenā)" means (ti): Brahmā (brahmā) himself (attano), pointing out (niddisitvā) the syllable (akkharaṃ) "all (sabba)" as his (vāditāya) doctrine (idameva), and finding fault (dosam gaṇhanto) with the syllable (akkhare), spoke (āha).

But (pana) the Teacher (satthā), referring (sandhāya) to self-identity (sakkāyaṃ), speaks (vadati) "all (sabba)," while (brahmā) Brahmā (sandhāya) refers to all of everything (sabbasabbaṃ).

You (tvaṃ) say (vadasi), "all (sabba)," you say (vadasi), "unanubūta by everything through allness (sabbassa sabbattena ananubhūta)," if (yadi) all (sabbaṃ) is unexperienced (ananubhūtaṃ), [then] nothing (natthi), otherwise (atha), it has (assa) unexperienced (ananubhūtaṃ) existence (atthi).

"May it not be (mā heva te) merely empty (rittakameva)"it was (ahosi)merely vain (tucchakameva) it was (ahosī)" means (ti): he refutes (niggaṇhāti) the Teacher (satthāraṃ) with false speech (musāvādena), [saying] "may your (tuyhaṃ) speech (vacanaṃ) not be (mā hotu) empty (rittakaṃ), may it not be (mā hotūti) vain (tucchakaṃ)."

If indeed (sace kho) to you (te), Mārisa, everything through allness (sabbassa sabbattenā) May it not be (mā heva te) merely empty (rittakameva) it was (ahosi) merely vain (tucchakameva) it was (ahosī)
But (pana) the Teacher (satthā), [being] superior (vādītaro) to this (etasmā) Brahmā (brahmunā) by a hundredfold (sataguṇena), a thousandfold (sahassaguṇena), a hundred thousandfold (satasahassaguṇena), therefore (tasmā), declaring (āharanto) a reason (kāraṇaṃ) for the subduing (vādamaddanatthaṃ) of his (tassa) doctrine (vādaṃ), spoke (āha), beginning with "consciousness (viññāṇa)."

There (tattha), "consciousness (viññāṇa)" means (nti) should be understood (vijānitabbaṃ).

"invisible (anidassana)" means (nti): because of the non-access (anupagamanato) of the range (āpāthaṃ) of eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇassa), it is called (nāma) invisible (anidassanaṃ), even (api) by the two terms (padadvayena) Nirvāṇa (nibbānameva) is stated (vuttaṃ).

"endless (ananta)" means (nti): because it (tayidaṃ) is without (rahitattā) arising (uppāda), passing away (vaya), and the intermediate state (antara), it is called (nāma) endless (anantaṃ).
This (hetaṃ) was also spoken (vuttampi) –

Consciousness (viññāṇa) Consciousness (viññāṇa) Invisible (anidassana) Endless (ananta)

‘‘Beings (bhūtāni) are finite (antavantāni), the unbecome (asambhūtaṃ) is endless (anantakaṃ);
In beings (bhūte) ends (antāni) are seen (dissantī), in beings (bhūte) ends (antā) are declared (pakāsitā).’’

All-illuminating (sabbatopabha)" means (nti): endowed with light (pabhāsampannaṃ) in every way (sabbaso).

Indeed (hi), there is no other (añño) dharma (dhammo) more radiant (sapabhataro vā), more shining (jotivantataro vā), more purified (parisuddhataro vā), or more brilliant (paṇḍarataro vā) than (nibbānato) Nirvāṇa (natthi).

Or (vā) everywhere (sabbato) it is (eva) thus (tathā) shining (pabhūtaṃ), there is no (na) place where it is not (katthaci natthīti), [therefore it is] all-illuminating (sabbatopabhaṃ).

Indeed (hi), it should not be said (na vattabbaṃ) that (iti) there is no (natthi) Nirvāṇa (nibbānaṃ) by name (nāma) in such and such a direction (asukadisāya) among (ādīsu) the eastern directions (puratthimadisā).

Or (atha vā) "light (pabha)" is (nti) the name (nāmaṃ) of a ford (titthassa), the all-shining (sabbato pabhamassāti) [is] all-illuminating (sabbatopabhaṃ).

It is said (kira) that (yathā) just as (nibbānassa) for Nirvāṇa (mahāsamuddassa) for the great ocean (yato yato), from wherever (hotī), they wish to descend (otaritukāmā), that (taṃ) itself (tadeva) is a ford (titthaṃ), there is no (natthi) place that is not a ford (atitthaṃ nāma).

Even so (evamevaṃ), in the thirty-eight (aṭṭhatiṃsāya) meditation subjects (kammaṭṭhānesu), by whatever (yena yena) means (mukhena) they wish to descend (otaritukāmā honti) to Nirvāṇa (nibbānaṃ), that (taṃ) itself (tadeva) is a ford (titthaṃ).
There is no meditation subject called nibbāna that is unequalled. Therefore, it is called "all-illuminating." That nibbāna is not experienced by earth through its earth-nature, nor by water and the others through their respective natures. Thus, by virtue of its all-illuminating nature, that consciousness, which is the object of your kind and the aggregate of all ten-world-dharmas, establishes the doctrine that it is invisible, endless, all-pervading, and unexperienced.

Then Brahmā, being dismissed by the Teacher after having been repeatedly pressed but seeing nothing that should be taken, desiring to make a jest, said, "Come now, venerable sir, I will disappear." There, "I will disappear" means "I will perform the miracle of invisibility."

"If you are able" means, "If you are able to disappear from me, perform the miracle of disappearing." "He is not able to disappear from me" means that he is simply not able to disappear from me. What did he desire to do? He desired to return to the original state of rebirth. For the Brahmās, indeed, the state of having the original rebirth is subtle, unapproachable to others, and they abide merely with a body that is compounded.

The Teacher did not allow him to return to the original rebirth. Even without going to the original rebirth, by whatever darkness he could make himself unseeable by disappearing, the Teacher dispelled that darkness. Therefore, he was not able to disappear. Being unable, he hid in a celestial mansion, he hid in a wish-fulfilling tree, he sat squatting on his heels.

The Brahma-host made sport, "This Brahmā, indeed, is hiding in a celestial mansion, he is hiding in a wish-fulfilling tree, he is sitting squatting on his heels, O Brahmā, I have disappeared from you!" Thus they produced a perception, so it is said. Humiliated by the Brahma-host, he felt disgraced.

"Thus spoke I, monks" means, "O monks, when thus addressed by that Brahmā, 'Come now, venerable sir, I will disappear,' when he was thus addressed, seeing that he was unable to disappear, I spoke thus."

"I spoke this verse" means, "Why did the Blessed One speak a verse?" In order that there should not be an opportunity for the Brahma-host to say, "How can the existence or non-existence of the ascetic Gotama in this place be known?" the Teacher, being invisible, spoke a verse.

"Thus spoke I, monks," "I spoke this verse." There, "Having seen fear in existence" means "I alone have seen fear in existence." "And the seeker of becoming" means "Having seen this becoming, and also the seven kinds of existence, beginning with desire-existence, in its various forms, as a seeker of becoming, searching and seeking for prosperity again and again in existence..." "I did not address becoming" means "I did not address any becoming by way of craving and views; I did not seek it," is the meaning. "And I did not take up delight" means "I did not approach craving for existence; I did not grasp it," is the meaning.

Thus, the Teacher, proclaiming the four noble truths, taught the Dharma. At the end of the teaching, having established them in the chamber of insight in accordance with the teaching, ten measures of thousands of Brahmās drank the nectar of the path and its fruit.

"Having seen fear in existence," "And the seeker of becoming," "I did not address becoming," "And I did not take up delight." "Having minds of wonder and amazement" means that they were those who had wonder, those who had amazement, and those who had joy. "He uprooted becoming together with its root" means that at the bodhimaṇḍa, by that teaching of his, he uprooted, eradicated, and tore up becoming, together with its root, from many other gods and humans as well, is the meaning.

"Having minds of wonder and amazement," "He uprooted becoming together with its root."

505. ‘‘Atha kho, bhikkhave, māro pāpimā aññataraṃ brahmapārisajjaṃ anvāvisitvā maṃ etadavoca – ‘sace kho tvaṃ, mārisa, evaṃ pajānāsi, sace tvaṃ evaṃ anubuddho, mā sāvake upanesi, mā pabbajite; mā sāvakānaṃ dhammaṃ desesi, mā pabbajitānaṃ; mā sāvakesu gedhimakāsi, mā pabbajitesu. Ahesuṃ kho, bhikkhu, tayā pubbe samaṇabrāhmaṇā lokasmiṃ arahanto sammāsambuddhā paṭijānamānā . Te sāvake upanesuṃ pabbajite, sāvakānaṃ dhammaṃ desesuṃ pabbajitānaṃ, sāvakesu gedhimakaṃsu pabbajitesu, te sāvake upanetvā pabbajite, sāvakānaṃ dhammaṃ desetvā pabbajitānaṃ, sāvakesu gedhitacittā pabbajitesu, kāyassa bhedā pāṇupacchedā hīne kāye patiṭṭhitā. Ahesuṃ ye pana, bhikkhu, tayā pubbe samaṇabrāhmaṇā lokasmiṃ arahanto sammāsambuddhā paṭijānamānā. Te na sāvake upanesuṃ na pabbajite, na sāvakānaṃ dhammaṃ desesuṃ na pabbajitānaṃ, na sāvakesu gedhimakaṃsu na pabbajitesu, te na sāvake upanetvā na pabbajite, na sāvakānaṃ dhammaṃ desetvā na pabbajitānaṃ, na sāvakesu gedhitacittā na pabbajitesu, kāyassa bhedā pāṇupacchedā paṇīte kāye patiṭṭhitā. Taṃ tāhaṃ, bhikkhu, evaṃ vadāmi – iṅgha tvaṃ, mārisa, appossukko diṭṭhadhammasukhavihāramanuyutto viharassu, anakkhātaṃ kusalañhi, mārisa, mā paraṃ ovadāhī’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ vutte, ahaṃ, bhikkhave, māraṃ pāpimantaṃ etadavocaṃ – ‘jānāmi kho tāhaṃ, pāpima, mā tvaṃ maññittho – na maṃ jānātī’ti. Māro tvamasi, pāpima. Na maṃ tvaṃ, pāpima, hitānukampī evaṃ vadesi; ahitānukampī maṃ tvaṃ, pāpima, evaṃ vadesi. Tuyhañhi, pāpima, evaṃ hoti – ‘yesaṃ samaṇo gotamo dhammaṃ desessati, te me visayaṃ upātivattissantī’ti. Asammāsambuddhāva pana te , pāpima, samānā sammāsambuddhāmhāti paṭijāniṃsu. Ahaṃ kho pana, pāpima, sammāsambuddhova samāno sammāsambuddhomhīti paṭijānāmi. Desentopi hi, pāpima, tathāgato sāvakānaṃ dhammaṃ tādisova adesentopi hi, pāpima, tathāgato sāvakānaṃ dhammaṃ tādisova. Upanentopi hi, pāpima, tathāgato sāvake tādisova, anupanentopi hi, pāpima, tathāgato sāvake tādisova. Taṃ kissa hetu? Tathāgatassa, pāpima, ye āsavā saṃkilesikā ponobbhavikā sadarā dukkhavipākā āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇiyā – te pahīnā ucchinnamūlā tālāvatthukatā anabhāvaṃkatā āyatiṃ anuppādadhammā. Seyyathāpi, pāpima, tālo matthakacchinno abhabbo puna virūḷhiyā; evameva kho, pāpima, tathāgatassa ye āsavā saṃkilesikā ponobbhavikā sadarā dukkhavipākā āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇiyā – te pahīnā ucchinnamūlā tālāvatthukatā anabhāvaṃkatā āyatiṃ anuppādadhammāti. ‘‘Iti hidaṃ mārassa ca anālapanatāya brahmuno ca abhinimantanatāya, tasmā imassa veyyākaraṇassa brahmanimantanikaṃteva adhivacana’’nti. Then, monks, Māra the Evil One, taking possession of a certain attendant of the Brahma assembly, said this to me: ‘If, good sir, you understand thus, if you are awakened thus, do not lead disciples, do not lead those gone forth; do not teach the Dhamma to disciples, do not teach it to those gone forth; do not create desire among disciples, do not create it among those gone forth.’

There were, monk, before your time, ascetics and Brahmins in the world claiming to be worthy and rightly self-awakened.

They led disciples and those gone forth; they taught the Dhamma to disciples and those gone forth; they created desire among disciples and those gone forth; having led disciples and those gone forth, having taught the Dhamma to disciples and those gone forth, with minds full of desire among disciples and those gone forth, on the breakup of the body, after death, they were established in a lower body.

There were those, monk, before your time, ascetics and Brahmins in the world claiming to be worthy and rightly self-awakened.

They did not lead disciples or those gone forth; they did not teach the Dhamma to disciples or those gone forth; they did not create desire among disciples or those gone forth; not having led disciples or those gone forth, not having taught the Dhamma to disciples or those gone forth, with minds free of desire among disciples and those gone forth, on the breakup of the body, after death, they were established in a refined body.

So, monk, I say this to you: Come now, good sir, be without concern, dwell devoted to a pleasant abiding in the here-and-now; for skillfulness is undeclared, good sir, do not instruct others.’

When this was said, I said this to Māra the Evil One: ‘I know you, Evil One; do not think, “He does not know me.”

You are Māra, Evil One.

You do not speak to me thus out of sympathy for welfare; you speak to me thus out of sympathy for what is not welfare.

For you think thus, Evil One: “Those to whom Gotama the ascetic will teach the Dhamma will transgress my domain.”

Those ascetics and Brahmins, Evil One, who were not rightly self-awakened claimed to be rightly self-awakened.

But I, Evil One, being rightly self-awakened, claim to be rightly self-awakened ( sammāsambuddho’va samāno sammāsambuddho’mhīti paṭijānāmi ).

Even when teaching the Dhamma to his disciples, Evil One, the Tathāgata is Such; even when not teaching the Dhamma to his disciples, Evil One, the Tathāgata is Such.

Even when leading his disciples, Evil One, the Tathāgata is Such; even when not leading his disciples, Evil One, the Tathāgata is Such.

Why is that? Because, Evil One, the corruptions of the Tathāgata—defiling, leading to renewed existence, distressing, resulting in suffering, tending to future birth, aging, and death—are abandoned, their root cut off, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and subject to no future arising.

Just as a palm tree with its crown severed is incapable of growing again, so too, Evil One, the corruptions of the Tathāgata—defiling, leading to renewed existence, distressing, resulting in suffering, tending to future birth, aging, and death—are abandoned, their root cut off, made like a palm stump, obliterated, and subject to no future arising.’

Thus, because of the silencing of Māra and because of the invitation of Brahmā, therefore this exposition is entitled “The Brahmā Invitation.”

Then, at that time, Māra the Evil One, being overwhelmed with anger, [thought:] “While I was wandering around, the ascetic Gotama, by telling a Dhamma talk, caused about ten thousand Brahma-worlds to go beyond my control.” Overwhelmed by anger, he possessed the body of a certain Brahma-attendant in order to show that.

"Then, monks..." etc., he said, beginning thus.

There, "If you have awakened in this way..." means, "If you yourself have awakened to the four truths in this way."

"Do not lead disciples" means, "Do not lead either lay disciples or ordained disciples to that Dhamma."

"Established in a base body" means, "Established in the four woeful states."

"Established in a refined body" means, "Established in the Brahma-world."

With what in mind does he speak this? With the renunciants who have gone forth into external renunciations, ascetics and wanderers.

For, before the arising of a Buddha, clansmen, having gone forth into the ascetic life, without consulting anyone about anything, becoming solitary wanderers, having produced attainments, were reborn in the Brahma-world. With them in mind, he spoke thus.

"Indeed, unproclaimed is skill, sir" means, "This is better: skill that is unproclaimed to others, not advising, not speaking a Dhamma talk."

"Do not advise others" means, "Wandering from time to time to the human world, from time to time to the deva-world, from time to time to the Brahma-world, from time to time to the nāga-world, do not wander. Sit in one place and pass the time with the bliss of jhāna, path and fruition."

"Because of non-addressing" means, "Because of non-utterance."

"And because of Brahma's invitation" means, "And because of the invitation-speech of Baka Brahma, together with his embodied Brahma-state, by the means of the line, 'Indeed, sir, this is permanent,' etc."

"Therefore" means, "For that reason."

"Of this exposition, 'Brahma-invitation' is indeed the designation, the reckoning, the convention, the term."

The rest is clear throughout.
