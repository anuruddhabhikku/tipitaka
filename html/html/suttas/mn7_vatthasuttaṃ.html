Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's Park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: "Monks."

"Venerable sir," those monks replied.

The Blessed One said this:

"Bhikkhus, suppose a cloth were soiled and stained; whatever color a dyer might dip it in—whether blue, or yellow, or red, or madder dye (mañjiṭṭhakāya)—it would look badly dyed and impure in color. Why is that? Because of the impurity of the cloth, bhikkhus. So too, bhikkhus, when the mind is defiled (citte saṃkiliṭṭhe), an unhappy destination may be expected (duggati pāṭikaṅkhā).

Bhikkhus, suppose a cloth were pure and bright; whatever color a dyer might dip it in—whether blue, or yellow, or red, or madder dye—it would look well-dyed and pure in color. Why is that? Because of the purity of the cloth, bhikkhus. So too, bhikkhus, when the mind is undefiled (citte asaṃkiliṭṭhe), a happy destination may be expected (sugati pāṭikaṅkhā)."

77. "So mettāsahagatena cetasā" means that the teaching has come down by way of a natural sequence of meaning.

For suttas have three kinds of sequence of meaning: a sequence of meaning based on a question; a sequence of meaning dictated by inclination; and a sequence of meaning dictated by the natural structure of the teaching.

Herein, the pucchānusandhi should be understood as in the case of suttas answering questions, such as "When this was said, a certain monk said this to the Blessed One: ‘Venerable sir, could there be anguish apart from what is external?’ ‘There could be, monk,’ the Blessed One said" (Majjhima Nikāya 1.242).

The ajjhāsayānusandhi should be understood as in the case of suttas spoken after understanding the inclination of others, such as "'Now, brahmin, it might be that you think: "Perhaps the recluse Gotama is not free from lust..."'" (Majjhima Nikāya 1.55).

The yathānusandhi should be understood in the case of suttas in which the teaching arises at the beginning due to a certain principle, and in which the subsequent teaching comes in accordance with that principle, or by way of its opposite. For example, in the Ākaṅkheyya Sutta, the teaching is set up at the beginning by way of virtue (śīla) and culminates in the six kinds of direct knowledge (abhiññā). In the Simile of the Saw (kakacūpama), the teaching is set up by way of impatience (akkhanti) and culminates in the simile of the saw. In the Alagadda Sutta, the teaching is set up by way of the exposition of views (diṭṭhiparidīpana) and culminates in the declaration of emptiness in three cycles (tiparivaṭṭasuññatāpakāsanā). In the Cūḷaassapura Sutta, the teaching is set up at the beginning by way of the exposition of defilements (kilesaparidīpana) and culminates in the divine abodes (brahmavihārā). In the Kosambiya Sutta, the teaching is set up by way of schism (bhaṇḍana) and culminates in the principles of fraternal harmony (sāraṇīyadhammā). In this Vattha Sutta too, the teaching is set up at the beginning by way of the exposition of defilements (kilesaparidīpana) and culminates in the divine abodes (brahmavihārā).

Thus it is said: “This teaching has come down by way of a sequence of meaning dictated by the natural structure of the teaching.”

Moreover, in the Visuddhimagga, a full description of the absence of danger and the method of meditation in the divine abodes is given in every way.

The meaning of that is: that non-returner (anāgāmī), having thus cultivated the brahmavihāra dwelling, having arisen from whichever of these brahmavihāras it may be, distinguishes by way of name those very brahmavihāra qualities (brahmavihāradhamme) – relying on them, the heart-basis (hadayavatthu), the material phenomena which are the support of the basis – in this way, and also having discriminated the material phenomena (bhūtupādāyadhamme) by way of form (rūpavasena), knows "atthi ida," and by just that much, the establishment of the truth of suffering (dukkhasacca) has been done.

Then, investigating the means of abandoning that, [he] knows "atthi paṇīta," and by just that much, the establishment of the truth of the path (maggasacca) has been done.

80. “Evaṃ vutte,” this is manifestly stated due to the mentioning of such fearsome terrors.

“Eko vūpakaṭṭho” in these [phrases], “eko” [means] by physical seclusion.

"Vūpakaṭṭho" [means] by mental seclusion.

"Appamatto" [means] through non-abandonment of mindfulness in meditation subject.

"Ātāpī" [means] through the heat of zeal consisting of bodily and mental effort.

"Pahitatto" [means] through non-dependence on the body and on life.

"Viharanto" [means] through dwelling in some mode of conduct.

"Nacirassevā" is said taking ordination as the starting point.

"Kulaputtā" means two kinds of kulaputtā: jātikulaputtā and ācārakulaputtā; but this one is a kulaputto in both ways.

"Agārasmā" [means] from the house.

"Agāriyaṃ," [is] benefit to the house, is said to be activity such as agriculture, cattle-rearing, and supporting the family. "Natthi ettha agāriyanti anagāriyaṃ," where there is no benefit to the house, that is anagāriyaṃ, this is a designation for ordination.

"Pabbajantī" means they approach, they come near.

"Tadanuttara" means that unsurpassed.

"Brahmacariyapariyosāna" means the culmination of the path of the holy life, it is said to be the fruit of arhatship.

For that benefit, young men of good families become monks.

"Diṭṭheva dhamme" means in that very existence.

"Sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā" means having directly realized through one's own wisdom, having done [it] without reliance on another, this is the meaning.

"Upasampajja vihāsī" means having attained, having accomplished, he dwelt, thus dwelling, and "destroyed birth…pe… fully comprehended."

By this, he shows the ground of his reflection.

What then is his exhausted birth? And how did he fully comprehend it? It is said: Granted, this was also said in [the section on] fearsome terrors, nevertheless, in order to show the method of the connection in terms of the first person, we will again speak concisely.

It is not a past birth that is destroyed, for that was already destroyed previously.

It is not a future one, for there is no effort in regard to the future.

And it is not the present one, because that presently exists.

But because of the uncultivated nature of the path, that birth that would arise in the one, four, five-aggregate existences, the class of one, four, five-aggregate differentiations, that, because of the cultivated nature of the path, is exhausted by entering the state of non-arising. He knows, knowing that having reflected on those abandoned afflictions through that path-cultivation, even existing action in the absence of afflictions, becomes without future rebirth.

"Brahmacariya" is the holy life of the path.

"Kataṃ karaṇīya" is having completed the task in sixteen ways through the paths of full knowledge, abandonment, direct realization, and cultivation in the four truths, this is the meaning.

"Nāparaṃ itthattāyā" means now, there is no further cultivation of the path for this existence, for this condition of sixteen things to be done, or for the destruction of defilements.

Or else, "itthattāyā" means from this present aggregate continuum of this present condition, there is no further aggregate continuum.

However, these five aggregates are known, they stand, like a tree with its roots cut off, he fully comprehended.
