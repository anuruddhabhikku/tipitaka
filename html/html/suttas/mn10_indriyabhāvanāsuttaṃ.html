Atha kho uttaro māṇavo pārāsiviyantevāsī pārāsariyantevāsī (sī. pī.), pārāsiriyantevāsī (syā. kaṃ.) yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho uttaraṃ māṇavaṃ pārāsiviyantevāsiṃ bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘deseti, uttara, pārāsiviyo brāhmaṇo sāvakānaṃ indriyabhāvana’’nti? ‘‘Deseti, bho gotama, pārāsiviyo brāhmaṇo sāvakānaṃ indriyabhāvana’’nti. ‘‘Yathā kathaṃ pana, uttara, deseti pārāsiviyo brāhmaṇo sāvakānaṃ indriyabhāvana’’nti? ‘‘Idha, bho gotama, cakkhunā rūpaṃ na passati, sotena saddaṃ na suṇāti – evaṃ kho, bho gotama, deseti pārāsiviyo brāhmaṇo sāvakānaṃ indriyabhāvana’’nti. ‘‘Evaṃ sante kho, uttara, andho bhāvitindriyo bhavissati, badhiro bhāvitindriyo bhavissati; yathā pārāsiviyassa brāhmaṇassa vacanaṃ. Andho hi, uttara, cakkhunā rūpaṃ na passati, badhiro sotena saddaṃ na suṇātī’’ti. Evaṃ vutte, uttaro māṇavo pārāsiviyantevāsī tuṇhībhūto maṅkubhūto pattakkhandho adhomukho pajjhāyanto appaṭibhāno nisīdi. Atha kho bhagavā uttaraṃ māṇavaṃ pārāsiviyantevāsiṃ tuṇhībhūtaṃ maṅkubhūtaṃ pattakkhandhaṃ adhomukhaṃ pajjhāyantaṃ appaṭibhānaṃ viditvā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘aññathā kho, ānanda, deseti pārāsiviyo brāhmaṇo sāvakānaṃ indriyabhāvanaṃ, aññathā ca panānanda, ariyassa vinaye anuttarā indriyabhāvanā hotī’’ti. ‘‘Etassa, bhagavā, kālo; etassa, sugata, kālo yaṃ bhagavā ariyassa vinaye anuttaraṃ indriyabhāvanaṃ deseyya. Bhagavato sutvā bhikkhū dhāressantī’’ti. ‘‘Tenahānanda, suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato paccassosi. Bhagavā etadavoca –
Thus have I heard:

On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the territory of the Kajangala people, at Suveluvana.

Then Uttara, a young man and a student of the Pārāsariya, went to the Blessed One; having gone, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One.

Having concluded the conversation of courteous greetings, he sat down to one side. The Blessed One then said to Uttara, who was sitting to one side: “Uttara, does the Brahmin Pārāsariya teach his disciples the development of the faculties ( indriyabhāvana)?”

“Yes, Master Gotama, the Brahmin Pārāsariya teaches his disciples the development of the faculties.”

“But how, Uttara, does the Brahmin Pārāsariya teach his disciples the development of the faculties?”

“Here, Master Gotama, one does not see forms with the eye, one does not hear sounds with the ear – that is how, Master Gotama, the Brahmin Pārāsariya teaches his disciples the development of the faculties.”

“If that is so, Uttara, then a blind man will have developed faculties, a deaf man will have developed faculties; according to the word of the Brahmin Pārāsariya. For a blind man, Uttara, does not see forms with the eye, and a deaf man does not hear sounds with the ear.”

When this was said, Uttara, the young man and student of the Pārāsariya, sat silent, dismayed, with drooping shoulders, head down, pondering, and without a reply.

Then, knowing that Uttara, the young man and student of the Pārāsariya, was silent, dismayed, with drooping shoulders, head down, pondering, and without a reply, the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda, saying: “Ānanda, the Brahmin Pārāsariya teaches the development of the faculties in one way, but in the Discipline of the Noble One, the unsurpassed development of the faculties (anuttarā indriyabhāvana) is another.”

“Venerable sir, this is the time! O Well-gone One, this is the time for the Blessed One to teach the unsurpassed development of the faculties in the Noble One's Discipline. Having heard it from the Blessed One, the monks will remember it.”

“Then listen, Ānanda, and pay close attention, I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Ānanda replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

453. Evaṃ me suta iti the indriyabhāvanāsuttaṃ.

Tattha gajaṅgalāya iti in the settlement named Evaṃ.

Suveḷuvane iti suveḷu by name is a tree species. Covered by these is the great forest grove. There [he] dwells.

Cakkhunā rūpaṃ na passati, sotena saddaṃ na suṇātī iti through the eye, form is not to be seen; through the ear, sound is not to be heard; thus, with this intention, he speaks.

Aññathā ariyassa vinaye iti, by this the Blessed One (bhagavān) made a dwelling place for the purpose of speaking of the unequalled sense-faculty development in his own teaching.

Then venerable Ānanda thought: "The teacher shows a dwelling place, now what sense-faculty development talk shall I do for this assembly, the monastic community?" Thus, beseeching the teacher, he spoke, beginning with etassa bhagavā.

Then, as the Blessed One (bhagavān) was speaking of sense-faculty development, he spoke, beginning with tena hānandā.

Aññathā ariyassa vinaye etassa bhagavā tena hānandā.

454. ‘‘Kathañcānanda, ariyassa vinaye anuttarā indriyabhāvanā hoti? Idhānanda, bhikkhuno cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā uppajjati manāpaṃ, uppajjati amanāpaṃ, uppajjati manāpāmanāpaṃ. So evaṃ pajānāti – ‘uppannaṃ kho me idaṃ manāpaṃ, uppannaṃ amanāpaṃ, uppannaṃ manāpāmanāpaṃ . Tañca kho saṅkhataṃ oḷārikaṃ paṭiccasamuppannaṃ. Etaṃ santaṃ etaṃ paṇītaṃ yadidaṃ – upekkhā’ti. Tassa taṃ uppannaṃ manāpaṃ uppannaṃ amanāpaṃ uppannaṃ manāpāmanāpaṃ nirujjhati; upekkhā saṇṭhāti. Seyyathāpi, ānanda, cakkhumā puriso ummīletvā vā nimīleyya, nimīletvā vā ummīleyya; evameva kho, ānanda, yassa kassaci evaṃsīghaṃ evaṃtuvaṭaṃ evaṃappakasirena uppannaṃ manāpaṃ uppannaṃ amanāpaṃ uppannaṃ manāpāmanāpaṃ nirujjhati, upekkhā saṇṭhāti – ayaṃ vuccatānanda, ariyassa vinaye anuttarā indriyabhāvanā cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu.
454. And how, Ānanda, is there the unsurpassed development of the faculties (indriyabhāvanā) in the Noble One's Discipline (ariyassa vinaye)? Here, Ānanda, to a bhikkhu, on seeing a form (rūpaṃ) with the eye (cakkhunā), there arises what is agreeable (manāpaṃ), there arises what is disagreeable (amanāpaṃ), there arises what is both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ).

He understands thus: ‘There has arisen in me what is agreeable (manāpaṃ), there has arisen what is disagreeable (amanāpaṃ), there has arisen what is both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ).

But that is conditioned (saṅkhataṃ), gross (oḷārikaṃ), dependently arisen (paṭiccasamuppannaṃ).

This is peaceful (santaṃ), this is sublime (paṇītaṃ), that is, equanimity (upekkhā).’

The agreeable (manāpaṃ) that arose, the disagreeable (amanāpaṃ) that arose, and the both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ) that arose cease in him and equanimity (upekkhā) is established (saṇṭhāti).

Just as a man with good sight, Ānanda, having opened his eyes (ummīletvā) might shut them (nimīleyya) or having shut his eyes (nimīletvā) might open them (ummīleyya); so too concerning anything at all, Ānanda, the agreeable (manāpaṃ) that arose, the disagreeable (amanāpaṃ) that arose, and the both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ) that arose cease just as quickly, just as rapidly, just as easily, and equanimity (upekkhā) is established (saṇṭhāti). This is called in the Noble One’s Discipline (ariyassa vinaye) the unsurpassed development of the faculties (indriyabhāvanā) regarding forms (rūpesu) cognizable by the eye (cakkhuviññeyyesu).
454. Tatha yadidaṃ upekkhāti yā esā vipassanupekkhā nāma, esā santā esā paṇītā, atappikāti attho. Iti ayaṃ bhikkhu cakkhudvāre rūpārammaṇampi iṭṭhe ārammaṇe manāpaṃ, aniṭṭhe amanāpaṃ, majjhatte manāpāmanāpañca cittaṃ, tassa rajjituṃ vā dussituṃ vā muyhituṃ vā adatvāva pariggahetvā vipassanaṃ majjhatte ṭhapeti. Cakkhumāti sampannacakkhuvisuddhanetto. Cakkhābādhikassa hi uddhaṃ ummīlananimmīlanaṃ na hoti, tasmā so na gahito.
454. Furthermore, that which is equanimity (upekkhā), that which is called insight-equanimity (vipassanūpekkhā), that is serene, that is excellent, that is without torment—this is the meaning.

Thus, this bhikkhu, at the eye-door (cakkhudvāre), with regard to forms (rūpārammaṇa), whether an agreeable object in an agreeable sense-sphere, or a disagreeable object in a disagreeable sense-sphere, or a middling object that is both agreeable and disagreeable, without giving his mind (citta) over to attachment, aversion, or confusion, but rather taking hold of insight (vipassanā) in the middle way (majjhatte), he establishes it.

"One with vision" (cakkhumā) means one whose eye is perfected, whose vision is purified.

For one afflicted with an eye disease (cakkhābādhikassa), there is no upward opening and closing of the eyes (ummīlananimmīlanaṃ); therefore, he is not included.

Just as if a strong man were to let two or three drops of water fall onto an iron plate heated for a whole day,

One dwells perceiving the repulsive in the unrepulsive by suffusing it with the perception of unattractiveness (asubhapharaṇena) or by analyzing it as impermanent (aniccato upasaṃhārena).

The same method applies to the remaining phrases (sesapadesupi eseva nayo).

Having completely abandoned both (tadubhayaṃ abhinivajjetvāti), desiring to dwell in equanimity (majjhatto hutvā viharitukāmo), what does one do? When desirable and undesirable things come into the range of perception (iṭṭhāniṭṭhesu āpāthagatesu), one is neither glad (somanassiko) nor sad (domanassiko).

It was said:

"How does one dwell perceiving the unrepulsive in the repulsive (kathaṃ paṭikūle appaṭikūlasaññī viharati)? In an undesirable object (aniṭṭhasmiṃ vatthusmiṃ), one suffuses it with loving-kindness (mettāya vā pharati) or analyzes it into its elements (dhātuto vā upasaṃharati); thus, one dwells perceiving the unrepulsive in the repulsive.

How does one dwell perceiving the repulsive in the unrepulsive (kathaṃ appaṭikūle paṭikūlasaññī viharati)? In a desirable object (iṭṭhasmiṃ vatthusmiṃ), one suffuses it with the perception of unattractiveness (asubhāya vā pharati) or analyzes it as impermanent (aniccato vā upasaṃharati); thus, one dwells perceiving the repulsive in the unrepulsive.

How does one dwell perceiving the unrepulsive in both the repulsive and the unrepulsive (kathaṃ paṭikūle ca appaṭikūle ca appaṭikūlasaññī viharati)? In an undesirable and a desirable object (aniṭṭhasmiñca iṭṭhasmiñca vatthusmiṃ), one suffuses it with loving-kindness (mettāya vā pharati) or analyzes it into its elements (dhātuto vā upasaṃharati). Thus, one dwells perceiving the unrepulsive in both the repulsive and the unrepulsive.

How does one dwell perceiving the repulsive in both the unrepulsive and the repulsive (kathaṃ appaṭikūle ca paṭikūle ca paṭikūlasaññī viharati)? In a desirable and an undesirable object (iṭṭhasmiñca aniṭṭhasmiñca vatthusmiṃ), one suffuses it with the perception of unattractiveness (asubhāya vā pharati) or analyzes it as impermanent (aniccato vā upasaṃharati); thus, one dwells perceiving the repulsive in both the unrepulsive and the repulsive.

How does one, having completely abandoned both the repulsive and the unrepulsive (kathaṃ paṭikūle ca appaṭikūle ca tadubhayaṃ abhinivajjetvā), dwell in equanimity (upekkhako viharati) mindful (sato) and fully aware (sampajāno)? Here, a bhikkhu, seeing a form with the eye (cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā), is neither glad (neva sumano hoti) nor sad (na dummano); he dwells in equanimity mindful and fully aware…pe… knowing a dhamma with the mind (manasā dhammaṃ viññāya) , he is neither glad nor sad; he dwells in equanimity mindful and fully aware.

Thus, having completely abandoned both the repulsive and the unrepulsive, one dwells in equanimity mindful and fully aware."

The venerable Ānanda ( āyasmā ānando ), being pleased, rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words (bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti).

The Indriyabhāvanāsuttaṃ, the tenth, is finished.

The Saḷāyatanavagga, the fifth, is finished.

Its summary (tassuddānaṃ) –

Anāthapiṇḍika, Channa, Puṇṇa, Nandaka, and Rāhula;

Six sixes, Saḷāyatanika, Nagaravindeyya, and Suddhika;

Indriyabhāvanā also, the fifth discourse ( vaggo ovādapañcamoti ).

This is the chapter summary (vaggānamuddānaṃ) –

Devadaha, Anupada, Suññata, and Vibhaṅgaka;

Saḷāyatana the chapter, situated in the upper fifty ( uparipaṇṇāsake ṭhitāti ).

The upper fifty (uparipaṇṇāsakaṃ) is complete.

The middle collection (majjhimanikāyo) is complete, adorned with three fifties ( tīhi paṇṇāsakehi paṭimaṇḍito sakalo).
