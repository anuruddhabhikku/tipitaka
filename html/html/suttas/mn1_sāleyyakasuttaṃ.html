439. Thus I have heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the Kosala country with a large Saṅgha of monks, and he arrived at a town of Kosala called Sāla, a Brahmin village.

The brahmin householders of Sāla heard: “The recluse Gotama, the son of the Sakyans, who went forth from a Sakyan clan, has been wandering in the Kosalan country with a large Sangha of bhikkhus and has come to Sāla.

And concerning this Blessed Lord Gotama, a good report has been spread about: ‘Indeed, the Blessed One is an Arhat, a Fully Enlightened One (sammāsambuddho), endowed with knowledge and good conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), a Well-Gone One (sugato), knower of the world (lokavidū), unsurpassed trainer of persons to be tamed (anuttaro purisadammasārathi), teacher of gods and humans (satthā devamanussānaṃ), the Awakened One (buddho), the Blessed One (bhagavā)’.

He, having realized it by his own super-knowledge (abhiññā), proclaims this world with its gods (sadevakaṃ), with its Māras (samārakaṃ), and with its Brahmās (sabrahmakaṃ), this generation with its ascetics and Brahmins (sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ), its princes and people (sadevamanussaṃ).

He teaches the Dhamma (dhammaṃ) which is good in the beginning (ādikalyāṇaṃ), good in the middle (majjhekalyāṇaṃ), and good in the end (pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ), with the right meaning (sātthaṃ) and phrasing (sabyañjanaṃ); he reveals a life of purity (brahmacariyaṃ) that is utterly perfect (kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ) and purified (parisuddhaṃ)’.

It is good to see such arahants."

Then the Sāleyyakā Brahmin householders went to the Blessed One. On arrival, some bowed down to the Blessed One and sat to one side; some exchanged greetings with the Blessed One, and when this courteous and amiable talk was finished, sat down to one side; some extended their hands in reverential salutation towards the Blessed One and sat down to one side; some pronounced their name and clan in the Blessed One’s presence and sat down to one side; some kept silent and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, the Sāleyyakā Brahmin householders said to the Blessed One: “What is the cause and condition, Master Gotama, why some beings here, on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappear in states of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ)? Or what is the cause and condition, Master Gotama, why some beings here, on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappear in a good destination (sugatiṃ), even in the heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ)?”

“Householders, it is by reason of conduct not in accordance with the Dhamma (adhamma cariyā) and unrighteous conduct (visama cariyā) that some beings here, on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappear in states of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ).

Householders, it is by reason of conduct in accordance with the Dhamma (dhamma cariyā) and righteous conduct (sama cariyā) that some beings here, on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappear in a good destination (sugatiṃ), even in the heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ).”

“We do not understand the detailed meaning of Master Gotama’s utterance, which he has spoken in brief without expounding the detailed meaning.

It would be good if Master Gotama would teach us the Dhamma (dhammaṃ) so that we might understand the detailed meaning of his utterance."

“Then, householders, listen and attend closely to what I shall say.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” they replied.

439. Thus have I heard: the Sāleyyakasutta.

Therein, kosalesū means Kosala, namely, princes of a rural district. Their dwelling place, even a single district, is called Kosala by way of established convention. In that Kosala district, the elders said: "Because formerly, upon seeing Prince Mahāpanāda watching various dramas without even a slight smile, the king said, 'Whoever makes my son laugh, I will adorn him with all ornaments.'" Then, having cast aside even their plows, men gathered in the great assembly tried for seven years, displaying various games, but they were not able to make him laugh.

Then Sakka sent the divine dancer. He made him laugh by showing a divine drama. Then those men departed, each heading towards their own dwelling place. On the road, seeing friends and companions, they exchanged greetings, saying, "Is it well, friend? Is it well?" Therefore, taking up that saying, "Is it well? Is it well?", that region is called Kosala.

cārikaṃ caramāno means wandering on a journey without haste. mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhi means with a great community of monks, whether a hundred, a thousand, or a hundred thousand, thus with an immeasurable great community of monks. brāhmaṇagāmo means a village of Brahmins, a village where Brahmins assemble, or a village for the enjoyment of Brahmins. Here, a village of assembly is intended. tadavasarī means he approached that, the meaning is, he attained it.

Here, the monastery is unrestricted; therefore, not far from it, there will be an appropriate grove for the Buddhas; the teacher should be understood to have gone to that grove. assosu means they heard, they perceived. They knew according to the sound of the words arriving at the ear-door. kho is a particle used for emphasis or merely to fill out the line.

There, with emphasis, they only heard, and there was no obstruction to their hearing, this meaning should be understood. By filling out the line, there is only elegance of expression.

cārikaṃ caramāno, mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhi, brāhmaṇagāmo, tadavasarī, assosu, kho.

Now, in order to reveal what meaning they heard, samaṇo khalu, bho, gotamo etc. is said. There, samaṇo should be understood as one who has calmed down evil deeds. khalū is a particle indicating hearsay. bho is merely an address to one another. gotamo is a designation according to the family lineage of the Blessed One (Bhagavat). Therefore, samaṇo khalu, bho, gotamo here means, "It is said that the śramaṇa is of the Gotama lineage," this is how the meaning should be understood. sakyaputto means that this is a declaration of the Blessed One's high lineage. sakyakulā pabbajito means a declaration of having gone forth out of faith.

It is said that having abandoned that family without being overcome by any deprivation and while it was not yet diminished, he went forth out of faith. Here is the connection of words: "That Blessed One is such, since he is an Arhat, he is such, since he is a perfectly awakened one...pe...he is such, since he is a Blessed One."

It is said: By this reason and by this reason.

There, because of being far away, because of the killing of enemies and what should not be given, because of being worthy of offerings etc., because of the absence of secrecy in evil actions, by these reasons that Blessed One is worthy of offerings should be known. Having placed the outline in such a way, all these words have been expanded in the chapter on mindfulness of the Buddha in the Visuddhimagga, therefore their expansion should be taken from there.

Sādhu kho panā means beautiful indeed; it is said to bring benefit and happiness.

Tathārūpānaṃ arahata means the Arahants of such a form; as the Blessed Gotama is, the Arahants in the world who have obtained such fame, whose sight is difficult to obtain even in hundreds of thousands of millions of aeons, who are surrounded by a fathom's extent of light, who are adorned with eighty minor marks, jewel-like, who have bodies that are crowded with the best of the thirty-two marks of a great man, whose sight is pleasing, whose exceedingly sweet voice is like the sound of the Dharma, through the attainment of qualities that are in accordance with reality.

Dassanaṃ hotī means even just seeing is good, having opened eyes that are delightful and gentle.

If, with a voice complete with the eight qualities of Brahmā, we will be able to hear even one word of him teaching the Dharma, it will be even better, having made such an intention.

Yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsū means having abandoned all their duties, they came with joyful minds.

Etadavocu means indeed there are two kinds of questions, questions of householders and questions of those who have gone forth.

There, "What, venerable sir, is skillful, what is unskillful?" In this way, the question of householders has come.

"These, venerable sir, are the five aggregates of clinging." In this way, the question of those who have gone forth.

But these, asking a question of householders that was appropriate to themselves, said this: "What now, venerable Gotama, is the cause, what is the condition?" etc.

As the Blessed One was unable to make them understand, in that way, briefly answering the question, he said, "adhammacariyāvisamacariyāhetu kho gahapatayo" etc.

But why did the Blessed One answer in such a way that they were unable to understand? Because they were conceited know-it-alls; for if he established the outline from the beginning and then expanded the meaning in such a way that they could understand, they would despise the teaching, thinking, "The teaching is obvious, we ourselves, if we were speaking, would speak in just this way."

Therefore, as the Blessed One was unable to make them understand, in that way, he briefly answered the question.

Then, when those who were unable to understand requested an expanded teaching, he said in order to teach in detail, "tena hi gahapatayo" etc.

There, tena hī is a particle in the sense of cause.

Because you ask me, therefore it means.

Sīle patiṭṭhāya kasiṇaparikammaṃ katvā paṭhamajjhānaṃ nibbattetvā paṭhamajjhānabhūmiyaṃ nibbattati; dutiyādīni bhāvetvā dutiyajjhānabhūmiādīsu nibbattati; rūpāvacarajjhānaṃ pādakaṃ katvā vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhetvā anāgāmiphale patiṭṭhito pañcasu suddhāvāsesu nibbattati; rūpāvacarajjhānaṃ pādakaṃ katvā arūpāvacarasamāpattiṃ nibbattetvā catūsu arūpesu nibbattati; rūpārūpajjhānaṃ pādakaṃ katvā vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhetvā arahattaṃ pāpuṇāti. Asaññabhavo pana bāhirakānaṃ tāpasaparibbājakānaṃ āciṇṇoti idha na niddiṭṭho. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Sāleyyakasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
442. Sahabyataṃ upapajjeyya means sahabhāvaṃ upagaccheyyaṃ, "may I attain companionship".

Brahmakāyikānaṃ devāna means paṭhamajjhānabhūmidevānaṃ, "of the gods of the first jhāna realm."

Ābhānaṃ devāna means ābhā by name does not exist separately; this is a designation for parittābha, appamāṇābha, and ābhassara.

Parittābhāna etc. is a taking up of these very terms by way of distinction, not taking them up all together.

The same method applies to parittasubhāna, etc.

Thus, the Blessed One, having shown the destruction of the āsavas, concluded the teaching with the device of Arahantship.

Here, however, the devalokas are to be reckoned.

Firstly, according to the three jhāna realms, there are nine brahmalokas, five pure abodes (suddhāvāsā), and with the four immaterial realms (ārūpa), eighteen. With vehapphala, there are nineteen. Having included the realm of non-perception (asañña), there are twenty brahmalokas. Thus, with the six sensuous realms (kāmāvacara), there are twenty-six devalokas by name.

The Blessed One showed the arising of all of them through the ten wholesome courses of action (dasakusalakammapatha).

There, in the six sensuous realms, arising occurs only as a result of the ripening of the three kinds of good conduct (sucarita).

However, for the higher devalokas, these courses of action are said to be by way of decisive support (upanissaya).

The ten wholesome courses of action are indeed morality (sīla), and for one possessing morality, the preliminary practice with a kasiṇa succeeds.

Having established morality, and having performed the preliminary practice with a kasiṇa, having generated the first jhāna, one arises in the first jhāna realm. Having developed the second and subsequent jhānas, one arises in the second jhāna realm, etc. Having made a rūpāvacara jhāna a foundation, and having increased insight (vipassanā), one arises in the five pure abodes, established in the fruit of non-returning (anāgāmiphala). Having made a rūpāvacara jhāna a foundation, and having generated an arūpāvacara samāpatti, one arises in the four immaterial realms. Having made a rūpārūpa jhāna a foundation, and having increased insight, one attains Arahantship.

However, the realm of non-perception (asaññabhava) is habitual for external ascetics and wanderers (tāpasaparibbājaka), thus it is not indicated here.

The rest is clear in meaning throughout.

The Sāleyyakasutta Commentary in the Papañcasūdanī, commentary to the Majjhimanikāya, is concluded.

Upāsake no bhavaṃ gotamo dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupete pāṇupetaṃ (ka.) saraṇaṃ gate’’ti. Sāleyyakasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.
Thus having spoken, the Sāleyyaka Brāhmaṇa householders said this to the Blessed One: "Excellent, Venerable Gotama, excellent! Just as, Venerable Gotama, one might set upright what has been overturned, or reveal what has been hidden, or point out the way to one who has gone astray, or hold up an oil lamp in the darkness so that those with eyes might see forms. Just so has the Dhamma (dharma) been made clear in many ways by Venerable Gotama. We go to the Venerable Gotama for refuge (śaraṇaṃ gacchāma), and to the Dhamma (dharma), and to the Saṅgha (saṃgha) of monks. May the Venerable Gotama accept us as lay followers (upāsaka) who have taken refuge (saraṇaṃ gate) from this day forward, for life."

The Sāleyyakasuttaṃ, the first, is finished.
