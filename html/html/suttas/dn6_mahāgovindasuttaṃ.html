Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Rājagaha, on the Vulture Peak (Gijjhakūṭa) mountain.

Then, late in the night, Pañcasikha (Pañcasikho), son of a Gandharva, illuminating the entire Vulture Peak (Gijjhakūṭa) mountain with surpassing radiance, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and stood to one side.

Standing to one side, Pañcasikha, the Gandharva's son, said this to the Blessed One: "Venerable Sir (bhante), I will announce to the Blessed One what I have heard and directly received in the presence of the gods of the Thirty-three (tāvatiṃsānaṃ)."

"Tell me then, Pañcasikha," said the Blessed One.

293. Thus have I heard: the Mahāgovindasutta.

Here, there is a word-by-word explanation of terms that are not obvious: pañcasikho means one with five crests (pañcacūḷo), one with five earrings (pañcakuṇḍaliko). It is said that in the path of humans, while doing meritorious deeds, when he was young, as a boy with five crests, he was the eldest of the cowherds. Taking other boys, outside the village, at the crossroads, constructing halls, digging ponds, building bridges, leveling uneven roads, and removing trees that obstructed the axles of vehicles, he wandered about doing such meritorious deeds and passed away while still young. That existence of his was desired, lovely, and pleasing. After passing away, he was reborn in the heaven of the Four Great Kings, having taken a lifespan of ninety hundred thousand years. His form was three gāvutas in size, like a golden mountain peak. Adorning himself with ornaments worth a thousand cartloads, anointing himself with nine pots of perfume, wearing divine red clothes, wearing red golden earrings, he wanders about with an entourage like that of a five-crested boy, with five earrings circling around his back. Therefore, they recognize him as "Pañcasikha."

abhikkantāya rattiyā: abhikkantāya khīṇāya rattiyā, meaning, after one portion of the night has passed. abhikkantavaṇṇo: of exceedingly desired, lovely, pleasing color. By nature, he was of lovely color, but because he came adorned, he was of exceedingly lovely color. kevalakappaṃ: without remainder, completely. Here, the word kevala has the meaning of "without remainder." Like in kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ. The word kappa has the meaning of "completely," as in kevalakappaṃ jetavanaṃtiādīsu. obhāsetvā: having pervaded with radiance (ābhāya pharitvā), like the moon or the sun, having made it all one glow, all one illumination.

294. Sudhammāyaṃ sabhāya means in the Sudhammā assembly hall, which was created by the essence of the ratanamattakaṇṇika tree for a woman named Sudhammā.

Its ground is made of crystal, the nails are made of jewels, the pillars are made of gold, the pillar bases and roofs are made of silver, the balustrades are made of coral, the railings and side walls and doorways are made of seven precious substances, the roof is covered with indanīla bricks, the roof platform is made of gold, the spire is made of silver, and its length and breadth are three hundred yojanas, its circumference is nine hundred yojanas, and its height is five hundred yojanas. Such is the Sudhammā assembly hall.

Dhataraṭṭho etc., Dhṛtarāṣṭra is the Gandharva king, surrounded by a hundred thousand koṭis of Gandharva deities, having taken a hundred thousand koṭis of gold-made leaves and gold weapons, facing west in the eastern direction, with deities placed in front in the two divine worlds, sits down.

Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Virūḷhako is the Kumbhāṇḍa king, surrounded by a hundred thousand koṭis of Kumbhāṇḍa deities, having taken a hundred thousand koṭis of silver-made leaves and gold weapons, facing north in the southern direction, with deities placed in front in the two divine worlds, sits down.

Virūḷhako, Virūpākṣa is the Nāga king, surrounded by a hundred thousand koṭis of Nāgas, having taken a hundred thousand koṭis of jewel-made great leaves and gold weapons, facing east in the western direction, with deities placed in front in the two divine worlds, sits down.

Virūpākṣa, Vaiśravaṇa is the Yakṣa king, surrounded by a hundred thousand koṭis of Yakṣas, having taken a hundred thousand koṭis of coral-made great leaves and gold weapons, facing south in the northern direction, with deities placed in front in the two divine worlds, should be understood.

Vaiśravaṇa, "atha pacchā amhākaṃ āsanaṃ hotī" means after them, we get the opportunity to sit down. After that, we are unable to enter or see. The cause of assembly here has been previously stated as only fourfold. Among them, the rain-retreat collection has been expanded. Just as for the rain-retreat collection, so too for the great Pravāraṇā, having assembled on the full-moon day, they contemplate, "Where shall we go today, near whom shall we end the retreat?". There, Śakra, the lord of the gods, mostly ends the retreat in the Piyaṅgudīpa great monastery itself. The remaining deities, taking divine flowers and divine sandalwood powders etc. from the Pāricchattaka tree, go to their own agreeable places and end the retreat. Thus, they assemble for the sake of the Pravāraṇā collection.

“We ask you for a gift, please give us a gift!”
–
Here, agghanti means that which should be offered to a guest.
With this in mind, this seat was designated: “Sit here. This water is pure. Drink this water. Wash your feet. This commoner is oil mixed for the benefit of the feet, anoint your feet with this. This is honey-cake.”
A bodhisattva’s brahmacarya is not like the brahmacarya of others; he does not give rise to the thought, “This will be tomorrow, this will be the day after tomorrow.”
But honey-cakes are saltless, unfumigated, without buttermilk, and are vegetables cooked with water. Thinking about this, he said, “Eat this,” and “we ask you for a gift,” and so forth.
All these gifts belong to Brahmā.
They "ask you for a gift."

Iti kho, pañcasikha, sabbesaṃyeva imesaṃ kulaputtānaṃ amoghā pabbajjā pabbajā ahosi (ka.) avañjhā saphalā saudrayā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano pañcasikho gandhabbaputto bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā tatthevantaradhāyīti. Mahāgovindasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ.
330. And those of my disciples, Pañcasikha, who fully understand my teaching, realize for themselves, through direct knowledge, in this very life, the uncorrupted freedom of mind (cetovimutti) and freedom through wisdom (paññāvimutti), having eliminated the corruptions (āsavānaṃ khayā). And those who do not fully understand the teaching, through the destruction of the five lower fetters (pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā) will be reborn spontaneously (opapātikā) and will attain final emancipation (parinibbāyino) there, not returning from that world, their nature being not to return (anāvattidhammā tasmā lokā).

Those who do not fully understand the teaching, some, through the destruction of three fetters (tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā) and the attenuation of greed, hatred, and delusion (rāgadosamohānaṃ tanuttā), become once-returners (sakadāgāmino), and having come to this world only once (sakideva imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvā), will make an end of suffering (dukkhassantaṃ karissanti).

Those who do not fully understand the teaching, some, through the destruction of three fetters (tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā), become stream-winners (sotāpannā), their nature being not to fall into states of woe (avinipātadhammā), assured (niyatā) and destined for full enlightenment (sambodhiparāyaṇā).

Thus, Pañcasikha, for all these sons of good families (kulaputtānaṃ), the going forth (pabbajjā) is not in vain (amoghā), not fruitless (avañjhā), but fruitful (saphalā), and yielding happiness (saudrayā)’’.

This is what the Blessed One (bhagavā) said.

Pleased, Pañcasikha, the gandharva's son (gandhabbaputto), having rejoiced in the Blessed One's words (bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā), and having saluted the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā), circumambulated him to the right (padakkhiṇaṃ katvā) and vanished from there (tatthevantaradhāyīti).

The Mahāgovindasutta, the sixth, is concluded (mahāgovindasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ).
330.Ye na sabbenasabbanti ye cattāropi ariyamagge paripūretuṃ na jānanti, tīṇi vā dve vā ekaṃ vā nibbattenti. Sabbesaṃyeva imesaṃ kulaputtānanti brahmacariyaciṇṇakulaputtānaṃ. Amoghā…pe… saphalā saudrayāti arahattanikūṭena desanaṃ niṭṭhāpesi. Bhagavantaṃabhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvāti (dī. ni. 2.188) bhagavato dhammadesanaṃ cittena sampaṭicchanto abhinanditvā vācāya pasaṃsamāno anumoditvā mahantaṃ añjaliṃ sirasmiṃ patiṭṭhapetvā pasannalākhārase nimujjamāno viya dasabalassa chabbaṇṇarasmijālantaraṃ pavisitvā catūsu ṭhānesu vanditvā tikkhattuṃ padakkhiṇaṃ katvā bhagavantaṃ abhitthavanto abhitthavanto satthu purato antaradhāyitvā attano devalokameva agamāsīti. Bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā Iti sumaṅgalavilāsiniyā dīghanikāyaṭṭhakathāyaṃ Mahāgovindasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
330. "Because they are not all in every way" means that they do not know how to fully complete the four noble paths, or they produce three, two, or one.

"Of all these sons of good family" means of sons of good family who have practiced the holy life (brahmacarya).

"Not in vain... successful, with happiness" he concluded the teaching with the pinnacle of arahantship.

Having bowed down to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ) and circumambulated him (padakkhiṇaṃ katvā) (D. II. 188), accepting the Blessed One's teaching with his mind, rejoicing, praising with his voice, approving, placing a large añjali on his head, as if diving into pleasant lac juice, entering into the web of six-colored rays of the Ten-Powered One, having paid homage in the four places, having circumambulated three times, praising and extolling the Blessed One, he disappeared in front of the Teacher and went to his own heaven.
