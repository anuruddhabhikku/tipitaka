Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. So imaṃ lokaṃ sadevakaṃ samārakaṃ sabrahmakaṃ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ pajaṃ sadevamanussaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti. So dhammaṃ deseti ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’ti. Atha kho thullakoṭṭhikā brāhmaṇagahapatikā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā appekacce bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu; appekacce bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu, sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu; appekacce yena bhagavā tenañjaliṃ paṇāmetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu; appekacce bhagavato santike nāmagottaṃ sāvetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu; appekacce tuṇhībhūtā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinne kho thullakoṭṭhike brāhmaṇagahapatike bhagavā dhammiyā kathāya sandassesi samādapesi samuttejesi sampahaṃsesi.
293. THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the Kuru country with a large saṃgha of bhikkhus, and eventually he arrived at a Kuru town named Thullakoṭṭhika.

The brāhmaṇa householders of Thullakoṭṭhika heard: “The recluse Gotama, the son of the Sakyans who went forth from a Sakyan clan, has been wandering in the Kuru country with a large saṃgha of bhikkhus and has come to Thullakoṭṭhika.

concerning whom a good report has been spread about: “This Blessed Lord is an arahaṃ, a fully-enlightened buddha, perfected in knowledge and conduct (vijjācaraṇasampanno), a Well-Farer (sugato), Knower of the worlds (lokavidū), unequalled Trainer of men to be tamed (anuttaro purisadammasārathi), Teacher (satthā) of gods and humans (devamanussānaṃ), a Buddha, a Blessed Lord (bhagavā)”.

He proclaims this world with its gods (deva), Māras, and Brahmās, this generation with its ascetics (samaṇa) and brāhmaṇas, its princes and its people, which he has himself realised by direct knowledge (abhiññā).

He teaches the dharma that is good at the beginning, good in the middle, and good at the end, in the spirit and in the letter (sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ); he reveals the spiritual life (brahmacariyaṃ) that is utterly perfect and pure (kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ).

“It is good to see such arahats.”

Then the brāhmaṇa householders of Thullakoṭṭhika went to the Blessed One. Some paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side; some exchanged greetings with him, and when this courteous and amiable talk was finished, sat down at one side; some extended their hands in reverential salutation towards the Blessed One and sat down at one side; some pronounced their name and clan in the Blessed One’s presence and sat down at one side; some kept silent and sat down at one side.

When they were seated, the Blessed One instructed, urged, roused, and encouraged them with talk on the Dharma.
293.Evaṃme sutanti raṭṭhapālasuttaṃ. Tattha thullakoṭṭhikanti thullakoṭṭhaṃ paripuṇṇakoṭṭhāgāraṃ. So kira janapado niccasasso sadā bījabhaṇḍaṃ nikkhamati, khalabhaṇḍaṃ pavisati. Tena tasmiṃ nigame koṭṭhā niccapūrāva honti. Tasmā so thullakoṭṭhikanteva saṅkhaṃ gato.
293. Thus have I heard (evaṃ me suta): the Raṭṭhapāla Sutta.

There, thullakoṭṭhika means a full granary (thullakoṭṭhaṃ paripuṇṇakoṭṭhāgāraṃ). It is said that in that region, seeds and goods always went out, and harvested goods came in. Therefore, the granaries in that town were always full. Therefore, it was known as Thullakoṭṭhika.

294. At that time, a clansman named Raṭṭhapāla, the son of the leading clan in that same Thullakoṭṭhita, was sitting in that assembly.

Then the thought occurred to Raṭṭhapāla, the clansman: “As I understand the Dhamma taught by the Blessed One, it’s not easy, living at home, to practice the holy life (brahmacariya) totally perfect, totally pure, a polished shell. What if I were to shave off my hair & beard, put on the ochre robes (kāsāya), and go forth from the household life into homelessness?”

Then the brāhmaṇas and householders of Thullakoṭṭhika, having been shown, inspired, uplifted, and gladdened by the Blessed One with a talk on the Dhamma, delighted in and approved of the Blessed One’s words. Rising from their seats, worshipping and circumambulating the Fortunate One, they went away.

Then Raṭṭhapāla, the clansman, not long after the brāhmaṇas and householders of Thullakoṭṭhika had left, approached the Blessed One. After paying homage to the Blessed One, he sat down to one side.

Sitting to one side, Raṭṭhapāla, the clansman, said this to the Blessed One: “As I understand the Dhamma taught by the Blessed One, it is not easy while living in a home to lead the holy life (brahmacariya), utterly perfect and pure as a polished shell. I wish, venerable sir, to shave off my hair and beard, put on the yellow robes (kāsāya), and go forth from the home life into homelessness. May I receive the going forth (pravrajyā) in the Blessed One’s presence? May I receive the full admission (upasampadā)?"

“Let the Blessed One ordain me!”

“Raṭṭhapāla, have you been permitted by your mother and father to go forth from the household life into homelessness?”

“No, venerable sir, I have not been permitted by my mother and father to go forth from the household life into homelessness.”

“Raṭṭhapāla, the Tathāgatas do not give the going forth to anyone who does not have his parents’ permission.”

“Venerable sir, I shall see to it that my parents permit me to go forth from the home life into homelessness.”

294. "Why is he called raṭṭhapāla?" Because he is capable of maintaining and protecting a fractured kingdom, therefore he is called raṭṭhapāla.

When did this name arise?

In the time of Padumuttara, the Perfectly Awakened Buddha.

Long ago, one hundred thousand aeons ago, among humans with a lifespan of one hundred thousand years, a teacher named Padumuttara arose, surrounded by a hundred thousand monks. He travelled for the welfare of the world. With reference to this, it was said:

"The city was named Haṃsavatī, the khattiya was named Ānanda;

Sujātā was the mother, of Padumuttara, the teacher."

(bu. vaṃ. 12. 19);

However, even before Padumuttara appeared, two families in Haṃsavatī, full of faith, had established almsgiving to the wretched, the poor, beggars, and so on.

At that time, five hundred ascetics dwelling in the mountains arrived in Haṃsavatī.

Those two people attended to the group of ascetics in the middle.

Having lived there for a short time, the ascetics went to the foot of the mountain.

Two elder monks of the saṃgha declined.

Then they attended to them for as long as they lived.

While the ascetics were enjoying [the alms] and expressing their approval, one described the beauty of Śakra's palace, and the other described the palace of Bhūmindhara, the Nāga king.

One of the families, having made a wish for Śakra's palace, was reborn as Śakra. The other was [reborn] in the Nāga realm as a Nāga king named Pālitanāgarāja.

Śakra, seeing him come to his attendance, asked, "Do you find delight in the Nāga womb?"

He said, "I do not find delight."

"Then, having given alms to Padumuttara, the Blessed One, make a wish in this place. Both of us will live happily."

The Nāga king, having invited the Teacher, Padumuttara, the Blessed One, along with a retinue of one hundred thousand monks, gave a great alms-gift for a week. Seeing the sāmaṇera (novice) Uparevata, the son of Padumuttara, the possessor of ten powers, on the seventh day, having given divine cloths to the saṃgha with the Buddha at its head, he wished for the state of that sāmaṇera.

The Blessed One, looking to the future, seeing that "In the future, the son of the Buddha named Gotama will be Rāhula," said, "Your wish will be fulfilled."

The Nāga king told that matter to Śakra.

Śakra, hearing his words, having given alms for seven days in the same way, and having been reborn in a family capable of maintaining and protecting a fractured kingdom, seeing a son of good family named Raṭṭhapāla, who had gone forth in faith, [wished]: "May I also, in the future, in this world, when Buddhas like you have arisen, having been reborn in a family capable of maintaining and protecting a fractured kingdom, be a Raṭṭhapāla, who has gone forth in faith, like this son of good family."

The Teacher, knowing the nature of fulfillment, spoke this verse:

"A kingdom...

that is able to nourish the four castes;

a Raṭṭhapāla family by name, there this one will be born."

Thus, it should be understood that this name arose in the time of Padumuttara, the Perfectly Awakened Buddha.

What was "This came to be"? What does "However, as ... " mean?

Here is a summary of the story: I, from whatever cause I understand the dharma taught by the Blessed One, from that cause this occurs to me, being examined: "This threefold training (śikṣā), the brahmacarya, should be practiced entirely perfectly, even for one day, in order to attain the final state of mind; and it should be entirely pure, even for one day, having made [it] unpolluted by the impurities of defilements in order to attain the final state of mind, having made it like a conch shell written on, like a conch shell scrubbed clean, like a cleansed conch shell, it should be practiced. This is not easy for one dwelling in a house, living in the midst of a house, to practice entirely perfectly... to practice. What if I were to shave off my hair and beard, and having dressed in robes suitable for those practicing the brahmacarya, dyed in the juice of kāsāya, having gone forth from the house, were to go forth into homelessness?"

"This came to be. However, as ...":

"When the stout householders, brāhmaṇas, and householders had departed, Raṭṭhapāla approached the Blessed One." Among those who had not yet arisen, he did not ask the Blessed One for ordination.

Why? Because there were many relatives, blood relations, friends, and associates there. They would seize [him] even by the arm and pull [him back, saying]—"You are the only son of your mother and father; it is not possible for you to go forth." Therefore, there would be an obstacle to ordination. Having risen together with the assembly, having gone a little way, and then having returned, troubled by some bodily exertion, he approached the Blessed One and asked for ordination.
Therefore it was said: "Then, the noble Raṭṭhapāla, after the Thullakoṭṭhika people had recently departed... asked the Blessed One: 'May the Blessed One ordain me.'"

But since the Blessed One, from the time he had the prince Rāhula ordained, did not ordain a son without the permission of his parents, therefore he asked him: "Have you been permitted, Raṭṭhapāla, by your parents…to go forth?"

After the Thullakoṭṭhika brāhmaṇa householders had recently departed, he approached the Blessed One.

Have you been permitted, Raṭṭhapāla, by your parents…to go forth?

"yagghe": is a particle in the sense of imparting information.

"upasaṅkamī": Why did she approach? In a great family, women going outside receive censure, and this is an urgent matter, [she] thinks "I will announce it to the wealthy man." Therefore, she approached.

"aññataraṃ kuṭṭamūla": It seems that in that country, in the houses of donors there are halls, and here seats are arranged, water pots are set up. There, the renunciates, having wandered for alms, sit down and eat. If they wish, they take what belongs to the donors. Therefore, that too should be understood as the corner of a wall in such a hall of another family. For the renunciates do not eat sitting in an unsuitable place like wretched people.

upasaṅkamī aññataraṃ kuṭṭamūla

"atthi nāma tātā": Here, "atthi" is in the sense of existence, "nāma" is a particle in the sense of questioning or supposing. For this is what is said: "Is there indeed, dear Raṭṭhapāla, wealth for us? Are we not to be called impoverished, for whom you, sitting in such a place, will partake of stale gruel? Likewise, is there indeed, dear Raṭṭhapāla, life for us? Are we not to be called dead, for whom you, sitting in such a place, will partake of stale gruel? Likewise, I suppose, dear Raṭṭhapāla, you have recovered the qualities of a samaṇa relying on the teaching within you, that even though you were raised on delicious flavors, you will partake of this disgusting stale gruel without disgust, as if it were nectar." But that householder, unable to say this matter completely because of being oppressed by suffering, said only this much: "Is there indeed, dear Raṭṭhapāla, will you partake of stale gruel?" But the grammarians here state this characteristic: In the atthisadde (atthi word) used in the sense of unreflectiveness and impatience, the future tense "paribhuñjissasi" ("you will partake") is used. Its meaning is this: "Is there indeed... will you partake... I do not believe or tolerate even this directly." This much was spoken by the householder standing holding the edge of the elder's bowl. The elder too, while his father was standing holding the edge of the bowl, partook of that rotten gruel, like a rotten chicken egg smelling foul in a broken place, similar to a dog's vomit. It seems it is not possible for an ordinary person to partake of such gruel. But the elder, standing in the divine power, having partaken as if enjoying divine food, ambrosial taste, and having taken water with a water pot, and having washed the bowl, mouth, hands and feet, said "kuto no gahapatī" etc.

atthi nāma tātā atthī nāmā kuto no gahapatī

There, "kuto no" means "kuto nu" (why not?).

"neva dāna": Because of the law of giving, we did not receive any gift.

"na paccakkhāna": "What, dear Raṭṭhapāla, is it bearable for you? Have you come without hardship? Is food not being prepared in the house, dear?" We did not receive even a greeting in the manner of such hospitality. But why did the elder say this? Out of favor for his father. It seems it occurred to him thus: "As he speaks to me, I think he speaks thus to other renunciates as well. And in the Buddha's teaching, there is no end to monks whose hidden qualities are like a lotus in another's bowl, like fire covered with ashes, like the heartwood of sandalwood covered with bark, like a jewel covered with cloth, like the moon covered with clouds. He will not utter such words to those ones either, he will stand in restraint," out of favor he said this.

304. “Master Raṭṭhapāla, there are these four kinds of loss ( pārijuñña). Because they have undergone these four kinds of loss, some people here shave off their hair and beard, put on the yellow robes (kāsāya), and go forth from the household life (agāra) into homelessness (anagāriya).

What are the four? Loss through aging (jarāpārijuñña), loss through illness (byādhipārijuñña), loss of wealth (bhogapārijuñña), and loss of relatives (ñātipārijuñña).

And what, Master Raṭṭhapāla, is loss through aging? Here, Master Raṭṭhapāla, someone is old, aged, burdened with years, advanced in life, come to the last stage. He considers thus: ‘I am now old, aged, burdened with years, advanced in life, come to the last stage. It is no longer easy for me to acquire unacquired wealth or to augment wealth already acquired. Suppose I were to shave off my hair and beard, put on the yellow robes, and go forth from the household life into homelessness.’ So, because he has undergone that loss through aging, he shaves off his hair and beard, puts on the yellow robes, and goes forth from the household life into homelessness. This is called loss through aging, Master Raṭṭhapāla. But Master Raṭṭhapāla is now still young, a black-haired young man endowed with the blessing of youth, in the prime of life. Master Raṭṭhapāla has not undergone any loss through aging. What has Master Raṭṭhapāla known or seen or heard that he has gone forth from the home life into homelessness?”

"They touch contact": they touch death-contact.

"Just so, touched": just as a fool, so also the wise man is touched by death-contact; there is no one who is not touched; this, however, is the difference.

"The fool, overcome by foolishness, lies down": the fool, through foolishness, having encountered death-contact, lies down overcome, lies down struck down.

"What has not been done was truly good"–he moves, trembles, and throbs with such remorse.

"The wise man does not tremble": the wise man, seeing the sign of a good destiny, does not tremble, does not move.

They touch contact; just so, touched; the fool, overcome by foolishness, lies down; the wise man does not tremble.

"By which he attains consummation here": by which wisdom he attains in this world the consummation of all duties, arhatship; that is superior even to wealth.

"Because of incompleteness": because of non-consummation, failure to attain arhatship; that is the meaning.

"In existences and non-existences": in inferior and superior existences.

By which he attains consummation here; because of incompleteness; in existences and non-existences.

"He undergoes rebirth and the next world": those who, doing evil, whatever beings, undergoing the round of rebirth in succession, undergo rebirth and the next world.

"Of him [who is] unknowledgeable": another unknowledgeable person believes him of such a sort, unknowledgeable.

He undergoes rebirth and the next world; of him [who is] unknowledgeable.

"By his own actions":

"He is afflicted": he is afflicted by his own actions, by the force of actions done, by the causes of actions such as "they beat [him] with whips," and so on.

"Having gone to the next world": having gone from here to the next world, the world of woe.

By his own actions.

He is afflicted; having gone to the next world.

"With a disfigured form": with a varied form, with various natures; that is the meaning.

"In the qualities of sensual pleasure": having seen the misery in all the qualities of sensual pleasure in this very life and in the life to come.

"Young": young even from the state of the embryo.

"Old": having passed a hundred years.

"The blameless ascetic life is truly best": having ascertained that the blameless, unconflicted, unfaltering, absolutely liberating ascetic life is "best, superior, and more excellent," I have gone forth, O great king.

Therefore, what you say – "Having seen what, or having heard what?" – having seen and heard this, I have gone forth, so hold me; thus he concluded the discourse.

With a disfigured form; in the qualities of sensual pleasure; young; old; the blameless ascetic life is truly best.
