Tattha me, bhante, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ – ‘suññatāvihārenāhaṃ, ānanda, etarahi bahulaṃ viharāmī’ti. Kacci metaṃ, bhante, sussutaṃ suggahitaṃ sumanasikataṃ sūpadhārita’’nti? ‘‘Taggha te etaṃ, ānanda, sussutaṃ suggahitaṃ sumanasikataṃ sūpadhāritaṃ. Pubbepāhaṃ pubbecāhaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.), ānanda, etarahipi etarahi ca (sabbattha) suññatāvihārena bahulaṃ viharāmi. Seyyathāpi, ānanda, ayaṃ migāramātupāsādo suñño hatthigavassavaḷavena, suñño jātarūparajatena, suñño itthipurisasannipātena atthi cevidaṃ asuññataṃ yadidaṃ – bhikkhusaṅghaṃ paṭicca ekattaṃ; evameva kho, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā gāmasaññaṃ, amanasikaritvā manussasaññaṃ, araññasaññaṃ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṃ . Tassa araññasaññāya cittaṃ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati. So evaṃ pajānāti – ‘ye assu darathā gāmasaññaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā manussasaññaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṃ darathamattā yadidaṃ – araññasaññaṃ paṭicca ekatta’nti. So ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ gāmasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ manussasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṃ asuññataṃ yadidaṃ – araññasaññaṃ paṭicca ekatta’nti. Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṃ suññaṃ samanupassati, yaṃ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṃ hoti taṃ ‘santamidaṃ atthī’’’ti pajānāti. Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvatthī in the Eastern Park, in Migāramātā’s mansion.

Then Venerable Ānanda, arising from seclusion in the late afternoon, went to the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, Venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, on one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling among the Sakyans at a town of the Sakyans named Nagaraka.

There, venerable sir, I heard and learned this from the Blessed One's own lips: 'Now, Ānanda, I often abide in voidness ( śūnyatāvihāra ).'

Bhante, did I hear that correctly from the Blessed One, grasp it correctly, attend to it correctly, remember it correctly?"

"Indeed, Ānanda, you heard that correctly, grasped it correctly, attended to it correctly, remembered it correctly.

Before, Ānanda, and now, I often abide in voidness ( śūnyatāvihāra ).

Ānanda, just as this Migāramātā’s mansion is void of elephants, cattle, horses, and mares, void of gold and silver, void of the assembly of men and women, and there is present only this non-voidness, namely, the singleness dependent on the Saṅgha of bhikkhus; so too, Ānanda, a bhikkhu—not attending to the perception of village (grāmasaṃjñā), not attending to the perception of people (manuṣyasaṃjñā)—attends to the singleness dependent on the perception of forest (araṇyasaṃjñā).

His mind enters into that perception of forest (araṇyasaṃjñā) and acquires confidence, steadiness, and resolution.

He understands thus: ‘Whatever disturbances there might be dependent on the perception of village (grāmasaṃjñā), those are not present here; whatever disturbances there might be dependent on the perception of people (manuṣyasaṃjñā), those are not present here. There is present only this amount of disturbance, namely, the singleness dependent on the perception of forest (araṇyasaṃjñā).’

He understands: ‘This field of perception (saṃjñāgata) is void of the perception of village (grāmasaṃjñā); this field of perception is void of the perception of people (manuṣyasaṃjñā). There is present only this non-voidness, namely, the singleness dependent on the perception of forest (araṇyasaṃjñā).’

Thus he regards it as void of what is not there, but as to what remains there he understands that which is present thus: ‘This is present.’

Thus, Ānanda, this is his genuine, undistorted, pure descent into voidness (śūnyatāvakkanti).

176. Evaṃ me suta is the Cūḷasuññatasutta.

There, ekam idaṃ means: it seems that the Elder, having performed his duties to the Blessed One, went to his daytime dwelling, set a time limit, relinquished the emptiness-fruit attainment that had nibbāna as its object, and sat down. As the time limit expired, he arose. Then, emptiness presented itself to his mental formations (saṅkhārā). He became desirous of hearing a talk on emptiness. Then, this occurred to him: "It is not possible to go to the Blessed One and say, 'Venerable sir, please give me a talk on emptiness,' as if striking one burden against another. Suppose I recall that talk which the Blessed One gave when he was staying near Nagaraka. In this way, the Blessed One will give me a talk on emptiness." Recalling the Ten-Powered One, he said ekam idaṃ, and so on.

There, idaṃ is merely a particle.

Kaccimetaṃ, bhante: the Elder was capable of learning and retaining sixty thousand verses while standing in one place. Would he not be capable of retaining one word, "suññatāvihārena"? However, it is not proper to ask as if knowing, when one is desirous of hearing. He spoke thus as if not knowing, being desirous of hearing a talk on emptiness that was made manifest and expanded upon. One who does not know is like one who knows. What will the Elder do with such pretending? Showing respect to the Blessed One even in his place of knowing, he said kaccimetaṃ, and so on.

idaṃ kaccimetaṃ, bhante

Pubbe pi means: even at the time of the first enlightenment, while dwelling near Nagaraka.

Etarahi pi means: even now.

Having spoken thus, he thought: "Ānanda is desirous of hearing a talk on emptiness. One is capable of hearing, but not of learning. One is capable of both hearing and learning, but not of speaking. But Ānanda is capable of hearing, learning, and speaking; [therefore I will give] a talk on emptiness."

Thus, while speaking about him, he said seyyathāpi, and so on.

There, suñño hatthigavāssavaḷavena: there, elephants and so on made of wood, book covers, and paintings exist. And in the places of Vessavaṇa, Mandhātā, and others, things made by means of painting exist. And things arranged by means of jeweled windows, doorframes, couches, and chairs exist. And gold and silver placed for the purpose of renovating what is old exists. And men and women coming for the sake of hearing the Dhamma, asking questions, and so on, who are made of wood and so on, also exist. Therefore, it is not empty of these things. This was said having in mind the absence of elephants and so on that are bound by the senses and have consciousness, the absence of gold and silver that is to be used at any desired moment, and the absence of men and women who dwell in constant residence.

pubbe pi etarahi pi seyyathāpi suñño hatthigavāssavaḷavena

Bhikkhusaṅghaṃ paṭicca: even when the monks have entered for alms, it is not empty because of the monks who partake of monastery meals, and because of the monks who are diligent in tending to the sick, attending to the sick, giving instructions, doing robe work, and so on. Thus, he spoke thus because of the constant existence of monks.

Ekattaṃ means: a single state, meaning there is one non-emptiness. It has been said that there is one non-empty state.

Amanasikaritvā: not making it in the mind, not considering, not reflecting.

Gāmasaññaṃ: the perception of a village that has arisen by way of continuity or by way of defilements. The same method applies to the perception of human beings.

Araññasaññaṃ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṃ: regarding the perception of the forest, he focuses his mind on one thing, thinking, "This is the forest, this is the tree, this is the mountain, this is the blue-hued forest thicket." Thus, regarding only one forest, he focuses his mind on the perception of the forest.

Pakkhandati: he descends.

Adhimuccati: he is resolved that it is so.

Ye assu darathā: those disturbances, whether disturbances of continuity or disturbances of defilements, that would occur regarding the perception of the village, do not exist here in the perception of the forest. The same method applies in the second phrase as well.

Atthi cevāyaṃ: but this mere disturbance of continuity that arises regarding one perception of the forest exists.

bhikkhusaṅghaṃ paṭicca ekattaṃ amanasikaritvā gāmasaññaṃ araññasaññaṃ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṃ pakkhandati adhimuccati ye assu darathā atthi cevāyaṃ

Yañhi kho tattha na hoti: whatever sort of continuity-disturbance or defilement-disturbance arises by way of the perception of the village or the perception of human beings in this perception of the forest, just as the elephants and so on [were absent] in the Migāramātupāsāda, that does not exist.

Yaṃ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṃ: just as the community of monks [was present] in the Migāramātupāsāda, whatever mere continuity-disturbance is left there in the perception of the forest remains.

Taṃ santam idaṃ atthīti pajānāti: he understands that, just as it exists, "this exists." Suññatāvakkanti: the arising of emptiness.

177. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā manussasaññaṃ, amanasikaritvā araññasaññaṃ, pathavīsaññaṃ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṃ. Tassa pathavīsaññāya cittaṃ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati. Seyyathāpi, ānanda, āsabhacammaṃ saṅkusatena suvihataṃ vigatavalikaṃ; evameva kho, ānanda, bhikkhu yaṃ imissā pathaviyā ukkūlavikkūlaṃ nadīviduggaṃ khāṇukaṇṭakaṭṭhānaṃ pabbatavisamaṃ taṃ sabbaṃ sabbaṃ (ka.) amanasikaritvā pathavīsaññaṃ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṃ. Tassa pathavīsaññāya cittaṃ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati. So evaṃ pajānāti – ‘ye assu darathā manussasaññaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā araññasaññaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṃ darathamattā yadidaṃ – pathavīsaññaṃ paṭicca ekatta’nti. So ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ manussasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ araññasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṃ asuññataṃ yadidaṃ – pathavīsaññaṃ paṭicca ekatta’nti. Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṃ suññaṃ samanupassati, yaṃ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṃ hoti taṃ ‘santamidaṃ atthī’ti pajānāti. Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.
“Again, Ānanda, a bhikkhu - not attending to the perception of people (manussasaññaṃ), not attending to the perception of forest (araññasaññaṃ) - attends to the singleness dependent on the perception of earth (pathavīsaññaṃ).

His mind enters into that perception of earth (pathavīsaññāya) and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.

Just as a bull’s hide becomes free from folds when fully stretched with a hundred pegs; so too, Ānanda, a bhikkhu - not attending to any of the ridges and hollows of this earth, to the rivers and ravines, the tracts of stumps and thorns, the mountains and uneven places - attends to the singleness dependent on the perception of earth (pathavīsaññaṃ).

His mind enters into that perception of earth (pathavīsaññāya) and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.

He understands thus: ‘Whatever disturbances there might be dependent on the perception of people (manussasaññaṃ), those are not present here; whatever disturbances there might be dependent on the perception of forest (araññasaññaṃ), those are not present here. There is present only this amount of disturbance, namely, the singleness dependent on the perception of earth (pathavīsaññaṃ).’

He understands: ‘This field of perception is void of the perception of people (manussasaññāya); this field of perception is void of the perception of forest (araññasaññāya). There is present only this non-voidness, namely, the singleness dependent on the perception of earth (pathavīsaññaṃ).’

Thus he regards it as void of what is not there, but as to what remains there he understands that which is present thus: ‘This is present.’

Thus, Ānanda, this is his genuine, undistorted, pure descent into voidness (suññatāvakkanti).

177. Amanasikaritvā manussasañña means here that the perception of a village is not taken into consideration. Why? It seems that the following occurred to him: "Having dismissed the perception of a village by means of the perception of a human being, the perception of a human being by means of the perception of a forest, the perception of a forest by means of the perception of earth, the perception of earth by means of the perception of the sphere of infinite space… and the perception of the sphere of nothingness by means of the perception of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, having dismissed the perception of the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception by means of insight, and insight by means of the path, I will show complete emptiness gradually." Therefore, he began the teaching in this way.

Here, with regard to pathavīsaññaṃ, why, having abandoned the perception of a forest, does he attend to the perception of earth? Because of not attaining a distinction in the perception of a forest. Just as for a man, having seen a delightful field-site, even if he looks at the field-site seven times, thinking, "Here the rice and other crops will thrive well, I will gain great profit," the rice and other crops do not thrive at all; but if, having made that place free from stumps and thorns, he cultivates it and sows [seeds], thus being the case, they thrive. In the same way, even if one attends to the perception of a forest seven times, thinking, "This is a forest, this is a tree, this is a mountain, this is a grove with a blue gleam," one does not attain even proximity or concentration. However, by means of the perception of earth, constant cultivation is possible, and by making the earth kasiṇa the basis for work, having produced the jhānas and having developed insight even in the place where the jhānas are established, it is possible to attain arhatship. Therefore, having abandoned the perception of a forest, he attends to the perception of earth. Paṭicca means having originated dependently.

Now, in which earth kasiṇa is he one who has the perception of earth? For the purpose of showing a comparison of that, he said, beginning with seyyathāpī. There, āsabhaṃ means belonging to a bull (usabha). For other cattle, there are both swellings and blows. For when their hide is stretched out, it does not become free from folds; because of the bull's perfection of characteristics, those defects do not exist. Therefore, his hide is taken. Saṅkusatenā means with a hundred pegs (khila). Suvihataṃ means well-stretched, well-beaten. For [a hide] stretched with less than a hundred pegs does not become free from folds; [a hide] stretched with a hundred pegs becomes free from folds, like the surface of a drum. Therefore, he spoke thus. Ukkūlavikkūla means high and low places, elevated places, and low-lying places. Nadīvidugga means both rivers and difficult places. Pathavīsaññaṃ paṭicca manasi karoti ekatta means he attends to a single perception, originated dependently, only in the kasiṇa earth. Darathamattā means "to the extent of being agitated"; from here onward, "to the extent of being agitated" should be understood in terms of agitation occurring in all instances.

Just as a bull's hide, well-stretched with a hundred pegs, free from folds; uneven places; rivers and difficult passages; he attends to singleness dependent on the earth-perception; to the extent of being agitated.

Tasmātiha, ānanda, ‘parisuddhaṃ paramānuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja viharissāmā’ti viharissāmīti (pī. ka.) – evañhi vo te (ka.), ānanda, sikkhitabba’’nti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā ānando bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Cūḷasuññatasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.
184. “Ānanda, whatever ascetics (śramaṇa) and priests (brāhmaṇa) in the past entered upon and abided in pure, supreme, unsurpassed emptiness (śūnyatā), all entered upon and abided in this same pure, supreme, unsurpassed emptiness.

Ānanda, whatever ascetics and priests in the future will enter upon and abide in pure, supreme, unsurpassed emptiness, all will enter upon and abide in this same pure, supreme, unsurpassed emptiness.

Ānanda, whatever ascetics and priests in the present enter upon and abide in pure, supreme, unsurpassed emptiness, all enter upon and abide in this same pure, supreme, unsurpassed emptiness.

Therefore, Ānanda, ‘We shall enter upon and abide in pure, supreme, unsurpassed emptiness’ – thus, Ānanda, should you train yourselves.”

This the Blessed One (bhagavān) said. The venerable Ānanda, being pleased, rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words.

The Cūḷasuññatasuttaṃ, the first, is finished.
184.Parisuddhanti nirupakkilesaṃ. Anuttaranti uttaravirahitaṃ sabbaseṭṭhaṃ. Suññatanti suññataphalasamāpattiṃ. Tasmāti yasmā atītepi, buddhapaccekabuddhabuddhasāvakasaṅkhātā samaṇabrāhmaṇā. Anāgatepi, etarahipi buddhabuddhasāvakasaṅkhātā samaṇabrāhmaṇā imaṃyeva parisuddhaṃ paramaṃ anuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja vihariṃsu viharissanti viharanti ca, tasmā. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Cūḷasuññatasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
184. Parisuddha means without defilement (nirupakkilesaṃ).
Anuttara means without superior (uttaravirahitaṃ), the best of all (sabbaseṭṭhaṃ).
Suññata means fruition attainment through Voidness (suññataphalasamāpattiṃ).
Tasmā means because in the past (atītepi) there were ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā) who were designated as Buddhas, Paccekabuddhas, and Buddha's disciples (buddhapaccekabuddhabuddhasāvakasaṅkhātā).
Also in the future (anāgatepi), even now (etarahipi), ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā)—designated as Buddhas and Buddha's disciples (buddhabuddhasāvakasaṅkhātā)—having attained this very purified (parisuddhaṃ), supreme (paramaṃ), unsurpassed (anuttaraṃ), voidness (suññataṃ), lived (vihariṃsu), will live (viharissanti), and are living (viharanti ca); therefore (tasmā).
The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti).

The Cūḷasuññatasuttavaṇṇanā is finished in the Papañcasūdanī, a commentary on the Majjhimanikāya.
