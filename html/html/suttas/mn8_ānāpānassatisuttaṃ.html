Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Sāvattha in the Eastern Park, in the Palace of Migāra’s Mother, together with many very well-known elder disciples - the venerable Sāriputta, the venerable Mahā Moggallāna, the venerable Mahā Kassapa, the venerable Mahā Kaccāyana, the venerable Mahā Kotthita, the venerable Mahā Kappina, the venerable Mahā Cunda, the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Revata, and the venerable Ānanda, and other very well-known elder disciples.

Now on that occasion elder bhikkhus had been teaching and instructing new bhikkhus; some elder bhikkhus had been teaching and instructing ten new bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus had been teaching and instructing twenty new bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus had been teaching and instructing thirty new bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus had been teaching and instructing forty new bhikkhus. And the new bhikkhus, taught and instructed by the elder bhikkhus, achieved successive stages of high distinction.
144.Evaṃme sutanti ānāpānassatisuttaṃ. Tattha aññehi cāti ṭhapetvā pāḷiyaṃ āgate dasa there aññehipi abhiññātehi bahūhi sāvakehi saddhiṃ. Tadā kira mahā bhikkhusaṅgho ahosi aparicchinnagaṇano. Ovadanti anusāsantīti āmisasaṅgahena dhammasaṅgahena cāti dvīhi saṅgahehi saṅgaṇhitvā kammaṭṭhānovādānusāsanīhi ovadanti ca anusāsanti ca. Te cāti cakāro āgamasandhimattaṃ. Uḷāraṃ pubbenāparaṃ visesaṃ jānantīti sīlaparipūraṇādito pubbavisesato uḷārataraṃ aparaṃ kasiṇaparikammādivisesaṃ jānantīti attho. Ovadanti anusāsantī Te cā Uḷāraṃ pubbenāparaṃ visesaṃ jānantī
Thus have I heard: the Ānāpānassatisutta.

There, excluding "aññehi ca," the ten elders mentioned in the Pāli [text] were together with many other well-known disciples. At that time, it seems there was a great Saṅgha of monks, whose number was incalculable.

"Ovadanti anusāsantī": by means of two kinds of generosity, generosity with material things and generosity with the Dharma, having gathered them with instructions on the bases of action, they both advised and instructed. "Te cā": the "ca" is merely a euphonic addition. "Uḷāraṃ pubbenāparaṃ visesaṃ jānantī": they know a superior, further distinction than the previous distinction, beginning with the complete fulfillment of morality, a superior further distinction, such as the preliminary activities for the kasiṇa, etc.; this is the meaning.

Ovadanti anusāsantī, te cā, uḷāraṃ pubbenāparaṃ visesaṃ jānantī.

145. Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā tadahuposathe pannarase pavāraṇāya puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto abbhokāse nisinno hoti. Tasmātiha, bhikkhave, bhiyyosomattāya vīriyaṃ ārabhatha appattassa pattiyā, anadhigatassa adhigamāya , asacchikatassa sacchikiriyāya. Idhevāhaṃ sāvatthiyaṃ komudiṃ cātumāsiniṃ āgamessāmī’’ti. Te ca navā bhikkhū therehi bhikkhūhi ovadiyamānā anusāsiyamānā uḷāraṃ pubbenāparaṃ visesaṃ jānanti.
At that time, the Blessed One was seated in the open, surrounded by the Saṃgha of bhikkhus, on the Uposatha day of the fifteenth, the full moon night of the Pavarana ceremony.

Then, surveying the silent Saṃgha of bhikkhus, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus thus: "Oh bhikkhus, I am content with this progress; my mind is content with this progress. Therefore, oh bhikkhus, here exert yourselves all the more for the attainment of the unattained (appattassa pattiyā), for the accomplishment of the unaccomplished (anadhigatassa adhigamāya), for the realization of the unrealized (asacchikatassa sacchikiriyāya). I will remain right here in Sāvatthī until the fourth month of Komudī."

The bhikkhus of the countryside heard: "The Blessed One will remain there in Sāvatthī until the Komudī full moon of the fourth month."

Those bhikkhus of the countryside went to Sāvatthī to see the Blessed One. And those elder bhikkhus still more intensively taught and instructed new bhikkhus. Some elder bhikkhus taught and instructed ten new bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus taught and instructed twenty, some elder bhikkhus taught and instructed thirty, some elder bhikkhus taught and instructed forty new bhikkhus. And those new bhikkhus, being advised and instructed by the elder bhikkhus, came to know a superior and distinct (state) beyond the previous one.
145.Āraddhoti tuṭṭho. Appattassa pattiyāti appattassa arahattassa pāpuṇanatthaṃ. Sesapadadvayepi ayameva attho. Komudiṃ cātumāsininti pacchimakattikacātumāsapuṇṇamaṃ. Sā hi kumudānaṃ atthitāya komudī, catunnaṃ vassikānaṃ māsānaṃ pariyosānattā cātumāsinīti vuccati. Āgamessāmīti udikkhissāmi, ajja apavāretvā yāva sā āgacchati, tāva katthaci agantvā idheva vasissāmīti attho. Iti bhikkhūnaṃ pavāraṇasaṅgahaṃ anujānanto evamāha. Yassa pana samatho vā taruṇo hoti vipassanā vā, tassa diyyati. Sacepi saṭṭhivassā bhikkhū āgacchanti, tassa senāsanaṃ gahetuṃ na labhanti. Ayañca pana pavāraṇasaṅgaho ekassa dinnopi sabbesaṃ dinnoyeva hoti. 145. Āraddho means delighted. Appattassa pattiyā means for the purpose of attaining the unattained arahantship. The same is the meaning in the remaining two words as well. Komudiṃ cātumāsini means the last Kārttika full moon of the four months. That [night] is called komudī because of the existence of white lotuses (kumuda), and cātumāsinī because it is the end of the four rainy season months. Āgamessāmī means I will wait, having dismissed [them] today, I will stay right here without going anywhere else until that [day] comes. Thus, while approving the acceptance of the pavāraṇā-saṅgaha, he said this.

The pavāraṇā-saṅgaha is given through a formal motion and a second motion. To whom is it given, and to whom is it not given? It is not given to the inactive childish ordinary person, nor to one who has initiated insight meditation, nor to a noble disciple. But it is given to one whose serenity (samatha) or insight (vipassanā) is still young. The Blessed One, examining the conduct of the minds of the monks, knowing the young state of serenity and insight, [thought:] "Today, while I am holding pavāraṇā, the monks who have spent the rainy season in various directions will gather here. Then, if the senior monks take the lodgings, these monks will not be able to produce distinction. Even if I leave for a tour, it will be difficult for them to find a place to stay. But if I hold apavāraṇā, the monks will not come down to this Śrāvastī, and I too will not leave for a tour. Thus, the dwelling place of these monks will be unencumbered. They, living comfortably in their own dwelling places, having made their serenity and insight strong, will be able to produce distinction." So, having dismissed [them] that day, he approved the acceptance of the pavāraṇā-saṅgaha for the monks, [saying:] “I will hold the pavāraṇā on the Kārttika full moon." Indeed, when the pavāraṇā-saṅgaha is received, the teachers and preceptors of the one who has undertaken dependence may leave, and he may live until the last month of the hot season, [thinking:] "If a suitable giver of support comes, I will take dependence from him." Even if monks of sixty years come, they are not allowed to take his lodging. And this pavāraṇā-saṅgaha, even if given to one, is indeed given to all.

Coming down to Śrāvastī: This was said with reference to [those monks] who, having heard that the Blessed One had given the pavāraṇā-saṅgaha, lived for a month in the very place where they had heard it, according to their own nature, and then, having performed the uposatha on the Kārttika full moon, came down. Earlier and later: Here, having done work on young serenity and insight, they made serenity and insight strong. This is what is called the earlier distinction. Then, having properly contemplated the conditioned things with a concentrated mind, some realized the fruit of stream-entry... some realized arahantship. This is what is called the later, magnificent distinction.

147. ‘‘Santi, bhikkhave, bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe arahanto khīṇāsavā vusitavanto katakaraṇīyā ohitabhārā anuppattasadatthā parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanā sammadaññāvimuttā – evarūpāpi, bhikkhave, santi bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe . Santi, bhikkhave, bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātikā tattha parinibbāyino anāvattidhammā tasmā lokā – evarūpāpi, bhikkhave, santi bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe. Santi, bhikkhave, bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṃ tanuttā sakadāgāmino sakideva sakiṃ deva (ka.) imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvā dukkhassantaṃ karissanti – evarūpāpi, bhikkhave, santi bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe . Ānāpānassati, bhikkhave, bhāvitā bahulīkatā cattāro satipaṭṭhāne paripūreti. Cattāro satipaṭṭhānā bhāvitā bahulīkatā satta bojjhaṅge paripūrenti. Satta bojjhaṅgā bhāvitā bahulīkatā vijjāvimuttiṃ paripūrenti.
“In this Sangha of bhikkhus there are arahants with taints destroyed, who have lived the holy life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, reached the true goal, destroyed the fetters of being, and are completely liberated through final knowledge - such bhikkhus are there in this Sangha of bhikkhus.

“In this Sangha of bhikkhus there are bhikkhus who, with the destruction of the five lower fetters (orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ), are due to reappear spontaneously (opapātikā) in the Pure Abodes and there attain final Nibbana, without ever returning from that world - such bhikkhus are there in this Sangha of bhikkhus.

“In this Sangha of bhikkhus there are bhikkhus who, with the destruction of three fetters and with the attenuation of lust (rāga), hate (dosa), and delusion (moha), are once-returners (sakadāgāmino), returning once to this world to make an end of suffering - such bhikkhus are there in this Sangha of bhikkhus.

“In this Sangha of bhikkhus there are bhikkhus who, with the destruction of the three fetters (saṃyojanānaṃ), are stream-enterers (sotāpannā), no longer subject to perdition, bound for deliverance, headed for enlightenment - such bhikkhus are there in this Sangha of bhikkhus.

“In this Sangha of bhikkhus there are bhikkhus who abide devoted to the development of the four foundations of mindfulness (catunnaṃ satipaṭṭhānānaṃ bhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) - such bhikkhus are there in this Sangha of bhikkhus.

In this Sangha of bhikkhus there are bhikkhus who abide devoted to the development of the four right kinds of striving (catunnaṃ sammappadhānānaṃ bhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) . . . of the four bases for spiritual power (catunnaṃ iddhipādānaṃ) . . . of the five faculties (pañcannaṃ indriyānaṃ) . . . of the five powers (pañcannaṃ balānaṃ) . . . of the seven enlightenment factors (sattannaṃ bojjhaṅgānaṃ) . . . of the Noble Eightfold Path (ariyassa aṭṭhaṅgikassa maggassa bhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) - such bhikkhus are there in this Sangha of bhikkhus.

“In this Sangha of bhikkhus there are bhikkhus who abide devoted to the development of loving-kindness (mettābhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) . . . of compassion (karuṇābhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) . . . of appreciative joy (muditābhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) . . . of equanimity (upekkhābhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) . . . of the meditation on foulness (asubhabhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) . . . of the perception of impermanence (aniccasaññābhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā) - such bhikkhus are there in this Sangha of bhikkhus.

In this Sangha of bhikkhus there are bhikkhus who abide devoted to the development of mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānassatibhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā).

Bhikkhus, when mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānassati) is developed and cultivated, it is of great fruit and great benefit.

When mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānassati) is developed and cultivated, it fulfils the four foundations of mindfulness (cattāro satipaṭṭhāne paripūreti).

Just as here in "one who is devoted to the practice of the perception of impermanence (aniccasaññābhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā)," wisdom (paññā) is stated by means of perception (saññānāmena), so also here it should be understood that feeling (vedanā) is stated by means of attention (manasikāranāmena).

In this quatrain, in the first phrase, feeling (vedanā) is stated with joy (pītisīsena); in the second phrase, it is stated by its own nature as pleasure (sukha).

In the two phrases of mental formation (cittasaṅkhāra), because of the statement "perception and feeling are mental factors; these things, being bound up with cognizance, are cognizance formations (saññā ca vedanā ca cetasikā, ete dhammā cittapaṭibaddhā cittasaṅkhārā)," and because of the statement "having set aside thought and examination, all things associated with consciousness are included in the mental formations (vitakkavicāre ṭhapetvā sabbepi cittasampayuttakā dhammā cittasaṅkhāre saṅgahitā)," feeling (vedanā) is stated by means of mental formation (cittasaṅkhāranāmena).

Having included all that by means of attention (manasikāranāmena), here he said "rightful attention (sādhukaṃ manasikāra)."

Even so, because this feeling is not an object (ārammaṇaṃ), therefore, the contemplation of feeling is not fitting.

No, it is fitting; for even in the Satipaṭṭhāna commentary it was said, "having made the basis of such and such pleasure etc. the object, feeling feels; but depending on that occurrence of feeling, there is only the expression ‘I feel’ (taṃtaṃsukhādīnaṃ vatthuṃ ārammaṇaṃ katvā vedanāva vedayati, taṃ pana vedanāpavattiṃ upādāya ahaṃ vedayāmīti vohāramattaṃ hotī)."

Moreover, this problem has already been addressed in the explanation of the meaning of pītipaṭisaṃvedītiādīnaṃ.

For it was said in the Visuddhimagga –

"Joy is experienced in two ways: with the object and with non-confusion.

How is joy experienced with the object? One enters into the two jhānas with joy; for him, at the moment of attaining that attainment, joy is experienced with the object because of the object being experienced.

How is joy experienced with non-confusion? Having entered into the two jhānas with joy and having arisen, one contemplates the joy associated with the jhāna as decaying and vanishing; for him, at the moment of insight, joy is experienced with non-confusion by means of the penetration of the characteristics.

And this was said in the Paṭisambhidā: ‘For one who knows that the mind’s one-pointedness and non-distraction are due to long in-breaths, mindfulness is established; by that mindfulness, by that knowledge, that joy is experienced.’

The remaining terms should also be understood in meaning in this way."

Thus, just as joy, pleasure, and mental formations are experienced with the object by means of the attainment of jhāna, so also by this attainment of attention designated as feeling (vedanāsaṅkhātamanasikāra) associated with jhāna, feeling is experienced with the object.

Therefore, it is well said, "At that time, the monk dwells as an observer of feeling in feelings (vedanāsu vedanānupassī tasmiṃ samaye bhikkhu viharatī)."

Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, muṭṭhassatissa asampajānassāti: Here, this is the intention – Because a monk who proceeds in the manner of ‘I will breathe in experiencing the mind (cittapaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmīti),’ etc., although he makes the sign of the in-breath and out-breath the object, nevertheless, because of proceeding by establishing mindfulness and full awareness in the object of that mind, he is indeed called an observer of the mind in the mind (citte cittānupassīyeva nāmesa hoti).

For mindfulness of breathing is not possible for one who is unmindful and without full awareness (muṭṭhassatissa asampajānassa ānāpānassatibhāvanā atthi).

Therefore, at that time, the monk dwells as an observer of the mind in the mind by means of experiencing the mind etc. with the object.

So yaṃ taṃ abhijjhādomanassānaṃ pahānaṃ, taṃ paññāya disvā sādhukaṃ ajjhupekkhitā hotīti: Here, with abhijjhā is shown the desire for sensual pleasure hindrance (kāmacchandanīvaraṇaṃ), and by means of domanassa is shown the ill-will hindrance (byāpādanīvaraṇaṃ).
Here, this tetrad is spoken of only in terms of insight (vipassanā). And mindfulness of phenomena (dhammānupassanā) is sixfold in terms of the section on the hindrances (nīvaraṇapabbādivasena), etc. Of that, the section on the hindrances is the beginning. Even of that, this pair of hindrances is the beginning. Thus, to show the beginning of mindfulness of phenomena, he said, "Greed and displeasure."

Pahāna means it is intended as the knowledge that causes abandonment, such as abandoning the perception of permanence through contemplating impermanence (anicānupassanāya niccasaññaṃ pajahatīti evaṃ pahānakarañāṇaṃ adhippetaṃ).

"Having seen that with wisdom" (taṃ paññāya disvā) means having seen that abandoning knowledge (pahānañāṇaṃ), which is designated as knowledge of impermanence, detachment, cessation, and relinquishment, with another insight wisdom (aparāya vipassanāpaññāya), and that also with another. Thus he shows the succession of insight (vipassanāparamparaṃ).

"Remains equanimous" (ajjhupekkhitā hoti) means what he regards with equanimity is twofold: one who has undertaken serenity and one who regards attendance with equanimity.

There, there is equanimity towards co-arisen things and there is equanimity towards an object. Here, equanimity towards an object is intended.

"Therefore, here, monks" (tasmātiha, bhikkhave) means since one who contemplates impermanence is active in the way of "I will breathe in contemplating impermanence," not only towards phenomena such as the hindrances, but also towards the abandonment knowledge of phenomena spoken of with greed and displeasure as the head, he remains seeing with wisdom, therefore, "a monk dwells contemplating phenomena in phenomena at that time" is to be understood.

"Monks, I do not (say there is) mindfulness for one who is forgetful or uncomprehending."

"So, having seen with wisdom that abandonment of greed and displeasure, he remains properly equanimous."

152.Vivekanissitantiādīni vuttatthāneva. Ettha pana ānāpānapariggāhikā sati lokiyā hoti, lokiyā ānāpānā lokiyasatipaṭṭhānaṃ paripūrenti, lokiyā satipaṭṭhānā lokuttarabojjhaṅge paripūrenti, lokuttarā bojjhaṅgā vijjāvimuttiphalanibbānaṃ paripūrenti. Iti lokiyassa āgataṭṭhāne lokiyaṃ kathitaṃ, lokuttarassa āgataṭṭhāne lokuttaraṃ kathitanti. Thero panāha ‘‘aññattha evaṃ hoti, imasmiṃ pana sutte lokuttaraṃ upari āgataṃ, lokiyā ānāpānā lokiyasatipaṭṭhāne paripūrenti, lokiyā satipaṭṭhānā lokiye bojjhaṅge paripūrenti, lokiyā bojjhaṅgā lokuttaraṃ vijjāvimuttiphalanibbānaṃ paripūrenti, vijjāvimuttipadena hi idha vijjāvimuttiphalanibbānaṃ adhippeta’’nti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Ānāpānassatisuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
152. vivekanissita etc. [are] in the place where [it] was said.

Here, mindfulness (sati) that takes up mindfulness of breathing (ānāpāna) is worldly (lokiya); worldly mindfulness of breathing fills worldly mindfulness practices (lokiyasatipaṭṭhānaṃ); worldly mindfulness practices fill supramundane factors of enlightenment (lokuttarabojjhaṅge); supramundane factors of enlightenment fill the vidyāvimuktiphalanirvāṇa.

Thus, in the place where the worldly comes, the worldly is spoken of; in the place where the supramundane comes, the supramundane is spoken of.

The elder then said, "Elsewhere it is thus, but in this sutta, the supramundane comes above; worldly mindfulness of breathing fills worldly mindfulness practices, worldly mindfulness practices fill worldly factors of enlightenment, worldly factors of enlightenment fill the supramundane vidyāvimuktiphalanirvāṇa; for here, by the term vidyāvimutti, vidyāvimuktiphalanirvāṇa is intended."

The ānāpānassatisuttavaṇṇanā is finished in the papañcasūdanī commentary to the Majjhimanikāya.
