Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Na , bhikkhave, nevāpiko nivāpaṃ nivapati migajātānaṃ – ‘imaṃ me nivāpaṃ nivuttaṃ migajātā paribhuñjantā dīghāyukā vaṇṇavanto ciraṃ dīghamaddhānaṃ yāpentū’ti. Evañca kho, bhikkhave, nevāpiko nivāpaṃ nivapati migajātānaṃ – ‘imaṃ me nivāpaṃ nivuttaṃ migajātā anupakhajja mucchitā bhojanāni bhuñjissanti, anupakhajja mucchitā bhojanāni bhuñjamānā madaṃ āpajjissanti, mattā samānā pamādaṃ āpajjissanti, pamattā samānā yathākāmakaraṇīyā bhavissanti imasmiṃ nivāpe’ti.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was staying at Sāvatthī (sāvatthiyaṃ) in the Jeta Grove (jetavane), in Anāthapiṇḍika's (anāthapiṇḍikassa) park (ārāme).

There the Blessed One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: "Monks (bhikkhavo)!"

"Venerable sir (bhadante)," those monks (bhikkhū) replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this:

"Monks (bhikkhave), a deer-trapper (nevāpiko) does not lay down bait (nivāpaṃ nivapati) for a deer herd (migajātānaṃ), intending thus: 'May the deer herd (migajātā) enjoy (paribhuñjantā) this bait (imaṃ me nivāpaṃ nivuttaṃ) that I have laid down and so be long-lived (dīghāyukā), handsome (vaṇṇavanto) and endure (yāpentū) for a long time (ciraṃ dīghamaddhānaṃ).'

A deer-trapper (nevāpiko) lays down bait (nivāpaṃ nivapati) for a deer herd (migajātānaṃ) intending thus: 'The deer herd (migajātā) will eat (bhuñjissanti) food (bhojanāni) unwarily (anupakhajja) by going right in amongst (mucchitā) the bait (imaṃ me nivāpaṃ nivuttaṃ) that I have laid down; by so doing they will become intoxicated (anupakhajja mucchitā bhojanāni bhuñjamānā madaṃ āpajjissanti); when they are intoxicated (mattā samānā), they will fall into negligence (pamādaṃ āpajjissanti); when they are negligent (pamattā samānā), I can do with them as I like (yathākāmakaraṇīyā bhavissanti) on account of this bait (imasmiṃ nivāpe).' "

261. Thus, I have heard: the Nivāpa Sutta.

There, nevāpika means one who, for the purpose of catching deer, scatters grass seeds in the forest, thinking, "When deer come to eat this grass, I will easily catch them."

Nivāpa means sowing.

Nivutta means sown.

Migajātā means a group of deer.

Anupakhajjā means having approached.

Mucchitā means intoxicated with craving; intoxicated by the intoxication of craving, having entered the heart due to craving, it has reached the state of intoxication. This is the meaning.

Madaṃ āpajjissantī means they will indulge in the intoxication of pride.

Pamāda means a state of scattered mindfulness.

Yathākāmakaraṇīyā bhavissantī means they will be able to do as they please, as we wish, so it should be done.

Imasmiṃ nivāpe means in this nivāpa-place.

It is said that there is a certain grass called nivāpatiṇaṃ, which is excellent in the hot season. As the hot season progresses, it becomes a single mass like a nīvāra forest or a chain of clouds. Hunters, having tilled and sown it in a place with accessible water, make a fence, join a door, and guard it.

Then, when in the great heat all the grasses are dry, and even a tongue's tip of water is hard to find, then the deer, eating dry grasses and old leaves, wandering about as if trembling, smelling the scent of the nivāpatiṇa, not counting the dangers of death and imprisonment, enter, pushing through the fence.

For them, that nivāpatiṇaṃ is exceedingly dear and pleasing.

The nevāpika, having seen them, acts as if he is careless for two or three days, and stands having opened the door.

In the inner nivāpa place, here and there, there are also water-holes. The deer, having entered through the open door, having eaten and drunk only a little, depart. On the next day, thinking that no one will do anything, they go shaking their ears, eating, drinking, and not hurrying. On the next day, thinking that no one is the maker of anything, having eaten and drunk as much as they want, they enter the circular thicket and lie down.

The hunters, having known their state of carelessness, having closed the door, having surrounded them, starting from the edge, go striking and killing them. Thus, in that nivāpa, they become able to be treated as they please by the nevāpika.

262. ‘‘Tatra, bhikkhave, paṭhamā migajātā amuṃ nivāpaṃ nivuttaṃ nevāpikassa anupakhajja mucchitā bhojanāni bhuñjiṃsu, te tattha anupakhajja mucchitā bhojanāni bhuñjamānā madaṃ āpajjiṃsu, mattā samānā pamādaṃ āpajjiṃsu, pamattā samānā yathākāmakaraṇīyā ahesuṃ nevāpikassa amusmiṃ nivāpe. Evañhi te, bhikkhave, paṭhamā migajātā na parimucciṃsu nevāpikassa iddhānubhāvā.
“Now, bhikkhus, the deer of the first herd, without caution, approached and consumed the intoxicating food in that bait laid by the deer-trapper (nevāpika); by approaching and consuming that intoxicating food, they became intoxicated (mada); being intoxicated, they fell into negligence (pramāda); being negligent, they became subject to the deer-trapper’s will in that bait. Thus, bhikkhus, the deer of the first herd did not escape the power (iddhānubhāva) of the deer-trapper (nevāpika).
262.Tatra, bhikkhaveti, bhikkhave, tesu migajātesu. Paṭhamā migajātāti, migajātā paṭhamadutiyā nāma natthi. Bhagavā pana āgatapaṭipāṭivasena kappetvā paṭhamā, dutiyā, tatiyā , catutthāti nāmaṃ āropetvā dassesi. Iddhānubhāvāti yathākāmaṃ kattabbabhāvato; vasībhāvoyeva hi ettha iddhīti ca ānubhāvoti ca adhippeto.
There, bhikkhaves, that is, bhikkhaves, among those kinds of deer.

The first kind of deer, that is, there is no such thing as a second first kind of deer.

The Blessed One, however, having imagined it in the order in which it came, showed it by imposing the name first, second, third, fourth.

The power ofiddhi, that is, because of the possibility of doing as one wishes; for mastery alone is intended here as iddhi, and anubhāva.

apadaṃ vadhitvā māracakkhu means, according to that same method, just as Māra's eye becomes apadaṃ, nippadaṃ (without footing), appatiṭṭhaṃ (unsupported), nirārammaṇaṃ (without object), so he kills it. This is the meaning.

adassanaṃ gato pāpimato means, by that same method, he has gone out of sight of Māra, the evil one.

Indeed, he is not able to see with his fleshly eye the body of wisdom (ñāṇasarīraṃ) of that bhikkhu who has attained the jhāna that is the basis of insight.

paññāya cassa disvā āsavā parikkhīṇā hontī means, having seen the four noble truths with the wisdom of the path (maggapaññāya), the four āsavas are destroyed.

tiṇṇo loke visattika means visattikā has gone to such a designation, due to the fact of clinging (sattavisattabhāvena) in the world.

Or rather, "In what sense is visattikā visattikā? Because it is visata, it is visattikā; because it is visaṭa, it is visattikā; because it is vipula, it is visattikā; because it is visāla, it is visattikā; because it is visama, it is visattikā; because it visakkati, it is visattikā; because it removes poison (visaṃ harati), it is visattikā; because it is deceitful (visaṃvādikā), it is visattikā; because it has poisonous roots (visamūlā), it is visattikā; because it has poisonous fruits (visaphalā), it is visattikā; because it has poisonous enjoyment (visaparibhogā), it is visattikā; or rather, that desire (taṇhā) is extensive (visālā) regarding forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and tactile objects" (mahāni. 3; cūḷani. mettagūmāṇavapucchāniddesa 22, khaggavisāṇasuttaniddesa 124) visattikā.

Thus, visattikā has gone to such a designation: he has crossed over, completely crossed over, gone beyond that desire (taṇhaṃ).

Therefore, it is said: tiṇṇo loke visattika.

The Nivāpasutta Commentary in the Papañcasūdanī, the Commentary to the Majjhimanikāya, is finished.
