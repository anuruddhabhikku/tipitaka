468. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘cattārimāni, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānāni. Katamāni cattāri? Atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannasukhaṃ āyatiṃ dukkhavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave , dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannadukkhañceva āyatiñca dukkhavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannadukkhaṃ āyatiṃ sukhavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannasukhañceva āyatiñca sukhavipākaṃ’’.
468. Thus have I heard:

Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Sāvatthī (sāvatthiyaṃ), in the Jeta Grove (jetavane), Anāthapiṇḍika’s (anāthapiṇḍikassa) monastery (ārāme).

There the Blessed One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: “Monks (bhikkhavo)!”

“Venerable sir (bhadante),” those monks (bhikkhū) replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this (etadavoca): “Monks (bhikkhave), there are these four ways of undertaking things (dhammasamādānāni).

Which four (katamāni cattāri)? There is, monks (bhikkhave), a way of undertaking things (dhammasamādānaṃ) that is presently pleasant (paccuppannasukhaṃ) but ripens in the future (āyatiṃ) as pain (dukkhavipākaṃ); there is, monks (bhikkhave), a way of undertaking things (dhammasamādānaṃ) that is presently painful (paccuppannadukkhañceva) and ripens in the future (āyatiñca) as pain (dukkhavipākaṃ); there is, monks (bhikkhave), a way of undertaking things (dhammasamādānaṃ) that is presently painful (paccuppannadukkhaṃ) but ripens in the future (āyatiṃ) as pleasure (sukhavipākaṃ); there is, monks (bhikkhave), a way of undertaking things (dhammasamādānaṃ) that is presently pleasant (paccuppannasukhañceva) and ripens in the future (āyatiñca) as pleasure (sukhavipākaṃ).”
468.Evaṃme sutanti cūḷadhammasamādānasuttaṃ. Tattha dhammasamādānānīti dhammoti gahitagahaṇāni. Paccuppannasukhanti paccuppanne sukhaṃ, āyūhanakkhaṇe sukhaṃ sukaraṃ sukhena sakkā pūretuṃ. Āyatiṃ dukkhavipākanti anāgate vipākakāle dukkhavipākaṃ. Iminā upāyena sabbapadesu attho veditabbo.
468.
Thus, "I have heard" is the Cūḷadhammasamādāna Sutta.
There, dhammasamādānānī means the things that are taken hold of.
paccuppannasukha means happiness in the present, happiness at the moment of effort. It is easy to fulfill with ease.
āyatiṃ dukkhavipāka means a painful result in the future, at the time of the result.
By this method, the meaning should be understood in all instances.

469. ‘‘Katamañca, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannasukhaṃ āyatiṃ dukkhavipākaṃ? Santi, bhikkhave, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘natthi kāmesu doso’ti. Te kāmesu pātabyataṃ āpajjanti. Te kho moḷibaddhāhi moḷibandhāhi (syā. kaṃ. ka.) paribbājikāhi paricārenti. Te evamāhaṃsu – ‘kiṃsu nāma te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā kāmesu anāgatabhayaṃ sampassamānā kāmānaṃ pahānamāhaṃsu, kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapenti? Sukho imissā paribbājikāya taruṇāya mudukāya lomasāya bāhāya samphasso’ti te kāmesu pātabyataṃ āpajjanti. Te kāmesu pātabyataṃ āpajjitvā kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjanti. Te tattha dukkhā tibbā kharā kaṭukā vedanā vedayanti. Te evamāhaṃsu – ‘idaṃ kho te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā kāmesu anāgatabhayaṃ sampassamānā kāmānaṃ pahānamāhaṃsu, kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapenti, ime hi mayaṃ kāmahetu kāmanidānaṃ dukkhā tibbā kharā kaṭukā vedanā vedayāmā’ti. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, gimhānaṃ pacchime māse māluvāsipāṭikā phaleyya. Atha kho taṃ, bhikkhave, māluvābījaṃ aññatarasmiṃ sālamūle nipateyya. Atha kho, bhikkhave, yā tasmiṃ sāle adhivatthā devatā sā bhītā saṃviggā santāsaṃ āpajjeyya. Atha kho, bhikkhave, tasmiṃ sāle adhivatthāya devatāya mittāmaccā ñātisālohitā ārāmadevatā vanadevatā rukkhadevatā osadhitiṇavanappatīsu adhivatthā devatā saṅgamma samāgamma evaṃ samassāseyyuṃ – ‘mā bhavaṃ bhāyi, mā bhavaṃ bhāyi; appeva nāmetaṃ māluvābījaṃ moro vā gileyya moro vā gileyya, godhā vā khādeyya (ka.), mago vā khādeyya, davaḍāho vanadāho (ka.) vā ḍaheyya, vanakammikā vā uddhareyyuṃ, upacikā vā uṭṭhaheyyuṃ udrabheyyuṃ (sī. pī. ka.), abījaṃ vā panassā’ti. Atha kho taṃ, bhikkhave, māluvābījaṃ neva moro gileyya, na mago khādeyya, na davaḍāho ḍaheyya, na vanakammikā uddhareyyuṃ, na upacikā uṭṭhaheyyuṃ, bījañca panassa taṃ pāvussakena meghena abhippavuṭṭhaṃ sammadeva viruheyya. Sāssa māluvālatā taruṇā mudukā lomasā vilambinī, sā taṃ sālaṃ upaniseveyya. Atha kho, bhikkhave, tasmiṃ sāle adhivatthāya devatāya evamassa – ‘kiṃsu nāma te bhonto mittāmaccā ñātisālohitā ārāmadevatā vanadevatā rukkhadevatā osadhitiṇavanappatīsu adhivatthā devatā māluvābīje anāgatabhayaṃ sampassamānā saṅgamma samāgamma evaṃ samassāsesuṃ samassāseyyuṃ (ka.) – ‘‘mā bhavaṃ bhāyi mā bhavaṃ bhāyi, appeva nāmetaṃ māluvābījaṃ moro vā gileyya, mago vā khādeyya, davaḍāho vā ḍaheyya, vanakammikā vā uddhareyyuṃ, upacikā vā uṭṭhaheyyuṃ, abījaṃ vā panassā’’ti; sukho imissā māluvālatāya taruṇāya mudukāya lomasāya vilambiniyā samphasso’ti. Sā taṃ sālaṃ anuparihareyya. Sā taṃ sālaṃ anupariharitvā upari viṭabhiṃ viṭapaṃ (syā. ṭṭha.) kareyya. Upari viṭabhiṃ karitvā oghanaṃ janeyya. Oghanaṃ janetvā ye tassa sālassa mahantā mahantā khandhā te padāleyya. Atha kho, bhikkhave, tasmiṃ sāle adhivatthāya devatāya evamassa – ‘idaṃ kho te bhonto mittāmaccā ñātisālohitā ārāmadevatā vanadevatā rukkhadevatā osadhitiṇavanappatīsu adhivatthā devatā māluvābīje anāgatabhayaṃ sampassamānā saṅgamma samāgamma evaṃ samassāsesuṃ samassāseyyuṃ (ka.) – ‘‘mā bhavaṃ bhāyi mā bhavaṃ bhāyi, appeva nāmetaṃ māluvābījaṃ moro vā gileyya, mago vā khādeyya, davaḍāho vā ḍaheyya, vanakammikā vā uddhareyyuṃ, upacikā vā uṭṭhaheyyuṃ abījaṃ vā panassā’’ti. Yañcāhaṃ yaṃ vāhaṃ (ka.), svāhaṃ (syā. kaṃ.) māluvābījahetu dukkhā tibbā kharā kaṭukā vedanā vedayāmī’ti. Te kāmesu pātabyataṃ āpajjitvā kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjanti. Te tattha dukkhā tibbā kharā kaṭukā vedanā vedayanti. Te evamāhaṃsu – ‘idaṃ kho te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā kāmesu anāgatabhayaṃ sampassamānā kāmānaṃ pahānamāhaṃsu, kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapenti. Ime hi mayaṃ kāmahetu kāmanidānaṃ dukkhā tibbā kharā kaṭukā vedanā vedayāmā’ti. Idaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannasukhaṃ āyatiṃ dukkhavipākaṃ.
469.
What is the adoption of practices ( dhammasamādānaṃ) that are pleasant in the present but result in painful consequences in the future (āyatiṃ dukkhavipākaṃ)? There are, monks, some ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā) who speak and hold such views that "there is no fault in sensual pleasures (kāmesu doso)."

They indulge in sensual pleasures (kāmesu pātabyataṃ āpajjanti).

They attend to female wanderers (paribbājikāhi) who wear their hair bound in a topknot (moḷibaddhāhi).

They say thus: 'Why do these good ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā) speak of abandoning sensual pleasures (kāmānaṃ pahānamāhaṃsu), seeing a future fear (anāgatabhayaṃ sampassamānā) in sensual pleasures (kāmesu), and describe the full understanding (pariññaṃ paññapenti) of sensual pleasures (kāmānaṃ)? Pleasant is the touch (samphasso) of this female wanderer's (paribbājikāya) tender (taruṇāya), soft (mudukāya), downy (lomasāya) arm (bāhāya)!' They indulge in sensual pleasures (kāmesu pātabyataṃ āpajjanti).

Having indulged in sensual pleasures (kāmesu pātabyataṃ āpajjitvā), on the dissolution (bhedā) of the body (kāyassa), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), they reappear in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ upapajjanti).

There they feel painful (dukkha), racking (tibbā), piercing (kharā), bitter (kaṭukā) feelings (vedanā vedayanti).

They say thus: ‘It is by reason of sensual pleasures (kāmahetu), owing to sensual pleasures (kāmanidānaṃ) that we are now feeling painful (dukkha), racking (tibbā), piercing (kharā), bitter (kaṭukā) feelings (vedanā vedayāmā).’

Suppose, monks, that in the last month of the hot season (gimhānaṃ pacchime māse) a māluvā creeper (māluvāsipāṭikā) pod burst open (phaleyya).

Then a māluvā seed (māluvābījaṃ) would fall at the foot (mūle) of a sāla tree (sālamūle).

Then a deity (devatā) living (adhivatthā) in that sāla tree (tasmiṃ sāle) would become fearful (bhītā), perturbed (saṃviggā), and frightened (santāsaṃ āpajjeyya).

Then the friends and companions (mittāmaccā), kinsmen and relatives (ñātisālohitā) of the deity (devatāya) living (adhivatthāya) in that sāla tree (tasmiṃ sāle)—garden deities (ārāmadevatā), park deities (vanadevatā), tree deities (rukkadevatā), and deities living (adhivatthā) in medicinal herbs (osadhi), grass (tiṇa), and forest monarch trees (vanappatīsu)—would gather together (saṅgamma samāgamma) and reassure (samassāseyyuṃ) that deity thus: ‘Have no fear (mā bhavaṃ bhāyi), sir (bhavaṃ)! Perhaps a peacock (moro) will swallow (gileyya) that māluvā seed (māluvābījaṃ), or a wild animal (mago) will eat (khādeyya) it, or a forest fire (davaḍāho) will burn (ḍaheyya) it, or woodsmen (vanakammikā) will carry (uddhareyyuṃ) it off, or white ants (upacikā) will devour (uṭṭhaheyyuṃ) it, or it may not even be fertile (abījaṃ vā panassā)!’

But no peacock (moro) swallowed (gileyya) that māluvā seed (māluvābījaṃ), no wild animal (mago) ate (khādeyya) it, no forest fire (davaḍāho) burned (ḍaheyya) it, no woodsmen (vanakammikā) carried (uddhareyyuṃ) it off, no white ants (upacikā) devoured (uṭṭhaheyyuṃ) it, and it was in fact fertile (bījañca panassa taṃ). Then, being moistened by rain from a rain-bearing cloud (pāvussakena meghena abhippavuṭṭhaṃ), the seed in due course sprouted (sammadeva viruheyya).

And the māluvā creeper’s (māluvālatā) tender (taruṇā), soft (mudukā), downy (lomasā) tendril (vilambinī) wound itself around that sāla tree (sā taṃ sālaṃ upaniseveyya).

Then the deity (devatāya) living (adhivatthāya) in that sāla tree (tasmiṃ sāle) would think thus: ‘What future fear (anāgatabhayaṃ) did my friends and companions (mittāmaccā), kinsmen and relatives (ñātisālohitā)—garden deities (ārāmadevatā), park deities (vanadevatā), tree deities (rukkha devatā), and deities living (adhivatthā) in medicinal herbs (osadhi), grass (tiṇa), and forest monarch trees (vanappatīsu)—see in that māluvā seed (māluvābīje) when they gathered together (saṅgamma samāgamma) and reassured (samassāsesuṃ) me as they did? “Have no fear (mā bhavaṃ bhāyi), sir (bhavaṃ)! Perhaps a peacock (moro) will swallow (gileyya) that māluvā seed (māluvābījaṃ), or a wild animal (mago) will eat (khādeyya) it, or a forest fire (davaḍāho) will burn (ḍaheyya) it, or woodsmen (vanakammikā) will carry (uddhareyyuṃ) it off, or white ants (upacikā) will devour (uṭṭhaheyyuṃ) it, or it may not even be fertile (abījaṃ vā panassā)!” Pleasant is the touch (samphasso) of this māluvā creeper’s (māluvālatāya) tender (taruṇāya) soft (mudukāya) downy (lomasāya) tendril (vilambiniyā)!’

Then the creeper enfolded that sāla tree (sā taṃ sālaṃ anuparihareyya).

Having enfolded that sāla tree (sā taṃ sālaṃ anupariharitvā), it made a canopy (viṭabhiṃ kareyya) over it (upari).

Having made a canopy (viṭabhiṃ karitvā) over it (upari), it draped a curtain (oghanaṃ janeyya) all around it.

Having draped a curtain (oghanaṃ janetvā), it split (padāleyya) the main (mahantā mahantā) branches (khandhā) of that sāla tree (tassa sālassa).

Then the deity (devatāya) living (adhivatthāya) in that sāla tree (tasmiṃ sāle) would think thus: ‘What future fear (anāgatabhayaṃ) did my friends and companions (mittāmaccā), kinsmen and relatives (ñātisālohitā)—garden deities (ārāmadevatā), park deities (vanadevatā), tree deities (rukkha devatā), and deities living (adhivatthā) in medicinal herbs (osadhitiṇavanappatīsu)—see in that māluvā seed (māluvābīje) when they gathered together (saṅgamma samāgamma) and reassured (samassāsesuṃ) me as they did? “Have no fear (mā bhavaṃ bhāyi), sir (bhavaṃ)! Perhaps a peacock (moro) will swallow (gileyya) that māluvā seed (māluvābījaṃ), or a wild animal (mago) will eat (khādeyya) it, or a forest fire (davaḍāho) will burn (ḍaheyya) it, or woodsmen (vanakammikā) will carry (uddhareyyuṃ) it off, or white ants (upacikā) will devour (uṭṭhaheyyuṃ) it, or it may not even be fertile (abījaṃ vā panassā)!”

Because of that māluvā creeper seed, I am feeling painful (dukkha), racking (tibba), piercing (khara), and bitter (kaṭuka) sensations (vedanā)'.

In the same way, mendicants (bhikkhave), there are some ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā) who speak and hold the view that 'there is no fault in sensual pleasures (kāmesu)'.

They take to gulping down sensual pleasures (kāmesu).

They divert themselves with women wanderers (paribbājikāhi) who wear their hair bound in a topknot (moḷibaddhāhi).

They say thus: ‘What future fear (anāgatabhayaṃ) do these good ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā) see in sensual pleasures (kāmesu) when they speak of abandoning sensual pleasures (kāmānaṃ pahānaṃ) and describe the full understanding (pariññaṃ) of sensual pleasures (kāmānaṃ)? Pleasant is the touch of this young, tender, soft, hairy arm of the female wanderer (paribbājikāya)’.

They take to gulping down sensual pleasures (kāmesu).

Having taken to gulping down sensual pleasures (kāmesu), on the dissolution (bhedā) of the body (kāyassa), after death (maraṇā), they reappear in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ).

There they feel painful (dukkha), racking (tibba), piercing (khara), and bitter (kaṭuka) sensations (vedanā).

They say thus: ‘This is the future fear (anāgatabhayaṃ) those good ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā) saw in sensual pleasures (kāmesu) when they spoke of abandoning sensual pleasures (kāmānaṃ pahānaṃ) and described the full understanding (pariññaṃ) of sensual pleasures (kāmānaṃ).

For it is by reason of sensual pleasures (kāmahetu), owing to sensual pleasures (kāmanidānaṃ), that we are now feeling painful (dukkha), racking (tibba), piercing (khara), and bitter (kaṭuka) sensations (vedanā)’.

This, mendicants (bhikkhave), is called the way of undertaking things (dhammasamādānaṃ) that is pleasant (sukhaṃ) now (paccuppannaṃ) and ripens in the future (āyatiṃ) as pain (dukkhavipākaṃ).

Moḷibaddhāhīti moḷiṃ katvā baddhakesāhi. Paribbājikāhīti tāpasaparibbājikāhi. Evamāhaṃsūti evaṃ vadanti. Pariññaṃ paññapentīti pahānaṃ samatikkamaṃ paññapenti. Māluvāsipāṭikāti dīghasaṇṭhānaṃ māluvāpakkaṃ. Phaleyyāti ātapena sussitvā bhijjeyya. Sālamūleti sālarukkhassa samīpe. Santāsaṃ āpajjeyyāti kasmā āpajjati? Bhavanavināsabhayā. Rukkhamūle patitamāluvābījato hi latā uppajjitvā rukkhaṃ abhiruhati. Sā mahāpattā ceva hoti bahupattā ca, koviḷārapattasadisehi pattehi samannāgatā. Atha taṃ rukkhaṃ mūlato paṭṭhāya vinandhamānā sabbaviṭapāni sañchādetvā mahantaṃ bhāraṃ janetvā tiṭṭhati. Sā vāte vā vāyante deve vā vassante oghanaṃ janetvā tassa rukkhassa sabbasākhāpasākhaṃ bhañjati, bhūmiyaṃ nipāteti. Tato tasmiṃ rukkhe patiṭṭhitavimānaṃ bhijjati nassati. Iti sā bhavanavināsabhayā santāsaṃ āpajjati. Ārāmadevatāti tattha tattha pupphārāmaphalārāmesu adhivatthā devatā. Vanadevatāti andhavanasubhagavanādīsu vanesu adhivatthā devatā. Rukkhadevatāti abhilakkhitesu naḷerupucimandādīsu rukkhesu adhivatthā devatā. Osadhitiṇavanappatīsūti harītakīāmalakīādīsu osadhīsu tālanāḷikerādīsu tiṇesu vanajeṭṭhakesu ca vanappatirukkhesu adhivatthā devatā. Vanakammikāti vane kasanalāyanadāruāharaṇagorakkhādīsu kenacideva kammena vā vicarakamanussā. Uddhareyyunti khādeyyuṃ. Vilambinīti vātena pahatapahataṭṭhānesu keḷiṃ karontī viya vilambantī. Sukho imissāti evarūpāya māluvālatāya samphassopi sukho, dassanampi sukhaṃ. Ayaṃ me dārakānaṃ āpānamaṇḍalaṃ bhavissati, kīḷābhūmi bhavissati, dutiyaṃ me vimānaṃ paṭiladdhanti latāya dassanepi samphassepi somanassajātā evamāha. Padāleyyāti evaṃ oghanaṃ katvā puna tato paṭṭhāya yāva mūlā otiṇṇasākhāhi abhiruhamānā sabbasākhā paliveṭhentī matthakaṃ patvā teneva niyāmena puna orohitvā ca abhiruhitvā ca sakalarukkhaṃ saṃsibbitvā ajjhottharantī sabbasākhā heṭṭhā katvā sayaṃ upari ṭhatvā vāte vā vāyante deve vā vassante padāleyya. 470. And what, bhikkhus, is the way of undertaking things that is painful now and ripens in the future as pain? Here, bhikkhus, a certain person goes naked, rejecting conventions, licking his hands, not coming when asked, not stopping when asked; he does not accept food brought or food specially made or an invitation to a meal; he receives nothing from a pot, from a bowl, across a threshold, across a stick, across a pestle, from two eating together, from a pregnant woman, from a woman giving suck, from a woman lying with a man, from where food is advertised to be distributed, from where a dog is waiting, from where flies are swarming; he eats no fish or meat and drinks no rum or spirits or fermented rice-gruel.

He is a one-house man or a one-morsel man, a two-house man or a two-morsel man... he is a seven-house man or a seven-morsel man.

He lives on one saucerful a day, on two saucerfuls a day… on seven saucerfuls a day.

He takes food once a day, once every two days… once every seven days.

Thus, he dwells pursuing the practice of taking food at stated intervals, even up to once every fortnight.

He is an eater of greens or millet or wild rice or hide-parings or moss or rice-bran or rice-scum or sesamum flour or grass or cow-dung. He lives on forest roots and fruits, he feeds on fallen fruits.

He wears coarse hemp or mixed material, shrouds from corpses, rags from the dust-heap, garments of bark-fibre, antelope-skins, grass, bark, shavings, blankets of human hair or horse-hair, the wings of owls. He is a plucker-out of hair and beard, devoted to this practice; he is one who stands continuously, rejecting seats. He is one who squats continuously, devoted to maintaining the squatting position. He is one who uses a mattress of spikes; he makes a mattress of spikes his bed. He dwells pursuing the practice of bathing in water three times daily, including the evening.

Thus, in such a variety of ways, he dwells pursuing the practice of tormenting and mortifying the body.

On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, perdition, even in hell.

This is called the way of undertaking things that is painful now and ripens in the future as pain.

471. Tibbarāgajātiko means one whose nature is abundant in passion ( bahalarāgasabhāvo).

Rāgajaṃ dukkhaṃ domanassaṃ paṭisaṃvedeti means due to being by nature strongly passionate (tibbarāgajātikattā), he seizes upon the appearance (nimittaṃ gaṇhāti) in each and every object (diṭṭhe diṭṭhe ārammaṇe).

Then his teachers and preceptors (ācariyupajjhāyā) impose a penalty (daṇḍakammaṃ āṇāpenti).

He, constantly engaging in the penalty (abhikkhaṇaṃ daṇḍakammaṃ karonto), experiences suffering (dukkhaṃ), grief (domanassaṃ paṭisaṃvedeti), but does not transgress (natveva vītikkamaṃ karoti).

Tibbadosajātiko means he becomes angry with even a small matter (appamattikeneva kuppati), and he speaks while engaging in such things as touching hands with young novices (daharasāmaṇerehi saddhiṃ hatthaparāmāsādīni karontova katheti).

He also experiences suffering and grief due to the penalty (sopi daṇḍakammapaccayā dukkhaṃ domanassaṃ paṭisaṃvedeti).

Furthermore, one who is by nature deluded (mohajātiko pana idha) does not consider what is done (kataṃ) or what is not done (akataṃ), or the cause of what is done or not done (katato akatato na sallakkheti), and he spoils those very tasks (tāni tāni kiccāni virādheti).

He also experiences suffering and grief due to the penalty (sopi daṇḍakammapaccayā dukkhaṃ domanassaṃ paṭisaṃvedeti).

472. Bhikkhus, what is the way of undertaking things that is pleasant at present and ripens as pleasure in the future? Here, bhikkhus, someone by nature does not have a strong inclination to lust ( rāga), and so he does not constantly experience pain (duḥkha) and grief (daurmanasya) born of lust. By nature, he does not have a strong inclination to hate ( dveṣa), and so he does not constantly experience pain and grief born of hate. By nature, he does not have a strong inclination to delusion (moha), and so he does not constantly experience pain and grief born of delusion.

Quite secluded from sensuality (kāma), secluded from unskillful qualities (akuśala dharma), he dwells, having attained the first jhāna: rapture (prīti) and pleasure (sukha) born of seclusion, accompanied by directed thought (vitarka) and evaluation (vicāra).

With the stilling of applied and sustained thought, with internal assurance, unification of awareness, free from directed thought & evaluation, he enters and remains in the second jhāna: rapture and pleasure born of concentration (samādhija). …the third jhāna…the fourth jhāna.

At the dissolution of the body (kāya) after death (maraṇa), he reappears in a happy destination (sugati), even in the heavenly world (svarga loka).

This, bhikkhus, is called the way of undertaking things that is pleasant now and ripens in the future as pleasure.

These, bhikkhus, are the four ways of undertaking things.”

472. The na tibbarāgajātiko etc. should be understood by way of the stated counter-positions.

Why is it that here, one is of the nature of intense attachment, etc., and one is not of the nature of intense attachment, etc.? Because of the law of karma (kammaniyāmena).

For when, at the moment of accumulating kamma, greed (lobha) is strong and non-greed (alobha) is weak, non-hate (adosa) and non-delusion (amoha) are strong, and hate (dosa) and delusion (moha) are weak, then his weak non-greed (alobha) is unable to prevail over his greed (lobha), [but] non-hate (adosa) and non-delusion (amoha) are strong [and] are able to prevail over hate (dosa) and delusion (moha).

That is why, on being reborn through rebirth-linking given by that kamma, he has greed (luddho), is good-natured (sukhasīlo), unangry (akkodhano), and possesses understanding with knowledge like a lightning flash (paññavā vajirūpamañāṇo).

When, at the moment of another’s accumulating kamma, greed (lobha) and hate (dosa) are strong and non-greed (alobha) and non-hate (adosa) weak, and non-delusion (amoha) is strong and delusion (moha) weak, then in the way already stated he has both greed (luddho) and hate (duṭṭho), but he possesses understanding with knowledge like a lightning flash (paññavā vajirūpamañāṇo), like the Elder Dattābhaya.

“When, at the moment of his accumulating kamma, greed (lobhā), non-hate (adosa), and delusion (moha) are strong and the others are weak, then in the way already stated he both has greed (luddho) and is dull (dandha) but is good-tempered (sukhasīlako) and unangry (akkodhano).

Likewise when, at the moment of his accumulating kamma, the three, namely, greed (lobha), hate (dosa), and delusion (moha) are strong and non-greed (alobhā), etc., are weak, then in the way already stated he has both greed (luddho) and hate (duṭṭho) and is deluded (mūḷho).

When, at the moment of his accumulating kamma, non-greed (alobha), hate (dosa), and delusion (moha) are strong and the others are weak, then in the way already stated he has little defilement (appakileso) and is unshakable (niccalo) even on seeing a heavenly object (dibbārammaṇampi disvā), but he has hate (duṭṭho) and is slow in understanding (dandhapañño).

When, at the moment of his accumulating kamma, non-greed (alobhā), non-hate (adosa), and delusion (moha) are strong and the rest are weak, then in the way already stated he has no greed (aluddho) and is good-tempered (sukhasīlako), but is deluded (mūḷho).

Likewise when, at the moment of his accumulating kamma, non-greed (alobha), hate (dosa), and non-delusion (amohā) are strong and the rest are weak, then in the way already stated he has no greed (aluddho) and possesses understanding (paññavā), but has hate (duṭṭho) and is irascible (kodhano).

When, at the moment of his accumulating kamma, the three, that is, non-greed (alobhā), etc., are strong and greed (lobhā), etc., are weak, then in the way already stated he has no greed (aluddho), no hate (aduṭṭho), and possesses understanding (paññavā), like the Elder Mahā-Saṅgharakkhita.

The rest is easy to understand everywhere.
