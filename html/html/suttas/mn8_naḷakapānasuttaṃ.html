166. Thus have I heard: the Naḷakapāna Sutta.

There, Naḷakapāna is the name of a village.

Long ago, it seems, our bodhisattva was born in a monkey's womb. A great-bodied monkey king, surrounded by many thousands of monkeys, wandered at the foot of a mountain.

He was wise, moreover, and of great merit.

He advised the assembly thus: “At this mountain foot, fathers, there are fruits that are poison, and pools that are haunted by non-humans. You should eat only those fruits that you have eaten before, and drink only those waters that you have drunk before. Here, you should not have any need to ask me about what to do. But, without asking me, do not eat those fruits that you have not eaten before, and do not drink those waters that you have not drunk before.”

Then, one day, while wandering, they went to another mountain foot. After fetching their food, while looking for water, they saw a pool haunted by non-humans. Without drinking any of it suddenly, they surrounded it on all sides, watching for the great being's arrival.

The great being arrived and said, “Why do you not drink the water, fathers?”

“We are watching for your arrival.”

“Good, fathers!” Searching all around for a footprint, he saw only a footprint leading down, not a footprint leading up. Having seen this, he understood, “It is dangerous!”

At that very moment, a non-human that had arisen there, having split the water in two, arose. He had a white face, a blue belly, red hands and feet, great fangs, crooked teeth, a deformed and repulsive appearance, an aquatic demon.

He said thus: “Why do you not drink the water? Drink the sweet water! Why do you listen to this fellow's word?” The great being said, “You are a non-human dwelling here?” “Yes.”

“Are you able to catch those who have gone down into it here?” “Yes, I am able. But I will eat all of you!”

“You will not be able to, yakṣa!”

“Then will you drink the water?” “Yes, we will drink!”

“If that is so, not even one of you will escape!”

“We will drink the water, and we will not come under your control!” Having had a single reed brought, having taken it at the tip, he blew on it. It all became one-holed. Sitting on the bank, he drank the water. Having had individual reeds brought for the remaining monkeys, having blown on them, he gave them out.

All drank the water while the yakṣa looked on.

This was also said:

“Having seen the un-ascended footprint, having seen the un-crossed footprint;

We will drink the water with a reed, and you will never kill me.”

(Jā. 1.1.20);

From then on, up to this present day, in that place the reeds are one-holed.

Along with this, in this kalpa there are four marvels that are fixed for the kalpa: the hare-image on the moon, the uninterrupted journey of fire in the Vaṭṭaka Jātaka at the place of the act of truth, the raining down of the god at the dwelling place of the potter Ghaṭikāra's parents, and the one-holed-ness of the reeds on the bank of that pool.

Thus, that pool, because the water was drunk with a reed, obtained the name Naḷakapāna.

In another part, relying on that pool, a village was established. That too obtained the name Naḷakapananteva.

With reference to that, it was said, “Naḷakapāna”.

Palāsa Forest is the Kiṃsuka Forest.

Four wonders fixed for the kalpa, Naḷakapāna, Naḷakapananteva, Palāsa Forest.

“Not brought near by the king,” etc.—one commits an offense against the king and flees.

“Where is the king, sir, so-and-so?” “He has fled, good sir.”

But even one among these is not one gone forth by these causes; therefore, he said, “neva rājābhinīto (not brought near by the king),” etc.

bhayaṭṭo ājīvikāpakato (afflicted by fear, caused by livelihood)—viveka (discrimination) means having discriminated, having become solitary.

This is said—the pītisukhaṃ (joy and happiness) counted as the first and second dhyānas which should be attained by one secluded from desires and unwholesome dharmas; if, having discriminated, he does not attain that pītisukhaṃ (joy and happiness) by desires, having discriminated from unwholesome dharmas, or he does not attain another, more peaceful sukhaṃ (happiness), higher by way of the two dhyānas and the four paths, these covetousnesses, etc., stand pervading his mind.

There, aratī (dissatisfaction) means being disgusted with superior wholesome dharmas.
