462. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tena kho pana samayena subho māṇavo todeyyaputto sāvatthiyaṃ paṭivasati aññatarassa gahapatissa nivesane kenacideva karaṇīyena. Atha kho subho māṇavo todeyyaputto yassa gahapatissa nivesane paṭivasati taṃ gahapatiṃ etadavoca – ‘‘sutaṃ metaṃ, gahapati – ‘avivittā sāvatthī arahantehī’ti. Kaṃ nu khvajja samaṇaṃ vā brāhmaṇaṃ vā payirupāseyyāmā’’ti? ‘‘Ayaṃ, bhante, bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Taṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ payirupāsassū’’ti. Atha kho subho māṇavo todeyyaputto tassa gahapatissa paṭissutvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho subho māṇavo todeyyaputto bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, evamāhaṃsu – ‘gahaṭṭho ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ, na pabbajito ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusala’nti. Idha bhavaṃ gotamo kimāhā’’ti?
Thus have I heard:

Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park (jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

At that time, Subha, the student of Todeyya, was residing at the house of a certain householder in Sāvatthī for some particular reason.

Then Subha, the student of Todeyya, said to that householder in whose residence he was staying: “Householder, I have heard it said that Sāvatthī is not devoid of arhats (arahantehī).”

“Which śramaṇa (samaṇaṃ) or brāhmaṇa (brāhmaṇaṃ) should we visit today?”

“Venerable sir (bhante), the Blessed One (bhagavā) is staying in Sāvatthī, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park.

Venerable sir (bhante), you should visit that Blessed One (bhagavantam).”

Then Subha, the student of Todeyya, assented to that householder and went to where the Blessed One (bhagavā) was; having gone there, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One (bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi).

Having concluded their conversation of courteous greetings, he sat down at one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdi).

Seated to one side (ekamantaṃ nisinno), Subha, the student of Todeyya, said this to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ etadavoca): “Brahmins (brāhmaṇā), Master Gotama (bho gotama), say thus: ‘The householder (gahaṭṭho) is one who accomplishes the true way (ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ), the dharma, that is wholesome; the renunciate (pabbajito) is not one who accomplishes the true way, the dharma, that is wholesome.’”

“What does Master Gotama (bhavaṃ gotamo kimāha) say about this?”
462.Evaṃme sutanti subhasuttaṃ. Tattha todeyyaputtoti tudigāmavāsino todeyyabrāhmaṇassa putto. Ārādhako hotīti sampādako hoti paripūrako. Ñāyaṃ dhammanti kāraṇadhammaṃ. Kusalanti anavajjaṃ.
Thus have I heard: the Subha-sutta. There, todeyyaputto means the son of the Todeyya brahmin who lived in Tudigama. ārādhako hoti means he is the accomplisher, he is the perfecter. ñāyaṃ dhamma means the causal Dharma. kusala means faultless.

463. micchāpaṭipatti means unwholesome practice that does not lead out [of suffering]. sammāpaṭipatti means wholesome practice that leads out [of suffering].

In mahaṭṭha etc., because there is much benefit/meaning (attha) here through great assistants (veyyāvaccakara) or implements (upakaraṇa), [it is called] mahaṭṭhaṃ.

Because there are great undertakings (kicca) here, such as naming ceremonies (nāmaggahaṇamaṅgala) etc., [it is called] mahākiccaṃ.

Because there are great authoritative decisions (adhikaraṇa) here, consisting of "This should be done today, this tomorrow," [it is called] mahādhikaraṇaṃ.

Because there is a great undertaking (mahāsamārambho) consisting of oppression (pīḷā) due to the state of being connected and unconnected (yuttappayuttatāvasena) to many actions (kamme), [it is called] mahāsamārambhaṃ.

gharāvāsakammaṭṭhāna means household occupation (gharāvāsakammaṃ).

Thus, the meaning (attha) should be understood in all instances (sabbavāresu).

Here, in agriculture (kasikamme), mahaṭṭhatā [is] due to the search (pariyesana) for implements (upakaraṇa) with the ploughshare (naṅgalakoṭiṃ) as the beginning (ādiṃ katvā); in trade (vaṇijjāya), appaṭṭhatā [is] due to the exchange (parivattanavasena) after taking goods (bhaṇḍaṃ) just as they are placed (yathāṭhitaṃyeva). This should be understood (veditabbā).

vipajjamāna means that agriculture (kasikammaṃ) [suffers] from lack of rain, excessive rain, etc. (avuṭṭhiativuṭṭhiādīhi), and trade (vaṇijjakammaṃ) [suffers] from dishonesty, etc. (acchekatādīhi) in jewels, gold, etc. (maṇisuvaṇṇādīsu); [thus] there is little fruit (appaphalaṃ hoti), and even the root is destroyed (mūlacchedampi pāpuṇāti).

Conversely (vipariyāyena), flourishing (sampajjamānaṃ) yields great fruit (mahapphalaṃ), like a junior resident disciple (cūḷantevāsikassa viya).

mahaṭṭha, mahaṭṭhaṃ, mahākiccaṃ, mahādhikaraṇaṃ, mahāsamārambhaṃ.

Sopi nūnassa tādisova yo samaṇassa gotamassa paññāveyyattiyaṃ jāneyyā’’ti. ‘‘Uḷārāya khalu, bhavaṃ bhāradvājo, samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ pasaṃsāya pasaṃsatī’’ti. ‘‘Ko cāhaṃ, bho, ko ca samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ pasaṃsissāmi? Pasatthapasatthova so bhavaṃ gotamo seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ. Ye cime, bho, brāhmaṇā pañca dhamme paññapenti puññassa kiriyāya kusalassa ārādhanāya; cittassete samaṇo gotamo parikkhāre vadeti – yadidaṃ cittaṃ averaṃ abyābajjhaṃ tassa bhāvanāyā’’ti. vaḷabhīrathena (sī.) Evaṃ vutte, jāṇussoṇi brāhmaṇo sabbasetā vaḷavābhirathā orohitvā ekaṃsaṃ uttarāsaṅgaṃ karitvā yena bhagavā tenañjaliṃ paṇāmetvā udānaṃ udānesi – ‘‘lābhā rañño pasenadissa kosalassa, suladdhalābhā rañño pasenadissa kosalassa yassa vijite tathāgato viharati arahaṃ sammāsambuddho’’ti. Subhasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ navamaṃ.

When this was said, Subha, the brahmin student, Todeyya’s son, said this to the Blessed One: “Magnificent, Master Gotama! Magnificent, Master Gotama! Just as, Master Gotama, one might set upright what had been overturned, or reveal what was hidden, or point out the path to one who was lost, or hold up an oil lamp in the dark so that those with eyes might see forms, in the same way, the Dhamma has been made clear in many ways by Master Gotama.
I go to the revered Gotama for refuge, and to the Dhamma, and to the bhikkhu-saṃgha (community of monks).
May the revered Gotama remember me as a lay follower (upāsaka) who has gone to him for refuge for life.
And now, Master Gotama, we must go; we have much to do, many responsibilities."
"Now is the time, student (māṇava), to do as you think fit."
Then Subha, the brahmin student, Todeyya’s son, having delighted and rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words, rose from his seat, and after paying homage to the Blessed One, keeping him on his right, he departed.
At that time, the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi was going out from Sāvatthi in an all-white chariot drawn by mares, during the day.
The brahmin Jāṇussoṇi saw Subha, the brahmin student, Todeyya’s son, coming from afar.
Seeing Subha, the brahmin student, Todeyya’s son, he said this: “Well, from where is Master Bhāradvāja coming during the day?” “I am coming from the presence of the ascetic Gotama.”
“What do you think, Master Bhāradvāja, is the ascetic Gotama wise in his lucidity of wisdom?” “Who am I, sir, who am I to know the ascetic Gotama’s lucidity of wisdom? Surely, one would have to be his equal to know the ascetic Gotama’s lucidity of wisdom.”
“Master Bhāradvāja praises the ascetic Gotama with high praise indeed!”
“Who am I, sir, who am I to praise the ascetic Gotama? The revered Gotama is praised by those who are praised; he is the best of gods and humans.
Those five things that these brahmins prescribe for the performance of merit, for accomplishing the wholesome, the ascetic Gotama calls equipment of the mind, that is, for developing a mind that is without hostility and without ill will."

When this was said, the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi got down from his all-white chariot drawn by mares, and after arranging his upper robe on one shoulder, he extended his hands in reverential salutation towards the Blessed One and uttered this exclamation: “It is a gain for King Pasenadi of Kosala, it is a great gain for King Pasenadi of Kosala that the Tathāgata, the worthy one, the perfectly enlightened one, lives in his realm.”
The Subha Sutta, the ninth, is finished.
