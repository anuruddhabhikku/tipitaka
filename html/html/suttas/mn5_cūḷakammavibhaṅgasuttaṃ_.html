289. Thus have I heard: the Cūḷakammavibhaṅgasuttaṃ.

There, Subha was pleasing and delightful to see. Thus, because of his bodily beauty, they made the name Subha.

Māṇava is what they called him in his youth. So even in old age, he was called by that same term.

Todeyyaputta means the son of the brahmin Todeyya, who was the chaplain of King Pasenadi.

It is said that near Sāvatthi, there is a village named Tudigāma. Because he was the chief of that, he went by the designation Todeyya.

He was very rich, possessing eighty-seven koṭis of wealth. [But he was] extremely miserly, thinking, “There is no such thing as the exhaustion of possessions by giving,” he gave nothing to anyone.

It was also said:

“Having seen the destruction of ointment, and the accumulation of anthills;

And the collection of honey, the wise man should dwell at home.”

Thus, he only taught not giving.

While the Perfectly Awakened Buddha was dwelling at Dhuravihāra, [Todeyya], without giving even the amount of rice gruel in a small bowl or the amount of food in a ladle, because of greed for wealth, having made time, was reborn as a dog in that very house.

Subha was extremely fond of that dog, fed him only on the food that he himself ate, and lifting him up, had him lie on an excellent bed.

Then, one day, the Blessed One, surveying the world at dawn, saw that dog and [thought]: “Todeyya the brahmin, because of greed for wealth, having been reborn as a dog in his own house, today, when I have gone to Subha’s house, the dog will bark at me upon seeing me. Then I will speak one word to him, and he, thinking, ‘The renunciant Gotama knows me,’ will lie down in the charnel ground.

“From that cause, there will be a conversation between Subha and me, and having heard the Dharma, he will be established in the refuges. But the dog, having made time, will be reborn in hell.” Knowing this state of being established in the refuges for the youth, the Blessed One, having attended to his bodily needs that day, and having entered the village alone, when the youth had left, entered that house for alms.

The dog, seeing the Blessed One, barking, went near the Blessed One.

Then the Blessed One said this to him: “Todeyya, you were born as a dog having insulted me in the past with ‘bho bhoti’. Now, even now, having barked, you will go to Avīci.”

The dog, hearing that, [thinking], “The renunciant Gotama knows me,” becoming remorseful, having bent his neck, lay down in the charnel ground.

The people were not able to lift him up and make him lie on the bed.

Subha, coming, said, “By whom was this dog taken down from the bed?”

The people, saying, “By no one,” announced that occurrence.

The youth, hearing [this], [said]: “My father was reborn in the Brahma-world. There is no dog named Todeyya.

The renunciant Gotama makes my father into a dog! Whatever this one speaks is just hot air!” Angered, desiring to refute the Blessed One with a lie, he went to the monastery and asked about that occurrence.

The Blessed One also said the same to him and, for the sake of non-deception, said, “Is there, youth, wealth not declared by your father?”

“There is, Gotama. A golden garland worth one hundred thousand, golden slippers worth one hundred thousand, a golden dish worth one hundred thousand, and one hundred thousand kahāpaṇas.”

“Go, feed that dog rice gruel with little water, and having lifted him up and placed him on the bed, ask him when he has fallen slightly asleep. He will tell you everything.

Then you will know, ‘This is my father.’”

The youth [thought], “If it will be true, I will get wealth, [but] if not, I will refute the renunciant Gotama with a lie.” Delighted by both causes, he went and did thus.

The dog, [thinking], “I am known by this one,” having cried and making huṃ huṃ sounds, having gone to the place where the wealth was hidden, dug the earth with his foot and gave a sign. The youth, having taken the wealth, [thought], “The Blessed One knows even such a subtle rebirth interval that is covered by existence. Truly, this one is all-knowing.” With his mind pleased with the Blessed One, he arranged fourteen questions.

It is said that he was a teacher of aṅgavijjā. Therefore, this occurred to him: “Having taken this Dharma-gift, I will ask the renunciant Gotama questions.” With a second coming, he approached the Blessed One, by whom the questions had been asked. Then the Blessed One, dismissing the questions with a single strike, spoke the kammassakā-sayings and so on.

Kammassakā There, kammam is their own possession, [their] own belonging; therefore, they are kammassakā.
Kammadāyādā: because they are the heirs of actions, meaning karma is their inheritance.
Kammayonī: because karma is the womb and cause for them.
Kammabandhū: because karma is their relative; meaning, karma is their kin.
Kammapaṭisaraṇā: because karma is their refuge and support.

Hīnappaṇītatāyā: That which is "you are inferior, you are superior, you are short-lived, you are long-lived…pe… you are unwise, you are wise," thus, the distinction due to inferiority and superiority is not made by anyone else; karma alone thus distinguishes beings—this is the meaning.

The youth does not understand the meaning of what was said; it was as if his face was bound with a thick cloth, and sweetness was placed before him.

That conceited one, relying on pride, thinking himself a wise man, does not consider himself equal to others.

Then, thinking, “What does the renunciate Gotama say? What I know, that alone he says—let this pride not arise!”, the Blessed One, in order to break his pride, said, “From the beginning, I will make it difficult to understand, then I will speak. Then he will ask me, ‘Venerable Gotama, I do not know. Please explain it to me in detail.’ Then I will speak at the time of his request, and thus it will be meaningful for him," making it difficult to understand, he spoke.

Kammassakā, kammadāyādā, kammayonī, kammabandhū, kammapaṭisaraṇā.

Hīnappaṇītatāyā: Now, revealing his incomprehension, he said, "Na kho aha," and so on.

“Here, student, some woman or man is a killer of living beings, cruel, bloody-handed, devoted to violence and killing, and without compassion for living beings. “But here, student, some woman or man, abandoning the killing of living beings, abstains from killing living beings; with rod and weapon laid aside ( nihitadaṇḍo nihitasattho), conscientious, full of kindness, sympathetic for the welfare of all living beings ( sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampī viharati). Because of that action, performed and undertaken in such a way, at the break-up of the body, after death, one is reborn in a happy destination, in the heavenly world (sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapajjati). But if at the break-up of the body, after death, one is not reborn in a happy destination, in the heavenly world, but instead comes back to the human state, then wherever he is reborn he is long-lived. This is the path, student, that leads to long life: abandoning the killing of living beings, abstaining from killing living beings, with rod and weapon laid aside, conscientious, full of kindness, sympathetic for the welfare of all living beings.

290. Samattenā: by that which is complete. Samādinnena: by that which is taken, touched. Appāyukasaṃvattanikā esā, māṇava, paṭipadā yadidaṃ pāṇātipātī: that which is this act of killing living beings, this is the practice that leads to a short life.

How does this cause a short life? There are four kinds of karma: oppressive (upapīḷakaṃ), obstructive (upacchedakaṃ), generative (janakaṃ), and supportive (upatthambhakaṃ).

The oppressive karma, produced by strong karma, comes into being and speaks thus: "If I had known earlier, I would not have allowed you to be born here; I would have caused you to be born in one of the four woeful realms. Even if you are born somewhere, I, the oppressive karma, will torment you, making you weak, without essence, and wretched." From then on, it makes him such. What does it do? It brings about distress, it destroys possessions.

There, from the time of the child's birth in the mother's womb, there is no satisfaction or happiness for the mother; suffering arises for the parents. Thus, it brings about distress. But from the time of the child's birth in the mother's womb, possessions in the house are destroyed like salt in water due to kings and other causes, the milk cows that are milked in pots do not give milk, strong bulls become fierce, they are blind, they are hunchbacked, disease falls upon the cattle pen, servants and others do not do what is said, the sown crops do not grow, what is in the house is destroyed in the house, what is in the forest is destroyed in the forest, gradually only enough for food and clothing is difficult to obtain, there is no care for the embryo, at the time of birth the mother's breast milk is cut off, the child, not obtaining care, is tormented, weak, without essence, and wretched. This is called oppressive karma (upapīḷakakammaṃ).

The obstructive karma, for one born of long-life karma, comes and cuts off life. Just as a man, after having sexual intercourse, might shoot an arrow, and another might strike it with a club as soon as it is released from the bow, causing it to fall right there, so the obstructive karma cuts off the life of one born of long-life karma. What does it do? It causes [him] to enter a thieves' forest, it lowers [him] into a place of dangerous fish and water, or it brings [him] to some other place of danger. This is called obstructive karma (upacchedakakammaṃ); it is also called "destructive" (upaghātakaṃ).

The karma that produces rebirth is called generative karma (janakakammaṃ). The supportive karma, through bringing about the wealth of possessions, etc., for one born in a family with few possessions, etc., is called supportive karma (upatthambhakakammaṃ).

Among these four, the first two are unwholesome only, the generative is wholesome and unwholesome, the supportive is wholesome only. There, the karma of killing living beings, by means of obstructive karma, leads to a short life. Or, a wholesome karma done by a killer of living beings is not great, it is not able to generate a long-life rebirth. Thus, killing living beings leads to a short life. Or, having determined the rebirth itself, it makes [him] short-lived, or by the intention of resolution [to kill], [he] is born in hell, [and] by the earlier and later intentions, he is short-lived in the manner stated.

Dīghāyukasaṃvattanikā esā māṇava paṭipadā: Here, even if one is born through small karma, this abstaining from killing living beings, when it comes into being, says thus: "If I had known earlier, I would not have allowed you to be born here; I would have caused you to be born in the heaven world. Even if you are born somewhere, I, the supportive karma, will make a pillar for you," and it makes support. What does it do? It destroys distress, it produces possessions.

There, from the time of the child's birth in the mother's womb, there is only happiness and satisfaction for the parents. And whatever natural dangers from humans and non-humans there are, all of them disappear. Thus, it destroys distress. But from the time of the child's birth in the mother's womb, there is no limit to the possessions in the house, treasure pots rush into the house from front and back.
Parents also face the consequences of wealth left behind by others.

Cows are full of milk, bulls are easy to train, and crops flourish in the fields.

Either wealth associated with growth, or wealth given temporarily, they bring back unasked, slaves and the like are obedient, and businesses do not decline.

The child, from the womb onwards, receives care, and female doctors are constantly present.

One born in a householder's family obtains the position of a treasurer, one born in a minister's family obtains the position of a general, and so on.

Thus, he produces enjoyment.

That one, without danger and with enjoyment, lives long.

Thus, the action of abstaining from killing leads to a long life.

Furthermore, good actions done by one who abstains from killing are excellent, he is able to generate rebirth leading to a long life, and thus it leads to a long life.

Or, having determined the rebirth itself, he makes [it] a long life.

Or, through the intention of determination, he is reborn in the world of gods; through former and later intentions, in the manner described, he has a long life.

In this way, the meaning should be understood in all answers to questions.

Furthermore, actions of harming and so on, coming into being, and speaking according to the meaning, as if oppressing, causing lack of enjoyment, and not obtaining maintenance, or through the arising of diseases and so on, or through the unexcelled nature of the good done by those who harm and so on, or from the beginning through the determination of rebirth, or in the manner described, through the force of former and later intentions, they create many sicknesses and so on; just as abstaining from killing, so also abstaining from harming and so on [leads to] few sicknesses and so on.

295. “Here, student, some man or woman is obstinate and arrogant; he does not pay homage to one who should receive homage, does not rise up for one in whose presence he should rise up, does not offer a seat to one who deserves a seat, does not make way for one for whom he should make way, and does not honour, respect, revere, and venerate one who should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

Because of performing and undertaking such action, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, in perdition, even in hell.

But if instead he comes back to the human state, then wherever he is reborn he is low-born.

This is the way, student, that leads to low birth, namely, one is obstinate and arrogant… and does not honour, respect, revere, and venerate one who should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

“But here, student, some man or woman is not obstinate and arrogant; he pays homage to one who should receive homage, rises up for one in whose presence he should rise up, offers a seat to one who deserves a seat, makes way for one for whom he should make way, and honours, respects, reveres, and venerates one who should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

Because of performing and undertaking such action, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a happy destination, even in the heavenly world.

But if instead he comes back to the human state, then wherever he is reborn he is high-born.

This is the way, student, that leads to high birth, namely, one is not obstinate and arrogant…and honours, respects, reveres, and venerates one who should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.
295.Abhivādetabbanti abhivādanārahaṃ buddhaṃ vā paccekabuddhaṃ vā ariyasāvakaṃ vā. Paccuṭṭhātabbādīsupi eseva nayo. Imasmiṃ pana pañhavissajjane upapīḷakaupatthambhakakammāni na gahetabbāni. Na hi pavatte nīcakulinaṃ vā uccākulinaṃ vā sakkā kātuṃ, paṭisandhimeva pana niyāmetvā nīcakuliyaṃ kammaṃ nīcakule nibbatteti, uccākuliyaṃ kammaṃ uccākule.
" Abhivādetabba means one worthy of salutation, such as a Buddha, a Pratyekabuddha, or a noble disciple ( ariyasāvaka). The same method applies to paccuṭṭhātabbā and the like. In this question and answer, however, one should not take actions that are oppressive or supportive. For it is not possible to make someone of a low family or a high family once the process [of rebirth] has started; however, having determined rebirth, an action related to a low family produces [rebirth] in a low family, and an action related to a high family [produces rebirth] in a high family."

296. "One does not ask questions" - by not asking questions, one is not reborn in hell.

However, one who does not ask questions does not know what should be done and what should not be done; not knowing, one does not do what should be done, and does what should not be done.

By that, one is reborn in hell, the other in heaven.

"Thus, māṇava…pe… this is inferior and refined" - the teacher made the teaching reach in accordance with the connection.

“Thus, student, the practice that leads to short life brings about short life, the practice that leads to long life brings about long life; the practice that leads to much sickness brings about much sickness, the practice that leads to little sickness brings about little sickness; the practice that leads to ugliness brings about ugliness, the practice that leads to attractiveness brings about attractiveness; the practice that leads to little influence brings about little influence, the practice that leads to great influence brings about great influence; the practice that leads to little wealth brings about little wealth, the practice that leads to great wealth brings about great wealth; the practice that leads to low birth brings about low birth, the practice that leads to high birth brings about high birth; the practice that leads to little wisdom brings about little wisdom, the practice that leads to great wisdom brings about great wisdom.

"Beings are the owners of their karma (kammaṃ), student, the heirs of their karma (kamma), they originate from karma (kamma), are bound to karma (kamma), have karma (kamma) as their refuge.

It is karma (kammaṃ) that distinguishes beings as inferior (hīnappaṇītatā) and superior."
