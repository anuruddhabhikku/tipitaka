223. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe. Atha kho āyasmā bhūmijo pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena jayasenassa rājakumārassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Atha kho jayaseno rājakumāro yenāyasmā bhūmijo tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā bhūmijena saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho jayaseno rājakumāro āyasmantaṃ bhūmijaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘santi, bho bhūmija, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘āsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā carati, abhabbo (sī. pī.) evamuparipi ekavacaneneva dissati phalassa adhigamāya; anāsañcepi āsañca anāsañca cepi (aṭṭha.) karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāyā’ti. Idha bhoto bhūmijassa satthā kiṃvādī kiṃvādī kiṃdiṭṭhī (syā. kaṃ. ka.) kimakkhāyī’’ti? ‘‘Na kho metaṃ, rājakumāra, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ. Ṭhānañca kho etaṃ vijjati yaṃ bhagavā evaṃ byākareyya – ‘āsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya ; anāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya. Āsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāya ; anāsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāyā’ti. Na kho me taṃ, rājakumāra, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ. Ṭhānañca kho etaṃ vijjati yaṃ bhagavā evaṃ byākareyyā’’ti. ‘‘Sace kho bhoto bhūmijassa satthā evaṃvādī evaṃvādī evaṃdiṭṭhī (syā. kaṃ. ka.) evamakkhāyī, addhā bhoto bhūmijassa satthā sabbesaṃyeva puthusamaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ muddhānaṃ buddhānaṃ (ka.) muddhānantimuddhaṃ, matthakanti attho maññe āhacca tiṭṭhatī’’ti . Atha kho jayaseno rājakumāro āyasmantaṃ bhūmijaṃ sakeneva thālipākena parivisi.
223. Thus I have heard.

On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary ( veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe ).

Then, the venerable Bhumija ( āyasmā bhūmijo ), when it was morning, dressed, and taking his bowl and outer robe, went to the house of Prince Jayasena ( jayasenassa rājakumārassa nivesanaṃ ) and sat down on a seat made ready.

Then Prince Jayasena ( jayaseno rājakumāro ) went to the venerable Bhumija ( āyasmā bhūmijo ) and exchanged greetings with him.

When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

Having sat down to one side, Prince Jayasena ( jayaseno rājakumāro ) said to the venerable Bhumija ( āyasmantaṃ bhūmijaṃ ): “Master Bhumija ( bho bhūmija ), there are some recluses and brāhmaṇas who make such assertions and hold such views as this: ‘If one makes an aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is unable to procure any fruit ( phalassa adhigamāya ); if one makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still unable to procure any fruit ( phalassa adhigamāya ); if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still unable to procure any fruit ( phalassa adhigamāya ); if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still unable to procure any fruit."

"Here, what does the teacher (satthā) of Master Bhumija ( bhoto bhūmijassa ) proclaim, what does he make known?"

"I have not heard and learned that from the Blessed One’s ( bhagavato ) own lips, prince (rājakumāra ).

But it is possible that the Blessed One might say this: ‘If one makes an aspiration and one leads the holy life unwisely (ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is unable to procure any fruit ( phalassa adhigamāya ); if one makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life unwisely (ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still unable to procure any fruit ( phalassa adhigamāya ); if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life unwisely (ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still unable to procure any fruit ( phalassa adhigamāya ); if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life unwisely (ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still unable to procure any fruit.

However, if one makes an aspiration and one leads the holy life wisely (yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is able to procure fruit ( phalassa adhigamāya ); if one makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life wisely (yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still able to procure fruit (phalassa adhigamāya ); if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life wisely (yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still able to procure fruit (phalassa adhigamāya ); if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life wisely (yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti), one is still able to procure fruit.’

I have not heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s ( bhagavato ) own lips, prince (rājakumāra ).

But it is possible that the Blessed One would answer as I have stated.”

“If the teacher (satthā) of Master Bhumija ( bhoto bhūmijassa ) proclaimed that, then indeed the teacher (satthā) of Master Bhumija ( bhoto bhūmijassa ), I think, stands striking the heads of all the various recluses and brāhmaṇas.”

Then Prince Jayasena ( jayaseno rājakumāro ) served the venerable Bhumija ( āyasmantaṃ bhūmijaṃ ) from his own dish of milk rice (thālipākena parivisi).
223.Evaṃme sutanti bhūmijasuttaṃ. Tattha bhūmijoti ayaṃ thero jayasenarājakumārassa mātulo. Āsañca anāsañcāti kālena āsaṃ kālena anāsaṃ. Sakenathālipākenāti pakatipavattāya bhikkhāya attano niṭṭhitabhattatopi bhattena parivisi. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Bhūmijasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
223. Thus, me suta is the Bhūmijasutta.

There, bhūmijo is the maternal uncle of Jayasenarājakumāra.

Āsañca anāsañcā means I approached at the right time, I did not approach at the wrong time.

Sakena thālipākenā means he served the alms-food to the bhikkhu according to custom, even from his own finished meal.

The rest is clear throughout.

This is the commentary to the Majjhimanikāya by Papañcasūdanī.

The Bhūmijasuttavaṇṇanā is finished.

224. Atha kho āyasmā bhūmijo pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā bhūmijo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘idhāhaṃ, bhante, pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena jayasenassa rājakumārassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkamiṃ; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdiṃ. Atha kho, bhante, jayaseno rājakumāro yenāhaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā mayā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho, bhante, jayaseno rājakumāro maṃ etadavoca – ‘santi, bho bhūmija, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – āsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; anāsañcepi karitvā…pe… āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāyā’ti. ‘Idha bhoto bhūmijassa satthā kiṃvādī kimakkhāyī’ti? Evaṃ vutte ahaṃ, bhante, jayasenaṃ rājakumāraṃ etadavocaṃ – ‘na kho me taṃ, rājakumāra, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ. Ṭhānañca kho etaṃ vijjati yaṃ bhagavā evaṃ byākareyya – āsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; anāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya. Āsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; anāsañcepi karitvā…pe… āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā…pe… nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāyāti. Na kho me taṃ, rājakumāra, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ. Ṭhānañca kho etaṃ vijjati yaṃ bhagavā evaṃ byākareyyā’ti. ‘Sace bhoto bhūmijassa satthā evaṃvādī evamakkhāyī, addhā bhoto bhūmijassa satthā sabbesaṃyeva puthusamaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ muddhānaṃ maññe āhacca tiṭṭhatī’ti. ‘Kaccāhaṃ, bhante, evaṃ puṭṭho evaṃ byākaramāno vuttavādī ceva bhagavato homi, na ca bhagavantaṃ abhūtena abbhācikkhāmi, dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākaromi, na ca koci sahadhammiko vādānuvādo gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchatī’’’ti? ‘‘Taggha tvaṃ, bhūmija, evaṃ puṭṭho evaṃ byākaramāno vuttavādī ceva me hosi, na ca maṃ abhūtena abbhācikkhasi, dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākarosi, na ca koci sahadhammiko vādānuvādo gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchati. Ye hi keci, bhūmija, samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā micchādiṭṭhino micchāsaṅkappā micchāvācā micchākammantā micchāājīvā micchāvāyāmā micchāsatī micchāsamādhino te āsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; anāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya. Taṃ kissa hetu? Ayoni hesā, bhūmija, phalassa adhigamāya.
Then, the venerable Bhumija, having returned from his alms round after his meal, went to the Blessed One. Having approached, paid homage to the Blessed One, and sat down at one side.

Seated at one side, the venerable Bhumija said this to the Blessed One: "Here, venerable sir, in the forenoon, having dressed, taking my bowl and robe, I went to the residence of Prince Jayasena; having approached, I sat down on the seat that was prepared.

Then, venerable sir, Prince Jayasena came to me; having approached, he exchanged greetings with me.

Having concluded the courteous and amiable talk, he sat down at one side.

Seated at one side, venerable sir, Prince Jayasena said this to me: 'Master Bhumija, there are some ascetics and brāhmaṇas who assert such views as this: "Even if one makes an aspiration and practices the holy life (brahmacariya), one is incapable of attaining the fruit; even if one makes no aspiration… and so on … even if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and practices the holy life, one is incapable of attaining the fruit; even if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration, and practices the holy life, one is incapable of attaining the fruit."

'Here, what does Master Bhumija's teacher say, what does he declare?' When this was said, I said this to Prince Jayasena: 'Prince, I have not heard and learned this from the Blessed One's own lips.

But it is possible that the Blessed One might say this: "Even if one makes an aspiration and practices the holy life unwisely (ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ), one is incapable of attaining the fruit; even if one makes no aspiration and practices the holy life unwisely, one is incapable of attaining the fruit; even if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and practices the holy life unwisely, one is incapable of attaining the fruit; even if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and practices the holy life unwisely, one is incapable of attaining the fruit.

Even if one makes an aspiration and practices the holy life wisely (yoniso brahmacariyaṃ), one is capable of attaining the fruit; even if one makes no aspiration… and so on … even if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration… and so on … even if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and practices the holy life wisely, one is capable of attaining the fruit."

Prince, I have not heard and learned this from the Blessed One's own lips.

But it is possible that the Blessed One would answer as I have stated.'

'If Master Bhumija's teacher speaks thus, if he declares thus, then it certainly seems that the venerable Bhumija's teacher stands ahead of all the ordinary ascetics and brāhmaṇas.'

'Venerable sir, if I, having been asked thus, answer thus, am I one who speaks what has been said by the Blessed One, and do I not misrepresent the Blessed One with what is contrary to fact, and do I explain in accordance with the Dhamma, and does nothing which provides a ground for censure legitimately deduced from my assertion arise?'

'Surely, Bhumija, when you were asked such a question and answered thus, you said what has been said by me and did not misrepresent me with what is contrary to fact. You explained in accordance with the Dhamma in such a way that nothing which provides a ground for censure can be legitimately deduced from your assertion.

Whatever ascetics or brāhmaṇas have wrong view, wrong intention, wrong speech, wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, and wrong concentration, if they make an aspiration and practice the holy life, they are incapable of attaining the fruit; if they make no aspiration and practice the holy life, they are incapable of attaining the fruit; if they both make an aspiration and make no aspiration and practice the holy life, they are incapable of attaining the fruit; if they neither make an aspiration nor make no aspiration and practice the holy life, they are incapable of attaining the fruit.

What is the reason for that? Because that wrong path is not a proper method for procuring fruit.

225. “Suppose, Bhūmija, a man needing oil, seeking oil, wandering in search of oil, were to heap up gravel in a tub, sprinkle it all over with water, and press it.

Then, if he made an aspiration and acted thus, he would be unable to procure any oil; if he made no aspiration and acted thus, he would still be unable to procure any oil; if he both made an aspiration and made no aspiration and acted thus, he would still be unable to procure any oil; if he neither made an aspiration nor made no aspiration and acted thus, he would still be unable to procure any oil.

What is the reason? It is not the proper means, Bhūmija, for the attainment of oil.

So too, Bhūmija, whatever recluses (śramaṇa) and brāhmaṇas have wrong view, wrong intention, wrong speech, wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, and wrong concentration, if they make an aspiration and they lead the holy life (brahmacarya), they are unable to procure any fruit; if they make no aspiration and they lead the holy life, they are still unable to procure any fruit; if they both make an aspiration and make no aspiration and they lead the holy life, they are still unable to procure any fruit; if they neither make an aspiration nor make no aspiration and they lead the holy life, they are still unable to procure any fruit.

What is the reason? It is not the proper means, Bhūmija, for the attainment of fruit.

“Suppose, Bhūmija, a man needing milk, seeking milk, wandering in search of milk, were to pull a recently-calved cow by her horn.

Then, if he made an aspiration and acted thus, he would be unable to procure any milk; if he made no aspiration and acted thus…if he both made an aspiration and made no aspiration and acted thus…if he neither made an aspiration nor made no aspiration and acted thus, he would still be unable to procure any milk.

What is the reason? It is not the proper means, Bhūmija, for the attainment of milk.

So too, Bhūmija, whatever recluses (śramaṇa) and brāhmaṇas have wrong view…wrong concentration, if they make an aspiration and they lead the holy life (brahmacarya), they are unable to procure any fruit; if they make no aspiration and acted thus…if they both made an aspiration and made no aspiration and acted thus…if they neither made an aspiration nor made no aspiration and acted thus, they are still unable to procure any fruit.

What is the reason? It is not the proper means, Bhūmija, for the attainment of fruit.

226. “Suppose, Bhūmija, a man needing butter, seeking butter, wandering in search of butter, were to pour water into a churn and churn it with a churning-stick.

Then, if he made an aspiration and poured water into a churn and churned it with a churning stick, he would still be unable to procure any butter. 228. “Suppose, Bhūmija, a man needing fresh butter (navanīta), seeking fresh butter, wandering in search of fresh butter, were to pour curd (dadhi) into a churn and churn it with a churning-stick.

Why so? Because it is the source (yoni), Bhūmija, for the attainment (adhigama) of fresh butter.

So too, Bhūmija, whatever recluses (śramaṇa) and Brahmins (brāhmaṇa) have right view (sammādiṭṭhi) … right concentration (sammāsamādhi), if they make an aspiration and they lead the holy life (brahmacariya), they are able to procure fruit (phala); if they make no aspiration and act thus... if they both make an aspiration and make no aspiration and act thus… if they neither make an aspiration nor make no aspiration and lead the holy life, they are still able to procure fruit.

Why so? Because it is the source, Bhūmija, for the attainment of fruit.

“Bhūmija, if these four similes had occurred to Prince Jayasena, it would not have been a surprise if Prince Jayasena had acquired confidence in you, and being confident, had shown his confidence to you.”

“But how could these unprecedented similes, Bhante, occur to Prince Jayasena, unless it were through the Blessed One?”

Thus spoke the Lord.

The venerable Āyasmā Bhūmijo was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.
