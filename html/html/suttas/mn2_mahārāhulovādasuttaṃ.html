113. Thus have I heard.

On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī) in the Jetavana (Jetavana), in Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. Then, in the morning, having dressed and taken his bowl and robe, the Blessed One entered Sāvatthī for alms.

The venerable Rāhula (Rāhula) also dressed in the morning, and taking his bowl and robe, followed the Blessed One closely behind.

Then the Blessed One looked back and addressed the venerable Rāhula, "Rāhula, any form (rūpa) whatsoever, whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near, all form should be seen as it actually is with proper wisdom: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not myself.’"

"Is it only form, Blessed One? Is it only form, Well-Gone One?" "It is also form, Rāhula, and also feeling (vedanā), Rāhula, and also perception (saṃjñā), Rāhula, and also mental formations (saṃskāra), Rāhula, and also consciousness (vijñāna), Rāhula."

Then the venerable Rāhula considered thus: “Who would go into the town for alms today, having been personally admonished by the Blessed One?” Thus, he turned back and sat down at the root of a certain tree, folding his legs crosswise, setting his body erect, and establishing mindfulness in front of him.

The venerable Sāriputta (Śāriputra) saw the venerable Rāhula sitting at the root of a certain tree, folding his legs crosswise, setting his body erect, and establishing mindfulness in front of him. Having seen him, he addressed the venerable Rāhula: "Rāhula, develop mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānasmṛti). Mindfulness of breathing, Rāhula, when developed and cultivated, is of great fruit and great benefit."

113. Thus have I heard: the Great Rāhula Instruction Discourse.

There, "following closely behind" means following closely behind with continuous gait, not abandoning the sight, making no break in the going, but pursuing in the rear.

Then, indeed, the Blessed One, placing foot after foot, walks in front, in front, with graceful gait. The venerable Rāhula follows closely behind, step by step, of the Ten-Powered One.

There, the Blessed One, in the middle of a Sāla forest with very beautiful blossoms, shone forth like a magnificent, just-emerged elephant, come forth for the sake of descending to an excellent ground. And Rāhulabhadra [shone forth] like a young elephant calf, come forth behind the magnificent elephant.

The Blessed One, having emerged from the jewel cave at evening time and set out for alms, [was] like a lion with a flowing mane. And Rāhulabhadra, following the lion-king, [was] like a lion cub that has just emerged.

The Blessed One, from the jewel mountain with a forest grove abounding in beauty, [was] like a powerful, great tiger. And Rāhulabhadra [was] like a tiger cub following the tiger-king.

The Blessed One, from the Simali tree, [was] like a Garuda king who has just emerged. And Rāhulabhadra [was] like a Garuda chick that has just emerged behind the Garuda king.

The Blessed One, from Mount Citrakūṭa to the surface of the sky, [was] like a golden swan-king soaring up. And Rāhulabhadra [was] like a swan chick soaring up after the swan lord.

The Blessed One, plunging into the great lake, [was] like a great golden ship. And Rāhulabhadra [was] like a ship-calf following behind the golden ship.

The Blessed One, by the power of the wheel-jewel, on the surface of the sky, [was] like a wheel-turning king who has just set forth. And Rāhulabhadra [was] like the guide-jewel that has set forth afterwards the king.

The Blessed One, to the cloudless sky, [was] like a star-king who has just set out. And Rāhulabhadra [was] like a pure medicinal star who has set out on the path following the star lord.

The Blessed One, too, was born in the Okkāka royal lineage of the Great Appointed Lineage; Rāhulabhadra too.

The Blessed One, too, like milk poured into a conch shell, was born in a very pure, high-born Kṣatriya family; Rāhulabhadra too.

The Blessed One, too, having abandoned kingship, went forth; Rāhulabhadra too.

Indeed, one who is not attached to the agreeable and disagreeable, who is not hateful, is called "such a one."

"Agreeable and disagreeable": Here, the eight that are associated with greed-related thoughts are called agreeable (manāpa), the two that are associated with displeasure-related thoughts are called disagreeable (amanāpa).

"Thoughts will not overwhelm": These contacts, arising, will not be able to stand, overwhelming and seizing your mind as if making it fist-closed, [thinking,] "I am beautiful, my complexion is clear," desire and passion will not arise again, relying on self-regard.

Thus, in each case.

"Not established anywhere": He is not established in even one of the earth, mountains, trees, etc. If he were established on the earth, he would be broken together when the earth is broken; when the mountain falls, he would fall together; when the tree is cut, he would be cut together.

Below, the characteristic of such a state has been shown. It is not possible to become like that without a cause. Also, it is not that someone becomes like that due to these reasons: "I am born in a high family, learned, a gainer, and kings, great ministers, etc., attend on me." So satova assasati satova sato (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) passasati. ‘‘Dīghaṃ vā assasanto ‘dīghaṃ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, dīghaṃ vā passasanto ‘dīghaṃ passasāmī’ti pajānāti; rassaṃ vā assasanto ‘rassaṃ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṃ vā passasanto ‘rassaṃ passasāmī’ti pajānāti. ‘Sabbakāyappaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘sabbakāyappaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘passambhayaṃ kāyasaṅkhāraṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘passambhayaṃ kāyasaṅkhāraṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. ‘‘‘Pītippaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘pītippaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘sukhappaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘sukhappaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘cittasaṅkhārappaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘cittasaṅkhārappaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘passambhayaṃ cittasaṅkhāraṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘passambhayaṃ cittasaṅkhāraṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. ‘‘‘Cittappaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘cittappaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati ; ‘abhippamodayaṃ cittaṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘abhippamodayaṃ cittaṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘samādahaṃ cittaṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘samādahaṃ cittaṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘vimocayaṃ cittaṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘vimocayaṃ cittaṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. ‘‘‘Aniccānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘aniccānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘virāgānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘virāgānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘nirodhānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘nirodhānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘paṭinissaggānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati; ‘paṭinissaggānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. ‘‘Evaṃ bhāvitā kho, rāhula, ānāpānassati, evaṃ bahulīkatā mahapphalā hoti mahānisaṃsā. Evaṃ bhāvitāya, rāhula, ānāpānassatiyā, evaṃ bahulīkatāya yepi te carimakā assāsā tepi viditāva nirujjhanti no aviditā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā rāhulo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Mahārāhulovādasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ dutiyaṃ.
121. “Rāhula, develop mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānasmṛtiṃ, rāhula, bhāvanaṃ bhāvehi).

When mindfulness of breathing is developed and cultivated (ānāpānassati hi te, rāhula, bhāvitā bahulīkatā mahapphalā hoti mahānisaṃsā), it is of great fruit and great benefit.

And how is mindfulness of breathing developed (kathaṃ bhāvitā ca, rāhula, ānāpānassati) and cultivated, so that it is of great fruit and great benefit (kathaṃ bahulīkatā mahapphalā hoti mahānisaṃsā)? Here, Rāhula, a monk, gone to the forest or to the root of a tree or to an empty hut, sits down; having folded his legs crosswise, set his body erect, and established mindfulness in front of him (idha, rāhula, bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā nisīdati pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā ujuṃ kāyaṃ paṇidhāya parimukhaṃ satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā).

Simply mindful, he breathes in; mindful, he breathes out (so satova assasati satova passasati).

“Breathing in long, he understands: ‘I breathe in long’ (dīghaṃ vā assasanto ‘dīghaṃ assasāmī’ti pajānāti); or breathing out long, he understands: ‘I breathe out long’ (dīghaṃ vā passasanto ‘dīghaṃ passasāmī’ti pajānāti). Breathing in short, he understands: ‘I breathe in short’ (rassaṃ vā assasanto ‘rassaṃ assasāmī’ti pajānāti); or breathing out short, he understands: ‘I breathe out short’ (rassaṃ vā passasanto ‘rassaṃ passasāmī’ti pajānāti).

He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the whole body of breath’ (sabbakāyappaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the whole body of breath’ (sabbakāyappaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in tranquillising the bodily formation’ (passambhayaṃ kāyasaṅkhāraṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out tranquillising the bodily formation’ (passambhayaṃ kāyasaṅkhāraṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati).

“He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing rapture’ (pītippaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing rapture’ (pītippaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing pleasure’ (sukhappaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing pleasure’ (sukhappaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the mental formation’ (cittasaṅkhārappaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the mental formation’ (cittasaṅkhārappaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in tranquillising the mental formation’ (passambhayaṃ cittasaṅkhāraṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out tranquillising the mental formation’ (passambhayaṃ cittasaṅkhāraṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati).

“He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the mind’ (cittappaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the mind’ (cittappaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in gladdening the mind’ (abhippamodayaṃ cittaṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out gladdening the mind’ (abhippamodayaṃ cittaṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in concentrating the mind’ (samādahaṃ cittaṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out concentrating the mind’ (samādahaṃ cittaṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in liberating the mind’ (vimocayaṃ cittaṃ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out liberating the mind’ (vimocayaṃ cittaṃ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati).

He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating impermanence’ (aniccānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating impermanence’ (aniccānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating detachment’ (virāgānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating detachment’ (virāgānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating cessation’ (nirodhānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating cessation’ (nirodhānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati). He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating relinquishment’ (paṭinissaggānupassī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati); he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating relinquishment’ (paṭinissaggānupassī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati).

“Rāhula, that is how mindfulness of breathing is developed (evaṃ bhāvitā kho, rāhula, ānāpānassati) and cultivated, so that it is of great fruit and great benefit (evaṃ bahulīkatā mahapphalā hoti mahānisaṃsā). Rāhula, when mindfulness of breathing is thus developed (evaṃ bhāvitāya, rāhula, ānāpānassatiyā) and cultivated, even the final in-breaths and out-breaths are known as they cease, not unknown (evaṃ bahulīkatāya yepi te carimakā assāsā tepi viditāva nirujjhanti no aviditā’’ti).”

Thus spoke the Lord (idamavoca bhagavā). The venerable Rāhula was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words (attamano āyasmā rāhulo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti).

The Great Rāhula Instruction Discourse, the Second, is finished. (mahārāhulovādasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ dutiyaṃ).
121. Idāni therena pucchitaṃ pañhaṃ vitthārento ānāpānassatintiādimāha. Tattha idaṃ kammaṭṭhānañca kammaṭṭhānabhāvanā ca pāḷiattho ca saddhiṃ ānisaṃsakathāya sabbo sabbākārena visuddhimagge anussatiniddese vitthāritoyeva. Imaṃ desanaṃ bhagavā neyyapuggalavaseneva pariniṭṭhāpesīti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Māhārāhulovādasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
Now, while elaborating on the question asked by the Elder, he begins with "ānāpānassati" and so forth.

There, this meditation subject (kammaṭṭhāna), the cultivation of this meditation subject (kammaṭṭhānabhāvanā), and the meaning of the Pāli [text], along with the discourse on its benefits, have all been extensively explained in every way in the section on mindfulness (anussati) in the Visuddhimagga.

The Blessed One concluded this discourse solely based on those capable of being guided (neyyapuggala).
