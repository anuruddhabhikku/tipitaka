187. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho vacchagotto paribbājako yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho vacchagotto paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘Kiṃ nu kho, bho gotama, ‘sassato loko, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña’nti – evaṃdiṭṭhi evaṃdiṭṭhī (sī. syā. kaṃ. ka.) bhavaṃ gotamo’’ti? ‘‘Na kho ahaṃ, vaccha, evaṃdiṭṭhi – ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña’’’nti.
Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was living near Sāvatthi in Jeta’s Wood, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Then Vacchagotta the wanderer approached the Blessed One; having approached, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One (bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi).

When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

Seated at one side, Vacchagotta the wanderer said this to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ etadavoca):

“How is it, Master Gotama, when Master Gotama is asked if he holds the view ‘The cosmos is eternal, only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (sassato loko, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?

“No, Vaccha, I do not hold the view that ‘The cosmos is eternal, only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (sassato loko, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña).”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘The cosmos is not eternal: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (asassato loko, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘The cosmos is finite: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (antavā loko, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘The cosmos is infinite: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (anantavā loko, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘The soul & the body are the same: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīraṃ, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘The soul & the body are different: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīraṃ, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘After death a Tathāgata exists: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘After death a Tathāgata does not exist: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘After death a Tathāgata both exists & does not exist: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”

“Then does Master Gotama hold the view: ‘After death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist: only this is true, anything otherwise is worthless’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña)?” “No, Vaccha, I do not hold that view.”
187.Evaṃme sutanti aggivacchasuttaṃ. Tattha na kho ahanti paṭhamavāre nāhaṃ sassatadiṭṭhikoti vadati, dutiye nāhaṃ ucchedadiṭṭhikoti. Evaṃ antānantikādivasena sabbavāresu paṭikkhepo veditabbo. Hoti ca na ca hotīti ayaṃ panettha ekaccasassatavādo. Neva hoti na na hotīti ayaṃ amarāvikkhepoti veditabbo.
187. Thus, I have heard (evaṃ me sutaṃ): the Aggivacchasutta.

There, "No, indeed, I am not (na kho ahaṃ)"—in the first instance, he says, "I am not one who holds the eternalist view (nāhaṃ sassatadiṭṭhiko)"; in the second, "I am not one who holds the annihilationist view (nāhaṃ ucchedadiṭṭhiko)."

Thus, the rejection (paṭikkhepo) in all instances (sabbavāresu) should be understood (veditabbo) by way of [affirming or denying] end, endlessness, and so forth (antānantikādivasena).

"It is (hoti), and it is not (na ca hoti)"—this is the partial eternalism (ekaccasassatavādo) here.

"Neither is it (neva hoti) nor is it not (na na hoti)"—this should be understood as the eel-wriggler's evasion (amarāvikkhepoti veditabbo).

188. “‘Then, Master Gotama, is the cosmos eternal? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The cosmos is eternal; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, is the cosmos not eternal? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The cosmos is not eternal; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, is the cosmos finite? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The cosmos is finite; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, is the cosmos infinite? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The cosmos is infinite; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, is the soul the same as the body? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The soul is the same as the body; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, is the soul different from the body? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The soul is different from the body; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, does the Tathāgata exist after death? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The Tathāgata exists after death; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, does the Tathāgata not exist after death? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The Tathāgata does not exist after death; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, does the Tathāgata both exist and not exist after death? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The Tathāgata both exists and does not exist after death; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

“Then, Master Gotama, does the Tathāgata neither exist nor not exist after death? Is only this true, and anything else worthless?’—when asked this, are you of such a view?”

“No, Vatsa, I am not of such a view. The Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death; only this is true, and anything else worthless.”

189. ‘‘‘Sassato loko’ti kho, vaccha, diṭṭhigatametaṃ diṭṭhigahanaṃ diṭṭhikantāro diṭṭhikantāraṃ (sī. pī.) diṭṭhivisūkaṃ diṭṭhivipphanditaṃ diṭṭhisaṃyojanaṃ sadukkhaṃ savighātaṃ saupāyāsaṃ sapariḷāhaṃ, na nibbidāya na virāgāya na nirodhāya na upasamāya na abhiññāya na sambodhāya na nibbānāya saṃvattati . ‘Asassato loko’ti kho, vaccha…pe… ‘antavā loko’ti kho, vaccha…pe… ‘anantavā loko’ti kho, vaccha…pe… ‘taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti kho, vaccha…pe… ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti kho, vaccha…pe… ‘hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti kho, vaccha …pe… ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti kho, vaccha…pe… ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti kho, vaccha…pe… ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti kho, vaccha, diṭṭhigatametaṃ diṭṭhigahanaṃ diṭṭhikantāro diṭṭhivisūkaṃ diṭṭhivipphanditaṃ diṭṭhisaṃyojanaṃ sadukkhaṃ savighātaṃ saupāyāsaṃ sapariḷāhaṃ, na nibbidāya na virāgāya na nirodhāya na upasamāya na abhiññāya na sambodhāya na nibbānāya saṃvattati. Imaṃ kho ahaṃ, vaccha, ādīnavaṃ sampassamāno evaṃ imāni sabbaso diṭṭhigatāni anupagato’’ti. ‘‘Atthi pana bhoto gotamassa kiñci diṭṭhigata’’nti? ‘‘Diṭṭhigatanti kho, vaccha, apanītametaṃ tathāgatassa. Diṭṭhañhetaṃ, vaccha, tathāgatena – ‘iti rūpaṃ, iti rūpassa samudayo, iti rūpassa atthaṅgamo; iti vedanā, iti vedanāya samudayo, iti vedanāya atthaṅgamo; iti saññā, iti saññāya samudayo, iti saññāya atthaṅgamo; iti saṅkhārā, iti saṅkhārānaṃ samudayo, iti saṅkhārānaṃ atthaṅgamo; iti viññāṇaṃ, iti viññāṇassa samudayo, iti viññāṇassa atthaṅgamo’ti. 189. “'The cosmos is eternal (sassato loko)'—this, Vaccha, is a theoretical position, a theoretical thicket, a theoretical desert, a theoretical trick, a theoretical writhing, a theoretical fetter; it is accompanied by pain, torment, distress, & fever; it does not lead to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation; to calm, direct knowledge, self-awakening, unbinding.

‘The cosmos is not eternal (asassato loko)’…pe…

‘The cosmos is finite (antavā loko)’…pe…

‘The cosmos is infinite (anantavā loko)’…pe…

‘The soul & the body are the same (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīraṃ)’…pe…

‘The soul is one thing and the body another (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīraṃ)’…pe…

‘After death a Tathāgata exists (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)’…pe…

…pe… ‘After death a Tathāgata does not exist (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)’…pe…

‘After death a Tathāgata both exists & does not exist (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)’…pe…

‘After death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)’—this, Vaccha, is a theoretical position, a theoretical thicket, a theoretical desert, a theoretical trick, a theoretical writhing, a theoretical fetter; it is accompanied by pain, torment, distress, & fever; it does not lead to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation; to calm, direct knowledge, self-awakening, unbinding.

Seeing thus this drawback in these theoretical positions, I remain detached from all of them."

"But does Master Gotama have any theoretical position?"

"A theoretical position (diṭṭhigata), Vaccha, has been abandoned by the Tathāgata.

What a Tathāgata sees is this: 'Such is form (rūpaṃ), such its origination (samudayo), such its disappearance (atthaṅgamo); such is feeling (vedanā), such its origination, such its disappearance; such is perception (saññā), such its origination, such its disappearance; such are fabrications (saṅkhārā), such their origination, such their disappearance; such is consciousness (viññāṇaṃ), such its origination, such its disappearance.'

Because of this, I say, a Tathāgata—with the ending (khayā), fading away (virāgā), cessation (nirodhā), renunciation (cāgā), & relinquishment (paṭinissaggā) of all suppositions (sabbamaññitānaṃ), all excogitations (sabbamathitānaṃ), all I-making & mine-making & obsessions with conceit (sabbaahaṃkāramamaṃkāramānānusayānaṃ)—is, through lack of clinging/sustenance (anupādā), released (vimutto)"

189. Sadukkha means sadukkha due to the suffering of defilements (kilesadukkha) and the suffering of result (vipākadukkha).

Savighāta means having destruction (saupaghātaka) in terms of those same two.

Saupāyāsa means having distress (saupāyāsa) in terms of those same [two].

Sapariḷāha means having burning (sapariḷāha) in terms of those same [two].

Kiñci diṭṭhigata means he asks if there is any view (diṭṭhi) that was liked, accepted, and taken up.

Apanīta means removed, rejected.

Diṭṭha means seen with wisdom (paññāya).

Tasmā means because he saw the arising and passing away of the five aggregates (pañcannaṃ khandhānaṃ udayavayaṃ), therefore.

Sabbamaññitāna means of all the conceits (maññitāna) of craving (taṇhā), views (diṭṭhi), and pride (māna), all three.

Mathitāna means a synonym of those same.

Now, showing them by dividing them, he says sabbaahaṃkāra-mamaṃkāra-mānānusayāna.

Here, self-conceit (ahaṃkāro) is view (diṭṭhi), possessiveness (mamaṃkāro) is craving (taṇhā), the underlying tendency of pride (mānānusayo) is pride (māno).

Anupādā vimutto means liberated without clinging (anupādiyitvā vimutto), not clinging to any thing (dhammaṃ) with the four kinds of clinging (catūhi upādānehi).

190. na upetī ti na yujjati.

Here, "na upapajjati" iti should be permitted.

Because when this is said, that wandering ascetic would grasp annihilation (ucchedaṃ gaṇheyya), and "upapajjati" iti indeed [would be] permanence (sassatameva); "upapajjati ca na ca upapajjati" iti [would be] partial permanence (ekaccasassataṃ), "neva upapajjati na na upapajjati" iti [would be] an evasive answer (amarāvikkhepaṃ). Therefore, the Blessed One – "May this be without a foundation (appatiṭṭho), without support (anālambo), may [they] not obtain a place for easy entrance (sukhapavesanaṭṭhānaṃ mā labhatū)" – having stood not permitting (ananuññāya ṭhatvā) rejected the permission (anuññampi paṭikkhipi).

alaṃ ti means capable (samatthaṃ), complete (pariyattaṃ).

dhammo ti means the dhamma consisting of conditions (paccayākāradhammo).

aññatrayogenā ti means by another application (aññattha payogena).

aññatrācariyakenā ti means dwelling near other teachers who do not know the nature of conditions (paccayākāraṃ ajānantānaṃ aññesaṃ ācariyānaṃ santike vasantena).

192. “Yena rūpeṇa”: by whatever form one might designate the Tathāgata as having form.

“Gambhīro”: deep in qualities (guṇagambhīro).

“Appameyyo”: it is not possible to take a measure (pamāṇaṃ gaṇhituṃ na sakkuṇeyyo).

“Duppariyogāḷho”: difficult to fathom, difficult to know (duogāho dujjāno).

"Seyyathāpi mahāsamuddo": Just as the great ocean is deep (gambhīro), immeasurable (appameyyo), and difficult to know (dujjāno), even so is the one whose āsavas are destroyed (khīṇāsavopi).

It is not fitting to start with that, saying "it arises" etc. (taṃ ārabbha upapajjatītiādi sabbaṃ na yujjati).

How so? Just as it is not fitting to say, starting with a fire that has been extinguished (parinibbutaṃ aggiṃ ārabha), "it has gone to the eastern direction" etc. (puratthimaṃ disaṃ gatotiādi sabbaṃ na yujjati), so too.

“Aniccatā”: because of impermanence (aniccatāya).

“Sāre patiṭṭhita”: established in the essence of the supramundane Dharma (lokuttaradhammasāre patiṭṭhitaṃ).

The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti).

Impermanence. Established in essence. The Papañcasūdanī, commentary on the Majjhimanikāya.
