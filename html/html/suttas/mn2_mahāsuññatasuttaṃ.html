185. It is like this.

This is what I have heard: the Mahāsuññatasutta.

There, kāḷakhemakassā means he is black in color like the color of a corpse; but Khema is his name.

Vihāra means a dwelling made in that same Nigrodha's park, in one part, by erecting a surrounding wall, building a gate house, constructing dwellings like those called haṃsa-vaṭṭaka?, such as a meeting hall, a refectory, and so forth.

Sambahulāni senāsanāni means many resting places: bed, chair, mattress, pillow, straw mat, leather mat, spread grass, spread leaves, spread straw and so forth were prepared. They were placed bed touching bed … spread straw touching spread straw, so that it resembled the dwelling place of bhikkhus who have formed themselves into a society.

Sambahulā nu kho...

Then it occurred to him: “This social life is developed to the utmost in the round of becoming. As water collects into rivers, so social life is developed by beings in hell, in the animal world, in the realm of ghosts, and in the Asura group; and also in the human world, the divine world, and the Brahma world.

For hell (nirayo) is ten-thousand leagues across and is crammed with beings like a tube packed with bath powder. There is no counting or reckoning the beings in the place of torture by the fivefold binding; likewise in the places of paring with adzes and so forth. Such is the way they roast in society.

As to the animal world (tiracchānayoniyaṃ) there is no counting or reckoning the termites in a single termite hill; and likewise ants in each ants’ nest.

Such is social life in the animal world too.

Petanagarāni cities of departed spirits were full of spirits, and were a gāvuta in extent or half a yojana.

Such is social life in the ghost realm, too.

The ten-thousand league sphere of the Asura abode (asurabhavanaṃ) is like an earring or hole when the needle is put into the ear .

Such is social life in the Asura Group too.

As regards the human world (manussaloke), there were 5,700,000 in the large clans living at Sāvatthī, and inside and outside Rājagaha 1,800,000 people 18 koṭis.

Such is social life in certain places of the human world too.

Beginning with the earth deities (bhummadevatā), there is social life in the divine world and the Brahma world also.

Each deity has two and a half koṭis of dancers, even up to nine koṭis. Also there are ten thousand Brahmās living in one place.”

Nirayo, tiracchānayoniyaṃ, petanagarāni, asurabhavanaṃ, manussaloke, bhummadevatā

Thereupon he thought: “For four incalculable ages and a hundred thousand aeons the Perfections have been fulfilled by me for the purpose of undoing living in societies. And as soon as these bhikkhus have formed themselves into a society and get to delight in society, they will act contrary to that.”

Then feeling concern for the dhamma, he thought again: “If it were possible to announce a training precept that two bhikkhus should not live in one place I would do so; but that is not possible.

Come now, I will explain the discourse called the Mahāsuññatāpaṭipatti, which will be like a looking-glass for those clansmen who desire training, and like the announcement of training precepts at the city gate.

After that, just as Khattiyas nobles and so on, seeing their blemishes in that looking glass get rid of them and become unblemished, so indeed, even for 5,000 years after I have attained complete extinction, clansmen delighting in solitude will make an end of the suffering due to the round of becoming by harkening to this discourse and avoiding society.”

And the number of clansmen who, by harkening to this discourse and avoiding society have, as though fulfilling the Blessed One’s wish, made an end of suffering and attained complete extinction is past reckoning.

For in the Vālikapiṭṭhi Monastery in Ceylon the Abhidhamma scholar called the Elder Abhaya, after reciting this discourse together with a number of bhikkhus on the occasion of taking up residence for the rainy season, exclaimed: “The Fully Enlightened One enjoins us to act thus; and what are we doing?”

And by avoiding society and delighting in solitude all of them attained arahatship within that same rainy season.

This discourse is called the “breaker-up of societies.”

186. "Ghaṭāyā" means of Sakka named thus.

"Vihāre" means this monastery is like the monastery of Kāḷakhemaka in one part of Nigrodhārāma, so it should be understood.

"Cīvarakamma" means the accessories that have been prepared with cleaning, dyeing and so on, of old dirty robes; it also applies to the arrangement that has not been prepared with consideration, sewing and so on, of cloths that have arisen for robes; but here, the arrangement that has not been prepared is intended.

People had given the Elder Ānanda material for robes (cīvarasāṭake).

Therefore, the Elder, having taken a number of monks, did work on robes (cīvarakammaṃ) there.

And those bhikkhus, sitting from the time announced for the needle work in the morning, get up from it some time unannounced.

When the sewing is finished we shall set our resting places in order,” they had not set them in order.

"Cīvarakārasamayo no" the Elder thought: "Indeed, the resting places have not been put in order by these bhikkhus, and the Blessed One will see them."

Consequently the Master is displeased and desires to give a severe reproof. I will be a support for those bhikkhus.” That is why he spoke as he did.

The intention here is this: "Venerable Sir, these bhikkhus are living in this way not just because they delight in being busy, but on account of robe-making."

"Na kho, Ānandā" means, Ānanda, whether it is an instance of being busy or whether it is an instance of robe-making or not, still a bhikkhu who delights in company does not shine forth.

Do not be a support where there is no occasion for support.”

"Saṅgaṇikā" means gathering with one’s own community.

"Gaṇo" means gathering with various people.

So whether he delights in company or in society, in either case a bhikkhu who likes the fullness of society, who is bound by the ties of society, does not shine forth.

But it is when a bhikkhu sweeps out his daytime quarters after his meal and, after washing his feet well, takes up his basic meditation subject and devotes himself to delight in solitude, he shines forth in the Buddha’s Dispensation.

"Nekkhammasukha" means the bliss in him who has renounced sense-desires.

193. How, Ānanda, does a teacher's undoing come about? Here, Ānanda, a certain teacher frequents a secluded dwelling place, a forest, the foot of a tree, a mountain, a glen, a mountain cave, a charnel ground, a forest thicket, an open space, or a heap of straw. As he dwells thus withdrawn, Brahmins, householders, townsfolk, and country people gather around him. He then longs for honor (mucchaṃ nikāmayati) from those who gather around him—Brahmins, householders, townsfolk, and country people—he falls into greed (gedhaṃ āpajjati), and reverts to luxury (āvattati bāhullāya).

This, Ānanda, is called the undoing of a teacher (upaddavo ācariyo). That is how, Ānanda, a teacher’s undoing comes about (ācariyūpaddavo hoti).

But why is it said that the undoing of the dweller in the life of purity has a more painful result (duḥkhavipākataro), a more bitter result (kaṭukavipākataro) and it even leads to rebirth in states of woe (vinipātāya ca saṃvattatī)?

Going into homelessness outside the Dispensation brings small gain; there, there is no outstandingly great special quality (nibbattetabbaguṇo) to be developed, but only the eight meditative attainments (aṭṭhasamāpatti) and the five supernormal powers (pañcābhiññā) are achieved.

Accordingly, just as there is no great suffering (dukkhaṃ) for one who falls from the back of a donkey, so, since in a sect outside the Dispensation he falls only from worldly special qualities (lokiya-guṇa), it is not said in this way of the first two kinds of undoing.

But going into homelessness in the Dispensation brings great gain. Here, the four paths (cattāro maggā), the four fruitions (cattāri phalāni), and nirvāṇa are outstandingly special qualities (adhigantabbaguṇā) to be attained.

Accordingly, just as when a noble youth, wellborn on both parental sides, traversing a city and seated in the place of honor on the back of an elephant, falls from the elephant’s back, he comes to great suffering; so, since in falling away from the Dispensation he falls away from the nine supramundane special qualities (lokuttaraguṇa).

it is said in this way of the undoing of the dweller in the life of purity (brahmacārupaddavo).

Therefore (tasmā): Because the brahmacārupaddava [state of existence] is more prone to suffering (duḥkhavipākataro) due to the remaining disturbances (sesupaddavehi), and because one who transgresses (vītikkamanto) the proper conduct towards a co-celibate (sapattapaṭipattiṃ) tends toward (saṃvattati) prolonged (dīgharattaṃ) suffering (duḥkhāya) and harm (ahitāya), therefore (tasmā) proper conduct towards a friend (mittapaṭipatti) leads to welfare (hitāya). By "conduct befitting a co-celibate" (sapattavatāyā) is meant hostile conduct (verapaṭipattiyā).

Transgressing (vokkamma) the Teacher's instruction (satthusāsanā): Even something as small as an improper or wrongly spoken word (dukkaṭadubbhāsitamattampi) is considered a transgression (vītikkamanto vokkamma vattati nāma) if done intentionally (sañcicca). Not transgressing (avītikkamanto) means not deviating (na vokkamma vattati nāma).

Transgressing, and The Teacher's instruction, O Ānanda, I will not behave towards you as [the potter behaves]. Meaning, I will not treat you like that (ahaṃ tumhesu tathā na paṭipajjissāmi).

"Raw" (āmake) means "unripe" (apakke).

"Being raw" (āmakamatte) means in a raw (āmake), not-too-dry (nātisukkhe) vessel (bhājane).

For a potter (kumbhakāro), indeed, takes the raw (āmakaṃ), not too dry (nātisukkhaṃ), unripe (apakkaṃ) [clay pot] gently (saṇhikaṃ) with both hands (ubhohi hatthehi), [thinking,] "May it not break!" (‘‘mā bhijjatū’’ti). Thus (iti), just as (yathā) a potter (kumbhakāro) behaves (paṭipajjati) in that way (tattha), I (ahaṃ) will not behave (na paṭipajjissāmi) towards you (tumhesu) in that way (tathā).

"Reproving, reproving" (niggayha niggayhā) means I will not be silent (tuṇhī na bhavissāmi) after advising (ovaditvā) once (sakiṃ). Rather, having reproved (niggaṇhitvā niggaṇhitvā) again and again (punappunaṃ), I will advise (ovadissāmi) and instruct (anusāsissāmi).

"Rejecting, rejecting" (pavayha pavayhā) means having washed away (pavāhetvā pavāhetvā) the faults (dose).

Just as (yathā) among cooked pots (pakkabhājanesu) a potter (kumbhakāro), having discarded (pavāhetvā) the cracked (bhinna), broken (chinna), and fragile (jajjarāni) ones, and having gathered (ekato katvā) only the well-cooked ones (supakkāneva), tapping (ākoṭetvā ākoṭetvā), he takes [them], just so (evameva) I also (ahampi), having washed away (pavāhetvā pavāhetvā) again and again (punappunaṃ), will advise (ovadissāmi) and instruct (anusāsissāmi).

"He who is sound, he will endure" (yo sāro so ṭhassatī) means: thus (evaṃ), of you (vo) who are being advised (ovadiyamānānaṃ) by me (mayā), he who (yo) is of the essence of the path and its fruit (maggaphalasāro), he (so) will endure (ṭhassati).

Furthermore (apica), even worldly qualities (lokiyaguṇāpi) are intended (adhippetā) here (idha) by "sound" (sāro).

The rest (sesaṃ) is clear (uttānamevāti) throughout (sabbattha).
