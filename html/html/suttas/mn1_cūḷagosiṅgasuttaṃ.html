THUS HAVE I HEARD.

On one occasion the Blessed One was living near (one of) the Nādikas in the Brick House. At this time Venerable Anuruddha, Venerable Nandiya, and Venerable Kimila were staying at the Park of the Gosinga Sala-tree Wood.

Then, when it was evening, the Blessed One rose from meditation and went to the Park of the Gosinga Sala-tree Wood.

The park keeper saw the Blessed One coming in the distance.

Having seen the Blessed One, he said this: “Ascetic, don’t enter this park. There are three young men living there, desiring their own good. Please, don’t disturb them.”

Venerable Anuruddha heard the park warden conversing with the Blessed One.

Having heard, he said this to the park warden: “Friend park keeper, do not keep the Blessed One out. Our Teacher, the Blessed One, has arrived.”

Then the venerable Anuruddha went to the venerable Nandiya and the venerable Kimbila; having gone, he said this to the venerable Nandiya and the venerable Kimbila: “Come out, Venerables, our teacher has arrived.”

Then Venerable Anuruddha, Venerable Nandiya, and Venerable Kimila went out to greet the Blessed One. One took the Blessed One's bowl and robe, one prepared a seat, and one set out water for washing the feet.

The Blessed One sat down on the seat made ready.

Having sat down, the Blessed One washed his feet.

Then those venerable ones paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side.

325. Thus.

This is what I heard: the Cūḷagosiṅga Sūtra.

There, “at Nātika dwelling” means that Nātika was the name of a certain pond, and relying on that were two villages belonging to the sons of Cūḷapiti and Mahāpiti, and [the Buddha was] in one village among those.

"At Giñjakāvasatha" means at the brick-built dwelling place.

Once upon a time, the Blessed One, while making a great gathering of people, wandering on tour in the Vajji country, arrived at Nātika.

The people dwelling at Nātika, after giving a great donation to the Blessed One, hearing the Dharma talk, with minds full of joy, thinking, “We will make a dwelling place for the Teacher,” having consulted, having built a palace showing wall-staircase-pillars, various forms of Vāḷa, etc., having plastered it with lime, having finished garland-work, creeper-work, etc., having prepared ground coverings, couches, chairs, etc., they offered it to the Teacher.

Then, other people there had night-quarters, day-quarters, pavilions, walkways, etc. made for the monastic community.

Thus, that vihāra was great.

With reference to that it was said, "At Giñjakāvasatha."

“At Gosiṅgasālavanadāya” means that there, from the trunk of a certain large tree, a branch arose having the shape of a cow's horn, taking hold of that tree, all of that forest went to the designation "Gosiṅgasālavana."

"Dāya" is a name of the forest without distinction.

Therefore, "At Gosiṅgasālavanadāya" means in the Gosiṅgasālavana forest, this is the meaning.

“Dwelling” means they dwell experiencing the taste of harmony.

For these sons of good family, the time of ordinary people was spoken of in the upper fifty, here [it is] the time of those with taints destroyed.

Then, indeed, they, having gained flavor, having gained support, having attained analytical insight, with taints destroyed, experiencing the taste of harmony, dwelt there.

With reference to this it was said.

At Gosiṅgasālavanadāya, Gosiṅgasālavana, Dāya, At Gosiṅgasālavanadāya, Dwelling

“By which Gosiṅgasālavanadāya he approached” means either to the elders Dhammasenāpati Mahāmoggallāna or to the eighty great disciples, or, at least, without addressing anyone including the elder Ānanda, treasurer of the Dharma, taking his bowl and robe himself, like an elephant come out of the herd, like a black lion come out of the pack, like a cloud torn by the wind, he approached alone.

Why, in this case, did the Blessed One himself go? Three sons of good family dwell experiencing the taste of harmony; because of encouraging them, because of showing compassion to the later generation, and because of reverence for the Dharma.

Thus, it occurred to him: “I will encourage these sons of good family, uplifting them, making conversation, I will teach them the Dharma.”

Thus, first, he went to encourage them.

Furthermore, it occurred to him: “In the future, sons of good family – the perfectly awakened Buddha, having gone himself to the presence of those dwelling in harmonious community, having made conversation, having spoken the Dharma, encouraged the three sons of good family, who would not dwell in harmonious community? – thinking harmonious community should be dwelt in, quickly they will make an end of suffering.”

Thus, he went also out of compassion for the later generation.

For Buddhas are reverent toward the Dharma by nature, and their reverence for the Dharma was evident in the relay chariot.

Thus, from this reverence for the Dharma, too, [thinking] I will encourage the Dharma, he went.

By which Gosiṅgasālavanadāya he approached

“Dāyapāla” means forest-protector.

That one, after people enter that forest with their desired places, there they do not take either flowers or fruit or sap or wood materials; thus, he, sitting at the joined gate of that forest surrounded by a fence, protects, guards that forest.

Therefore, he is called "Dāyapāla."

"Attakāmarūpa" means they dwell having the nature of desiring their own welfare.

For whoever, in this teaching, even having gone forth, maintains his livelihood by means of medical work, messenger work, dispatched going, etc., by means of twenty-one wrong modes of livelihood, he is not called one who desires his own welfare.

But whoever, in this teaching, having gone forth, abandoning the twenty-one wrong modes of livelihood, having been established in the fourfold purity of ethical conduct, having learned the Buddha's word, having undertaken the suitable ascetic practices, taking a meditation subject agreeable to his mind among the thirty-eight objects of meditation, abandoning the edge of the village, having entered the forest, having produced attainments, practicing the work of insight, dwells, he is called one who desires his own welfare.

Those three sons of good family, too, were of this kind.
Therefore, it was said, "They live assuming any shape at will."

The dāyapāla, assuming any shape at will, forbade the Blessed One, saying, "Do not cause discomfort to them," meaning, "Do not cause discomfort to them."

Thus, it occurred to him: "These noble sons live harmoniously. But in the place they have gone to, strife, contention, and disputes arise. Like a fierce-horned, wild bull, he wanders about, piercing [others]. And going by a single path is not possible for two [people]. Sometimes, even he, doing thus, might disrupt the harmonious dwelling of these noble sons."

He is pleasing, of golden color, fond of delicious flavors, I think. From the time he departed, he might disrupt the diligent practice of these noble sons by means of praising the excellent donors, his own attendants, and so forth.

Also, the dwelling places of these noble sons are fixed and defined: three leaf huts, three walkways, three daytime dwellings, [and] three couches.

This śramaṇa, however, is large-bodied and older, I think, he will be.

He will cause these noble sons to leave their lodgings at an improper time.

Thus, in every way, discomfort will arise for them."

Not desiring that, he forbade the Blessed One, saying, "Do not cause discomfort to them."

What? Knowing, did he forbid [him], or not knowing? Not knowing.

Although, indeed, from the time the Tathāgata took rebirth, miracles such as the shaking of ten thousand world-systems occurred, the forest-dwellers, however, are weak people, devoted to their own work; they are not able to notice those things.

And when the Sammāsambuddha, by name, surrounded by a retinue of many thousands of monks, wanders about displaying the Buddha's power with a fathom's-length aura, with the eighty minor marks, and with the glory of the thirty-two marks of a great man, then it must be known without asking, "Who is this?"

Then, however, the Blessed One, covering up all that Buddha's power with his robe-womb, and being covered by a cloud-womb, like a full moon, himself took his bowl and robe and went in an unknown guise.

Thus, not knowing him, the dāyapāla forbade [him].

"He said thus," means the elder, having heard the dāyapāla's talk, "He is not a śramaṇa," thought, "We three people live here. There are no other renunciates. And this dāyapāla speaks with him as if he were a renunciate. Who, indeed, will he be?" Having arisen from his daytime dwelling, standing at the door, looking at the path, he saw the Blessed One.

The Blessed One, too, at the very sight of the elder, released a bodily radiance. The fathom's-length aura, adorned with the eighty minor marks, shone like a stretched-out golden cloth.

The elder [thought]: "This dāyapāla, extending his hand to grasp a venomous snake with raised hood, does not know that he is speaking with the foremost person in the world, but speaks as if with just any monk." Forbidding him, he spoke this speech, "No, friend dāyapāla," and so forth.

"He approached him," means why did he approach without making a welcoming gesture towards the Blessed One? Thus, it occurred to him: "We three people live dwelling harmoniously. If I alone were to make a welcoming gesture, harmonious dwelling would not exist." [Therefore,] having taken his beloved friends, he will make a welcoming gesture.

And just as the Blessed One is dear to me, so also are my companions dear to me. Desiring to make a welcoming gesture together with them, he approached without making [one] himself.

Some, however, say: "For those elders, the path of the Blessed One's arrival was at the edge of the walkway at the leaf hut's door. Therefore, the elder went giving a sign to them."

"Come here," means "Come here from this place."

"He washed his feet," means having taken jeweled-colored water with hands resembling blossoming lotuses, having sprinkled the water on the golden-colored backs of his feet, he washed [them] while rubbing foot against foot.

Dirt and grime do not cling to the bodies of Buddhas. Why did he wash his feet? For the sake of holding the body high and for the sake of greatly gladdening their minds.

"The Blessed One washed his feet with water brought by us, he made use of it." The minds of those monks were delighted because of their strong gladness. Therefore, he washed his feet.

"The Blessed One said this to the venerable Anuruddha," means he was the eldest of them.

Devatāpi me means, "Venerable Anuruddha ( bhante anuruddha ), today the venerable Nandiya ( ayyo nandiyatthero ), today the venerable Kimila ( ayyo kimilatthero ) has passed beyond this and this attainment ( samāpattiyā vītināmesīti)," this is the meaning of "they announced thus (evamārocesunti attho)."

331. Then the spirit Dīgha Parājana went to the Blessed One and on arrival, having bowed down to the Blessed One, stood to one side.

Standing to one side, Dīgha Parājana, the spirit, said this to the Blessed One: “It is a gain, venerable sir, for the Vajjis, a great gain for the Vajjian people, that the Tathāgata, accomplished and fully enlightened, dwells among them, and these three clansmen—the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Nandiya, and the venerable Kimbila!”

On hearing the words of Dīgha Parājana, the spirit, the earth gods exclaimed: “It is a gain, venerable sir, for the Vajjis, a great gain for the Vajjian people, that the Tathāgata, accomplished and fully enlightened, dwells among them, and these three clansmen—the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Nandiya, and the venerable Kimbila!”

On hearing the words of the earth gods, the gods of the heaven of the Four Great Kings…the gods of the heaven of the Thirty-three…the Yama gods…the gods of the Tusita heaven…the gods who delight in creating…the gods who wield power over others’ creations…the gods of Brahma’s retinue exclaimed: “It is a gain, venerable sir, for the Vajjis, a great gain for the Vajjian people, that the Tathāgata, accomplished and fully enlightened, dwells among them, and these three clansmen—the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Nandiya, and the venerable Kimbila!”

Thus, at that instant, at that moment, those venerable ones were known as far as the Brahma-world.

“So it is, Dīgha, so it is, Dīgha! Dīgha, if the clan from which those three clansmen went forth from the house life into homelessness should remember them with confident heart, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of that clan for a long time.

Dīgha, if the retinue of the clan from which those three clansmen went forth from the house life into homelessness should remember them with confident heart, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of that retinue of the clan for a long time.

Dīgha, if the town from which those three clansmen went forth from the house life into homelessness should remember them with confident heart, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of that town for a long time.

Dīgha, if the city from which those three clansmen went forth from the house life into homelessness should remember them with confident heart, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of that city for a long time.

Dīgha, if the country from which those three clansmen went forth from the house life into homelessness should remember them with confident heart, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of that country for a long time.

Dīgha, if all nobles should remember those three clansmen with confident heart, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of the nobles for a long time.

Dīgha, if all brahmins…all merchants…all workers should remember those three clansmen with confident heart, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of the workers for a long time.

Dīgha, if the world with its gods, its Māras, and its Brahmās, this generation with its recluses and brāhmaṇas, its princes and its people, should remember those three clansmen with confident heart, that would lead to the welfare and happiness of the world, with its gods, its Māras, and its Brahmās, this generation with its recluses and brāhmaṇas, its princes and its people, for a long time.

"dīgho" means, as in, "maṇi māṇivaro dīgho, atho serīsako sahā" (Dīgha Nikāya 2. 0143).

(Dī. Ni. 3.293) Thus, Āgata, an inner one of the twenty-eight yakṣa generals, is a deva-king.

"parajano" is the name of that very yakṣa.

"yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamī" means that he, sent by Vessavaṇa, while going to that place, having taken the bowl and robe himself, saw the Blessed One between Giñjakāvasatha and the Gosiṅga Sāla Forest, and the Blessed One, having taken the bowl and robe himself, went to the presence of three sons of good family in the Gosiṅga Sāla Forest.

Today there will be a great Dharma teaching.

"May I also be a partaker in that teaching," [thinking thus,] by an invisible body, going closely behind the Teacher, having stood not far away, having heard the Dharma, even though the Teacher was going, he did not go, – "what will these elders do?" – for the purpose of showing this, he remained right there.

Then, having seen those two elders embracing Anuruddha, [he thought,] – "these elders, relying on the Blessed One, even having attained all the qualities beginning with ordination, are jealous of the Blessed One himself; they are not able to bear it, they hide themselves exceedingly, they conceal [him]; now I will not give [them] an opportunity to conceal [him]; from the earth up to the Brahma realm I will proclaim their virtues" – having thought [this], he approached the Blessed One.

"lābhā vata, bhante" means, "O Blessed One, those inhabitants of the Vajji country who obtain the opportunity to see the Blessed One and these three sons of good family, obtain the opportunity to worship, obtain the opportunity to give offerings of Dharma, obtain the opportunity to hear the Dharma, their gain, O Blessed One, is the Vajjis," is the meaning.

"saddaṃ sutvā" means that he, by the power of his own yakṣa, having made a great sound, while overwhelming the entire Vajji country, uttered that speech.

And by that sound, the deities dwelling in those trees, mountains, and so on, heard the sound.

With reference to that, it was said – "saddaṃ sutvā".

"anussāvesu" means, having heard the great sound, they announced [it].

This method [applies] everywhere.

"yāva brahmalokā" means up to the Akaniṣṭha Brahma realm.

"tañcepi kula" means, "having gone forth from our family, these sons of good family are ordained, are virtuous, are endowed with good qualities, are accomplished in conduct, [and possess] auspicious qualities," thus, if that family should call to mind those three sons of good family with a serene mind, thus, the meaning should be regarded everywhere.

Thus, the Blessed One brought the teaching to a conclusion in accordance with the connection.

lābhā vata, bhante, saddaṃ sutvā, anussāvesu, yāva brahmalokā, tañcepi kula.
