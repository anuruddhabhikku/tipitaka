Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘ahaṃ kho, bhikkhave, aññatreva rattibhojanā rattibhojanaṃ (ka.) bhuñjāmi. Aññatra kho panāhaṃ, bhikkhave, rattibhojanā bhuñjamāno appābādhatañca sañjānāmi appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañca. Etha, tumhepi, bhikkhave, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjatha. Aññatra kho pana, bhikkhave, tumhepi rattibhojanā bhuñjamānā appābādhatañca sañjānissatha appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañcā’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Atha kho bhagavā kāsīsu anupubbena cārikaṃ caramāno yena kīṭāgiri nāma kāsīnaṃ nigamo tadavasari. Tatra sudaṃ bhagavā kīṭāgirismiṃ viharati kāsīnaṃ nigame.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the Kasi country together with a large bhikkhu community.

There the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus, "I, bhikkhus, abstain from eating at night.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, by abstaining from eating at night, I experience freedom from illness (appābādhatā), freedom from affliction (appātaṅkatā), lightness (lahuṭṭhāna), strength (bala), and comfortable living (phāsuvihāra).

Come, bhikkhus, you too should abstain from eating at night.

Furthermore, bhikkhus, by abstaining from eating at night, you too will experience freedom from illness (appābādhatā), freedom from affliction (appātaṅkatā), lightness (lahuṭṭhāna), strength (bala), and comfortable living (phāsuvihāra)."

"Yes, lord" (evaṃ bhante), those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One.

Then, as the Blessed One was wandering by stages in the Kasi country, he eventually arrived at a Kasi town called Kitagiri.

There the Blessed One lived in this Kasi town, Kitagiri.

174. Thus, I have heard. This is the Kīṭāgirisutta.

There, in a country named Kāsīsū, [he said:]

"Come, monks!" Thus, [he said,] "Come, monks, perceiving these five advantages, partake of food only at the proper time."

Thus, the Blessed One, having caused the abandonment of untimely food at night and untimely food during the day, having caused the abandonment of these two foods at once, at one time, he caused the abandonment of untimely food during the day, and again, having spent the time, causing the abandonment of untimely food at night, he spoke thus.

Why? For these two foods, being current, are accustomed to [being eaten] in the circle, are thoroughly accustomed, like water descending into a river, flowing downwards. And those sons of good families, well-nourished, delicate, having enjoyed fine foods in places sheltered from the wind and in houses, grow weary abandoning the two foods at once.

Therefore, not causing the abandonment at once, in the Bhaddāli Sutta, he caused the abandonment of untimely food during the day; here, untimely food at night.

However, causing abandonment, not rebuking or scolding, but because of the abandonment of these [foods], you will recognize [an increase in] non-affliction. Thus, having shown the advantage, he caused abandonment.

Kīṭāgiri is the name of that market town.

Then a number of monks went to the monks Assaji and Punabbasuka, and having approached them, they said to the monks Assaji and Punabbasuka: “Friends, the Blessed One and the Sangha of monks now abstain from eating at night. 175. Assajipunabbasukā means Assaji and Punabbasu. Among the six Chabbaggiyas, these two were teachers of a group.

Paṇḍuka, Lohitaka, Mettiya, and Bhummajaka, Assaji and Punabbasu, these six people are called Chabbaggiyā.

Among them, Paṇḍuka and Lohitaka, taking their own retinue, dwell in Sāvatthi; Mettiya and Bhummajaka [dwell] in Rājagaha; these two people are residents (āvāsikā) in Kīṭāgiri.

Āvāsikā means those who are permanent residents. Because of that connection, they build a monastic dwelling (senāsana) that has not been made [before], they restore the dilapidated, they are lords over what has been made.

Kālika means the benefit to be obtained in a future time.

"Mayā cetaṃ, bhikkhave" – what does this signify here? Monks, one who, upon enjoying three times during the day, only generates pleasant feeling (sukha-vedanā), is not called a "doer" in this dispensation (śāsana). This teaching (deśanā) was begun in order to show that this much feeling is to be cultivated, and that much is not to be cultivated.

"Evarūpaṃ sukhavedanaṃ pajahatha" – this was said in terms of joy (saumanasya) based on household life. "Upasampajja viharatha" – and this was said in terms of joy based on renunciation.

Furthermore, in the two remaining instances, the meaning should be understood in terms of sorrow (daumanasya) and equanimity (upekṣā) based on household life and renunciation.

“And what, bhikkhus, is the individual who is liberated in both ways (ubhatobhāgavimutto)? Here, bhikkhus, some individual dwells having contacted with his body those peaceful immaterial liberations that transcend form, and his taints are destroyed by his seeing with wisdom. This, bhikkhus, is called an individual liberated in both ways (ubhatobhāgavimutto), and of such a bhikkhu, bhikkhus, I say, ‘There is no more to be done through diligence.’ Why is that? Here, bhikkhus, some individual does not dwell having contacted with his body those peaceful immaterial liberations that transcend form, but his taints are destroyed by his seeing with wisdom. This, bhikkhus, is called an individual liberated by wisdom (paññāvimutto). And of such a bhikkhu, bhikkhus, I say, ‘There is no more to be done through diligence.’ Why is that? “Bhikkhus, there is a four-phrased statement, and when it is recited a wise man would quickly understand it. I will recite it to you, bhikkhus; listen to me.”

“But who are we, venerable sir, and who are the knowers of the Dhamma?”

“Bhikkhus, even with a teacher who is concerned with material things, an heir to material things, attached to material things, such haggling by his disciples would not be proper: ‘If we get this, we will do it; if we don’t get this, we won’t do it’; so what should be said when the teacher is the Tathāgata, who is utterly detached from material things?

184. Yassuddiṭṭhassā means "of whom it was pointed out."

Yopi so, bhikkhave, satthā means "he shows a teacher who is an outsider, O monks."

Evarūpī means "of such a kind."

Paṇopaṇaviyā means "selling dear and cheap."

Na upetī means "does not approach," that is, it is without meaning when increasing or decreasing the price during a time of buying and selling.

"This ox, how much is it worth? Twenty is what it is worth," saying this, it is called paṇati.

"Not twenty is what it is worth, ten is what it is worth," saying this, it is called opaṇati.

Rejecting this, he said, "He does not approach selling dear and cheap."

Now, to show that selling dear and cheap, he said, "Thus it will not be for us, and what should we do? It will not be thus for us, and we should not do it."

Kiṃ pana, bhikkhave means, "O monks, since the Tathāgata dwells completely detached from sensual pleasures, how could such selling dear and cheap be fitting for a teacher who is thus detached?" Pariyogāhiya vattato means "for one speaking after having investigated, lifted up, and taken hold of."

Ayamanudhammo means "this is the nature."

Jānāti bhagavā, nāhaṃ jānāmī means "the Blessed One knows the advantages of eating a single meal, but I do not know," and because of faith in me, he abandons three meals of the day and eats a single meal.

Ruḷahanīya means rohanīyaṃ.

Ojavanta means sinehavantaṃ.

Kāmaṃ taco cā means "by this, he shows the vigor of the four components."

Here, skin is one component, sinew is one, bone is one, and flesh and blood are one; thus, having undertaken the vigor endowed with the four components, he shows that, not having attained arhatship, he will not get up.

The rest is clear throughout.

However, the Blessed One brought the teaching to completion in accordance with a person who is to be guided, with the pinnacle of arhatship.
