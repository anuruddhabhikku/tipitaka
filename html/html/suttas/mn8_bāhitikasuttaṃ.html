Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī) in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park.

Then, in the morning, the venerable Ānanda, having dressed, taking his bowl and robe, entered Sāvatthī for alms.

Having wandered for alms in Sāvatthī and having returned from his alms round, after his meal he went to the Eastern Park, to the Palace of Migāra’s Mother, for the day’s abiding.

Now at that time, King Pasenadi of Kosala, having mounted the elephant Ekapundarika, was riding out from Sāvatthī at midday.

King Pasenadi of Kosala saw the venerable Ānanda coming in the distance.

Having seen him, he addressed Sirivaḍḍha, the high official: “That is the venerable Ānanda, Sirivaḍḍha.”

“Yes, your majesty, that is the venerable Ānanda.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala told a certain man: “Come, good man, go to the venerable Ānanda and, having approached him, pay homage with your head at the feet of the venerable Ānanda in my name, saying: ‘The king, venerable sir, Pasenadi of Kosala, pays homage with his head at the feet of the venerable Ānanda.’

And say this: ‘If, venerable sir, the venerable Ānanda has no urgent business, may the venerable Ānanda come for a moment, out of compassion.’”

“Yes, sire,” the man replied to King Pasenadi of Kosala, and he went to the venerable Ānanda; having approached, he paid homage to the venerable Ānanda and stood at one side.

Standing to one side, that man said to the venerable Ānanda: “The king, venerable sir, Pasenadi of Kosala, pays homage with his head at the feet of the venerable Ānanda; and he says this: ‘If, venerable sir, the venerable Ānanda has no urgent business, may the venerable Ānanda come for a moment, out of compassion.’”

The venerable Ānanda consented in silence (tuṇhībhāvena).

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala went by elephant as far as the elephant could go, and then, dismounting from the elephant and going on foot, he approached the venerable Ānanda; having approached, he paid homage to the venerable Ānanda and stood at one side.

Standing to one side, King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the venerable Ānanda: “If, venerable sir, the venerable Ānanda has no urgent business, it would be good if the venerable Ānanda would go to the bank of the Aciravatī (Ajiaravati) River, out of compassion.”

The venerable Ānanda consented in silence (tuṇhībhāvena).

358. Thus, I heard: the Bāhitika Sutta.

There, " ekapuṇḍarīkaṃ nāga " means an elephant of such a name.

It is said that above its ribs, there was a white spot the size of a palm fruit; therefore they gave it the name ekapuṇḍarīka.

"sirivaḍḍhaṃ mahāmatta" means a great minister of such a name, who, having ascended his own elephant, was going along together to a place suitable for conversation.

"āyasmāno " here, " no " is a particle of questioning.

The great minister, having considered the manner in which the elder was wearing his saṅghāṭī robe and bowl, said, "Yes, Great King!"

359. Then venerable Ānanda went to the bank of the river Aciravatī (अचिरवती); after going there, he sat down on a prepared seat at the root of a certain tree.

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala went by elephant as far as the elephant could go, and dismounting from the elephant, he proceeded on foot to where venerable Ānanda was; after approaching, he paid homage to venerable Ānanda and stood to one side.

Standing to one side, King Pasenadi Kosala said this to venerable Ānanda: "Here, venerable sir (bhante), may venerable Ānanda be seated on this elephant rug."

"Enough, great king (mahārāja). You sit down; I am sitting on my own seat."

King Pasenadi of Kosala sat down on the prepared seat.

Having sat down, King Pasenadi Kosala said this to venerable Ānanda: "What kind of bodily conduct (kāyasamācāraṃ), venerable Ānanda, would the Blessed One (bhagavā) engage in, such that his bodily conduct would be criticized by ascetics (samaṇehi) and Brahmins (brāhmaṇehi)?"

"No, great king, the Blessed One would not engage in such bodily conduct that his bodily conduct would be criticized by ascetics and Brahmins."

"And what kind of verbal conduct (vacīsamācāraṃ)... or mental conduct (manosamācāraṃ), venerable Ānanda, would the Blessed One engage in, such that his mental conduct would be criticized by ascetics and Brahmins?"

"No, great king, the Blessed One would not engage in such mental conduct that his mental conduct would be criticized by ascetics and Brahmins."

"Amazing, venerable sir, astonishing, venerable sir! For what we were unable to fully answer with a question, venerable sir, has been fully answered by venerable Ānanda with an explanation of the question.

Those who, venerable sir, are foolish (bālā), unskilled (abyattā), without investigating (ananuvicca), without careful examination (apariyogāhetvā), speak praise or dispraise of others, we do not accept that as essential. But those who, venerable sir, are wise (paṇḍitā), skilled (viyattā), intelligent (medhāvino), having investigated, having carefully examined, speak praise or dispraise of others, we accept that as essential."

360. “Now, venerable Ānanda, what kind of bodily behaviour is censured by wise śrāmaṇas (samaṇa) and brāhmaṇas (brāhmaṇa)?” “Any bodily behaviour that is unwholesome, great king.”

“Now, venerable sir, what kind of bodily behaviour is unwholesome?” “Any bodily behaviour that is blameworthy, great king.”

“Now, venerable sir, what kind of bodily behaviour is blameworthy?” “Any bodily behaviour that brings affliction, great king.”

“Now, venerable sir, what kind of bodily behaviour brings affliction?” “Any bodily behaviour that has painful results, great king.”

“Now, venerable sir, what kind of bodily behaviour has painful results?” “Any bodily behaviour, great king, that leads to one’s own affliction (ātmavyābādhāya), or to the affliction of others (paravyābādhāya), or to the affliction of both (ubhayavyābādhāya), and on account of which unwholesome states (akuśalā dharma) increase (abhivaḍḍhanti) and wholesome states (kuśalā dharma) diminish (parihāyanti). Such bodily behaviour is censured by wise śrāmaṇas and brāhmaṇas, great king.”

“Now, venerable Ānanda, what kind of verbal behaviour…pe… mental behaviour is censured by wise śrāmaṇas and brāhmaṇas?” “Any mental behaviour that is unwholesome, great king.”

“Now, venerable sir, what kind of mental behaviour is unwholesome?” “Any mental behaviour that is blameworthy, great king.”

“Now, venerable sir, what kind of mental behaviour is blameworthy?” “Any mental behaviour that brings affliction, great king.”

“Now, venerable sir, what kind of mental behaviour brings affliction?” “Any mental behaviour that has painful results, great king.”

“Now, venerable sir, what kind of mental behaviour has painful results?” “Any mental behaviour, great king, that leads to one’s own affliction, or to the affliction of others, or to the affliction of both, and on account of which unwholesome states increase and wholesome states diminish. Such mental behaviour is censured by wise śrāmaṇas and brāhmaṇas, great king.”

“Now, venerable Ānanda, does the Blessed One (bhagavān) commend the abandoning of all unwholesome states?” “The Tathāgata (tathāgata) has abandoned all unwholesome states and is endowed with wholesome states, great king.”
360.Akusaloti akosallasambhūto. Sāvajjoti sadoso. Sabyābajjhoti sadukkho. Dukkhavipākoti idha nissandavipāko kathito. Tassāti tassa evaṃ attabyābādhādīnaṃ atthāya pavattakāyasamācārassa. Sabbākusaladhammapahīno kho, mahārāja, tathāgato kusaladhammasamannāgatoti ettha sabbesaṃyeva akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānaṃ vaṇṇetīti. Āma vaṇṇetīti vutte yathā pucchā, tathā attho vutto bhaveyya. Evaṃ byākaraṇaṃ pana na bhāriyaṃ. Appahīnaakusalopi hi pahānaṃ vaṇṇeyya, bhagavā pana pahīnākusalatāya yathākārī tathāvādīti dassetuṃ evaṃ byākāsi. Sukkapakkhepi eseva nayo. Sabbākusaladhammapahīno kho, mahārāja, tathāgato kusaladhammasamannāgato
"Akusalo" means arising from unskillfulness. "Sāvajjo" means with fault. "Sabyābajjho" means with suffering. "Dukkhavipāko" means here the result flowing [from it] is spoken of. "Tassā" means of that bodily conduct which occurs for the purpose of harm to self, etc.

"sabbākusaladhammapahīno kho, mahārāja, tathāgato kusaladhammasamannāgato" means here it praises the abandoning of all unwholesome things.

"Yes, it praises," when said thus, the meaning would be spoken according to the question. However, this explanation is not weighty. Even one who has not abandoned the unwholesome might praise abandonment, but the Blessed One, because of having abandoned the unwholesome, is a speaker in accordance with his actions, thus he explained. And the same rule applies in the case of the bright side.

362. ‘‘Acchariyaṃ , bhante, abbhutaṃ, bhante! Yāva subhāsitaṃ cidaṃ subhāsitamidaṃ (sī.), bhante, āyasmatā ānandena. Iminā ca mayaṃ, bhante, āyasmato ānandassa subhāsitena attamanābhiraddhā. Evaṃ attamanābhiraddhā ca mayaṃ , bhante, āyasmato ānandassa subhāsitena. Sace, bhante, āyasmato ānandassa hatthiratanaṃ kappeyya, hatthiratanampi mayaṃ āyasmato ānandassa dadeyyāma. Sace, bhante, āyasmato ānandassa assaratanaṃ kappeyya, assaratanampi mayaṃ āyasmato ānandassa dadeyyāma. Sace, bhante, āyasmato ānandassa gāmavaraṃ kappeyya, gāmavarampi mayaṃ āyasmato ānandassa dadeyyāma. Api ca, bhante, mayampetaṃ mayameva taṃ (sī.), mayampanetaṃ (syā. kaṃ.) jānāma – ‘netaṃ āyasmato ānandassa kappatī’ti. Ayaṃ me, bhante, bāhitikā raññā māgadhena ajātasattunā vedehiputtena vatthanāḷiyā chattanāḷiyā (syā. kaṃ. pī.) pakkhipitvā pahitā soḷasasamā āyāmena, aṭṭhasamā vitthārena . Taṃ, bhante, āyasmā ānando paṭiggaṇhātu anukampaṃ upādāyā’’ti. ‘‘Alaṃ, mahārāja, paripuṇṇaṃ me ticīvara’’nti. ‘‘Ayaṃ , bhante, aciravatī nadī diṭṭhā āyasmatā ceva ānandena amhehi ca. Yadā uparipabbate mahāmegho abhippavuṭṭho hoti, athāyaṃ aciravatī nadī ubhato kūlāni saṃvissandantī gacchati; evameva kho, bhante, āyasmā ānando imāya bāhitikāya attano ticīvaraṃ karissati. Yaṃ panāyasmato ānandassa purāṇaṃ ticīvaraṃ taṃ sabrahmacārīhi saṃvibhajissati. Evāyaṃ amhākaṃ dakkhiṇā saṃvissandantī maññe gamissati. Paṭiggaṇhātu, bhante, āyasmā ānando bāhitika’’nti. Paṭiggahesi kho āyasmā ānando bāhitikaṃ. Atha kho rājā pasenadi kosalo āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘handa ca dāni mayaṃ, bhante ānanda, gacchāma; bahukiccā mayaṃ bahukaraṇīyā’’ti. ‘‘Yassadāni tvaṃ, mahārāja, kālaṃ maññasī’’ti. Atha kho rājā pasenadi kosalo āyasmato ānandassa bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā pakkāmi.
362. Wonderful, venerable sir, amazing, venerable sir! How well that has been expressed by the venerable Ānanda!

And we are satisfied and pleased by what has been so well expressed by the venerable Ānanda.

Venerable sir, we are so satisfied and pleased with what has been so well expressed by the venerable Ānanda.

that if the elephant-treasure (hatthiratana) were allowed to the venerable Ānanda, we would give the elephant-treasure (hatthiratana) to him;

if the horse-treasure (assaratana) were allowed to the venerable Ānanda, we would give the horse-treasure (assaratana) to him;

if the boon of a village (gāmavara) were allowed to the venerable Ānanda, we would give the boon of a village (gāmavara) to him.

But we know that these are not allowable for the venerable Ānanda.

But there is this cloak (bāhitikā) of mine, venerable sir, which was sent to me packed in a royal umbrella case by King Ajātasattu of Magadha, sixteen hands long and eight hands wide.

Let the venerable Ānanda accept it out of compassion.

“It is not necessary, great king. My triple robe (ticīvara) is complete.”

“Venerable sir, this river Aciravati has been seen both by the venerable Ānanda and by ourselves.

when a great cloud has rained heavily on the mountains; then this river Aciravati overflows both its banks. So too, venerable sir, the venerable Ānanda can make a triple robe (ticīvara) for himself out of this cloak (bāhitikā),

and he can share out his old triple robe (ticīvara) among his companions in the holy life.

In this way, our offering (dakkhiṇā) will overflow.

Venerable sir, let the venerable Ānanda accept the cloak (bāhitika).”

So the venerable Ānanda accepted the cloak (bāhitikaṃ).

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala said to the venerable Ānanda: “And now, venerable sir, Ānanda, we depart. We are busy and have much to do.”

“Do whatever seems fit, great king.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala, having delighted and rejoiced in the venerable Ānanda’s words, rose from his seat, and after paying homage to the venerable Ānanda, keeping him on his right, he departed.
362.Bāhitikāti bāhitiraṭṭhe uṭṭhitavatthassetaṃ nāmaṃ. Soḷasasamā āyāmenāti āyāmena samasoḷasahatthā. Aṭṭhasamā vitthārenāti vitthārena samaaṭṭhahatthā.
bāhitikā means this name is for a cloth that originated in the country of Bāhiti.

soḷasasamā āyāmena means sixteen hands in length.

aṭṭhasamā vitthārena means eight hands in width.

363. Atha kho āyasmā ānando acirapakkantassa rañño pasenadissa kosalassa yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā ānando yāvatako ahosi raññā pasenadinā kosalena saddhiṃ kathāsallāpo taṃ sabbaṃ bhagavato ārocesi. Tañca bāhitikaṃ bhagavato pādāsi. Atha kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘lābhā, bhikkhave, rañño pasenadissa kosalassa, suladdhalābhā, bhikkhave, rañño pasenadissa kosalassa; yaṃ rājā pasenadi kosalo labhati ānandaṃ dassanāya, labhati payirupāsanāyā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Bāhitikasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.
Then, soon after venerable Ānanda had departed from King Pasenadi of Kosala, he approached the Blessed One (bhagavā). Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdi).

Seated to one side (ekamantaṃ nisinno), venerable Ānanda reported to the Blessed One everything that had been discussed with King Pasenadi of Kosala.

And he presented the cloak (bāhitikaṃ) to the Blessed One.

Then the Blessed One addressed the monks (bhikkhū): "It is a gain (lābhā), monks (bhikkhave), for King Pasenadi of Kosala; it is a great gain (suladdhalābhā), monks (bhikkhave), for King Pasenadi of Kosala, that King Pasenadi of Kosala has the opportunity of seeing Ānanda, and the opportunity of paying respect."

The Blessed One said this (idamavoca bhagavā).

Satisfied (attamanā), the monks (bhikkhū) rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words (bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti).

The Bāhitika Sutta, the eighth, is finished.
363.Bhagavatopādāsīti bhagavato niyyātesi. Datvā ca pana gandhakuṭiyaṃ vitānaṃ katvā bandhi. Tato paṭṭhāya gandhakuṭi bhiyyosomattāya sobhi. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānameva. Neyyapuggalassa pana vasena ayaṃ desanā niṭṭhitāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Bāhitikasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
Bhagavato pādāsīti, he offered it to the Blessed One. Furthermore, having given it, he erected a canopy in the Gandhakuti and fastened it. From then on, the Gandhakuti shone all the more. The rest is clear throughout. This teaching is concluded according to the capacity of the person to be guided.

The Bāhitikasuttavaṇṇanā is concluded in the Papañcasūdanī, commentary on the Majjhimanikāya.
