Thus I have heard. On one occasion the venerable Mahāmoggallāna was living in the Bhagga country at Susumāragiri in the Bhesakaḷāvana Deer Park.
Then, at that time, the venerable Mahāmoggallāna was walking up and down in the open.
Then, at that time, Māra the Evil One had gone into the belly of the venerable Mahāmoggallāna and was sitting inside his bowels.
Then the venerable Mahāmoggallāna considered thus: “Why is my belly so heavy, as if it were full [of beans]?”
Then the venerable Mahāmoggallāna descended from the walking place, entered his dwelling, and sat down on the prepared seat.
Having sat down, the venerable Mahāmoggallāna gave thorough attention to himself.
The venerable Mahāmoggallāna saw Māra the Evil One gone into his belly and sitting inside his bowels.
Having seen Māra the Evil One, he said this: “Get out, Evil One! Get out, Evil One! Do not harass the Tathāgata, do not harass the Tathāgata's disciple.
Do not let it be to your harm and suffering for a long time.”
Then Māra the Evil One thought: “This samaṇa does not know me, he does not see me, when he says thus: ‘Get out, Evil One! Get out, Evil One! Do not harass the Tathāgata, do not harass the Tathāgata's disciple.
Do not let it be to your harm and suffering for a long time.’”
“Even his teacher would not know me so quickly, so how could this disciple know me?” Then the venerable Mahāmoggallāna said this to Māra the Evil One: “Even so, Evil One, I know you. Do not think, ‘He does not know me.’
You are Māra, Evil One; for you it occurs thus: ‘This samaṇa does not know me, he does not see me, when he says thus: Get out, Evil One! Get out, Evil One! Do not harass the Tathāgata, do not harass the Tathāgata's disciple.
Do not let it be to your harm and suffering for a long time.’"

Thus have I heard: The Māratajjanīya Sutta.

There, "having sat down inside the koṭṭha" means having entered the stomach and sat down inside the intestines, having sat in the place of the digested matter.

"Garugaro viyā" means like something very heavy, stiff, like a heap of stones.

"Māsācitaṃ maññe" means like the stomach of one who has eaten a month's worth of food, like a bag filled with month-old food, like month-old curds, such is the meaning.

"Vihāraṃ pavisitvā" means if this heaviness is due to a fault of the food, and walking in the open air is not suitable, having descended from the walking place, having entered the leaf-hut, he sits down on the seat prepared by custom.

"Paccattaṃ yoniso manasākāsī" means, reflecting, "What could this be?", he applied his mind by his own means.

If the Elder, reflecting on his own morality, were to touch his stomach with his hand [thinking], "May whatever undigested food was consumed yesterday, the day before yesterday, or the day before that, or any other disturbing fault, be digested and comfortable," Māra the Evil One would dissolve and depart.

But the Elder, not doing so, wisely applied his mind.

"Mā tathāgataṃ vihesesī" means just as parents are harmed when their children are harmed, teachers and preceptors are harmed when their co-residents and disciples are harmed, and a king is harmed when his kingdom is harmed, so too the tathāgata is harmed when a disciple of the tathāgata is harmed.

Therefore, he said, "Do not harm the tathāgata."

"Paccaggaḷe aṭṭhāsī" means he stood right at the bolt.

"Aggaḷaṃ" is said to be the door; having opened [it] with his mouth, having left the leaf-hut, he stood leaning on the door of the outer leaf-hut, such is the meaning.

507. Why did the Elder begin this discourse with "Having been in the past, I was an evil-doer"? The Elder, it seems, thought: "Even the smell of humans afflicts the deities dwelling in the sky who are stationed hundreds of yojanas away.

It was said, 'Indeed, Your Majesty, the smell of humans distresses the gods hundreds of yojanas away' (Dī. Ni. 2.415). But this Māra, a townsman, very pure, of great power, endowed with anubhāva, resembling a divine king, having entered my belly, being seated inside my intestines in a place for excrement, will be exceedingly corrupted. What other improper thing will there be for one able to enter and sit down in such a disgusting, repulsive place? What else will he be ashamed of? If it is said, 'You are my relative,' there is no tenderness to which he will not submit." Thinking, "Having pierced his lineage, I will dismiss him by a gentle means," he began this discourse.

"So, you are my nephew" - so you were my nephew at that time. This was said in the way of tradition. In the world of gods, however, there is no lineage of Māra's father, no lineage of his grandfather, doing kingship. By virtue of merit, he was reborn in the world of gods, having become a divine king, and after living as long as his life lasted, he passed away. Another, a certain one, by his own deed, becomes an overlord in that place, having become an overlord. Thus, this Māra, too, having passed away from there at that time, and again having done good, should be understood to have been reborn as an overlord in that place at this time.

"So, you are my nephew."

Vidhuro means without a yoke; that is, dissimilar to others. Appakasirenā means with little difficulty. Pasupālakā means goat-herds. Pathāvino means those who have entered upon the path. Kāye upacinitvā means having piled up a pyre all around. Aggim datvā pakkamiṃsū means having ascertained the measure of the pyre, thinking, "The body will reach complete consumption in so much," they gave fire in the four directions and departed. The pyre blazed like a lamp flame; it was as if it was the time when the Elder entered a water-cell and sat down. Cīvarāni papphoṭetvā means having risen from attainment, when the smoke had disappeared, he shook out the robes, trampling on the embers of kiṃsuka color. In his body, there was not even a bit of warmth; in his robes, not even a bit of soot was seen; this is the fruit of attainment.

Vidhuro, appakasirenā, pasupālakā, pathāvino, kāye upacinitvā, aggiṃ datvā pakkamiṃsū, cīvarāni papphoṭetvā.
