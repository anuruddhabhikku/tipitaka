Thus have I heard. At one time the Blessed One was staying near Campā on the banks of the Gaggarā Lake together with a large Saṅgha of monks.

Then Pessa, the elephant driver's son, and Kandaraka the wanderer, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, Pessa, the elephant driver's son, paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side.

Then Kandaraka the wanderer exchanged greetings with the Blessed One. Having engaged in courteous, amiable talk, he stood to one side. Standing to one side, Kandaraka the wanderer surveyed the Saṅgha of monks, who were utterly silent, and said this to the Blessed One: "It is wonderful, venerable Gotama! It is amazing, venerable Gotama, how well the Saṅgha of monks has been guided by venerable Gotama! Those Blessed Ones, perfected ones, fully awakened Buddhas who existed in the past also guided the Saṅgha of monks to the highest attainment, just as the Saṅgha of monks is now guided by venerable Gotama. And those Blessed Ones, perfected ones, fully awakened Buddhas who will exist in the future will also guide the Saṅgha of monks to the highest attainment, just as the Saṅgha of monks is now guided by venerable Gotama."

The Blessed One, hearing this, said, "Kandaraka, you say that the bhikkhu-saṃgha is peaceful (upasantoti), but the reason for this bhikkhu-saṃgha's peacefulness is unknown to you. You have not fulfilled the thirty perfections (pāramitā), ripened the roots of good (kusalamūla), or penetrated all-knowing wisdom (sabbaññutaññāṇa) at the bodhi-seat. But I have fulfilled the perfections, brought the practice for the benefit of relatives (ñātatthacariya), the practice for the benefit of the world (lokatthacariya), and the practice for the benefit of the Buddha (buddhatthacariya) to completion, attained all-knowing wisdom (sabbaññutaññāṇa) at the bodhi-seat, and the reason for their peacefulness is known to me." To demonstrate this, he began this discourse.

"The presence of Kandaraka" is another phrase that links to the preceding context.

The Blessed One thought, "This wandering ascetic (paribbājaka) says that this bhikkhu-saṃgha is peaceful, but this bhikkhu-saṃgha, having only fashioned and conceived the appearance of peace through hypocrisy (kuhakabhāvena), does not show the nature of peace with their minds, being unpeaceful."

Here in the bhikkhu-saṃgha are bhikkhus who are fulfilling the practice (paṭipadaṃ pūrayamānāpi), bhikkhus who have completed the practice (paṭipadaṃ pūretvā) and attained the summit (matthakaṃ patvā), and ṭhitabhikkhūpi. Of those, those who have completed the practice (paṭipadaṃ pūretvā) and attained the summit (matthakaṃ pattā) are peaceful through the qualities they have penetrated themselves (attanā paṭividdhaguṇeheva upasantā), those who are fulfilling the practice (paṭipadaṃ pūrayamānā) are peaceful through insight (vipassanāya) into the higher path (uparimaggassa), and those who are liberated (muttā) from this, the remaining ones, are peaceful through the four establishments of mindfulness (catūhi smṛtyupasthānehi upasantā).

"I will show them the reason for this peacefulness (upasantakāraṇaṃ dassessāmī)," he said, "Because of this and this reason, this bhikkhu-saṃgha is peaceful (upasanto)," to show this, he began with "The presence of Kandaraka" (santi hi kandarakā).

14. ‘‘So evaṃ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte pubbenivāsānussatiñāṇāya cittaṃ abhininnāmeti. So anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati, seyyathidaṃ – ekampi jātiṃ dvepi jātiyo tissopi jātiyo catassopi jātiyo pañcapi jātiyo dasapi jātiyo vīsampi jātiyo tiṃsampi jātiyo cattālīsampi jātiyo paññāsampi jātiyo jātisatampi jātisahassampi jātisatasahassampi anekepi saṃvaṭṭakappe anekepi vivaṭṭakappe anekepi saṃvaṭṭavivaṭṭakappe – ‘amutrāsiṃ evaṃnāmo evaṃgotto evaṃvaṇṇo evamāhāro evaṃsukhadukkhappaṭisaṃvedī evamāyupariyanto, so tato cuto amutra udapādiṃ; tatrāpāsiṃ evaṃnāmo evaṃgotto evaṃvaṇṇo evamāhāro evaṃsukhadukkhappaṭisaṃvedī evamāyupariyanto, so tato cuto idhūpapanno’ti. Iti sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati.
“When his concentrated mind is thus purified, bright, unblemished, rid of imperfection, malleable, wieldy, steady, and attained to imperturbability, he directs it to knowledge of the recollection of past lives ( pubbenivāsānussatiñāṇāya cittam abhininnāmeti ).

He recollects his numerous past lives, that is, one birth, two births, three, four, or five births; ten, twenty, thirty, forty, or fifty births; a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births; many aeons of world contraction, many aeons of world expansion, many aeons of world contraction and expansion: ‘There I had such a name, belonged to such a clan, had such an appearance; such was my food, such my experience of pleasure and pain, such my span of life. Passing away from that, I arose in another state of existence; there too I had such a name, belonged to such a clan, had such an appearance; such was my food, such my experience of pleasure and pain, such my span of life.

Passing away from that state, I re-arose here.’

Thus he recollects his manifold past lives in their modes & details (sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati).
15
15. ‘‘So evaṃ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte sattānaṃ cutūpapātañāṇāya cittaṃ abhininnāmeti. So dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passati cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāti – ‘ime vata bhonto sattā kāyaduccaritena samannāgatā vacīduccaritena samannāgatā manoduccaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ upavādakā micchādiṭṭhikā micchādiṭṭhikammasamādānā, te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapannā; ime vā pana bhonto sattā kāyasucaritena samannāgatā vacīsucaritena samannāgatā manosucaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ anupavādakā sammādiṭṭhikā sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā, te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannā’ti. Iti dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passati cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāti.
“When his mind is thus concentrated, purified, bright, unblemished, rid of imperfection, malleable, wieldy, steady, and attained to imperturbability, he directs it to knowledge of the passing away and reappearance of beings.

With the divine eye (dibbaṃ cakkhuṃ), which is purified and surpasses the human, he sees beings passing away and reappearing, inferior and superior, fair and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate. He understands how beings pass on according to their actions thus: ‘These worthy beings who were ill-conducted in body (kāya-duccarita), speech (vacī-duccarita), and mind (mano-duccarita), revilers of noble ones (ariyānaṃ upavādakā), wrong in their views (micchādiṭṭhikā), giving effect to wrong view in their actions (micchādiṭṭhikammasamādānā), on the dissolution of the body, after death, have reappeared in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in a bad destination (duggatiṃ), in the downfall (vinipātaṃ), in hell (nirayaṃ); while these other worthy beings who were well-conducted in body (kāya-sucarita), speech (vacī-sucarita), and mind (mano-sucarita), non-revilers of noble ones (ariyānaṃ anupavādakā), right in their views (sammādiṭṭhikā), giving effect to right view in their actions (sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā), on the dissolution of the body, after death, have reappeared in a good destination (sugatiṃ), in a heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ).'

Thus with the divine eye, which is purified and surpasses the human, he sees beings passing away and reappearing, inferior and superior, fair and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate, and he understands how beings pass on according to their actions.

16. ‘‘So evaṃ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte āsavānaṃ khayañāṇāya cittaṃ abhininnāmeti. So ‘idaṃ dukkha’nti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. ‘Ayaṃ dukkhasamudayo’ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. ‘Ayaṃ dukkhanirodho’ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. ‘Ayaṃ dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā’ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. ‘Ime āsavā’ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. ‘Ayaṃ āsavasamudayo’ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. ‘Ayaṃ āsavanirodho’ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti . ‘Ayaṃ āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā’ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Tassa evaṃ jānato evaṃ passato kāmāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccati, bhavāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccati, avijjāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāti. Ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, puggalo nevattantapo nāttaparitāpanānuyogamanuyutto, na parantapo na paraparitāpanānuyogamanuyutto . So attantapo aparantapo diṭṭheva dhamme nicchāto nibbuto sītībhūto sukhappaṭisaṃvedī brahmabhūtena attanā viharatī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Kandarakasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.
16. “When his mind is thus concentrated (samāhite citte), purified (parisuddhe), cleansed (pariyodāte), unblemished (anaṅgaṇe), rid of defilement (vigatūpakkilese), malleable (mudubhūte), wieldy (kammaniye), steady (ṭhite), and attained to imperturbability (āneñjappatte), he directs his mind (cittaṃ abhininnāmeti) to the knowledge of the destruction of the taints (āsavānaṃ khayañāṇāya).

He understands as it really is (yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti): ‘This is suffering (idaṃ dukkhaṃ).’
He understands as it really is (yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti): ‘This is the origin of suffering (ayaṃ dukkhasamudayo).’
He understands as it really is (yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti): ‘This is the cessation of suffering (ayaṃ dukkhanirodho).’
He understands as it really is (yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti): ‘This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering (ayaṃ dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā).’
He understands as it really is (yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti): ‘These are the taints (ime āsavā).’
He understands as it really is (yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti): ‘This is the origin of the taints (ayaṃ āsavasamudayo).’
He understands as it really is (yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti): ‘This is the cessation of the taints (ayaṃ āsavanirodho).’
He understands as it really is (yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti): ‘This is the way leading to the cessation of the taints (ayaṃ āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā).’

When he knows and sees thus (evaṃ jānato evaṃ passato), his mind is liberated (cittaṃ vimuccati) from the taint of sensual desire (kāmāsavāpi), from the taint of being (bhavāsavāpi), and from the taint of ignorance (avijjāsavāpi). When it is liberated (vimuttasmiṃ), there comes the knowledge: ‘It’s liberated (vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti).’

He understands (pajānāti): ‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the spiritual life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more for this state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāya).’

This one (ayaṃ vuccati), mendicants (bhikkhave), is called a person (puggalo) who neither torments himself (nevattantapo), nor is given to the practice of self-mortification (nāttaparitāpanānuyogamanuyutto), nor torments others (na parantapo), nor is given to the practice of mortifying others (na paraparitāpanānuyogamanuyutto).

He (so), not tormenting himself (attantapo) or others (aparantapo), is here and now hungerless (diṭṭheva dhamme nicchāto), extinguished (nibbuto), and cooled (sītībhūto), and abides experiencing bliss (sukhappaṭisaṃvedī), having himself become holy (brahmabhūtena attanā viharati).”

The Buddha (bhagavā) spoke thus (idamavoca). Elated (attamanā), those mendicants (te bhikkhū) delighted (abhinandunti) in the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) statement (bhāsitaṃ).

The Kandaraka Sutta is finished, the first.
