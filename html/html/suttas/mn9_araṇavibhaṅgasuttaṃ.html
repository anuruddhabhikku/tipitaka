323. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘araṇavibhaṅgaṃ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Na kāmasukhamanuyuñjeyya hīnaṃ gammaṃ pothujjanikaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, na ca attakilamathānuyogamanuyuñjeyya dukkhaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ. Ete kho, bhikkhave ete kho (sī.), ete te (syā. kaṃ. pī.), ubho ante anupagamma majjhimā paṭipadā tathāgatena abhisambuddhā, cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattati. Ussādanañca jaññā, apasādanañca jaññā; ussādanañca ñatvā apasādanañca ñatvā nevussādeyya, na apasādeyya nāpasādeyya (sī.), dhammameva deseyya. Sukhavinicchayaṃ jaññā; sukhavinicchayaṃ ñatvā ajjhattaṃ sukhamanuyuñjeyya. Rahovādaṃ na bhāseyya, sammukhā na khīṇaṃ nātikhīṇaṃ (syā. kaṃ. ka.) bhaṇe. Ataramānova bhāseyya, no taramāno. Janapadaniruttiṃ nābhiniveseyya, samaññaṃ nātidhāveyyāti – ayamuddeso araṇavibhaṅgassa. ete kho (sī.), ete te (syā. kaṃ. pī.) nāpasādeyya (sī.) nātikhīṇaṃ (syā. kaṃ. ka.)
Thus have I heard:

Once the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī) in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapindika’s park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying, “Bhikkhus!”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: “I will teach you an exposition of non-conflict ( araṇavibhaṅgaṃ), monks.

Listen to that, pay close attention, I will speak.”

“As you say, lord,” those monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“One should not pursue sensual pleasure (kāmasukha), which is low, coarse, worldly, ignoble, and connected with harm. Nor should one pursue self-mortification (attakilamatha), which is painful, ignoble, and connected with harm.

Avoiding both of these extremes, monks, the middle way (majjhimā paṭipadā) realized by the Tathāgata—producing vision, producing knowledge—leads to calm (upasamāya), to direct knowledge (abhiññāya), to self-awakening (sambodhāya), to Unbinding (nibbānāya).

One should know what it is to extol (ussādana) and what it is to disparage (apasādana); and knowing both, one should neither extol nor disparage, but should teach only the Dharma.

One should know how to define pleasure (sukhavinicchaya), and knowing that, one should pursue pleasure within oneself (ajjhattaṃ sukha).

One should not utter covert speech (rahovāda), and one should not utter overt sharp speech (khīṇaṃ).

One should speak unhurriedly (ataramānova), not hurriedly.

One should not insist on local language (janapadaniruttiṃ), and one should not override normal usage (samaññaṃ)—this is the summary of the exposition of non-conflict (araṇavibhaṅga).”
323.Evaṃme sutanti araṇavibhaṅgasuttaṃ. Tattha nevussādeyya na apasādeyyāti gehasitavasena kañci puggalaṃ neva ukkhipeyya na avakkhipeyya. Dhammameva deseyyāti sabhāvameva katheyya. Sukhavinicchayanti vinicchitasukhaṃ. Raho vādanti parammukhā avaṇṇaṃ, pisuṇavācanti attho. Sammukhā na khīṇanti sammukhāpi khīṇaṃ ākiṇṇaṃ saṃkiliṭṭhaṃ vācaṃ na bhaṇeyya. Nābhiniveseyyāti na adhiṭṭhahitvā ādāya vohareyya. Samaññanti lokasamaññaṃ lokapaṇṇattiṃ. Nātidhāveyyāti nātikkameyya.
323.
Thus, "I have heard" refers to the Araṇavibhaṅgasutta.
Therein, "one should neither elevate nor degrade" means one should neither uplift nor put down any person due to attachment to household life.
"One should teach only the Dhamma" means one should speak only about the true nature (of things).
Sukhavinicchaya means vinicchitasukhaṃ.
Raho vāda means backbiting, bad-mouthing, that is the meaning.
Sammukhā na khīṇa means one should not utter corrupt, confused, or defiled speech even face to face.
Nābhiniveseyyā means one should not take hold (of anything) after determining (it), one should not use (it).
Samañña means worldly convention, worldly designation (lokasamaññaṃ lokapaṇṇattiṃ).
Nātidhāveyyā means one should not overstep (nātikkameyya).

324. ‘‘‘Na kāmasukhamanuyuñjeyya hīnaṃ gammaṃ pothujjanikaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, na ca attakilamathānuyogamanuyuñjeyya dukkhaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhita’nti – iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ; kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? Yo kāmapaṭisandhisukhino somanassānuyogo hīno gammo pothujjaniko anariyo anatthasaṃhito, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā. Yo kāmapaṭisandhisukhino somanassānuyogaṃ ananuyogo hīnaṃ gammaṃ pothujjanikaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, adukkho eso dhammo anupaghāto anupāyāso apariḷāho; sammāpaṭipadā. Yo attakilamathānuyogo dukkho anariyo anatthasaṃhito, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā. Yo attakilamathānuyogaṃ ananuyogo dukkhaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, adukkho eso dhammo anupaghāto anupāyāso apariḷāho; sammāpaṭipadā. ‘Na kāmasukhamanuyuñjeyya hīnaṃ gammaṃ pothujjanikaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, na ca attakilamathānuyogaṃ anuyuñjeyya dukkhaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhita’nti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ.
324.
‘‘‘One should not pursue sensual pleasure (kāmasukha), which is low, vulgar, coarse (gammaṃ), ignoble (anārya), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhitaṃ); and one should not pursue self-mortification (attakilamathānuyoga), which is painful (dukkhaṃ), ignoble (anārya), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhitaṃ)’ – So it was said. And with reference to what was this said? “The pursuit of the enjoyment of one whose pleasure is linked to sensual desires (kāmapaṭisandhisukhino somanassānuyoga) - low, vulgar, coarse (gammo), ignoble (anārya), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhito) - is a state beset by suffering (sadukkho), vexation (sa-upaghāto), despair (saupāyāso), and fever (sapariḷāho); it is the wrong way (micchāpaṭipadā).
Disengagement from the pursuit of the enjoyment of one whose pleasure is linked to sensual desires (kāmapaṭisandhisukhino somanassānuyogaṃ) - low, vulgar, coarse (gammaṃ), ignoble (anārya), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhitaṃ) - is a state without suffering (adukkho), vexation (anupaghāto), despair (anupāyāso), and fever (apariḷāho); it is the right way (sammāpaṭipadā).
“The pursuit of self-mortification (attakilamathānuyoga) - painful (dukkho), ignoble (anārya), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhito) - is a state beset by suffering (sadukkho), vexation (saupaghāto), despair (saupāyāso), and fever (sapariḷāho); it is the wrong way (micchāpaṭipadā).
Disengagement from the pursuit of self-mortification (attakilamathānuyogaṃ) - painful (dukkhaṃ), ignoble (anārya), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhitaṃ) - is a state without suffering (adukkho), vexation (anupaghāto), despair (anupāyāso), and fever (apariḷāho); it is the right way (sammāpaṭipadā).
‘One should not pursue sensual pleasure (kāmasukha), which is low, vulgar, coarse (gammaṃ), ignoble (anārya), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhitaṃ); and one should not pursue self-mortification (attakilamathānuyogaṃ) which is painful (dukkhaṃ), ignoble (anārya), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhitaṃ)’ – So it was said. So it was with reference to this that it was said.
324.Kāmapaṭisandhisukhinoti kāmapaṭisandhinā kāmūpasaṃhitena sukhena sukhitassa. Sadukkhoti vipākadukkhena saṃkilesadukkhenapi sadukkho. Saupaghātoti vipākūpaghātakilesūpaghāteheva saupaghāto. Tathā sapariḷāho. Micchāpaṭipadāti ayāthāvapaṭipadā akusalapaṭipadā.
324.
kāmapaṭisandhisukhino means [one who is] happy with the rebirth of desire, [that is,] one who is gladdened by the pleasure associated with the rebirth of desire.
sadukkho means [one who is] with suffering, [namely,] with the suffering of result (vipāka-dukkha) and also with the suffering of defilement (saṃkilesa-dukkha).
saupaghāto means [one who is] with affliction, [namely,] just as with the afflictions of the affliction of result (vipāka-upaghāta-kilesūpaghāta).
Likewise, sapariḷāho [means one who is] with burning.
micchāpaṭipadā means wrong practice, unwholesome practice (akusalapaṭipadā).

325. ‘‘‘Ete kho ubho ante anupagamma majjhimā paṭipadā tathāgatena abhisambuddhā, cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattatī’ti – iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ. Kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? Ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi , sammāsaṅkappo, sammāvācā, sammākammanto, sammāājīvo, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati, sammāsamādhi. ‘Ete kho ubho ante anupagamma majjhimā paṭipadā tathāgatena abhisambuddhā, cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattatī’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ.
"‘Avoiding both of these extremes, the Middle Way was fully awakened to by the Tathāgata; it gives rise to vision, it gives rise to knowledge, it leads to peace, to direct knowledge, to full awakening, to Nibbāna.’ So it was said.

And with reference to what was this said? This noble eightfold path (ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo), namely: right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right thought (sammāsaṅkappo), right speech (sammāvācā), right action (sammākammanto), right livelihood (sammāājīvo), right effort (sammāvāyāmo), right mindfulness (sammāsati), right concentration (sammāsamādhi).

So it was with reference to this that it was said: ‘Avoiding both of these extremes, the Middle Way was fully awakened to by the Tathāgata; it gives rise to vision, it gives rise to knowledge, it leads to peace, to direct knowledge, to full awakening, to Nibbāna.’"

326. ‘‘‘Ussādanañca jaññā, apasādanañca jaññā; ussādanañca ñatvā apasādanañca ñatvā nevussādeyya, na apasādeyya, dhammameva deseyyā’ti – iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ. Kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? Kathañca, bhikkhave, ussādanā ca hoti apasādanā ca, no ca dhammadesanā? ‘Ye kāmapaṭisandhisukhino somanassānuyogaṃ anuyuttā hīnaṃ gammaṃ pothujjanikaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, sabbe te sadukkhā saupaghātā saupāyāsā sapariḷāhā micchāpaṭipannā’ti – iti vadaṃ iti paraṃ (ka.) ittheke apasādeti. ‘‘‘Ye kāmapaṭisandhisukhino somanassānuyogaṃ ananuyuttā hīnaṃ gammaṃ pothujjanikaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, sabbe te adukkhā anupaghātā anupāyāsā apariḷāhā sammāpaṭipannā’ti – iti vadaṃ ittheke ussādeti. ‘‘‘Ye attakilamathānuyogaṃ anuyuttā dukkhaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, sabbe te sadukkhā saupaghātā saupāyāsā sapariḷāhā micchāpaṭipannā’ti – iti vadaṃ ittheke apasādeti. ‘‘‘Ye attakilamathānuyogaṃ ananuyuttā dukkhaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, sabbe te adukkhā anupaghātā anupāyāsā apariḷāhā sammāpaṭipannā’ti – iti vadaṃ ittheke ussādeti. ‘‘‘Yesaṃ kesañci bhavasaṃyojanaṃ appahīnaṃ, sabbe te sadukkhā saupaghātā saupāyāsā sapariḷāhā micchāpaṭipannā’ti – iti vadaṃ ittheke apasādeti. ‘‘‘Yesaṃ kesañci bhavasaṃyojanaṃ pahīnaṃ, sabbe te adukkhā anupaghātā anupāyāsā apariḷāhā sammāpaṭipannā’ti – iti vadaṃ ittheke ussādeti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, ussādanā ca hoti apasādanā ca, no ca dhammadesanā.
326. “One should know what it is to extol (ussādana) and what it is to disparage (apasādana); and knowing what it is to extol and what it is to disparage, one should neither extol nor disparage, but should teach only the dharma.” So it was said.

What was said with reference to this? And how, bhikkhus, does extolling occur, and disparaging, and not teaching the dharma? "All those engaged in the pursuit of the enjoyment of one whose pleasure is linked to sensual desires—low, vulgar, worldly, ignoble, and unbeneficial—are beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and they have entered upon the wrong way," thus saying, some disparage.

“All those disengaged from the pursuit of the enjoyment of one whose pleasure is linked to sensual desires—low, vulgar, worldly, ignoble, and unbeneficial—are without suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and they have entered upon the right way," thus saying, some extol.

“All those engaged in the pursuit of self-mortification—painful, ignoble, and unbeneficial—are beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and they have entered upon the wrong way," thus saying, some disparage.

“All those disengaged from the pursuit of self-mortification—painful, ignoble, and unbeneficial—are without suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and they have entered upon the right way," thus saying, some extol.

“All those who have not abandoned the fetter of being are beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and they have entered upon the wrong way," thus saying, some disparage.

“All those who have abandoned the fetter of being are without suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and they have entered upon the right way," thus saying, some extol. Thus, bhikkhus, there is extolling and disparaging, and not teaching only the dharma.
326.Ittheke apasādetīti evaṃ gehasitavasena ekacce puggale apasādeti. Ussādanepi eseva nayo. Bhavasaṃyojananti bhavabandhanaṃ, taṇhāyetaṃ nāmaṃ. Subhūtitthero kira imaṃ catukkaṃ nissāya etadagge ṭhapito. Bhagavato hi dhammaṃ desentassa puggalānaṃ ussādanāapasādanā paññāyanti, tathā sāriputtattherādīnaṃ. Subhūtittherassa pana dhammadesanāya ‘‘ayaṃ puggalo appaṭipannako anārādhako’’ti vā, ‘‘ayaṃ sīlavā guṇavā lajjipesalo ācārasampanno’’ti vā natthi, dhammadesanāya panassa ‘‘ayaṃ micchāpaṭipadā, ayaṃ sammāpaṭipadā’’tveva paññāyati. Tasmā bhagavā ‘‘etadaggaṃ, bhikkhave, mama sāvakānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ araṇavihārīnaṃ yadidaṃ subhūtī’’ti āha.
326. "ittheke apasādetī" means thus he rejects some people due to attachment to the household life.

And the same rule applies in the case of uplifting.

"bhavasaṃyojana" means bond of existence, this is a name for craving (taṇhā).

The venerable Subhūti, relying on this tetrad, was established in the foremost position.

For the Blessed One, when teaching the Dharma, uplifting and downgrading of individuals are evident, and likewise for Sāriputta Thera and others.

But for Subhūti Thera, in teaching the Dharma, there is no "this person is not practicing, is not pleasing," or "this person is virtuous, has good qualities, is modest, is amiable, is accomplished in conduct," but in teaching the Dharma, only "this is wrong practice, this is right practice" is evident.

Therefore, the Blessed One said, "Monks, this Subhūti is the foremost of my disciples, monks who are forest-dwelling."

327. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, nevussādanā hoti na apasādanā, dhammadesanā ca dhammadesanāva (syā. kaṃ.)? ‘Ye kāmapaṭisandhisukhino somanassānuyogaṃ anuyuttā hīnaṃ gammaṃ pothujjanikaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, sabbe te sadukkhā saupaghātā saupāyāsā sapariḷāhā micchāpaṭipannā’ti – na evamāha. ‘Anuyogo ca kho, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā’ti – iti vadaṃ dhammameva deseti. ‘‘‘Ye kāmapaṭisandhisukhino somanassānuyogaṃ ananuyuttā hīnaṃ gammaṃ pothujjanikaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, sabbe te adukkhā anupaghātā anupāyāsā apariḷāhā sammāpaṭipannā’ti – na evamāha. ‘Ananuyogo ca kho, adukkho eso dhammo anupaghāto anupāyāso apariḷāho; sammāpaṭipadā’ti – iti vadaṃ dhammameva deseti. ‘‘‘Ye attakilamathānuyogaṃ anuyuttā dukkhaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, sabbe te sadukkhā saupaghātā saupāyāsā sapariḷāhā micchāpaṭipannā’ti – na evamāha. ‘Anuyogo ca kho , sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā’ti – iti vadaṃ dhammameva deseti. ‘‘‘Ye attakilamathānuyogaṃ ananuyuttā dukkhaṃ anariyaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ, sabbe te adukkhā anupaghātā anupāyāsā apariḷāhā sammāpaṭipannā’ti – na evamāha. ‘Ananuyogo ca kho, adukkho eso dhammo anupaghāto anupāyāso apariḷāho; sammāpaṭipadā’ti – iti vadaṃ dhammameva deseti. ‘‘‘Yesaṃ kesañci bhavasaṃyojanaṃ appahīnaṃ, sabbe te sadukkhā saupaghātā saupāyāsā sapariḷāhā micchāpaṭipannā’ti – na evamāha . ‘Bhavasaṃyojane ca kho appahīne bhavopi appahīno hotī’ti – iti vadaṃ dhammameva deseti. ‘‘‘Yesaṃ kesañci bhavasaṃyojanaṃ pahīnaṃ, sabbe te adukkhā anupaghātā anupāyāsā apariḷāhā sammāpaṭipannā’ti – na evamāha. ‘Bhavasaṃyojane ca kho pahīne bhavopi pahīno hotī’ti – iti vadaṃ dhammameva deseti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, nevussādanā hoti na apasādanā, dhammadesanā ca. ‘Ussādanañca jaññā, apasādanañca jaññā; ussādanañca ñatvā apasādanañca ñatvā nevussādeyya, na apasādeyya, dhammameva deseyyā’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ.
327. And how, monks, is there neither extolling nor disparaging, but only the teaching of the Dharma ( dharma)?

When one does not say: ‘All those who are attached to the pleasures associated with sensual rebirths, who are engaged in the pursuit of joy, are low, vulgar, common, ignoble, and unbeneficial, all of them are beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and have entered upon the wrong way,’

but instead says: ‘The pursuit is a state beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and it is the wrong way,’ then one teaches only the Dharma (dharma).

When one does not say: ‘All those who are disengaged from the pleasures associated with sensual rebirths, who are disengaged from the pursuit of joy, are low, vulgar, common, ignoble, and unbeneficial, all of them are without suffering, without vexation, without despair, without fever, and have entered upon the right way,’

but instead says: ‘The disengagement is a state without suffering, without vexation, without despair, without fever, and it is the right way,’ then one teaches only the Dharma (dharma).

When one does not say: ‘All those who are engaged in the pursuit of self-mortification are painful, ignoble, and unbeneficial; all of them are beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and have entered upon the wrong way,’

but instead says: ‘The pursuit is a state beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and it is the wrong way,’ then one teaches only the Dharma (dharma).

When one does not say: ‘All those who are disengaged from the pursuit of self-mortification are painful, ignoble, and unbeneficial; all of them are without suffering, without vexation, without despair, without fever, and have entered upon the right way,’

but instead says: ‘The disengagement is a state without suffering, without vexation, without despair, without fever, and it is the right way,’ then one teaches only the Dharma (dharma).

When one does not say: ‘All those who have not abandoned the fetter of being are beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and have entered upon the wrong way,’

but instead says: ‘As long as the fetter of being is unabandoned, being too is unabandoned,’ then one teaches only the Dharma (dharma).

When one does not say: ‘All those who have abandoned the fetter of being are without suffering, without vexation, without despair, without fever, and have entered upon the right way,’

but instead says: ‘When the fetter of being is abandoned, being also is abandoned,’ then one teaches only the Dharma (dharma).

Thus, monks, there is neither extolling nor disparaging, but only the teaching of the Dharma (dharma).

One should know what it is to extol and what it is to disparage, and knowing both, one should neither extol nor disparage, but should teach only the Dharma (dharma). So it was said with reference to this.

328. ‘‘‘Sukhavinicchayaṃ jaññā; sukhavinicchayaṃ ñatvā ajjhattaṃ sukhamanuyuñjeyyā’ti – iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ. Kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? Pañcime, bhikkhave, kāmaguṇā. Katame pañca? Cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā, sotaviññeyyā saddā… ghānaviññeyyā gandhā… jivhāviññeyyā rasā… kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā – ime kho, bhikkhave, pañca kāmaguṇā. Yaṃ kho, bhikkhave, ime pañca kāmaguṇe paṭicca uppajjati sukhaṃ somanassaṃ idaṃ vuccati kāmasukhaṃ mīḷhasukhaṃ puthujjanasukhaṃ anariyasukhaṃ. ‘Na āsevitabbaṃ, na bhāvetabbaṃ, na bahulīkātabbaṃ, bhāyitabbaṃ etassa sukhassā’ti – vadāmi. Idha , bhikkhave, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. 328. “One should know how to define pleasure (sukhavinicchayaṃ jaññā); knowing how to define pleasure, one should pursue pleasure within oneself (ajjhattaṃ sukhamanuyuñjeyyā’ti)’ So it was said.

For what reason was this said? These are the five cords of sensual pleasure (pañcime, bhikkhave, kāmaguṇā).

What are the five? Forms cognizable by the eye (cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā) that are wished for, desired, agreeable, pleasing, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust (iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā); Sounds cognizable by the ear (sotaviññeyyā saddā)… Odours cognizable by the nose (ghānaviññeyyā gandhā)… Flavours cognizable by the tongue (jivhāviññeyyā rasā)… Tangibles cognizable by the body (kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā) that are wished for, desired, agreeable, pleasing, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust (iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā) – these are the five cords of sensual pleasure (ime kho, bhikkhave, pañca kāmaguṇā).

Now, bhikkhus, whatever pleasure or happiness arises in dependence on these five strands of sensuality, that is called sensual pleasure (kāmasukhaṃ), filthy pleasure (mīḷhasukhaṃ), coarse pleasure (puthujjanasukhaṃ), ignoble pleasure (anariyasukhaṃ).

‘It should not be pursued (na āsevitabbaṃ), it should not be developed (na bhāvetabbaṃ), it should not be cultivated (na bahulīkātabbaṃ), it should be feared (bhāyitabbaṃ etassa sukhassā’ti)’ – I say.

Here, bhikkhus, quite secluded from sensual pleasures (kāmehi), secluded from unwholesome states (akusalehi dhammehi), a bhikkhu enters and dwells in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by thought and examination (savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ), with rapture and happiness born of seclusion (vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati).

With the subsiding of thinking and pondering (vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā), by gaining inner tranquility (ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ) and oneness of mind (cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ), he enters and remains in the second jhāna, which is without thinking and pondering (avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ), born of concentration (samādhijaṃ), filled with delight and joy (pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati).

With the fading away as well of rapture (pītiyā ca virāgā)… he abides in equanimity (upekkhako ca viharati)… the third jhāna (tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ)… the fourth jhāna (catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati).

This is called the bliss of renunciation (nekkhamma sukhaṃ), the bliss of seclusion (paviveka sukhaṃ), the bliss of peace (upasama sukhaṃ), the bliss of enlightenment (sambodhi sukhaṃ).

‘It should be pursued (āsevitabbaṃ), it should be developed (bhāvetabbaṃ), it should be cultivated (bahulīkātabbaṃ), it should not be feared (na bhāyitabbaṃ etassa sukhassā’ti)’ – I say.

‘One should know how to define pleasure (sukhavinicchayaṃ jaññā); knowing how to define pleasure, one should pursue pleasure within oneself (ajjhattaṃ sukhamanuyuñjeyyā’ti)’ – So it was with reference to this that it was said.

338. ‘‘Tatra, bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ rahovādo abhūto ataccho anatthasaṃhito, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo saraṇo. Tatra, bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ rahovādo bhūto taccho anatthasaṃhito, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo saraṇo. Tatra, bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ rahovādo bhūto taccho atthasaṃhito, adukkho eso dhammo anupaghāto anupāyāso apariḷāho; sammāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo araṇo.
338. “Here, bhikkhus, covert speech that is untrue (abhūta), incorrect (ataccha), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhita) is a state beset by suffering (sadukkha), vexation (saupaghāta), despair (saupāyāsa), and fever (sapariḷāha); it is wrong practice (micchāpaṭipadā).
Therefore, this is a state with conflict (saraṇo).
Here, bhikkhus, covert speech that is true (bhūta), correct (taccha), and unbeneficial (anarthasaṃhita) is a state beset by suffering (sadukkha), vexation (saupaghāta), despair (saupāyāsa), and fever (sapariḷāha); it is wrong practice (micchāpaṭipadā).
Therefore, this is a state with conflict (saraṇo).
Here, bhikkhus, covert speech that is true (bhūta), correct (taccha), and beneficial (arthasaṃhita) is a state without suffering (adukkha), vexation (anupaghāta), despair (anupāyāsa), and fever (apariḷāha); it is right practice (sammāpaṭipadā).
Therefore, this is a state without conflict (araṇo).

339. ‘‘Tatra , bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ sammukhā khīṇavādo abhūto ataccho anatthasaṃhito, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo saraṇo. Tatra, bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ sammukhā khīṇavādo bhūto taccho anatthasaṃhito, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo saraṇo. Tatra, bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ sammukhā khīṇavādo bhūto taccho atthasaṃhito, adukkho eso dhammo anupaghāto anupāyāso apariḷāho; sammāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo araṇo.
339.
"Here, bhikkhus, overt sharp speech that is untrue, incorrect, and unbeneficial (anatthasaṃhito) is a state beset by suffering (sadukkho), with conflict (saupaghāto), with vexation (saupāyāso), and with despair (sapariḷāho); [this is] wrong practice (micchāpaṭipadā).

Therefore this is a state with conflict (saraṇo).

Here, bhikkhus, overt sharp speech that is true, correct (taccho), and unbeneficial (anatthasaṃhito) is a state beset by suffering (sadukkho), with conflict (saupaghāto), with vexation (saupāyāso), and with despair (sapariḷāho); [this is] wrong practice (micchāpaṭipadā).

Therefore this is a state with conflict (saraṇo).

Here, bhikkhus, overt sharp speech that is true, correct (taccho), and beneficial (atthasaṃhito) is a state without suffering (adukkho), without conflict (anupaghāto), without vexation (anupāyāso), and without despair (apariḷāho); [this is] right practice (sammāpaṭipadā).

Therefore this is a state without conflict (araṇo).

340. ‘‘Tatra, bhikkhave, yamidaṃ taramānassa bhāsitaṃ, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo saraṇo. Tatra, bhikkhave , yamidaṃ ataramānassa bhāsitaṃ, adukkho eso dhammo anupaghāto anupāyāso apariḷāho; sammāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo araṇo.
Here, O monks, the speech of one who speaks hurriedly is a state beset by suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and it is the wrong way (mithyāpratipadā).

Therefore this is a state with conflict (saraṇo).

Here, O monks, the speech of one who does not speak hurriedly is a state without suffering, vexation, despair, and fever, and it is the right way (samyakpratipadā).

Therefore this is a state without conflict (araṇo).

341. ‘‘Tatra, bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ janapadaniruttiyā ca abhiniveso samaññāya ca atisāro, sadukkho eso dhammo saupaghāto saupāyāso sapariḷāho; micchāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo saraṇo. Tatra bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ janapadaniruttiyā ca anabhiniveso samaññāya ca anatisāro, adukkho eso dhammo anupaghāto anupāyāso apariḷāho; sammāpaṭipadā. Tasmā eso dhammo araṇo. ‘‘Tasmātiha, bhikkhave, ‘saraṇañca dhammaṃ jānissāma, araṇañca dhammaṃ jānissāma; saraṇañca dhammaṃ ñatvā araṇañca dhammaṃ ñatvā araṇapaṭipadaṃ paṭipajjissāmā’ti evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṃ. Subhūti ca pana, bhikkhave, kulaputto araṇapaṭipadaṃ paṭipanno’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Araṇavibhaṅgasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ navamaṃ.
341.
“Here, bhikkhus, insistence on local language and overriding of normal usage is a state beset by suffering ( sadukkho), conflict ( saupaghāto), despair ( saupāyāso), and fever ( sapariḷāho); [it is] wrong practice ( micchāpaṭipadā).

Therefore, this is a state with conflict ( saraṇo dhammo).

Here, bhikkhus, non-insistence on local language and non-overriding of normal usage is a state without suffering ( adukkho dhammo), conflict ( anupaghāto), despair ( anupāyāso), and fever ( apariḷāho); [it is] right practice ( sammāpaṭipadā).

Therefore, this is a state without conflict ( araṇo dhammo).

“Therefore, bhikkhus, you should train yourselves thus: ‘We shall know the state with conflict ( saraṇañca dhammaṃ jānissāma), and we shall know the state without conflict ( araṇañca dhammaṃ jānissāma); and knowing the state with conflict ( saraṇañca dhammaṃ ñatvā) and knowing the state without conflict ( araṇañca dhammaṃ ñatvā), we shall enter upon the way without conflict ( araṇapaṭipadaṃ paṭipajjissāmā).’” Thus, bhikkhus, you should train yourselves.

Now, bhikkhus, Subhūti is a clansman who has entered upon the way without conflict ( kulaputto araṇapaṭipadaṃ paṭipanno).”

This the Blessed One ( bhagavā) said.

Gratified, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s words ( bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti).

The Araṇavibhaṅgasutta, the ninth, is finished ( araṇavibhaṅgasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ navamaṃ).
