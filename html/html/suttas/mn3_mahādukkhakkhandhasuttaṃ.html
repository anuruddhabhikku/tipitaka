163. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho sambahulā bhikkhū pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pāvisiṃsu. Atha kho tesaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ etadahosi – ‘‘atippago kho tāva sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya carituṃ, yaṃ nūna mayaṃ yena aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ ārāmo tenupasaṅkameyyāmā’’ti. Atha kho te bhikkhū yena aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ ārāmo tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā tehi aññatitthiyehi paribbājakehi saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu; sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinne kho te bhikkhū te aññatitthiyā paribbājakā etadavocuṃ – ‘‘samaṇo, āvuso, gotamo kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapeti, mayampi kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapema; samaṇo, āvuso, gotamo rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapeti, mayampi rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapema; samaṇo, āvuso, gotamo vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapeti, mayampi vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapema; idha no, āvuso, ko viseso, ko adhippayāso, kiṃ nānākaraṇaṃ samaṇassa vā gotamassa amhākaṃ vā – yadidaṃ dhammadesanāya vā dhammadesanaṃ, anusāsaniyā vā anusāsani’’nti? Atha kho te bhikkhū tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ bhāsitaṃ neva abhinandiṃsu, nappaṭikkosiṃsu; anabhinanditvā appaṭikkositvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkamiṃsu – ‘‘bhagavato santike etassa bhāsitassa atthaṃ ājānissāmā’’ti.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Savatthi in Jeta’s Grove, Anathapindika’s Park.

Then a number of monks, having dressed in the morning, taking their bowl and robe, entered Savatthi for alms. Then the thought occurred to them, “It is still too early to walk for alms in Savatthi. What if we were to go to the park of the wanderers of other persuasions?”

Then those monks went to the park of the wanderers of other persuasions. Having arrived, they exchanged courteous greetings with the wanderers of other persuasions. After an exchange of friendly greetings and courtesies, they sat to one side.

As they were sitting there, the wanderers of other persuasions said to them, “Friends, Gotama the śramaṇa (ascetic) describes the full knowledge (pariññā) of sensual pleasures (kāma). We, too, describe the full knowledge of sensual pleasures. He describes the full knowledge of forms (rūpa). We, too, describe the full knowledge of forms. He describes the full knowledge of feelings (vedanā). We, too, describe the full knowledge of feelings. So what is the difference, what the distinction, what the distinguishing factor between him and us in terms of his teaching of the Dharma (dhamma-desanā) or his instruction (anusāsani)?”

Then those monks neither delighted in nor rejected the statement of those wanderers. Without delighting in it, without rejecting it, they rose from their seats and left, thinking, “We shall learn the meaning of this statement in the presence of the Blessed One.”

163. Thus I have heard: the Mahādukkhakkhandhasutta. There, according to the Vinaya method, three people are said to be "many," and beyond that, the Saṅgha. According to the Sūtra method, there are only three, and beyond that, they are said to be "many." Here, "many" should be understood according to the Sūtra method.

"Entered for alms" means they entered. However, they had not yet entered, but because they had left with the intention of entering, they are said to have "entered." Just as a person who has left intending to go to a village, even before reaching that village, if asked, "Where is so-and-so?" it is said, "He has gone to the village," in the same way.

"The hermitage of the wanderers" means that not far from Jetavana, there was a hermitage of wanderers belonging to other sects, and referring to that, they said this.

"Ascetic, friend," means, "Friend, your teacher is the ascetic Gotama."

"Understanding of sensual pleasures" means he teaches the abandoning and overcoming of sensual pleasures. The same method applies to forms and feelings as well.

There, knowing their own doctrine, the sectarians might teach the understanding of sensual pleasures, speaking of the first jhāna; they might teach the understanding of forms, speaking of the formless realm; they might teach the understanding of feelings, speaking of the realm of non-perception. However, they do not even know, "This is the first jhāna, this is the realm of form, this is the formless realm." Unable to teach, they only say, "We teach, we teach." The Tathāgata teaches the understanding of sensual pleasures with the path of the non-returner (anāgāmin), and the understanding of forms and feelings with the path of arahantship.

Even with such a great difference existing, they said, "Here, friend, what is the distinction?"

Here, "here" means in this teaching.

"Teaching of the Dharma or teaching of the Dharma" means, "What is it called when, in relation to the teaching of the Dharma of the ascetic Gotama, it is said that there is a difference between our teaching of the Dharma and theirs, or between their teaching of the Dharma and ours?" The same method applies to the second phrase as well. Thus, in the middle, like impure gold, they made their own doctrinal statement as pleasant as the teaching, merely through words.

"Neither rejoiced" means they did not accept it as "This is so."

"Nor rejected" means they did not deny it as "This is not so." Why? Because those sectarians are said to be like the blind, whether they speak knowing or not knowing, they neither rejoiced, and because there was a slight scent of the teaching in the word "understanding (pariñña)," they did not reject it. Or, the inhabitants of the region were not very skilled in their own and others' doctrines, so they did not do either.

164. Atha kho te bhikkhū sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya caritvā pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkantā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘idha mayaṃ, bhante, pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pāvisimha. Tesaṃ no, bhante, amhākaṃ etadahosi – ‘atippago kho tāva sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya carituṃ, yaṃ nūna mayaṃ yena aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ ārāmo tenupasaṅkameyyāmā’ti. Atha kho mayaṃ, bhante, yena aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ ārāmo tenupasaṅkamimha; upasaṅkamitvā tehi aññatitthiyehi paribbājakehi saddhiṃ sammodimha; sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdimha. Ekamantaṃ nisinne kho amhe, bhante, te aññatitthiyā paribbājakā etadavocuṃ – ‘samaṇo, āvuso, gotamo kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapeti, mayampi kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapema . Samaṇo, āvuso, gotamo rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapeti, mayampi rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapema. Samaṇo, āvuso, gotamo vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapeti, mayampi vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññapema. Idha no, āvuso, ko viseso, ko adhippayāso, kiṃ nānākaraṇaṃ samaṇassa vā gotamassa amhākaṃ vā, yadidaṃ dhammadesanāya vā dhammadesanaṃ anusāsaniyā vā anusāsani’nti. Atha kho mayaṃ, bhante, tesaṃ aññatitthiyānaṃ paribbājakānaṃ bhāsitaṃ neva abhinandimha, nappaṭikkosimha; anabhinanditvā appaṭikkositvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkamimha – ‘bhagavato santike etassa bhāsitassa atthaṃ ājānissāmā’’’ti.
Then, having gone for alms in Sāvatthī, after their meal, returning from their alms round, the monks went to the Blessed One and, on arrival, having bowed down to him, sat to one side.

Sitting to one side, the monks said this to the Blessed One: “Here, venerable sir, in the forenoon, having dressed, taking our bowl and robe, we entered Sāvatthī for alms. Then, venerable sir, this thought occurred to us: ‘It’s still too early to go into Sāvatthī for alms. What if we were to visit the park of the wanderers of other persuasions?’ Then, venerable sir, we went to the park of the wanderers of other persuasions. On arrival, we exchanged courteous greetings with those wanderers of other persuasions. After an exchange of friendly greetings & courtesies, we sat to one side. As we were sitting there, venerable sir, those wanderers of other persuasions said to us, ‘Friends, Gotama the contemplative describes the comprehension of sensuality (kāmānaṃ parijñā). We, too, describe the comprehension of sensuality. Gotama describes the comprehension of forms (rūpānaṃ parijñā). We, too, describe the comprehension of forms. Gotama describes the comprehension of feelings (vedanānaṃ parijñā). We, too, describe the comprehension of feelings. So what is the difference, friends, what the distinction, what the distinguishing factor between Gotama the contemplative and us in terms of his teaching of the Dhamma or the teaching of the Dhamma, in terms of his instruction or instruction?’ Then, venerable sir, we neither delighted in nor rejected the statement of those wanderers of other persuasions. Without delighting in it, without rejecting it, we rose from our seats and left, thinking, ‘We shall learn the meaning of this statement in the presence of the Blessed One.’”

165. ‘‘Evaṃvādino, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evamassu vacanīyā – ‘ko panāvuso, kāmānaṃ assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇaṃ? Ko rūpānaṃ assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇaṃ? Ko vedanānaṃ assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇa’nti? Evaṃ puṭṭhā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā na ceva sampāyissanti, uttariñca vighātaṃ āpajjissanti. Taṃ kissa hetu? Yathā taṃ, bhikkhave, avisayasmiṃ. Nāhaṃ taṃ, bhikkhave, passāmi sadevake loke samārake sabrahmake sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya yo imesaṃ pañhānaṃ veyyākaraṇena cittaṃ ārādheyya, aññatra tathāgatena vā tathāgatasāvakena vā, ito vā pana sutvā.
165. Bhikkhus, other wanderers of other sects who speak thus should be addressed in this way: ‘But, friends, what is the gratification (assāda) in sense pleasures (kāma)? What is the danger (ādīnava)? What is the escape (nissaraṇa)? What is the gratification in forms (rūpa)? What is the danger? What is the escape? What is the gratification in feelings (vedanā)? What is the danger? What is the escape?’ Bhikkhus, when asked thus, the other wanderers of other sects will not be able to satisfy [you], and will, moreover, incur frustration.

Why is that? Because, bhikkhus, that is not within their domain (avisayasmiṃ).

Bhikkhus, I do not see anyone in this world with its devas, Māras, and Brahmās, in this generation with its ascetics and brāhmaṇas, its princes and its people, who could satisfy the mind (cittaṃ ārādheyya) with an answer to these questions, except the Tathāgata or a disciple (sāvaka) of the Tathāgata, or one who has heard it from them.
165.Na ceva sampāyissantīti sampādetvā kathetuṃ na sakkhissanti. Uttariñca vighātanti asampāyanato uttarimpi dukkhaṃ āpajjissanti. Sampādetvā kathetuṃ asakkontānaṃ nāma hi dukkhaṃ uppajjati. Yathā taṃ, bhikkhave, avisayasminti ettha yathāti kāraṇavacanaṃ, tanti nipātamattaṃ. Yasmā avisaye pañho pucchito hotīti attho. Sadevaketi saha devehi sadevake. Samārakādīsupi eseva nayo. Evaṃ tīṇi ṭhānāni loke pakkhipitvā dve pajāyāti pañcahipi sattalokameva pariyādiyitvā etasmiṃ sadevakādibhede loke taṃ devaṃ vā manussaṃ vā na passāmīti dīpeti. Ito vā pana sutvāti ito vā pana mama sāsanato sutvā atathāgatopi atathāgatasāvakopi ārādheyya paritoseyya. Aññathā ārādhanaṃ nāma natthīti dasseti.
"na ceva sampāyissantī" means they will not accomplish, i.e., they will not be able to accomplish and tell.

"uttariñca vighāta" means from non-accomplishment, further suffering will befall them.

Indeed, suffering arises for those who are unable to accomplish and tell.

"yathā taṃ, bhikkhave, avisayasmi" means, "Just as that, monks, in the non-sphere..." Here, "yathā" means a reason, and "ta" is merely a particle.

Because in the non-sphere, a question is asked, this is the meaning.

"sadevake" means with the gods, in the world with the gods.

"samārakā" and so on, this is the same method.

Thus, having placed the three locations in the world, two are born; even with the five, having completely taken hold of the world of beings, in this world divided into gods and so on, he illuminates that he does not see that god or human.

"ito vā pana sutvā" means, having heard from here, or having heard from my teaching, even a non-Tathāgata or a disciple of a non-Tathāgata would be able to please and satisfy [him].

Otherwise, he shows that there is no such thing as worship.

Cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā, sotaviññeyyā saddā…pe… ghānaviññeyyā gandhā … jivhāviññeyyā rasā… kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā – ime kho, bhikkhave, pañca kāmaguṇā. Yaṃ kho, bhikkhave, ime pañca kāmaguṇe paṭicca uppajjati sukhaṃ somanassaṃ – ayaṃ kāmānaṃ assādo.
166. Now what, monks, is the allure of sensuality? These, monks, are the five cords of sensual pleasure (pañca kāmaguṇā).

What are the five? Forms cognizable by the eye that are wished for, desired, agreeable, gratifying, arousing desire, sounds heard by the ear… smells smelt by the nose… tastes savored by the tongue… contacts felt by the body that are agreeable,. . .arousing desire - these, monks, are the five cords of sensual pleasure.

Now whatever pleasure and happiness arises dependent on these five cords of sensual pleasure, that is the allure of sensuality.

166. Now, showing how to delight the mind by explaining those questions, he says, "What, monks?" and so on.

Kāmaguṇā: kāmā because of the meaning of being desirable. Guṇā because of the meaning of binding.

"I allow, monks, a double saṅghāṭi of unhemmed cloths" (Mahāva. 348). Here, guṇa has the meaning of a layer.

"Times pass and nights speed on, the qualities of age gradually abandon [one]" (Saṃ. Ni. 1.4). Here, guṇa has the meaning of a collection.

"A hundredfold merit is to be expected from a donation" (Ma. Ni. 3.379). Here, guṇa has the meaning of benefit.

"One should make many garland-bindings, having an end with a binding end (Khu. Pā. 3 Dvattiṃsākāre; Dī. Ni. 2.377)" (Dha. Pa. 53). Here, guṇa has the meaning of binding.

Here also, this same meaning is intended; therefore, it was said, "Guṇā because of the meaning of binding."

Cakkhuviññeyyā: to be seen by eye-consciousness (cakṣurvijñāna). In this way, the meaning should be understood in sotaviññeyyā and the rest as well.

Iṭṭhā: whether they are very desirable or not, it means being the object of desirable sense objects (iṣṭhārammaṇabhūta).

Kantā: lovely.

Manāpā: pleasing to the mind.

Piyarūpā: of a lovable nature.

Kāmūpasaṃhitā: associated with desire arising by making [something] an object.

Rajanīyā: causing passion, it means being the cause of the arising of lust (rāguppattikāraṇabhūta).

167. ‘‘Ko ca, bhikkhave, kāmānaṃ ādīnavo? Idha, bhikkhave, kulaputto yena sippaṭṭhānena jīvikaṃ kappeti – yadi muddāya yadi gaṇanāya yadi saṅkhānena saṅkhāya (ka.) yadi kasiyā yadi vaṇijjāya yadi gorakkhena yadi issatthena yadi rājaporisena yadi sippaññatarena – sītassa purakkhato uṇhassa purakkhato ḍaṃsamakasavātātapasarīṃsapasamphassehi rissamāno īrayamāno (ka.), samphassamāno (cūḷani. khaggavisāṇasutta 136) khuppipāsāya mīyamāno; ayampi, bhikkhave, kāmānaṃ ādīnavo sandiṭṭhiko, dukkhakkhandho kāmahetu kāmanidānaṃ kāmādhikaraṇaṃ kāmānameva hetu. ‘‘Tassa ce, bhikkhave, kulaputtassa evaṃ uṭṭhahato ghaṭato vāyamato te bhogā nābhinipphajjanti. Ayampi, bhikkhave, kāmānaṃ ādīnavo sandiṭṭhiko dukkhakkhandho kāmahetu kāmanidānaṃ kāmādhikaraṇaṃ kāmānameva hetu. ‘‘Tassa ce, bhikkhave, kulaputtassa evaṃ uṭṭhahato ghaṭato vāyamato te bhogā abhinipphajjanti. So tesaṃ bhogānaṃ ārakkhādhikaraṇaṃ dukkhaṃ domanassaṃ paṭisaṃvedeti – ‘kinti me bhoge neva rājāno hareyyuṃ, na corā hareyyuṃ, na aggi daheyya, na udakaṃ vaheyya vāheyya (ka.), na appiyā dāyādā hareyyu’nti. “Again, monks, one might see that very same woman as a corpse cast away in a charnel ground—a skeleton with flesh and blood, held together with sinews, a skeleton smeared with flesh and blood, connected with tendons, a skeleton without flesh or blood, connected with tendons, bones detached from their tendons, scattered in all directions—here a hand bone (hatthaṭṭhikaṃ), there a foot bone (pādaṭṭhikaṃ), here an ankle bone (gop hakaṭṭhikaṃ), there a shin bone (jaṅghaṭṭhikaṃ), here a thigh bone (ūruṭṭhikaṃ), here a hip bone (kaṭiṭṭhikaṃ), here a rib (phāsukaṭṭhikaṃ), there a back bone (piṭṭhiṭṭhikaṃ), here a shoulder bone (kandhaṭṭhikaṃ), there a neck bone (gīvaṭṭhikaṃ), here a jaw bone (hanukaṭṭhikaṃ), there a tooth (dantaṭṭhikaṃ), here a skull (sīsakaṭāhaṃ).

What do you think, monks? Has her earlier beauty and charm vanished, and the drawback appeared? ‘Yes, venerable sir.’

This too, monks, is the drawback of forms (rūpānaṃ ādīnavo).

“Again, monks, one might see that very same woman as a corpse cast away in a charnel ground—bones bleached white, the color of shells, bones heaped up, more than a year old, bones rotted and crumbled to dust.

What do you think, monks? Has her earlier beauty and charm vanished, and the drawback appeared? ‘Yes, venerable sir.’

This too, monks, is the drawback of forms (rūpānaṃ ādīnavo).

“And what, monks, is the escape from forms? It is, monks, the subduing of desire and passion, the abandoning of desire and passion in regard to forms – this is the escape from forms (rūpānaṃ nissaraṇaṃ).

“Monks, those ascetics and Brahmins who do not truly understand the allure in allure, the drawbacks in drawbacks, and the escape in escape with regards to forms, they are not able to fully comprehend forms by themselves, and they cannot establish others in suchness so that those who practice will fully comprehend forms. That is not possible. But, monks, those ascetics and Brahmins who truly understand the allure in allure, the drawbacks in drawbacks, and the escape in escape with regards to forms, they are able to fully comprehend forms by themselves, and they can establish others in suchness so that those who practice will fully comprehend forms. That is possible.

173. ‘‘Ko ca, bhikkhave, vedanānaṃ assādo? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Yasmiṃ samaye, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati, neva tasmiṃ samaye attabyābādhāyapi ceteti, na parabyābādhāyapi ceteti, na ubhayabyābādhāyapi ceteti ; abyābajjhaṃyeva tasmiṃ samaye vedanaṃ vedeti. Abyābajjhaparamāhaṃ, bhikkhave, vedanānaṃ assādaṃ vadāmi. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati…pe… yasmiṃ samaye, bhikkhave, bhikkhu pītiyā ca virāgā, upekkhako ca viharati, sato ca sampajāno sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṃvedeti yaṃ taṃ ariyā ācikkhanti – ‘upekkhako satimā sukhavihārī’ti tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati…pe… yasmiṃ samaye, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sukhassa ca pahānā dukkhassa ca pahānā pubbeva somanassadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamā adukkhamasukhaṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati, neva tasmiṃ samaye attabyābādhāyapi ceteti, na parabyābādhāyapi ceteti, na ubhayabyābādhāyapi ceteti; abyābajjhaṃyeva tasmiṃ samaye vedanaṃ vedeti. Abyābajjhaparamāhaṃ, bhikkhave, vedanānaṃ assādaṃ vadāmi.
173. What, monks, is the allure of feelings (vedanā)? Here, monks, a monk, quite secluded from sense-desires (kāmehi), secluded from unwholesome mental states (akusalehi dhammehi), enters and remains in the first jhāna: rapture (pīti) & pleasure (sukha) born from seclusion, accompanied by directed thought (vitakka) & evaluation (vicāra).

At a moment when, monks, a monk, quite secluded from sense-desires, secluded from unwholesome mental states, enters and remains in the first jhāna: rapture & pleasure born from seclusion, accompanied by directed thought & evaluation, at that time he does not intend his own affliction, the affliction of others, or the affliction of both; He feels a feeling (vedanā) totally unafflicted.

The unafflicted, monks, I tell you, is the highest allure of feelings (vedanānam assādaṃ).

Again, monks, with the stilling of directed thoughts (vitakka) & evaluations (vicāra), internal assurance, unification of awareness, enters & remains in the second jhāna: rapture (pīti) & pleasure (sukha) born of concentration (samādhi), without directed thought & evaluation…

At a moment when, monks, a monk, with the fading of rapture (pītiyā), remains equanimous (upekkhako), mindful (sato) & alert (sampajāno), and senses pleasure (sukha) with the body (kāyena). The noble ones declare, ‘Equanimous & mindful, he has a pleasant abiding’… He enters & remains in the third jhāna.

At a moment when, monks, a monk, with the abandoning of pleasure (sukha) & the abandoning of pain (dukkhassa), as well as the previous disappearance of joy (somanassa) & sorrow (domanassa), enters & remains in the fourth jhāna: purity of equanimity (upekkhā) & mindfulness (sati), which is neither pleasure nor pain. At that time he does not intend his own affliction, the affliction of others, or the affliction of both. He feels a feeling totally unafflicted.

The unafflicted, monks, I tell you, is the highest allure of feelings (vedanānam assādaṃ).
173.Neva tasmiṃ samaye attabyābādhāyāti tasmiṃ samaye attanopi dukkhatthāya na ceteti. Abyābajjhaṃyevāti niddukkhameva .
"At that time, one does not consider it to be for the sake of harming oneself, nor does one think that suffering is for oneself. [One thinks,] 'It is indeed without affliction,' that is, truly without suffering."

174. ‘‘Ko ca, bhikkhave, vedanānaṃ ādīnavo? Yaṃ, bhikkhave, vedanā aniccā dukkhā vipariṇāmadhammā – ayaṃ vedanānaṃ ādīnavo. ‘‘Kiñca, bhikkhave, vedanānaṃ nissaraṇaṃ? Yo, bhikkhave, vedanāsu chandarāgavinayo, chandarāgappahānaṃ – idaṃ vedanānaṃ nissaraṇaṃ. ‘‘Ye hi keci, bhikkhave, samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā evaṃ vedanānaṃ assādañca assādato ādīnavañca ādīnavato nissaraṇañca nissaraṇato yathābhūtaṃ nappajānanti, te vata sāmaṃ vā vedanaṃ parijānissanti, paraṃ vā tathattāya samādapessanti yathā paṭipanno vedanaṃ parijānissatīti – netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati. Ye ca kho keci, bhikkhave, samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā evaṃ vedanānaṃ assādañca assādato ādīnavañca ādīnavato nissaraṇañca nissaraṇato yathābhūtaṃ pajānanti te vata sāmaṃ vā vedanaṃ parijānissanti, paraṃ vā tathattāya samādapessanti yathā paṭipanno vedanaṃ parijānissatīti – ṭhānametaṃ vijjatī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Mahādukkhakkhandhasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ tatiyaṃ.
174. “And what, monks, is the unsatisfactoriness (ādīnava) of feelings (vedanā)? The fact that feeling is inconstant (aniccā), suffering ( dukkhā), subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammā) - this is the unsatisfactoriness of feelings.

“And what, monks, is the escape (nissaraṇaṃ) from feelings? The subduing of desire and lust (chandarāgavinayo) in regard to feelings, the abandonment of desire and lust (chandarāgappahānaṃ) - this is the escape from feelings.

“Monks, any ascetics (samaṇā) or Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) who do not truly understand the allure (assāda) of feelings as allure, the unsatisfactoriness (ādīnava) of feelings as unsatisfactoriness, and the escape (nissaraṇaṃ) from feelings as escape, would not themselves comprehend feeling, nor would they rouse another with the truth so that, in line with what he has practiced, he would comprehend feeling: That is impossible.

But monks, any ascetics or Brahmins who truly understand the allure of feelings as allure, the unsatisfactoriness of feelings as unsatisfactoriness, and the escape from feelings as escape, would themselves comprehend feeling, and they would rouse another with the truth so that, in line with what he has practiced, he would comprehend feeling: That is possible.”

This is what the Blessed One said.

Elated, those monks delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Mahādukkhakkhandhasutta, the third, is finished.
174.Yaṃ, bhikkhave, vedanā aniccāti, bhikkhave, yasmā vedanā aniccā, tasmā ayaṃ aniccādiākārova vedanāya ādīnavoti attho, nissaraṇaṃ vuttappakāramevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Mahādukkhakkhandhasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.

"Whatever feeling (vedanā), bhikkhus, is impermanent (aniccā)," because whatever feeling, bhikkhus, is impermanent, therefore this aspect, beginning with impermanence, is the misery (ādīnava) of feeling, such is the meaning. Escape (nissaraṇa) is just as it was said.
