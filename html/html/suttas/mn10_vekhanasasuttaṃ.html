278. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho vekhanaso vekhanasso (sī. pī.) paribbājako yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho vekhanaso paribbājako bhagavato santike udānaṃ udānesi – ‘‘ayaṃ paramo vaṇṇo, ayaṃ paramo vaṇṇo’’ti. ‘‘Kiṃ pana tvaṃ, kaccāna, evaṃ vadesi – ‘ayaṃ paramo vaṇṇo, ayaṃ paramo vaṇṇo’ti? Katamo, kaccāna, so paramo vaṇṇo’’ti? ‘‘Yasmā, bho gotama, vaṇṇā añño vaṇṇo uttaritaro vā paṇītataro vā natthi so paramo vaṇṇo’’ti. ‘‘Katamo pana so, kaccāna, vaṇṇo yasmā vaṇṇā añño vaṇṇo uttaritaro vā paṇītataro vā natthī’’ti? ‘‘Yasmā, bho gotama, vaṇṇā añño vaṇṇo uttaritaro vā paṇītataro vā natthi so paramo vaṇṇo’’ti. ‘‘Dīghāpi kho te esā, kaccāna, phareyya – ‘yasmā, bho gotama, vaṇṇā añño vaṇṇo uttaritaro vā paṇītataro vā natthi so paramo vaṇṇo’ti vadesi, tañca vaṇṇaṃ na paññapesi. Seyyathāpi, kaccāna, puriso evaṃ vadeyya – ‘ahaṃ yā imasmiṃ janapade janapadakalyāṇī, taṃ icchāmi taṃ kāmemī’ti. Tamenaṃ evaṃ vadeyyuṃ – ‘ambho purisa, yaṃ tvaṃ janapadakalyāṇiṃ icchasi kāmesi, jānāsi taṃ janapadakalyāṇiṃ – khattiyī vā brāhmaṇī vā vessī vā suddī vā’ti? Iti puṭṭho ‘no’ti vadeyya. Tamenaṃ evaṃ vadeyyuṃ – ‘ambho purisa, yaṃ tvaṃ janapadakalyāṇiṃ icchasi kāmesi, jānāsi taṃ janapadakalyāṇiṃ ‘evaṃnāmā evaṃgottāti vāti…pe… dīghā vā rassā vā majjhimā vā kāḷī vā sāmā vā maṅguracchavī vāti… amukasmiṃ gāme vā nigame vā nagare vā’ti? Iti puṭṭho ‘no’ti vadeyya. Tamenaṃ evaṃ vadeyyuṃ – ‘ambho purisa, yaṃ tvaṃ na jānāsi na passasi, taṃ tvaṃ icchasi kāmesī’’’ti? Iti puṭṭho ‘āmā’ti vadeyya. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kaccāna, nanu evaṃ sante tassa purisassa appāṭihīrakataṃ bhāsitaṃ sampajjatī’’ti? ‘‘Addhā kho, bho gotama, evaṃ sante tassa purisassa appāṭihīrakataṃ bhāsitaṃ sampajjatī’’ti. ‘‘Evameva kho tvaṃ, kaccāna, ‘yasmā, bho gotama, vaṇṇā añño vaṇṇo uttaritaro vā paṇītataro vā natthi so paramo vaṇṇo’ti vadesi; tañca vaṇṇaṃ na paññapesī’’ti. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, maṇi veḷuriyo subho jātimā aṭṭhaṃso suparikammakato paṇḍukambale nikkhitto bhāsate ca tapate ca virocati ca, evaṃ vaṇṇo attā hoti arogo paraṃ maraṇā’’ti.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthī in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's Park.

Then the wanderer Vekhanasa went to the Blessed One and exchanged greetings with him. Having concluded the exchange of courteous and amiable greetings, he stood to one side. Standing to one side, the wanderer Vekhanasa exclaimed to the Blessed One, "This is the ultimate splendor, this is the ultimate splendor!"

"But Kaccāna, why do you say thus: 'This is the ultimate splendor, this is the ultimate splendor'? What, Kaccāna, is that ultimate splendor?"

"Because, Master Gotama, there is no other splendor that is higher or more excellent than that splendor; that is the ultimate splendor."

"But what, Kaccāna, is that splendor, than which there is no other splendor higher or more excellent?"

"Because, Master Gotama, there is no other splendor that is higher or more excellent than that splendor; that is the ultimate splendor."

"Kaccāna, you might continue in this way for a long time. You say: 'Because, Master Gotama, there is no other splendor that is higher or more excellent than that splendor; that is the ultimate splendor,' but you do not point out that splendor. Just as, Kaccāna, a man might say: 'I desire and love the most beautiful woman in this country.' Then they might say to him: 'Good man, this most beautiful woman in this country whom you desire and love, do you know whether she is a noblewoman, a Brahmin woman, a merchant woman, or a worker woman?' If so asked, he would say, 'No.' Then they might say to him: 'Good man, this most beautiful woman in this country whom you desire and love, do you know her name, her clan…whether she is tall, short, or of medium height…whether she is dark, brown or golden-skinned…in what particular village, town, or city she lives?' If so asked, he would say, 'No.' Then they might say to him: 'Good man, you do not know or see her, and yet you desire and love her'?" If so asked, he would say, "Yes."

"What do you think, Kaccāna, would not the speech of that man in such a case be groundless?" "Assuredly, Master Gotama, the speech of that man in such a case would be groundless." "Just so, Kaccāna, you say: 'Because, Master Gotama, there is no other splendor that is higher or more excellent than that splendor; that is the ultimate splendor,' but you do not point out that splendor."

"Just as, Master Gotama, a beryl gem, pure, of good quality, eight-faceted, well-cut, placed on a woolen blanket, would shine and radiate and glow; even so, the self (ātman) is of such splendor, free from disease (aroga), beyond death (maraṇā)."
278.Evaṃme sutanti vekhanasasuttaṃ. Tattha vekhanasoti ayaṃ kira sakuludāyissa ācariyo, so ‘‘sakuludāyī paribbājako paramavaṇṇapañhe parājito’’ti sutvā ‘‘mayā so sādhukaṃ uggahāpito, tenāpi sādhukaṃ uggahitaṃ, kathaṃ nu kho parājito, handāhaṃ sayaṃ gantvā samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ paramavaṇṇapañhaṃ pucchitvā jānissāmī’’ti rājagahato pañcacattālīsayojanaṃ sāvatthiṃ gantvā yena bhagavā, tenupasaṅkami, upasaṅkamitvā pana ṭhitakova bhagavato santike udānaṃ udānesi. Tattha purimasadisaṃ vuttanayeneva veditabbaṃ.
278. Evaṃ me sutaṃ is the Vekhanasa Sutta.

There, vekhanaso means: This was, they say, the teacher of Sakuludāyī. Having heard that "Sakuludāyī the wanderer has been defeated in the debate about the highest beauty," he thought, "I had him learn it well, and he also learned it well. How then has he been defeated? Come, I will go myself and ask the ascetic Gotama about the question of the highest beauty and find out." So he went from Rājagaha to Sāvatthī, a distance of forty-five yojanas, and approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he stood there and uttered an exclamation in the presence of the Blessed One.

There, what is similar to the former should be understood in the way it was said.

279. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kaccāna, yo vā maṇi veḷuriyo subho jātimā aṭṭhaṃso suparikammakato paṇḍukambale nikkhitto bhāsate ca tapate ca virocati ca, yo vā rattandhakāratimisāya kimi khajjopanako imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ katamo vaṇṇo abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti? ‘‘Yvāyaṃ, bho gotama, rattandhakāratimisāya kimi khajjopanako, ayaṃ imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kaccāna, yo vā rattandhakāratimisāya kimi khajjopanako, yo vā rattandhakāratimisāya telappadīpo, imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ katamo vaṇṇo abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti? ‘‘Yvāyaṃ, bho gotama, rattandhakāratimisāya telappadīpo, ayaṃ imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kaccāna, yo vā rattandhakāratimisāya telappadīpo, yo vā rattandhakāratimisāya mahāaggikkhandho, imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ katamo vaṇṇo abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti? ‘‘Yvāyaṃ, bho gotama, rattandhakāratimisāya mahāaggikkhandho, ayaṃ imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kaccāna, yo vā rattandhakāratimisāya mahāaggikkhandho, yā vā rattiyā paccūsasamayaṃ viddhe vigatavalāhake deve osadhitārakā, imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ katamo vaṇṇo abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti? ‘‘Yvāyaṃ, bho gotama, rattiyā paccūsasamayaṃ viddhe vigatavalāhake deve osadhitārakā, ayaṃ imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kaccāna, yā vā rattiyā paccūsasamayaṃ viddhe vigatavalāhake deve osadhitārakā, yo vā tadahuposathe pannarase viddhe vigatavalāhake deve abhido aḍḍharattasamayaṃ cando, imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ katamo vaṇṇo abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti? ‘‘Yvāyaṃ, bho gotama, tadahuposathe pannarase viddhe vigatavalāhake deve abhido aḍḍharattasamayaṃ cando, ayaṃ imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kaccāna, yo vā tadahuposathe pannarase viddhe vigatavalāhake deve abhido aḍḍharattasamayaṃ cando, yo vā vassānaṃ pacchime māse saradasamaye viddhe vigatavalāhake deve abhido majjhanhikasamayaṃ sūriyo, imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ katamo vaṇṇo abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti? ‘‘Yvāyaṃ, bho gotama, vassānaṃ pacchime māse saradasamaye viddhe vigatavalāhake deve abhido majjhanhikasamayaṃ sūriyo – ayaṃ imesaṃ ubhinnaṃ vaṇṇānaṃ abhikkantataro ca paṇītataro cā’’ti. ‘‘Ato kho te, kaccāna, bahū hi bahutarā devā ye imesaṃ candimasūriyānaṃ ābhā nānubhonti, tyāhaṃ pajānāmi. Atha ca panāhaṃ na vadāmi – ‘yasmā vaṇṇā añño vaṇṇo uttaritaro ca paṇītataro ca natthī’ti. Atha ca pana tvaṃ, kaccāna, ‘yvāyaṃ vaṇṇo kiminā khajjopanakena nihīnataro ca patikiṭṭhataro ca so paramo vaṇṇo’ti vadesi; tañca vaṇṇaṃ na paññapesi’’.
279. “What do you think, Kaccāna? Which of these two is better: A beautiful beryl gem (veḷuriya maṇi) of fine quality, eight-faceted, well-cut, placed on a yellow woolen blanket, which shines, glows, and radiates, or a glowworm (khajjopanaka) in the thick darkness of the night (rattandhakāratimisāya)? Which of these two kinds of splendor (varṇa) is more excellent (abhikkantataro) and sublime (paṇītataro)?”

“Venerable Gotama (bho Gotama), this glowworm in the thick darkness of the night is the more excellent and sublime of these two kinds of splendor.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna? Which of these two is better: A glowworm in the thick darkness of the night, or an oil lamp (telappadīpo) in the thick darkness of the night? Which of these two kinds of splendor is more excellent and sublime?”

“Venerable Gotama, this oil lamp in the thick darkness of the night is the more excellent and sublime of these two kinds of splendor.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna? Which of these two is better: An oil lamp in the thick darkness of the night, or a great bonfire (mahāaggikkhandho) in the thick darkness of the night? Which of these two kinds of splendor is more excellent and sublime?”

“Venerable Gotama, this great bonfire in the thick darkness of the night is the more excellent and sublime of these two kinds of splendor.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna? Which of these two is better: A great bonfire in the thick darkness of the night, or the morning star (osadhitārakā) at dawn (paccūsasamayaṃ) in a clear, cloudless (vigatavalāhake) sky? Which of these two kinds of splendor is more excellent and sublime?”

“Venerable Gotama, this morning star at dawn in a clear, cloudless sky is the more excellent and sublime of these two kinds of splendor.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna? Which of these two is better: The morning star at dawn in a clear, cloudless sky, or the moon (cando) at midnight (aḍḍharattasamayaṃ) on the fifteenth, the day of the uposatha, in a clear, cloudless sky? Which of these two kinds of splendor is more excellent and sublime?”

“Venerable Gotama, this moon at midnight on the fifteenth, the day of the uposatha, in a clear, cloudless sky is the more excellent and sublime of these two kinds of splendor.”

“What do you think, Kaccāna? Which of these two is better: The moon at midnight on the fifteenth, the day of the uposatha, in a clear, cloudless sky, or the sun (sūriyo) at midday (majjhanhikasamayaṃ) in autumn (saradasamaye), in the last month (pacchime māse) of the rainy season (vassānaṃ), in a clear, cloudless sky? Which of these two kinds of splendor is more excellent and sublime?”

“Venerable Gotama, this sun at midday in autumn, in the last month of the rainy season, in a clear, cloudless sky, is the more excellent and sublime of these two kinds of splendor.”

“Therefore, Kaccāna, there are very many gods (devā) whose splendor (ābhā) is not experienced (nānubhonti) by these sun and moon. I know (pajānāmi) this.

Furthermore, I do not say that there is no other splendor higher or more sublime than this splendor.

But you, Kaccāna, say of that splendor which is lower and meaner than a glowworm’s: ‘This is the supreme splendor,’ yet you do not indicate what that splendor is.”

280. ‘‘Pañca kho ime, kaccāna, kāmaguṇā. Katame pañca? Cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā, sotaviññeyyā saddā…pe… ghānaviññeyyā gandhā… jivhāviññeyyā rasā… kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā – ime kho, kaccāna, pañca kāmaguṇā. Yaṃ kho, kaccāna, ime pañca kāmaguṇe paṭicca uppajjati sukhaṃ somanassaṃ idaṃ vuccati kāmasukhaṃ. Iti kāmehi kāmasukhaṃ, kāmasukhā kāmaggasukhaṃ tattha aggamakkhāyatī’’ti. Evaṃ vutte, vekhanaso paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘acchariyaṃ, bho gotama, abbhutaṃ, bho gotama! Yāva subhāsitaṃ cidaṃ bhotā gotamena – ‘kāmehi kāmasukhaṃ, kāmasukhā kāmaggasukhaṃ tattha aggamakkhāyatī’ti. (‘Kāmehi, bho gotama, kāmasukhaṃ, kāmasukhā kāmaggasukhaṃ, tattha aggamakkhāyatī’ti) ( ) sī. syā. kaṃ. pī. potthakesu natthi – ‘‘dujjānaṃ kho etaṃ, kaccāna, tayā aññadiṭṭhikena aññakhantikena aññarucikena aññatrayogena aññatrācariyakena – kāmā kāmaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) vā kāmasukhaṃ vā kāmaggasukhaṃ vā. Ye kho te, kaccāna, bhikkhū arahanto khīṇāsavā vusitavanto katakaraṇīyā ohitabhārā anuppattasadatthā parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanā sammadaññā vimuttā te kho etaṃ jāneyyuṃ – kāmā vā kāmasukhaṃ vā kāmaggasukhaṃ vā’’ti. ( ) sī. syā. kaṃ. pī. potthakesu natthi kāmaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)
“Kaccana, there are these five cords of sensual pleasure (pañca kāmaguṇā).

What are the five? Forms cognizable by the eye (cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā) that are wished for (iṭṭhā), desired (kantā), agreeable (manāpā), and likeable (piyarūpā), connected with sensual desire (kāmūpasaṃhitā) and provocative of lust (rajanīyā). Sounds cognizable by the ear (sotaviññeyyā saddā)… Odours cognizable by the nose (ghānaviññeyyā gandhā)… Flavours cognizable by the tongue (jivhāviññeyyā rasā)… Tangibles cognizable by the body (kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā) that are wished for (iṭṭhā), desired (kantā), agreeable (manāpā), and likeable (piyarūpā), connected with sensual desire (kāmūpasaṃhitā) and provocative of lust (rajanīyā). These are the five cords of sensual pleasure (pañca kāmaguṇā).

Now, Kaccana, the pleasure (sukhaṃ) and joy (somanassaṃ) that arise dependent on these five cords of sensual pleasure (pañca kāmaguṇe) are called sensual pleasure (kāmasukhaṃ).

Thus sensual pleasure (kāmasukhaṃ) arises through sensual pleasures (kāmehi), but beyond sensual pleasure (kāmasukhā) there is a pleasure higher than the sensual (kāmagga sukhaṃ), and that is declared to be the highest among them.”

When this was said, the wanderer Vekhanasa said to the Blessed One: “Amazing, Master Gotama! Wonderful, Master Gotama! How well this was spoken by Master Gotama: ‘Thus sensual pleasure arises through sensual pleasures, but beyond sensual pleasure there is a pleasure higher than the sensual, and that is declared to be the highest among them.’

(‘Thus sensual pleasure arises through sensual pleasures, but beyond sensual pleasure there is a pleasure higher than the sensual, and that is declared to be the highest among them.’) – “Kaccana, for you who are of another view (aññadiṭṭhikena), who accept another teaching (aññakhantikena), who approve of another teaching (aññarucikena), who pursue a different training (aññatrayogena), who follow a different teacher (aññatrācariyakena) – what is sensuality (kāmā), or what is sensual pleasure (kāmasukhaṃ) or what is the pleasure higher than the sensual (kāmagga sukhaṃ)?

But those monks (bhikkhū) who are arahants (arahanto) with taints destroyed (khīṇāsavā), who have lived the holy life (vusitavanto), done what had to be done (katakaraṇīyā), laid down the burden (ohitabhārā), reached the true goal (anuppattasadatthā), destroyed the fetters of being (parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanā), and are completely liberated through final knowledge (sammadaññā vimuttā) - it is they who would know what sensuality is (kāmā), what sensual pleasure is (kāmasukhaṃ), and what the pleasure higher than the sensual is (kāmagga sukhaṃ).”
280.Pañca kho imeti kasmā ārabhi? Agāriyopi ekacco kāmagaruko kāmādhimutto hoti, ekacco nekkhammagaruko nekkhammādhimutto hoti. Pabbajitopi ca ekacco kāmagaruko kāmādhimutto hoti, ekacco nekkhammagaruko nekkhammādhimutto hoti. Ayaṃ pana kāmagaruko kāmādhimutto hoti. So imāya kathāya kathiyamānāya attano kāmādhimuttattaṃ sallakkhessati, evamassāyaṃ desanā sappāyā bhavissatīti imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi. Kāmaggasukhanti nibbānaṃ adhippetaṃ.
Paragraph 280.

Why did he begin with, "These five (pañca kho ime)"? Even a householder is sometimes intent on sense pleasures and is devoted to them. Others are intent on renunciation and devoted to it.

And even a renunciant is sometimes intent on sense pleasures and devoted to them. Others are intent on renunciation and devoted to it.

But this person is intent on sense pleasures and devoted to them.

As this teaching is being spoken, he will consider himself devoted to sense pleasures. Thus, this teaching will be suitable for him. For this reason, he began this teaching.

"Supreme sensual happiness (kāmaggasukha)" means nirvāṇa is intended.

Paribbājakavaggo niṭṭhito tatiyo. Tassuddānaṃ – Puṇḍarī-aggisaha-kathināmo, dīghanakho puna bhāradvājagotto; Sandakaudāyimuṇḍikaputto, maṇiko tathākaccāno varavaggo.

When this was said, the wanderer Vekhanassa was angry and displeased, and he reviled, disparaged, and censured the Blessed One, saying: “The recluse Gotama will be worsted.” He then said to the Blessed One: “So then there are some recluses and brāhmaṇas here who, without knowing the past (pūrvānta) and without seeing the future (aparantam), yet claim: ‘Jāti is destroyed, the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ) has been lived, what had to be done (karaṇīyaṃ) has been done, there is no more coming to any state of being’—so they claim.

But what they say turns out to be ridiculous; it turns out to be mere words, empty and hollow.”

“If any recluses and brāhmaṇas, Kaccāna, without knowing the past (pūrvānta) and without seeing the future (aparantam), yet claim: ‘Jāti is destroyed, the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ) has been lived, what had to be done (karaṇīyaṃ) has been done, there is no more coming to any state of being’—they can be confuted in accordance with the dharma.

Rather, let the past (pūrvānta) be, Kaccāna, and let the future (aparantam) be.

Let an intelligent man come to me who is sincere, honest and straightforward, and I will instruct him, I will teach him the dharma.

I instruct him, I teach him the dharma in such a way that by practising as instructed he will soon know and see for himself: “Thus, indeed, there rightly comes to be liberation from the bond, that is, from the bond of avidyā.

Suppose, Kaccāna, there were a young tender infant lying prone, bound by stout bonds at the four limbs with the fifth at the neck; and later on, as a result of his growth and the maturing of his faculties, those bonds loosened, then he would know ‘I am free’ and there would be no more bondage.

So too, let a wise man come…I instruct him, I teach him the dharma in such a way that by practising as instructed he will soon know and see for himself: ‘Thus, indeed, there rightly comes to be liberation from the bond, that is, from the bond of avidyā.’”

When this was said, the wanderer Vekhanassa said to the Blessed One: “Magnificent, Master Gotama! …From today let Master Gotama remember me as a lay follower who has gone to him for refuge for life.”

The Vekhanassa Sutta, the tenth, is finished.

The Vagga on Wanderers, the third, is finished.

He will be made to attain the state of unknowing, thinking, "He will be."

"It merely comes to be named," means it merely comes to be an empty statement.

"Let the former end remain, let the latter end remain," because you lack the fitting knowledge of former existences regarding the past, and you lack the fitting divine eye knowledge regarding the future, therefore he said, "Let both of these remain."

"By means of thread-bindings" means by means of bindings made of thread.

For his protection, they bind threads on his hands and feet, and also on his neck.

This was said with reference to those.

But in old age, those same [threads] either fall off by themselves, becoming rotten, or they cut [them] and take [them] away.

Just as, by this, it is shown – like the time of unknowing of thread-bindings for a young boy, the unknowing at the earliest limit of ignorance, for it is not possible to know the earliest limit of ignorance; but at the time of release, the knowing similar to knowledge, release from the bondage of ignorance occurs by means of the path of the arhat, thus knowing.

The remainder is clear throughout.
