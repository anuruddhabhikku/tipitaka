THUS HAVE I HEARD.

Once the Blessed One was staying near Rājagaha on Vulture Peak Mountain, not long after Devadatta had left.

There, regarding Devadatta, the Blessed One addressed the monks:

“Here, monks, some clansman goes forth from the household life into homelessness out of faith, thinking, ‘I am overwhelmed by birth, aging, and death; by sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress. I am overwhelmed by suffering, beset by suffering. Perhaps an end to this entire mass of suffering might be known.’

When he has gone forth thus, he acquires gain, honor, and renown (lābhasakkārasilokaṃ abhinibbatteti).

He is pleased with that gain, honor, and renown (lābhasakkārasilokena attamano hoti paripuṇṇasaṅkappo).

On account of that gain, honor, and renown (lābhasakkārasilokena attānukkaṃseti paraṃ vambheti), he lauds himself and disparages others, thinking, ‘I have gain, honor, and renown, but these other monks are unknown and of no account.’

On account of that gain, honor, and renown (lābhasakkārasilokena), he becomes intoxicated, grows negligent, falls into negligence, and being negligent, he lives in suffering.

“Suppose, monks, a man needing heartwood, seeking heartwood, wandering in search of heartwood, came to a great tree standing possessed of heartwood. Passing over its heartwood, its sapwood, its inner bark, and its outer bark, he would cut off its twigs and leaves and take them away thinking they were heartwood.

Then a man with good sight, seeing him, might say: ‘This good man did not know the heartwood, the sapwood, the inner bark, the outer bark, or the twigs and leaves.

Thus, while needing heartwood, seeking heartwood, wandering in search of heartwood, he came to a great tree standing possessed of heartwood, and passing over its heartwood, its sapwood, its inner bark, and its outer bark, cut off its twigs and leaves and took them away thinking they were heartwood.

Whatever it was this good man had to make with heartwood, his purpose will not be served.’

So too, monks, here some clansman goes forth from the household life into homelessness out of faith, thinking, ‘I am overwhelmed by birth, aging, and death; by sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress. I am overwhelmed by suffering, beset by suffering. Perhaps an end to this entire mass of suffering might be known.’

When he has gone forth thus, he acquires gain, honor, and renown (lābhasakkārasilokaṃ abhinibbatteti).

He is pleased with that gain, honor, and renown (lābhasakkārasilokena attamano hoti paripuṇṇasaṅkappo).

On account of that gain, honor, and renown (lābhasakkārasilokena), he lauds himself and disparages others, thinking, ‘I have gain, honor, and renown, but these other monks are unknown and of no account.’

On account of that gain, honor, and renown (lābhasakkārasilokena), he becomes intoxicated, grows negligent, falls into negligence, and being negligent, he lives in suffering.

This bhikkhu, monks, is said to have grasped only the twigs and leaves of the holy life, reaching only a dead end."

307. Thus, I have heard: the Great Sāropama Sutta.

There, "recently departed" means having broken the Saṃgha, having committed the act of causing blood to flow, recently departed, born in a separate womb with a mark.

Here, "a certain clansman, O monks" means, although not specified as being a clansman of such and such a family, it is to be understood as referring to Devadatta.
He is a clansman by birth, because he was born in the Okkāka lineage of the unmixed Greatly Honored Tradition.
"Attached" means, one whose birth has entered within; he is called attached by birth.
The same rule applies to old age, and so on.
In the case of gain, honor, and so on, "gain" means the four requisites.
"Honor" means the state of being well-done by them.
"Praise" means the act of speaking virtues.
"Generates" means produces.
"Unrecognized" means they are not recognized in the standing place of two people, they do not obtain even mere food and clothing.
"With few associates" means with few followers, they do not obtain one going in front or behind.

Here, "a certain clansman, O monks" "attached" "gain, honor, and so on" "gain" "honor" "praise" "generates" "unrecognized" "with few associates"
"To be done with the core for the sake of the core" means whatever is to be done with the core of a tree, such as axle-wheels, yokes, ploughs, and so on.
"He grasped the branch and foliage of the spiritual life" means the four requisites are called the branch and foliage of the essence of the path and fruit, of the dispensation and spiritual life; he grasped that.
"And he reached the end" means, by that much alone, "I have attained the core," he reached the end.

To be done with the core for the sake of the core, he grasped the branch and foliage of the spiritual life, and he reached the end.

“Here, bhikkhus, a certain clansman goes forth out of faith from the house life into homelessness, [thinking]: ‘I am a victim of birth (jāti), aging (jarā), and death (maraṇa), of sorrow (śoka), lamentation (parideva), pain (dukkha), grief (daumanasya), and despair (upāyāsa); I am a victim of suffering, a prey to suffering. Surely an ending of this whole mass of suffering (duḥkhakkhandha) can be known.’

When he has gone forth thus, he acquires gain (lābha), honor (satkāra), and renown (śloka). He is not pleased with that gain, honor, and renown, and his intention is not fulfilled. He does not, on account of it, laud himself and disparage others. He does not become intoxicated with that gain, honor, and renown; he does not grow negligent and fall into negligence.

Being diligent, he achieves the attainment of virtue (śīlasampadā). He is pleased with that attainment of virtue and his intention is fulfilled. On account of it he lauds himself and disparages others thus: ‘I am virtuous (śīlavā), of good character (kalyāṇadharma), but these other bhikkhus are immoral (duśśīla), of evil character (pāpadharma).’ He becomes intoxicated with that attainment of virtue, grows negligent, falls into negligence, and being negligent, he lives in suffering (dukkhaṃ viharati).

“Suppose, bhikkhus, a man needing heartwood (sāratthika), seeking heartwood (sāragavesī), wandering in search of heartwood (sārapariyesaṇa), came to a great tree (rukkhassa) standing possessed of heartwood. Passing over its heartwood, its sapwood (phegguṃ), and its inner bark (tacaṃ), he would cut off its outer bark (papaṭikaṃ) and take it away thinking it was heartwood (sāra). Then a man with good sight, seeing him, might say: “This good man did not know the heartwood, did not know the sapwood (phegguṃ), did not know the inner bark (tacaṃ), did not know the outer bark (papaṭikaṃ), or the twigs and leaves (śākhāpalāśaṃ). Thus, while needing heartwood, seeking heartwood, wandering in search of heartwood, he came to a great tree standing possessed of heartwood, and passing over its heartwood, its sapwood (phegguṃ), its inner bark (tacaṃ), and its outer bark (papaṭikaṃ), he cut off its twigs and leaves and took them away thinking they were heartwood. And what should have been done with the heartwood, that he will not experience its benefit.”

So too, bhikkhus, here some clansman goes forth out of faith from the house life into homelessness, [thinking]: ‘I am a victim of birth, aging, and death, of sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair; I am a victim of suffering, a prey to suffering. Surely an ending of this whole mass of suffering can be known.’ When he has gone forth thus, he acquires gain, honor, and renown. He is not pleased with that gain, honor, and renown, and his intention is not fulfilled. He does not, on account of it, laud himself and disparage others. He does not become intoxicated with that gain, honor, and renown; he does not grow negligent and fall into negligence. Being diligent, he achieves the attainment of virtue. He is pleased with that attainment of virtue and his intention is fulfilled. On account of it he lauds himself and disparages others thus: ‘I am virtuous, of good character, but these other bhikkhus are immoral, of evil character.’ He becomes intoxicated with that attainment of virtue, grows negligent, falls into negligence, and being negligent, he lives in suffering.

This bhikkhu, bhikkhus, is called one who has taken the outer bark (papaṭikaṃ) of the holy life (brahmacariya); and with that, he has come to an end.

This is said to be its meaning ayam etassa atthoti vuttaṃ hoti.

"Etaṃ sāra," this fruit of arhatship arahattaphalaṃ is the essence sāraṃ of the holy life brahmacariyassa. "Etaṃ pariyosāna," this fruit of arhatship arahattaphalaṃ is the culmination pariyosānaṃ of the holy life brahmacariyassa, this is the limit esā koṭi, there is nothing further to be attained na ito paraṃ pattabbaṃ atthīti, thus he concluded the teaching desanaṃ niṭṭhapesīti in accordance with the connection yathānusandhināva.

Unshakeable liberation of mind akuppā cetovimuttī, therefore etadattham, this is the essence etaṃ sāra, this is the culmination etaṃ pariyosāna.

The Papañcasūdanī commentary to the Majjhimanikāya

The commentary on the Mahāsāropamasutta is finished mahāsāropamasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
