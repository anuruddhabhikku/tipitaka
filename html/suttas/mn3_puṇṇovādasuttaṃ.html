395. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo sāyanhasamayaṃ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā puṇṇo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘sādhu maṃ, bhante, bhagavā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadatu, yamahaṃ bhagavato dhammaṃ sutvā eko vūpakaṭṭho appamatto ātāpī pahitatto vihareyya’’nti. ‘‘Tena hi, puṇṇa, suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā puṇṇo bhagavato paccassosi. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Santi kho, puṇṇa, cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā. Taṃ ce bhikkhu abhinandati abhivadati ajjhosāya tiṭṭhati. Tassa taṃ abhinandato abhivadato ajjhosāya tiṭṭhato uppajjati nandī nandi (syā. kaṃ.). ‘Nandīsamudayā dukkhasamudayo, puṇṇā’ti vadāmi. nandi (syā. kaṃ.) ‘‘Santi kho, puṇṇa, sotaviññeyyā saddā… ghānaviññeyyā gandhā… jivhāviññeyyā rasā… kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā… manoviññeyyā dhammā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā. Taṃ ce bhikkhu abhinandati abhivadati ajjhosāya tiṭṭhati. Tassa taṃ abhinandato abhivadato ajjhosāya tiṭṭhato uppajjati nandī. ‘Nandīsamudayā dukkhasamudayo, puṇṇā’ti vadāmi. ‘‘Santi ca kho, puṇṇa, cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā. Taṃ ce bhikkhu nābhinandati nābhivadati nājjhosāya tiṭṭhati. Tassa taṃ anabhinandato anabhivadato anajjhosāya tiṭṭhato nandī nirujjhati. ‘Nandīnirodhā dukkhanirodho, puṇṇā’ti vadāmi. ‘‘Santi ca kho, puṇṇa, sotaviññeyyā saddā… ghānaviññeyyā gandhā… jivhāviññeyyā rasā… kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā… manoviññeyyā dhammā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā. Taṃ ce bhikkhu nābhinandati nābhivadati nājjhosāya tiṭṭhati. Tassa taṃ anabhinandato anabhivadato anajjhosāya tiṭṭhato nandī nirujjhati. ‘Nandīnirodhā dukkhanirodho, puṇṇā’ti vadāmi. ‘‘Iminā ca tvaṃ puṇṇa, mayā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadito katarasmiṃ janapade viharissasī’’ti? ‘‘Imināhaṃ, bhante, bhagavatā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadito, atthi sunāparanto nāma janapado, tatthāhaṃ viharissāmī’’ti.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Savatthi in the Jeta Grove, Anathapindika’s monastery.

Then the venerable Puṇṇa, in the evening, rose from seclusion and went to the Blessed One. Having approached, paid homage to the Blessed One, and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, the venerable Puṇṇa said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, it would be good if the Blessed One would advise me with a concise teaching, so that, having heard the Dharma from the Blessed One, I might live alone, withdrawn, diligent, ardent, and resolute.”

“Well then, Puṇṇa, listen and attend carefully to what I shall say.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Puṇṇa replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“There are, Puṇṇa, forms cognizable by the eye that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust.

If a bhikkhu delights in them, welcomes them, and remains holding to them, delight arises in him.

With the arising of delight, there is the arising of suffering, I say, Puṇṇa.

There are, Puṇṇa, sounds cognizable by the ear… odours cognizable by the nose… flavours cognizable by the tongue… tangibles cognizable by the body… mind-objects cognizable by the mind that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust.

If a bhikkhu delights in them, welcomes them, and remains holding to them, delight arises in him.

With the arising of delight, there is the arising of suffering, I say, Puṇṇa.

There are, Puṇṇa, forms cognizable by the eye that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust.

If a bhikkhu does not delight in them, does not welcome them, and does not remain holding to them, delight ceases in him.

With the cessation of delight, there is the cessation of suffering, I say, Puṇṇa.

There are, Puṇṇa, sounds cognizable by the ear… odours cognizable by the nose… flavours cognizable by the tongue… tangibles cognizable by the body… mind-objects cognizable by the mind that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust.

If a bhikkhu does not delight in them, does not welcome them, and does not remain holding to them, delight ceases in him.

With the cessation of delight, there is the cessation of suffering, I say, Puṇṇa.

“And in which country, Puṇṇa, will you live, having been advised by me with this concise teaching?”

“Having been advised by the Blessed One with this concise teaching, venerable sir, there is a country called Sunāparanta, there I will live.”
395.Evaṃme sutanti puṇṇovādasuttaṃ. Tattha paṭisallānāti ekībhāvā. Taṃ ceti taṃ cakkhuñceva rūpañca. Nandīsamudayā dukkhasamudayoti nandiyā taṇhāya samodhānena pañcakkhandhadukkhassa samodhānaṃ hoti. Iti chasu dvāresu dukkhaṃ samudayoti dvinnaṃ saccānaṃ vasena vaṭṭaṃ matthakaṃ pāpetvā dassesi. Dutiyanaye nirodho maggoti dvinnaṃ saccānaṃ vasena vivaṭṭaṃ matthakaṃ pāpetvā dassesi. Iminā ca tvaṃ puṇṇāti pāṭiyekko anusandhi. Evaṃ tāva vaṭṭavivaṭṭavasena desanaṃ arahatte pakkhipitvā idāni puṇṇattheraṃ sattasu ṭhānesu sīhanādaṃ nadāpetuṃ iminā ca tvantiādimāha.
Thus, "me suta" is the Puṇṇovādasutta.

There, "paṭisallānā" means becoming one (ekībhāva).

"Taṃ ce" means that which is the eye (cakkhu) and form (rūpa).

"Nandīsamudayā dukkhasamudayo" means, due to the combination of delight (nandī) and craving (taṇhā), there is a combination of the suffering of the five aggregates (pañcakkhandhadukkha).

Thus, in the six doors (dvāresu), suffering (dukkhaṃ) arises (samudayo); in reliance on the two truths (dvinnaṃ saccānaṃ vasena), having caused the cycle (vaṭṭaṃ) to reach its peak (matthakaṃ), he showed (dassesi).

In the second way (dutiyanaye), cessation (nirodho) is the path (maggo); in reliance on the two truths (dvinnaṃ saccānaṃ vasena), having caused the unfolding (vivaṭṭaṃ) to reach its peak (matthakaṃ), he showed (dassesi).

"Iminā ca tvaṃ puṇṇā" is a distinct (pāṭiyekko) connection (anusaṃdhi).

Thus, having placed the teaching (desanaṃ) in arahantship (arahatte) by way of the cycle and the unfolding (vaṭṭavivaṭṭavasena), now, in order to cause Puṇṇatthera to roar the lion's roar (sīhanādaṃ nadāpetuṃ) in the seven places (sattasu ṭhānesu), he said "iminā ca tvaṃ," etc. (ntiādimāha).

396. ‘‘Caṇḍā kho, puṇṇa, sunāparantakā manussā; pharusā kho, puṇṇa, sunāparantakā manussā. Sace taṃ, puṇṇa, sunāparantakā manussā akkosissanti paribhāsissanti, tattha te, puṇṇa, kinti bhavissatī’’ti? ‘‘Sace maṃ, bhante, sunāparantakā manussā akkosissanti paribhāsissanti, tattha me evaṃ bhavissati – ‘bhaddakā bhadrakā (ka.) vatime sunāparantakā manussā, subhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, yaṃ me nayime pāṇinā pahāraṃ dentī’ti. Evamettha evammettha (?), bhagavā, bhavissati; evamettha, sugata, bhavissatī’’ti. ‘‘Sace pana te, puṇṇa, sunāparantakā manussā pāṇinā pahāraṃ dassanti, tattha pana te, puṇṇa, kinti bhavissatī’’ti? ‘‘Sace me, bhante, sunāparantakā manussā pāṇinā pahāraṃ dassanti, tattha me evaṃ bhavissati – ‘bhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, subhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, yaṃ me nayime leḍḍunā pahāraṃ dentī’ti. Evamettha, bhagavā, bhavissati; evamettha, sugata, bhavissatī’’ti. ‘‘Sace pana te, puṇṇa, sunāparantakā manussā leḍḍunā pahāraṃ dassanti, tattha pana te, puṇṇa, kinti bhavissatī’’ti? ‘‘Sace me, bhante, sunāparantakā manussā leḍḍunā pahāraṃ dassanti, tattha me evaṃ bhavissati – ‘bhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, subhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, yaṃ me nayime daṇḍena pahāraṃ dentī’ti. Evamettha, bhagavā, bhavissati; evamettha, sugata, bhavissatī’’ti. ‘‘Sace pana te, puṇṇa, sunāparantakā manussā daṇḍena pahāraṃ dassanti, tattha pana te, puṇṇa, kinti bhavissatī’’ti? ‘‘Sace me, bhante, sunāparantakā manussā daṇḍena pahāraṃ dassanti, tattha me evaṃ bhavissati – ‘bhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, subhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, yaṃ me nayime satthena pahāraṃ dentī’ti. Evamettha, bhagavā, bhavissati; evamettha, sugata, bhavissatī’’ti. ‘‘Sace pana te, puṇṇa, sunāparantakā manussā satthena pahāraṃ dassanti, tattha pana te, puṇṇa, kinti bhavissatī’’ti? ‘‘Sace me, bhante, sunāparantakā manussā satthena pahāraṃ dassanti, tattha me evaṃ bhavissati – ‘bhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, subhaddakā vatime sunāparantakā manussā, yaṃ maṃ yaṃ me (sī. pī. ka.) nayime tiṇhena satthena jīvitā voropentī’ti. Evamettha, bhagavā, bhavissati; evamettha, sugata, bhavissatī’’ti. yaṃ me (sī. pī. ka.) ‘‘Sace pana taṃ, puṇṇa, sunāparantakā manussā tiṇhena satthena jīvitā voropessanti, tattha pana te, puṇṇa, kinti bhavissatī’’ti? ‘‘Sace maṃ, bhante, sunāparantakā manussā tiṇhena satthena jīvitā voropessanti, tattha me evaṃ bhavissati – ‘santi kho bhagavato sāvakā kāye ca jīvite ca aṭṭīyamānā harāyamānā jigucchamānā satthahārakaṃ pariyesanti. Taṃ me idaṃ apariyiṭṭhaṃyeva satthahārakaṃ laddha’nti. Evamettha, bhagavā, bhavissati; evamettha, sugata, bhavissatī’’ti. ‘‘Sādhu, sādhu, puṇṇa! Sakkhissasi kho tvaṃ, puṇṇa, iminā damūpasamena samannāgato sunāparantasmiṃ janapade viharituṃ. Yassadāni tvaṃ, puṇṇa, kālaṃ maññasī’’ti.
“Punna, the people of Sunāparanta are wild and rough; Punna, the people of Sunāparanta are harsh."

"If the people of Sunāparanta abuse and revile you, what will you think about that?"

"Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta abuse and revile me, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are kind, truly kind, in that they do not give me a blow with the fist.’"

"Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Fortunate One.”

"But, Punna, if the people of Sunāparanta do give you a blow with the fist, what will you think then?"

"Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta give me a blow with the fist, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are kind, truly kind, in that they do not give me a blow with a clod.’"

"Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Fortunate One.”

"But, Punna, if the people of Sunāparanta do give you a blow with a clod, what will you think then?"

"Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta do give me a blow with a clod, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are kind, truly kind, in that they did not give me a blow with a stick.’"

"Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Fortunate One.”

"But, Punna, if the people of Sunāparanta do give you a blow with a rod, what will you think about that?"

"Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta give me a blow with a rod, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are kind, truly kind, in that they do not stab me with a knife.’"

"Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Fortunate One.”

"But, Punna, if the people of Sunāparanta do give you a blow with a knife, what will you think about that?"

"Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta give me a blow with a knife, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunāparanta are kind, truly kind, in that they do not deprive me of life with a sharp knife.’"

"Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Fortunate One.”

"But, Punna, if the people of Sunāparanta were to deprive you of life with a sharp knife, what would you think then?"

"Venerable sir, if the people of Sunāparanta were to deprive me of life with a sharp knife, then I will think: ‘There are indeed disciples of the Blessed One who, disgusted, ashamed, and loathing with the body and with life, seek someone to kill them. But I have had my life deprived by the knife without seeking for it.’"

"Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Fortunate One.”

"Good, good, Punna! Possessing such self-control and peacefulness, you will be able to dwell in the Sunāparanta country. Now, Punna, it is time to do as you think fit.”
396.Caṇḍāti duṭṭhā kibbisā. Pharusāti kakkhaḷā. Akkosissantīti dasahi akkosavatthūhi akkosissanti. Paribhāsissantīti kiṃ samaṇo nāma tvaṃ, idañca idañca te karissāmāti tajjessanti. Evametthāti evaṃ mayhaṃ ettha bhavissati. Daṇḍenāti catuhatthena daṇḍena vā ghaṭikamuggarena vā. Satthenāti ekatodhārādinā. Satthahārakaṃ pariyesantīti jīvitahārakaṃ satthaṃ pariyesanti. Idaṃ thero tatiyapārājikavatthusmiṃ asubhakathaṃ sutvā attabhāvena jigucchantānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ satthahārakapariyesanaṃ sandhāyāha. Damūpasamenāti ettha damoti indriyasaṃvarādīnaṃ etaṃ nāmaṃ. ‘‘Saccena danto damasā upeto, vedantagū vusitabrahmacariyo’’ti (saṃ. ni. 1.195; su. ni. 467) ettha hi indriyasaṃvaro damoti vutto. ‘‘Yadi saccā damā cāgā, khantyā bhiyyodha vijjatī’’ti (saṃ. ni. 1.246; su. ni. 191) ettha paññā damoti vutto. ‘‘Dānena damena saṃyamena saccavajjenā’’ti (dī. ni. 1.166; ma. ni. 2.226) ettha uposathakammaṃ damoti vuttaṃ. Imasmiṃ pana sutte khanti damoti veditabbā. Upasamoti tasseva vevacanaṃ. Daṇḍenā Satthenā Satthahārakaṃ pariyesantī Damūpasamenā damo Upasamo
396. Caṇḍā means cruel and wicked.

Pharusā means harsh.

Akkosissantī means they will scold with ten grounds for scolding.

Paribhāsissantī means they will threaten, saying, "What! Are you a samaṇa? We will do this and that to you!"

Evamettha means thus it will be for me here.

Daṇḍenā means with a four- cubit staff, or with a ghaṭikamuggara.

Satthenā means with something like a single-edged knife.

Satthahārakaṃ pariyesantī means they seek a knife to take life.

Here, the Elder spoke with reference to monks who, having heard talk of unattractiveness in the context of the third pārājika matter, were disgusted with their own existence and sought a knife to take life.

Damūpasamenā means here, dama is a name for things like the restraint of the senses.

“One is tamed by truth, endowed with self-control (damasā upeto), gone to the end of the vedas, one who has lived the holy life” (S.

N.

1. 195; S.

N.

467). Here, restraint of the senses is said to be dama.

"If anything better than truth, self-control (damā), generosity, and patience is found here" (S.

N.

1. 246; S.

N.

191), here wisdom is said to be dama.

“Through giving, self-control (damena), restraint (saṃyamena), and truthful speech” (D.

N.

1. 166; M.

N.

2. 226), here the uposatha observance is said to be dama.

In this sutta, patience is to be understood as dama.

Upasamo means a synonym for that very thing.

With a staff, with a knife, seeking a knife to take life, with self-control, self-control, tranquility.

397. Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā senāsanaṃ saṃsāmetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena sunāparanto janapado tena cārikaṃ pakkāmi. Anupubbena cārikaṃ caramāno yena sunāparanto janapado tadavasari. Tatra sudaṃ āyasmā puṇṇo sunāparantasmiṃ janapade viharati. Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo tenevantaravassena pañcamattāni upāsakasatāni paṭivedesi paṭipādesi (sī. pī.), paṭidesesi (syā. kaṃ.), tenevantaravassena pañcamattāni upāsikasatāni paṭivedesi, tenevantaravassena tisso vijjā sacchākāsi. Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo aparena samayena parinibbāyi. Atha kho sambahulā bhikkhū yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘yo so, bhante, puṇṇo nāma kulaputto bhagavatā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadito so kālaṅkato. Tassa kā gati, ko abhisamparāyo’’ti? ‘‘Paṇḍito, bhikkhave, puṇṇo kulaputto paccapādi saccavādī dhammavādī (ka.) dhammassānudhammaṃ, na ca maṃ dhammādhikaraṇaṃ viheṭhesi. Parinibbuto, bhikkhave, puṇṇo kulaputto’’ti. saccavādī dhammavādī (ka.) Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Puṇṇovādasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ tatiyaṃ.

Then the venerable Puṇṇa, having rejoiced and gratefully accepted the Blessed One's words, rose from his seat, paid homage to the Blessed One, circumambulated him, put his dwelling in order, took his robe and bowl, and left on a walking tour towards the Sunāparanta country. Wandering by stages, he eventually arrived in the Sunāparanta country. There, the venerable Puṇṇa lived in the Sunāparanta country. Then, during that rainy season, the venerable Puṇṇa established five hundred male lay followers, five hundred female lay followers, and realized the three knowledges. On a later occasion, the venerable Puṇṇa attained final parinirvāṇa.

Then a number of monks went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, sat down at one side. Sitting to one side, those monks said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, the clansman Puṇṇa, who was given brief advice by the Blessed One, has died. What is his destination, what is his future state?" "Monks, the clansman Puṇṇa was wise, he practiced the Dharma in accordance with the Dharma, and he did not trouble me with issues related to the Dharma. The clansman Puṇṇa has attained final parinirvāṇa."

This is what the Blessed One said. Gratified, the monks delighted in the Blessed One's words.

The Puṇṇovādasutta is finished, the third.
397.Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇoti ko panesa puṇṇo, kasmā panettha gantukāmo ahosīti . Sunāparantavāsiko eva eso, sāvatthiyaṃ pana asappāyavihāraṃ sallakkhetvā tattha gantukāmo ahosi. Tatrāyaṃ anupubbikathā – sunāparantaraṭṭhe kira ekasmiṃ vāṇijakagāme ete dve bhātaro. Tesu kadāci jeṭṭho pañca sakaṭasatāni gahetvā janapadaṃ gantvā bhaṇḍaṃ āharati, kadāci kaniṭṭho. Imasmiṃ pana samaye kaniṭṭhaṃ ghare ṭhapetvā jeṭṭhabhātiko pañca sakaṭasatāni gahetvā janapadacārikaṃ caranto anupubbena sāvatthiṃ patvā jetavanassa nātidūre sakaṭasatthaṃ nivāsetvā bhuttapātarāso parijanaparivuto phāsukaṭṭhāne nisīdi. Tena ca samayena sāvatthivāsino bhuttapātarāsā uposathaṅgāni adhiṭṭhāya suddhuttarāsaṅgā gandhapupphādihatthā yena buddho yena dhammo yena saṅgho, tanninnā tappoṇā tappābbhārā hutvā dakkhiṇadvārena nikkhamitvā jetavanaṃ gacchanti. So te disvā ‘‘kahaṃ ime gacchanti’’ti ekamanussaṃ pucchi. Kiṃ tvaṃ ayyo na jānāsi, loke buddhadhammasaṅgharatanāni nāma uppannāni, iccesa mahājano satthu santike dhammakathaṃ sotuṃ gacchatīti. Tassa buddhoti vacanaṃ chavicammādīni chinditvā aṭṭhimiñjaṃ āhacca aṭṭhāsi. Atha attano parijanaparivuto tāya parisāya saddhiṃ vihāraṃ gantvā satthu madhurassarena dhammaṃ desentassa parisapariyante ṭhito dhammaṃ sutvā pabbajjāya cittaṃ uppādesi. Atha tathāgatena kālaṃ viditvā parisāya uyyojitāya satthāraṃ upasaṅkamitvā vanditvā svātanāya nimantetvā dutiyadivase maṇḍapaṃ kāretvā āsanāni paññapetvā buddhappamukhassa saṅghassa mahādānaṃ datvā bhuttapātarāso uposathaṅgāni adhiṭṭhāya bhaṇḍāgārikaṃ pakkosāpetvā, ettakaṃ bhaṇḍaṃ vissajjitaṃ, ettakaṃ na vissajjitanti sabbaṃ ācikkhitvā – ‘‘imaṃ sāpateyyaṃ mayhaṃ kaniṭṭhassa dehī’’ti sabbaṃ niyyātetvā satthu santike pabbajitvā kammaṭṭhānaparāyaṇo ahosi. 397. Now, the venerable Puṇṇa: who was this Puṇṇa, and why did he want to go there?

He was a resident of Sunāparanta. Having considered the unsuitable monastery in Sāvatthi, he wanted to go there.

Here is a sequential account of it. In Sunāparanta, in a certain merchant village, there were two brothers.

Among them, sometimes the elder brother, taking five hundred carts, would go to the countryside and bring merchandise, and sometimes the younger brother [would do the same].

Now, at that time, having left the younger brother at home, the elder brother, taking five hundred carts and traveling in the countryside, gradually arrived at Sāvatthi. Not far from Jetavana, having stationed the caravan of carts, after eating breakfast, surrounded by his attendants, he sat down in a comfortable place.

At that time, the residents of Sāvatthi, having eaten breakfast and undertaken the Uposatha vows, with clean upper garments and flowers and incense in their hands, [thinking] "where the Buddha is, where the Dharma is, where the Saṅgha is," being inclined, disposed, and tending towards that, having gone out through the southern gate, were going to Jetavana.

Having seen them, he asked a certain man, "Where are these people going?"

"Are you not aware, sir, that the Jewels of the Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha have arisen in the world? This great crowd is going to hear a Dhamma talk near the Teacher."

The word "Buddha" struck his skin, hide, etc., and stood striking his bone marrow.

Then, surrounded by his own attendants, having gone to the monastery with that assembly, standing at the edge of the assembly while the Teacher was teaching the Dharma in a sweet voice, having heard the Dharma, he generated the thought of ordination.

Then, the Tathāgata, knowing the time, having dismissed the assembly, [the elder brother] approached the Teacher, paid homage, invited [him] for the next day, and on the second day, having had a pavilion made, having arranged seats, having given a great donation to the Saṅgha with the Buddha at its head, after eating breakfast, having undertaken the Uposatha vows, having summoned the treasurer, having accounted for everything—"This much wealth has been spent, this much has not been spent"—having entrusted all the wealth to his younger brother, [saying], "Give this property to my younger brother," having renounced everything near the Teacher, he became devoted to meditation.

Then, while he was attending to the meditation subject, the meditation subject did not appear to him.

Then he thought, "This region is unsuitable for me. Why don't I take a meditation subject near the Teacher and go to my own place?"

Then, in the early morning, having wandered for alms, in the evening, having risen from seclusion, having approached the Blessed One, having discussed the meditation subject, having roared seven lion's roars, he departed.

Therefore it was said: "Then the venerable Puṇṇa...pe...lived."

Where did he live? He lived in four places. Having first entered Sunāparanta, he entered a village near Ajjuhatthapabbata for alms.

Then the younger brother, recognizing him and giving him alms, having made a promise, [saying], "Venerable Sir, do not go elsewhere, live here," had him live there.

At Ajjuhatthapabbata, then he went to a monastery named Samuddagirivihāra.

There, having carved out a walking path with iron-magnet stones, there was a walking path that no one was able to walk on.

There, ocean waves, coming and striking against the iron-magnet stones, were making a great noise.

The Elder, [thinking], "May there be a comfortable dwelling for those attending to the meditation subject," made the ocean silent and resolved.

From Samuddagirivihāra, then he went to a place named Mātulagiri.

There, a flock of birds was excited; night and day the sound was continuous. The Elder [thinking], "This place is uncomfortable," then went to a monastery named Makulakārāmavihāra.

That [monastery] was not too far from the merchant village, [and] not too close, well-provided with comings and goings, secluded, [and] with little noise.

The Elder [thinking], "This place is comfortable," having had night dwellings, day dwellings, walking paths, etc., made there, he spent the rainy season.

Thus, he lived in four places.

From Mātulagiri and Makulakārāmavihāra, one day, in that same rainy season, five hundred merchants, [thinking], "We will go to the far ocean," loaded merchandise onto a ship.
On the day of embarking, the elder's younger brother, after feeding the elder, having taken the precepts from the elder's presence, and while departing, having bowed, said, "Venerable sir, the great ocean is immeasurable, full of various dangers, may you consider us," and having spoken thus, he boarded the ship.

The ship, proceeding with great speed, reached a certain island.

The people, thinking, "We will have breakfast," disembarked on the island.

In that island, there was nothing else; it was just a sandalwood forest.

Having coughed, he knocked the latch.

Then, one skilled with an axe, having struck a tree and knowing the nature of red sandalwood, said, "Friends, let us go to the further ocean for gain; there is no further gain here; a piece four fingers wide is worth a hundred thousand, let us take away the merchandise fitted to be taken away and fill it with sandalwood."

They did so.

Non-human beings dwelling in the sandalwood forest, becoming angry, thinking, "These ones have destroyed our sandalwood forest, we will kill them," thinking, "Having killed them right here, the whole forest will become a single corpse; we will sink their ship in the middle of the ocean," they spoke.

Then, having boarded their ship, having stirred up a whirlwind just a moment after setting out, those non-human beings themselves showed frightening forms.

The frightened people paid homage to their respective deities.

The elder's younger brother, Cūḷapuṇṇakuṭumbika, thinking, "May my brother be a refuge," stood worshipping the elder.

The elder too, it seems, at that very moment, having considered and knowing of their arising disaster, having risen up into the sky, stood in front.

The non-human beings, having seen the elder, thinking, "The venerable Puṇṇathera is coming," departed, the whirlwind subsided.

The elder, having reassured them, saying, "Do not fear," asked, "Where do you intend to go?"

"Venerable sir, we are going to our own place."

The elder, having stepped on the ship's plank, resolved, "May it go to their desired place."

The merchants, having gone to their own place, having announced that occurrence to their children and wives, having established themselves in the three refuges together with five hundred of their own families and five hundred women, declared themselves lay followers.

Then, having unloaded the goods from the ship, having made one portion for the elder, they said, "This, venerable sir, is your portion."

The elder said, "For me, there is no separate business of a portion; but has the Teacher been seen by you?"

"He has not been seen before, venerable sir."

"Then make a maṇḍalamāḷa for the Teacher with this; in this way, you will see the Teacher."

They said, "Good, venerable sir," and with that portion and with their own portions they began to make a maṇḍalamāḷa.

The Teacher too, it seems, from the time of beginning, used the paribhoga.

The watchmen, having seen the light at night, made the sign, "There is a deity of great power."

The lay followers, having completed the maṇḍalamāḷa and the dwellings for the Saṅgha of monks and having prepared the requisites for giving, announced to the elder, "Venerable sir, our work has been done, call the Teacher."

The elder, having gone to Sāvatthi at evening time by his power, requested the Blessed One, "Venerable sir, the inhabitants of the merchants' village wish to see you; have compassion on them."

The Blessed One consented.

The elder, knowing the Blessed One's consent, returned to his own place.

The Blessed One then addressed the elder Ānanda, "Ānanda, tomorrow, at the edge of the Sunāparanta, in the merchants' village, we will wander for alms; you give a ticket to the five hundred minus one monks."

The elder, having announced that matter to the Saṅgha of monks, saying, "Good, venerable sir," said, "May the monks who travel in the sky take a ticket."

On that day, the elder Kuṇḍadhāna first took a ticket.

The inhabitants of the merchants' village too, thinking, "Tomorrow, it seems, the Teacher will come," having made a maṇḍapa in the middle of the village, prepared the place for giving alms.
The Blessed One, having performed the morning body care, entered the Perfumed Cell, and sat absorbed in fruition attainment.

At that moment, the throne of Śakka, adorned with a blanket of yellow cloth, became hot.

He reflected, "What is this?" Seeing the Teacher's journey to Sunāparanta, he addressed Viśvakarma, "Dear Tata, today the Blessed One will make his alms round over three hundred yojanas. Construct five hundred peaked dwellings, make them ready for travel, and place them at the gate tower of Jetavana."

He did so.

The Blessed One's peaked dwelling had four faces, the two chief disciples' had two faces each, and the remaining ones had one face each.

The Teacher, emerging from the Perfumed Cell, entered the foremost peaked dwelling among the rows of prepared peaked dwellings.

The two chief disciples, beginning with them, and also four hundred ninety-nine monks went to the peaked dwelling and sat down.

One peaked dwelling was empty; the five hundred peaked dwellings rose into the sky.

Having reached the mountain named Saccabandha, the Teacher placed the peaked dwelling in the sky.

On that mountain dwells an ascetic named Saccabandha, a holder of wrong views, who causes many people to adopt wrong views, having become successful in gain, honor, and fame.

Within him, in his inner bowl, a predisposition to arahantship burns like a lamp.

Seeing him, thinking, "I will preach the Dharma to him," he went and taught the Dharma.

At the end of the teaching, the ascetic attained arahantship; knowledge arose in him as if by the path itself.

Having become an ehibhikṣu (a monk ordained with the words "Come, monk"), having become one who possesses a bowl and robes made by iddhi (supernormal power), he entered the peaked dwelling.

The Blessed One, together with the five hundred monks dwelling in the peaked dwelling, went to the merchants' village, made the peaked dwellings invisible, and entered the merchants' village.

The merchants, having given a great gift to the Saṅgha of monks with the Buddha at its head, led the Teacher to Makulakārāma.

The Teacher entered the maṇḍalamāla.

While the multitude was waiting for the Teacher to finish his meal, they ate their breakfast, undertook the observance day vows, and taking many perfumes and flowers, returned to the ārāma to hear the Dharma.

The Teacher preached the Dharma.

Liberation from bondage arose for the multitude; there was a great commotion of the Buddha.

The Teacher, for the sake of the multitude, stayed there for a few days, but aroused Aruṇa in the great Perfumed Cell itself.

Having stayed there for a few days, having wandered for alms in the merchants' village, having sent Puṇṇathera back, saying, "You should dwell here," he went to the bank of the Nammadā River.

The Nāga king of the Nammadā, having performed the welcoming ceremony for the Teacher, led him into the Nāga-dwelling and showed honor to the three jewels.

The Teacher, having spoken the Dharma to him, emerged from the Nāga-dwelling.

He requested, "Bhante, give me something to care for." The Blessed One showed him the foot-shrine on the bank of the Nammadā River.

When the waves come, it is covered; when they recede, it is uncovered; it became one that attained great honor.

The Teacher, departing from there, went to Saccabandha Mountain and said to Saccabandha, "You have led many people onto the path of misfortune; you, dwelling right here, having dispelled their views, should establish them on the path of nirvāṇa."

He too requested something to care for.

The Teacher showed him the foot-shrine on a solid flat stone like a mark on a lump of wet clay, then he went to Jetavana itself.

With reference to this matter, it was said, "with that same inner rain..."
Tenevantaravassenā Parinibbāyī means he attained complete nirvāṇa (parinirvāṇa) through the element of nirvāṇa (nirvāṇadhātu) without remainder.
The great multitude, having performed the bodily veneration (śarīrapūja) of the Elder for seven days, having piled up many fragrant pieces of wood, cremated the body and taking up the relics, made a shrine (cetiya).
Sambahulā bhikkhū means the monks standing at the cremation ground of the Elder.
The rest is clear throughout.
