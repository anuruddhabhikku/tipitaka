<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Paṇḍito, bhikkhave, sāriputto; mahāpañño, bhikkhave, sāriputto; puthupañño, bhikkhave, sāriputto; hāsapañño <note>hāsupañño (sī. pī.)</note>, bhikkhave, sāriputto; javanapañño, bhikkhave, sāriputto; tikkhapañño, bhikkhave, sāriputto; nibbedhikapañño, bhikkhave, sāriputto; sāriputto, bhikkhave, aḍḍhamāsaṃ anupadadhammavipassanaṃ vipassati. <note>hāsupañño (sī. pī.)</note>
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: “Monks.”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied.

The Blessed One said this:

“Monks, Sāriputta is wise (paṇḍito); monks, Sāriputta has great wisdom (mahāpañño); monks, Sāriputta has wide wisdom (puthupañño); monks, Sāriputta has joyous wisdom (hāsapañño); monks, Sāriputta has quick wisdom (javanapañño); monks, Sāriputta has keen wisdom (tikkhapañño); monks, Sāriputta has penetrative wisdom (nibbedhikapañño). Monks, Sāriputta contemplates insight into states one by one as they occur for half a month (aḍḍhamāsaṃ anupadadhammavipassanaṃ vipassati).

Now, monks, Sāriputta’s insight into states one by one as they occur (anupadadhammavipassanā) was this:

94. Here, monks, Sāriputta, quite secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters and remains in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion.

And the states in the first jhāna - the applied thought (vitakka), the sustained thought (vicāra), the rapture (pīti), the pleasure (sukha), and the unification of mind (cittekaggatā); the contact (phassa), feeling (vedanā), perception (saññā), volition (cetanā), mind (citta), zeal (chanda), decision (adhimokkha), energy (vīriya), mindfulness (sati), equanimity (upekkhā), and attention (manasikāra) - these states were defined by him one by one as they occurred.

Known to him those states arose, known they were present, known they disappeared.

He understands thus: ‘So indeed, these states, not having been, come into being; having been, they vanish.’

Regarding those states, he abides unattracted, unrepelled, independent, detached, free, dissociated, with a mind rid of barriers.

He understood: ‘There is an escape beyond.’

and with the cultivation of that attainment, he confirmed that there is.

And the states in the third jhāna - the pleasure (sukha), the mindfulness (sati), the full awareness (sampajañña), and the unification of mind (cittekaggatā); the contact (phassa), feeling (vedanā), perception (saññā), volition (cetanā), mind (citta), zeal (chanda), decision (adhimokkha), energy (vīriya), mindfulness (sati), equanimity (upekkhā), and attention (manasikāra) - these states were defined by him one by one as they occurred; known to him those states arose, known they were present, known they disappeared.

He understood: ‘There is an escape beyond.’

and with the cultivation of that attainment, he confirmed that there is.

Again, monks, Sāriputta, with the abandoning of pleasure and pain, and with the previous disappearance of joy and grief, entered upon and abided in the fourth jhāna, which has neither-pain-nor-pleasure and purity of mindfulness due to equanimity (upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ).

And the states in the fourth jhāna - the equanimity (upekkhā), the neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, the mental unconcern due to tranquility, the purity of mindfulness, and the unification of mind (cittekaggatā); the contact (phassa), feeling (vedanā), perception (saññā), volition (cetanā), mind (citta), zeal (chanda), decision (adhimokkha), energy (vīriya), mindfulness (sati), equanimity (upekkhā), and attention (manasikāra) - these states were defined by him one by one as they occurred.

95. Again, bhikkhus, by completely surmounting the base of nothingness (ākiñcanyāyatanaṃ), Sāriputta entered upon and abided in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ).

He emerged mindful from that attainment (samāpattiyā).

Having done so, he recalled the past states, which had ceased and changed, thus: ‘So indeed, these states, not having been, come into being; having been, they vanish.’

Regarding those states (dharmeshu), he abided unattracted, unrepelled, independent, detached, free, dissociated, with a mind rid of barriers.

He understood: ‘There is an escape beyond (nissaraṇa)’.

and with the cultivation of that attainment, he confirmed that there is.

“Again, bhikkhus, by completely surmounting the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ), Sāriputta entered upon and abided in the cessation of perception and feeling (saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ upasampajja viharati).

And his taints (āsavā) are destroyed by his seeing with wisdom (paññāya).

He emerged mindful from that attainment (samāpattiyā).

Having done so, he contemplated the past states (dhammā atītā), which had ceased (niruddhā) and changed (vipariṇatā), thus: ‘So indeed, these states, not having been, come into being (evaṃ kirame dhammā ahutvā sambhonti); having been, they vanish (hutvā paṭiventī).’

Regarding those states (dhammesu), he abided unattracted (anupāyo), unrepelled (anapāyo), independent (anissito), detached (appaṭibaddho), free (vippamutto), dissociated (visaṃyutto), with a mind rid of barriers (vimariyādīkatena cetasā).

He understood: ‘There is an escape beyond (natthi uttari nissaraṇa)’

Through repeatedly doing this, non-existence becomes his.

<p rend="centre">Anupadasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.

“Bhikkhus, rightly speaking, were it to be said of anyone: ‘He has attained mastery and perfection in noble virtue, attained mastery and perfection in noble concentration, attained mastery and perfection in noble wisdom, attained mastery and perfection in noble deliverance,’ it is of Śāriputra indeed that rightly speaking this should be said: ‘He has attained mastery and perfection in noble virtue, attained mastery and perfection in noble concentration, attained mastery and perfection in noble wisdom, attained mastery and perfection in noble deliverance.’

“Bhikkhus, rightly speaking, were it to be said of anyone: ‘He is the son of the Blessed One, born of his breast, born of his mouth, born of the Dhamma, created by the Dhamma, an heir in the Dhamma, not an heir in material things,’ it is of Śāriputra indeed that rightly speaking this should be said: ‘He is the son of the Blessed One, born of his breast, born of his mouth, born of the Dhamma, created by the Dhamma, an heir in the Dhamma, not an heir in material things.’ Śāriputra properly continues to keep in motion the unsurpassed Wheel of the Dhamma (anuttaraṃ dhammacakkaṃ) set in motion by the Tathāgata.”

This is what the Blessed One said. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Anupada Sutta, the first, is finished.
