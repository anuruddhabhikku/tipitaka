435. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā aṅgesu viharati assapuraṃ nāma aṅgānaṃ nigamo. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘samaṇā samaṇāti vo, bhikkhave, jano sañjānāti. Tumhe ca pana ‘ke tumhe’ti puṭṭhā samānā ‘samaṇāmhā’ti paṭijānātha. Tesaṃ vo, bhikkhave, evaṃsamaññānaṃ sataṃ evaṃpaṭiññānaṃ sataṃ – ‘yā samaṇasāmīcippaṭipadā taṃ paṭipajjissāma; evaṃ no ayaṃ amhākaṃ samaññā ca saccā bhavissati paṭiññā ca bhūtā; yesañca mayaṃ cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāraṃ paribhuñjāma, tesaṃ te kārā amhesu mahapphalā bhavissanti mahānisaṃsā, amhākañcevāyaṃ pabbajjā avañjhā bhavissati saphalā saudrayā’ti. Evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṃ.
435. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the Aṅga country at a town of the Aṅgas named Assapura.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks (saying): "Bhikkhus."

"Venerable sir," those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: "'Recluses, recluses,' monks, that is how people perceive you.

And when you are asked, ‘What are you? ‘, you claim that you are recluses.

Since that is what you are designated and what you claim to be : ‘We will undertake and practise that good conduct that makes one a recluse; thus our designation will be true and our claims genuine; and those from whom we receive robes, alms food, lodging, and medicine for the sick will have great reward and great benefit from us, and this going forth of ours will not be in vain, but fruitful and blessed.’

That is how you should train, bhikkhus.
435.Evaṃme sutanti cūḷaassapurasuttaṃ. Tassa desanākāraṇaṃ purimasadisameva. Samaṇasāmīcippaṭipadāti samaṇānaṃ anucchavikā samaṇānaṃ anulomappaṭipadā.
435. evaṃ me suta is the Cūḷaassapurasuttaṃ. The reason for its teaching is just like the previous one. samaṇasāmīcippaṭipadā means conduct befitting ascetics, conduct in accordance with ascetics.

436. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na samaṇasāmīcippaṭipadaṃ paṭipanno hoti? Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno abhijjhālussa abhijjhā appahīnā hoti, byāpannacittassa byāpādo appahīno hoti, kodhanassa kodho appahīno hoti, upanāhissa upanāho appahīno hoti, makkhissa makkho appahīno hoti, paḷāsissa paḷāso appahīno hoti, issukissa issā appahīnā hoti, maccharissa macchariyaṃ appahīnaṃ hoti , saṭhassa sāṭheyyaṃ appahīnaṃ hoti, māyāvissa māyā appahīnā hoti, pāpicchassa pāpikā icchā appahīnā hoti, micchādiṭṭhikassa micchādiṭṭhi appahīnā hoti – imesaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, samaṇamalānaṃ samaṇadosānaṃ samaṇakasaṭānaṃ āpāyikānaṃ ṭhānānaṃ duggativedaniyānaṃ appahānā ‘na samaṇasāmīcippaṭipadaṃ paṭipanno’ti vadāmi. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, matajaṃ nāma āvudhajātaṃ ubhatodhāraṃ pītanisitaṃ. Tadassa saṅghāṭiyā sampārutaṃ sampaliveṭhitaṃ. Tathūpamāhaṃ, bhikkhave, imassa bhikkhuno pabbajjaṃ vadāmi.
“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu not practicing the proper course of a samaṇa? In the case of whatever bhikkhu, bhikkhus, whose covetousness (abhijjhā) is not abandoned (appahīna), whose ill-will (byāpāda) is not abandoned, whose anger (kodha) is not abandoned, whose hostility (upanāha) is not abandoned, whose detraction (makkha) is not abandoned, whose insolence (paḷāsa) is not abandoned, whose envy (issā) is not abandoned, whose stinginess (macchariya) is not abandoned, whose deceit (sāṭheyya) is not abandoned, whose hypocrisy (māyā) is not abandoned, whose evil desires (pāpikā icchā) are not abandoned, whose wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi) is not abandoned—because of the non-abandonment (appahāna) of these defilements of a samaṇa (samaṇamala), faults of a samaṇa (samaṇadosa), impurities of a samaṇa (samaṇakasaṭa), things leading to misfortune (āpāyika), and resulting in suffering (duggativedanīya), I say [of such a bhikkhu] that he is ‘not practicing the proper course of a samaṇa.

“Just as, bhikkhus, a weapon (āvudha) called a mataja, sharpened on both sides and keen-edged, might be completely wrapped and swathed in a cloak (saṅghāṭī), so too, bhikkhus, do I declare the going forth (pabbajja) of this bhikkhu."
436.Samaṇamalānantiādīsu ete dhammā uppajjamānā samaṇe maline karonti malaggahite, tasmā ‘‘samaṇamalā’’ti vuccanti. Etehi samaṇā dussanti, padussanti, tasmā samaṇadosāti vuccanti. Ete uppajjitvā samaṇe kasaṭe niroje karonti milāpenti, tasmā samaṇakasaṭāti vuccanti. Āpāyikānaṃ ṭhānānanti apāye nibbattāpakānaṃ kāraṇānaṃ. Duggativedaniyānanti duggatiyaṃ vipākavedanāya paccayānaṃ. Matajaṃ nāmāti manussā tikhiṇaṃ ayaṃ ayena sughaṃsitvā taṃ ayacuṇṇaṃ maṃsena saddhiṃ madditvā koñcasakuṇe khādāpenti. Te uccāraṃ kātuṃ asakkontā maranti. No ce maranti, paharitvā mārenti. Atha tesaṃ kucchiṃ phāletvā naṃ udakena dhovitvā cuṇṇaṃ gahetvā maṃsena saddhiṃ madditvā puna khādāpentīti evaṃ satta vāre khādāpetvā gahitena ayacuṇṇena āvudhaṃ karonti. Susikkhitā ca naṃ ayakārā bahuhatthakammamūlaṃ labhitvā karonti. Taṃ matasakuṇato jātattā ‘‘mataja’’nti vuccati, atitikhiṇaṃ hoti. Pītanisitanti udakapītañceva silāya ca sunighaṃsitaṃ. Saṅghāṭiyāti kosiyā. Sampārutanti pariyonaddhaṃ. Sampaliveṭhitanti samantato veṭhitaṃ.
436. Samaṇamalānaṃ etc.: These things, when arising, make ascetics impure, [and] are seized by impurity. Therefore, they are called "impurities of ascetics" (samaṇamalā).

By these, ascetics are corrupted and completely corrupted. Therefore, they are called "faults of ascetics" (samaṇadosā).

Having arisen, these make ascetics rough and unrefined, [and] cause [them to] wither. Therefore, they are called "roughness of ascetics" (samaṇakasaṭā).

Āpāyikānaṃ ṭhānānaṃ: Of the causes that bring about rebirth in states of loss (apāya).

Duggativedaniyānaṃ: Of the conditions for the experience of the result in a bad state (duggati).

Matajaṃ nāmā: People sharpen a very sharp iron [tool] with iron, and having crushed that iron powder together with meat, they feed it to herons.

Unable to excrete, they die.

If they do not die, they strike and kill [them].

Then, having cut open their stomachs, washed [the contents] with water, taken the powder, crushed it together with meat, and fed it again, they make a weapon with the iron powder taken after feeding [it to the bird] in this way seven times.

And skilled ironworkers, having obtained the means for much handwork, make it.

Because it is born from a dead bird, it is called mataja, and it is very sharp.

Pītanisitaṃ: Having been drunk with water and well-sharpened on a stone.

Saṅghāṭiyā: Of the cloak. ( kosiya )

Sampārutaṃ: Completely bound. ( pariyonaddhaṃ )

Sampaliveṭhitaṃ: Completely wrapped around. ( samantato veṭhitaṃ )

437. ‘‘Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, saṅghāṭikassa saṅghāṭidhāraṇamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, acelakassa acelakamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, rajojallikassa rajojallikamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave , udakorohakassa udakorohaṇamattena udakorohakamattena (sī. pī.) sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, rukkhamūlikassa rukkhamūlikamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, abbhokāsikassa abbhokāsikamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, ubbhaṭṭhakassa ubbhaṭṭhakamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, pariyāyabhattikassa pariyāyabhattikamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, mantajjhāyakassa mantajjhāyakamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, jaṭilakassa jaṭādhāraṇamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. ‘‘Saṅghāṭikassa ce, bhikkhave, saṅghāṭidhāraṇamattena abhijjhālussa abhijjhā pahīyetha, byāpannacittassa byāpādo pahīyetha, kodhanassa kodho pahīyetha, upanāhissa upanāho pahīyetha, makkhissa makkho pahīyetha, paḷāsissa paḷāso pahīyetha, issukissa issā pahīyetha, maccharissa macchariyaṃ pahīyetha, saṭhassa sāṭheyyaṃ pahīyetha, māyāvissa māyā pahīyetha, pāpicchassa pāpikā icchā pahīyetha, micchādiṭṭhikassa micchādiṭṭhi pahīyetha, tamenaṃ mittāmaccā ñātisālohitā jātameva naṃ saṅghāṭikaṃ kareyyuṃ, saṅghāṭikattameva saṃghāṭīkatte ceva (ka.) samādapeyyuṃ – ‘ehi tvaṃ, bhadramukha, saṅghāṭiko hohi, saṅghāṭikassa te sato saṅghāṭidhāraṇamattena abhijjhālussa abhijjhā pahīyissati, byāpannacittassa byāpādo pahīyissati, kodhanassa kodho pahīyissati, upanāhissa upanāho pahīyissati, makkhissa makkho pahīyissati, paḷāsissa paḷāso pahīyissati, issukissa issā pahīyissati, maccharissa macchariyaṃ pahīyissati, saṭhassa sāṭheyyaṃ pahīyissati, māyāvissa māyā pahīyissati, pāpicchassa pāpikā icchā pahīyissati, micchādiṭṭhikassa micchādiṭṭhi pahīyissatī’ti. Yasmā ca kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, saṅghāṭikampi idhekaccaṃ passāmi abhijjhāluṃ byāpannacittaṃ kodhanaṃ upanāhiṃ makkhiṃ paḷāsiṃ issukiṃ macchariṃ saṭhaṃ māyāviṃ pāpicchaṃ micchādiṭṭhikaṃ, tasmā na saṅghāṭikassa saṅghāṭidhāraṇamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. saṃghāṭīkatte ceva (ka.) ‘‘Acelakassa ce, bhikkhave…pe… rajojallikassa ce, bhikkhave…pe… udakorohakassa ce, bhikkhave…pe… rukkhamūlikassa ce, bhikkhave…pe… abbhokāsikassa ce, bhikkhave…pe… ubbhaṭṭhakassa ce, bhikkhave…pe… pariyāyabhattikassa ce, bhikkhave…pe… mantajjhāyakassa ce, bhikkhave…pe… jaṭilakassa ce, bhikkhave, jaṭādhāraṇamattena abhijjhālussa abhijjhā pahīyetha, byāpannacittassa byāpādo pahīyetha , kodhanassa kodho pahīyetha, upanāhissa upanāho pahīyetha, makkhissa makkho pahīyetha, paḷāsissa paḷāso pahīyetha, issukissa issā pahīyetha, maccharissa macchariyaṃ pahīyetha, saṭhassa sāṭheyyaṃ pahīyetha, māyāvissa māyā pahīyetha, pāpicchassa pāpikā icchā pahīyetha, micchādiṭṭhikassa micchādiṭṭhi pahīyetha, tamenaṃ mittāmaccā ñātisālohitā jātameva naṃ jaṭilakaṃ kareyyuṃ, jaṭilakattameva jaṭilakatte ceva (ka.) samādapeyyuṃ – ‘ehi tvaṃ, bhadramukha, jaṭilako hohi, jaṭilakassa te sato jaṭādhāraṇamattena abhijjhālussa abhijjhā pahīyissati byāpannacittassa byāpādo pahīyissati, kodhanassa kodho pahīyissati…pe… pāpicchassa pāpikā icchā pahīyissati micchādiṭṭhikassa micchādiṭṭhi pahīyissatī’ti. Yasmā ca kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, jaṭilakampi idhekaccaṃ passāmi abhijjhāluṃ byāpannacittaṃ kodhanaṃ upanāhiṃ makkhiṃ palāsiṃ issukiṃ macchariṃ saṭhaṃ māyāviṃ pāpicchaṃ micchādiṭṭhiṃ, tasmā na jaṭilakassa jaṭādhāraṇamattena sāmaññaṃ vadāmi. jaṭilakatte ceva (ka.)
“Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of wearing the outer robe (saṃghāṭī).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of being naked (acelaka).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of being covered in dust and grime (rajojallika).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of plunging into water periodically (udakorohaka).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of dwelling at the root of a tree (rukkh mūlika).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of dwelling in the open air (abbhokāsika).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of dwelling in a cemetery (ubbhaṭṭhaka).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of eating food obtained by gleaning (pariyāyabhattika).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of reciting mantras (mantajjhāyaka).
Monks, I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of wearing matted hair (jaṭilaka).

If, monks, by merely wearing the outer robe, one who is greedy (abhijjhālu) were to abandon greed (abhijjhā), one whose mind is malevolent (byāpannacitta) were to abandon malevolence (byāpāda), one who is angry (kodhana) were to abandon anger (kodha), one who is resentful (upanāhi) were to abandon resentment (upanāha), one who is denigrating (makkhi) were to abandon denigration (makkha), one who is controlling (palāsi) were to abandon being controlling (palāsa), one who is envious (issuki) were to abandon envy (issā), one who is miserly (macchari) were to abandon miserliness (macchariyaṃ), one who is deceitful (saṭha) were to abandon deceit (sāṭheyyaṃ), one who is deceptive (māyāvi) were to abandon deception (māyā), one who has evil desires (pāpiccha) were to abandon evil desires (pāpikā icchā), one who has wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi) were to abandon wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi), then their friends, companions, relatives, and kin would make them wear the outer robe from birth, urging them simply to wear the outer robe, saying: ‘Come, good sir, be a wearer of the outer robe. While you are wearing the outer robe, by merely wearing it, the greed of the greedy will be abandoned, the malevolence of the malevolent-minded will be abandoned, the anger of the angry will be abandoned, the resentment of the resentful will be abandoned, the denigration of the denigrating will be abandoned, the controlling nature of the controlling will be abandoned, the envy of the envious will be abandoned, the miserliness of the miserly will be abandoned, the deceit of the deceitful will be abandoned, the deception of the deceptive will be abandoned, the evil desires of those with evil desires will be abandoned, and the wrong view of those with wrong view will be abandoned.’ But since I see some wearers of the outer robe here who are greedy, malevolent-minded, angry, resentful, denigrating, controlling, envious, miserly, deceitful, deceptive, having evil desires, and having wrong view, therefore I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of wearing the outer robe.

If, monks, by merely being naked… If, monks, by merely being covered in dust and grime… If, monks, by merely plunging into water periodically… If, monks, by merely dwelling at the root of a tree… If, monks, by merely dwelling in the open air… If, monks, by merely dwelling in a cemetery… If, monks, by merely eating food obtained by gleaning… If, monks, by merely reciting mantras… If, monks, by merely wearing matted hair, one who is greedy were to abandon greed, one whose mind is malevolent were to abandon malevolence, one who is angry were to abandon anger, one who is resentful were to abandon resentment, one who is denigrating were to abandon denigration, one who is controlling were to abandon being controlling, one who is envious were to abandon envy, one who is miserly were to abandon miserliness, one who is deceitful were to abandon deceit, one who is deceptive were to abandon deception, one who has evil desires were to abandon evil desires, one who has wrong view were to abandon wrong view, then their friends, companions, relatives, and kin would make them wear matted hair from birth, urging them simply to wear matted hair, saying: ‘Come, good sir, be a wearer of matted hair. While you are wearing matted hair, by merely wearing it, the greed of the greedy will be abandoned, the malevolence of the malevolent-minded will be abandoned, the anger of the angry will be abandoned… the evil desires of those with evil desires will be abandoned, and the wrong view of those with wrong view will be abandoned.’ But since I see some wearers of matted hair here who are greedy, malevolent-minded, angry, resentful, denigrating, controlling, envious, miserly, deceitful, deceptive, having evil desires, and having wrong view, therefore I do not call someone a true ascetic simply because of wearing matted hair."
437.Rajojallikassāti rajojalladhārino. Udakorohakassāti divasassa tikkhattuṃ udakaṃ orohantassa. Rukkhamūlikassāti rukkhamūlavāsino. Abbhokāsikassāti abbhokāsavāsino. Ubbhaṭṭhakassāti uddhaṃ ṭhitakassa. Pariyāyabhattikassāti māsavārena vā aḍḍhamāsavārena vā bhuñjantassa. Sabbametaṃ bāhirasamayeneva kathitaṃ. Imasmiñhi sāsane cīvaradharo bhikkhu saṅghāṭikoti na vuccati. Rajojalladhāraṇādivatāni imasmiṃ sāsane natthiyeva. Buddhavacanassa buddhavacanameva nāmaṃ, na mantāti. Rukkhamūliko, abbhokāsikoti ettakaṃyeva pana labbhati. Tampi bāhirasamayeneva kathitaṃ. Jātamevananti taṃdivase jātamattaṃyeva naṃ. Saṅghāṭikaṃ kareyyunti saṅghāṭikaṃ vatthaṃ nivāsetvā ca pārupitvā ca saṅghāṭikaṃ kareyyuṃ. Esa nayo sabbattha.

"rajojallikassā": "one who possesses the rajojalla."
"udakorohakassā": "one who descends into the water three times a day."
"rukkhamūlikassā": "one who dwells at the root of a tree (ṛukkhamūlavāsin)."
"abbhokāsikassā": "one who dwells in the open air."
"ubbhaṭṭhakassā": "one who is standing upright."
"pariyāyabhattikassā": "one who eats according to a monthly or half-monthly rotation."
All this has been said according to external conventions.
In this teaching (sāsana), a monk who wears robes (cīvaradharaḥ bhikṣu) is not called a saṅghāṭika.
Practices such as wearing the rajojalla, etc., do not exist in this teaching (sāsane).
The word of the Buddha (buddhavacana) is only called the word of the Buddha, not a mantra.
Only the tree-root dweller (ṛukkhamūliko), and the open-air dweller (abbhokāsiko) are obtained thus.
That too has been said according to external conventions.
"jātameva": "as soon as [he is] born."
"na": on that day, just as [he is] born.
"saṅghāṭikaṃ kareyyuṃ": they should make a saṅghāṭika, having put on and wrapped the cloth of the saṅghāṭika, they should make a saṅghāṭika.
This method applies everywhere (esa nayo sabbattha).

438. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu samaṇasāmīcippaṭipadaṃ paṭipanno hoti? Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno abhijjhālussa abhijjhā pahīnā hoti, byāpannacittassa byāpādo pahīno hoti, kodhanassa kodho pahīno hoti, upanāhissa upanāho pahīno hoti, makkhissa makkho pahīno hoti, paḷāsissa paḷāso pahīno hoti, issukissa issā pahīnā hoti, maccharissa macchariyaṃ pahīnaṃ hoti, saṭhassa sāṭheyyaṃ pahīnaṃ hoti, māyāvissa māyā pahīnā hoti, pāpicchassa pāpikā icchā pahīnā hoti, micchādiṭṭhikassa micchādiṭṭhi pahīnā hoti – imesaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, samaṇamalānaṃ samaṇadosānaṃ samaṇakasaṭānaṃ āpāyikānaṃ ṭhānānaṃ duggativedaniyānaṃ pahānā ‘samaṇasāmīcippaṭipadaṃ paṭipanno’ti vadāmi. So sabbehi imehi pāpakehi akusalehi dhammehi visuddhamattānaṃ samanupassati ( ) (vimuttamattānaṃ samanupassati) (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.). Tassa sabbehi imehi pāpakehi akusalehi dhammehi visuddhamattānaṃ samanupassato ( ) (vimuttamattānaṃ samanupassato) (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) pāmojjaṃ jāyati, pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vedeti, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati. ‘‘So mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ, tathā tatiyaṃ, tathā catutthaṃ. Iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharati. Karuṇāsahagatena cetasā…pe… muditāsahagatena cetasā…pe… upekkhāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ, tathā tatiyaṃ, tathā catutthaṃ. Iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ upekkhāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharati. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, pokkharaṇī acchodakā sātodakā sītodakā setakā supatitthā ramaṇīyā. Puratthimāya cepi disāya puriso āgaccheyya ghammābhitatto ghammapareto kilanto tasito pipāsito. So taṃ pokkharaṇiṃ āgamma vineyya udakapipāsaṃ vineyya ghammapariḷāhaṃ…pe… pacchimāya cepi disāya puriso āgaccheyya…pe… uttarāya cepi disāya puriso āgaccheyya…pe… dakkhiṇāya cepi disāya puriso āgaccheyya. Yato kuto cepi naṃ puriso āgaccheyya ghammābhitatto ghammapareto, kilanto tasito pipāsito. So taṃ pokkharaṇiṃ āgamma vineyya udakapipāsaṃ, vineyya ghammapariḷāhaṃ. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, khattiyakulā cepi agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma, evaṃ mettaṃ karuṇaṃ muditaṃ upekkhaṃ bhāvetvā labhati ajjhattaṃ tamahaṃ (ka.) vūpasamaṃ tamahaṃ (ka.). 438. And how, monks, does a monk practice the proper course of conduct befitting a samaṇa? In whatsoever monk, monks, greed has been eliminated in one who is greedy, ill-will has been eliminated in one whose mind is full of ill-will, anger has been eliminated in one who is angry, hostility has been eliminated in one who is hostile, contempt has been eliminated in one who is contemptuous, insolence has been eliminated in one who is insolent, envy has been eliminated in one who is envious, stinginess has been eliminated in one who is stingy, deceit has been eliminated in one who is deceitful, hypocrisy has been eliminated in one who is hypocritical, evil desires have been eliminated in one with evil desires, wrong view has been eliminated in one with wrong view—it is through the elimination of these corruptions of a samaṇa, these faults of a samaṇa, these stains of a samaṇa, states that lead to misery, that lead to suffering, that I say [he] practices the proper course of conduct befitting a samaṇa.

He sees himself as purified of all these evil, unwholesome qualities. As he sees himself purified of all these evil, unwholesome qualities, gladness (prāmojjaṃ) arises. When he is gladdened, rapture (pīti) arises. When his mind is filled with rapture, his body becomes tranquil (passaddhi); tranquil in body, he experiences happiness (sukhaṃ); being happy, his mind becomes concentrated (samādhi).

He abides pervading one quarter with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettāsahagatena cetasā), likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth. Thus above, below, around, everywhere, in its entirety, he abides pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with loving-kindness—abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility (averena) and without ill-will (abyābajjhena). With a mind imbued with compassion (karuṇāsahagatena cetasā)… with a mind imbued with appreciative joy (muditāsahagatena cetasā)… with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhāsahagatena cetasā) he abides pervading one quarter, likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth. Thus above, below, around, everywhere, in its entirety, he abides pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with equanimity—abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility and without ill-will.

Suppose there were a pond with clean, agreeable, cool water, transparent, with smooth banks, delightful. And a man came from the east scorched and exhausted by hot weather, weary, parched, and thirsty. Having come to that pond, he would get rid of his thirst for water and the burning heat…. And a man came from the west…. And a man came from the north…. And a man came from the south. From whatever direction a man came, scorched and exhausted by hot weather, weary, parched, and thirsty, having come to that pond, he would get rid of his thirst for water and the burning heat.

In the same way, monks, if someone from a noble family has gone forth from the house life into homelessness, and having come to the dhamma and discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, thus cultivates loving-kindness, compassion, appreciative joy, and equanimity, he attains inner peace (ajjhattaṃ vūpasamaṃ). Through inner peace, I say [he] practices the proper course of conduct befitting a samaṇa. If someone from a brahmin family…if someone from a merchant family…if someone from a worker family…if someone from whatever family has gone forth from the house life into homelessness, and having come to the dhamma and discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, thus cultivates loving-kindness, compassion, appreciative joy, and equanimity, he attains inner peace. Through inner peace, I say [he] practices the proper course of conduct befitting a samaṇa.

If someone from a noble family has gone forth from the house life into homelessness…
And he, through the destruction of the āsavas (influxes, taints, defilements), having realized it himself with direct knowledge, in this very life enters and dwells in the taintless liberation of mind (anāsavaṃ cetovimuttiṃ), liberation by wisdom (paññāvimuttiṃ).

Through the destruction of the āsavas, one becomes a samaṇa (ascetic).

Even from a Brahmin family... or a Vessa (merchant) family... or a Suddha (laborer) family... from whatever family he has gone forth from the household life into homelessness, he, through the destruction of the āsavas, having realized it himself with direct knowledge, in this very life enters and dwells in the taintless liberation of mind (anāsavaṃ cetovimuttiṃ), liberation by wisdom (paññāvimuttiṃ).

Through the destruction of the āsavas, one becomes a samaṇa (ascetic).

Thus spoke the Blessed One (bhagavān). Those monks, uplifted, rejoiced at the Blessed One’s words.
438.Visuddhamattānaṃ samanupassatīti attānaṃ visujjhantaṃ passati. Visuddhoti pana na tāva vattabbo. Pāmojjaṃ jāyatīti tuṭṭhākāro jāyati. Pamuditassa pītīti tuṭṭhassa sakalasarīraṃ khobhayamānā pīti jāyati. Pītimanassa kāyoti pītisampayuttassa puggalassa nāmakāyo. Passambhatīti vigatadaratho hoti. Sukhaṃ vedetīti kāyikampi cetasikampi sukhaṃ vediyati. Cittaṃ samādhiyatīti iminā nekkhammasukhena sukhitassa cittaṃ samādhiyati, appanāpattaṃ viya hoti. So mettāsahagatena cetasāti heṭṭhā kilesavasena āraddhā desanā pabbate vuṭṭhavuṭṭhi viya nadiṃ yathānusandhinā brahmavihārabhāvanaṃ otiṇṇā. Tattha yaṃ vattabbaṃ siyā, taṃ sabbaṃ visuddhimagge vuttameva. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, pokkharaṇīti mahāsīhanādasutte maggo pokkharaṇiyā upamito, idha sāsanaṃ upamitanti veditabbaṃ. Āsavānaṃ khayā samaṇo hotīti sabbakilesānaṃ samitattā paramatthasamaṇo hotīti. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Cūḷaassapurasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā. 438. “He observes himself as pure (visuddhamattānaṃ samanupassatī)” means he sees himself becoming pure.

However, it is not yet to be called pure.

“Joy arises (pāmojjaṃ jāyatī)” means a state of satisfaction arises.

“For the joyful, rapture (pītī) [comes] (pamuditassa pītī)” means for the satisfied, rapture arises, agitating the whole body.

“The body of one with rapture (pītimanassa kāyo)” means the mental body (nāmakāya) of a person endowed with rapture.

“Becomes tranquil (passambhatī)” means he is without agitation.

“He experiences happiness (sukhaṃ vedetī)” means he experiences bodily happiness (kāyikampi) and mental happiness (cetasikampi).

“The mind becomes concentrated (cittaṃ samādhiyatī)” means the mind of one who is happy with this happiness of renunciation (nekkhamma-sukhena) becomes concentrated; it is as if it has attained absorption (appanā).

“He, with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness (so mettāsahagatena cetasā)” means the teaching begun below in terms of defilements, like rain falling on a mountain, has descended into the development (bhāvana) of the divine abodes (brahmavihāra) in accordance with the connection [of the text].

Whatever should be said there, all that has been said in the Visuddhimagga itself.

"Just as, monks, a lotus pond (seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, pokkharaṇī)" in the Mahāsīhanāda Sutta, the path is compared to a lotus pond; here, the dispensation (sāsana) is to be understood as compared.

“Through the destruction of the corruptions, he becomes a samaṇa (āsavānaṃ khayā samaṇo hotī)” means because of the calming (samatattā) of all defilements, he becomes a samaṇa in the ultimate sense (paramattha).

The rest is clear throughout.
