Thus have I heard: Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park (ārāma).
There the Blessed One addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: “Monks!”
“Venerable sir (bhadante),” those monks replied to the Blessed One.
The Blessed One said this:

“When a cowherd (gopālako), monks, possesses eleven qualities, he is incapable of keeping and rearing a herd of cattle (gogaṇaṃ).
What are the eleven? Here, monks, a cowherd has no knowledge of form (rūpaññū), he is unskilled in characteristics (lakkhaṇakusalo), he fails to pick out flies’ eggs (āsāṭikaṃ hāretā), he fails to dress wounds (vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā), he fails to smoke out the sheds (dhūmaṃ kattā), he does not know the watering place (titthaṃ jānāti), he does not know what it is to have drunk (pītaṃ jānāti), he does not know the road (vīthiṃ jānāti), he is unskilled in pastures (gocarakusalo), he milks dry (anavasesadohī) and he shows no extra veneration (atirekapūjāya pūjetā) to those bulls (usabhā) who are fathers (gopitaro) and leaders (gopariṇāyakā) of the herd.
When a cowherd, monks, possesses these eleven qualities, he is incapable of keeping and rearing a herd of cattle.

"Just so, monks, when a bhikkhu possesses eleven qualities, he is incapable of growth (vuddhiṃ), increase (virūḷhiṃ), and fulfilment (vepullaṃ) in this Dhamma and Discipline (dhammavinaye).
What are the eleven? Here, monks, a bhikkhu has no knowledge of form (rūpaññū), he is unskilled in characteristics (lakkhaṇakusalo), he fails to pick out flies’ eggs (āsāṭikaṃ hāretā), he fails to dress wounds (vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā), he fails to smoke out the sheds (dhūmaṃ kattā), he does not know the watering place (titthaṃ jānāti), he does not know what it is to have drunk (pītaṃ jānāti), he does not know the road (vīthiṃ jānāti), he is unskilled in pastures (gocarakusalo), he milks dry (anavasesadohī) and he shows no extra veneration (atirekapūjāya pūjetā) to those elder bhikkhus (bhikkhū therā) of long-standing (rattaññū) who have long gone forth (cirapabbajitā), the fathers (saṅghapitaro) and leaders (saṅghapariṇāyakā) of the Saṅgha."

346. Thus I have heard: the Mahāgopālaka Sūtra.

There, there are three discussions: ekanāḷikā, caturassā, and nisinnavattikā.

There, after stating the Pāḷi, the explanation of each and every word is called ekanāḷikā.

Having shown the unlearned cowherd, having shown the unlearned bhikkhu, having shown the learned cowherd, having shown the learned bhikkhu, the discussion by binding the quatrain is called caturassā.

Having shown the unlearned cowherd [and] the conclusion of the going, having shown the unlearned bhikkhu [and] the conclusion of the going, having shown the learned cowherd [and] the conclusion of the going, having shown the learned bhikkhu [and] the conclusion of the going, this is called nisinnavattikā.

This is practiced by all the teachers here.

By eleven factors, bhikkhus, that is, by eleven parts devoid of good qualities.

"Gogaṇa " means a herd of cows. (gomaṇḍalaṃ)

"Pariharitu" means having taken up, to wander about.

"Phātiṃ kātu" means to bring about growth.

"Idhā" means in this world (imasmiṃ loke).

"Na rūpaññū hoti" means he does not know form by counting or by color.

"Gaṇanato na jānāti" means he does not know the number, thinking, "There are one hundred or one thousand of his qualities."

He, after counting the herd of cows when the cows have been carried off or have fled, thinking, "Today, so many are not seen," does not search around two or three villages or in the forest. Even when his herd of cows has entered into the cows of others, after counting the herd of cows, he does not drive them out, striking [them] with a stick, [thinking,] "These so many cows are not ours." His lost cows are simply lost.

The cow owners, seeing him wandering about having taken the cows of others, scolding him, [thinking,] "For so long, this one takes our cows," take their own cows and go away.

His herd of cows also diminishes, and he is excluded even from the enjoyment of the five products of the cow.

"Vaṇṇato na jānāti" means he does not know by color, [thinking,] "So many cows are white, so many are red, so many are black, so many are mottled, so many are blue." He, when the cows have been carried off or...pe...is excluded even from the enjoyment of the five products of the cow.

By eleven factors, bhikkhus, to tend the gogaṇa, to bring about growth, here, he is not knowledgable about the appearance, by counting, by color.

"Na lakkhaṇakusalo hoti" means he does not know the characteristics, the different marks such as bows, spears, and tridents made on the bodies of the cows. He, when the cows have been carried off or have fled, today such and such characteristics and such and such characteristics the cows are not seen...pe...he is excluded even from the enjoyment of the five products of the cow.

"Na lakkhaṇakusalo hoti.

"Na āsāṭikaṃ hāretā" means there is a wound in the places struck by stumps, thorns, etc. of the cows.

There, blue flies lay eggs; that is the meaning of āsāṭika.

Having removed those with a stick, medicine should be given.

The foolish cowherd does not do that; therefore it was said: "He is not one who removes the āsāṭika."

His wounds of the cows grow, they are deep, worms enter the stomach, the cows, overcome by illness, are not able to eat grass to their satisfaction, nor are they able to drink water.

There, the milk of the cows is cut off, the swiftness of the oxen decreases, there is danger to the life of both.

Thus, his herd of cows also diminishes, and he is excluded even from the five products of the cow.

"Na āsāṭikaṃ hāretā.

"Na vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti" means the wound that has arisen in just the way said, after giving medicine, should be covered by binding with bark or with a bandage.

The foolish cowherd does not do that; then from the wounds of his cows, serum flows, those rub against each other, therefore other wounds also arise.

Thus the cows, overcome by illness, are not able to eat grass to their satisfaction...pe...he is excluded.

"Na vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti.

"Na dhūmaṃ kattā hoti" means when the herd of cows has entered the pen in the inner rains, at the time when gnats, mosquitoes, etc. are abundant, smoke should be made there in that place; the unlearned cowherd does not do that.

The herd of cows, afflicted all night by gnats, etc., not obtaining sleep, on the next day, lying down in the forest there in that place at the roots of trees, etc., sleeps, [and is] not able to eat grass to their satisfaction...pe...he is excluded even from the enjoyment of the five products of the cow.
Not making smoke."

"He does not know the tirtha (bathing place or ford)." Because he does not know whether the tirtha is level or uneven, shallow or deep, he drives the cows down to an improper tirtha.
When they step on stones, etc. in the uneven tirtha, their feet are broken. Those who have gone down into a tirtha that is deep and has crocodiles, are seized by crocodiles, and so on.
Today this many cows are lost, today that many cows are lost, he is liable to be spoken of in this way.
Thus his herd of cows also declines, and he is excluded from the five products of the cow as well.

"He does not know the tirtha (watering place)."

"He does not know what has been drunk." He knows neither what has been drunk nor what has not been drunk.
Indeed, the cowherd should know thus: "By this cow water has been drunk; by this cow water has not been drunk; by this cow an opportunity has been obtained at the watering tirtha; by this cow an opportunity has not been obtained." Thus he should know what has been drunk and not drunk.
But this one, thinking, "Having protected the herd of cows in the forest during the day, I will give them water to drink," goes to a river or a pond, taking them there.
There, the great bulls, the lesser bulls, the strong cows, the weak ones, the old cows— striking each other with their horns or ribs, making an opportunity for themselves, entering water the measure of their thighs, drink as they please.
The remaining ones, not obtaining an opportunity, stand on the bank and drink muddy, mixed water, or they are just ones that have not drunk.
Then the cowherd strikes them on their backs and again drives them into the forest. There, the cows that have not drunk, languishing with thirst, are not able to eat grass to their satisfaction. There, the cows' milk is cut off, the bulls' vigor declines...and so on... he is excluded.

"He does not know what has been drunk."

"He does not know the vīthi (path)." Because he does not know, "This path is level and safe, this is uneven, dangerous, and full of fear."
Avoiding a level and safe path, he leads the herd of cows along another path. There, the cows, overcome by the smell of lions, tigers, and so on, or by the danger of thieves, stand with their necks raised up, sharing the nature of frightened deer, not eating grass to their satisfaction, not drinking water. There, the cows' milk is cut off...and so on... he is excluded.

"He does not know the path."

"He is not skilled in pasture." Indeed, the cowherd should be skilled in pasture. He should know whether it is a five-day turn or a seven-day turn. Having pastured the herd of cows in one direction, he should not pasture them there again on the following day.
Indeed, by a large herd of cows, a place that has been grazed is made clean like the surface of a drum, devoid of grass, and even the water is stirred up.
Therefore, it is fitting to pasture them there again on the fifth or seventh day. Indeed, by this much even the grass grows back, and the water becomes clear.
But this one does not know this five-day turn or seven-day turn. Day after day, he protects them in the same protected place.
Then his herd of cows does not obtain green grass. Eating dry grass, they drink muddy, mixed water. There, the cows' milk is cut off...and so on... he is excluded.

"He is not skilled in pasture."

"He is also one who milks without leaving any remainder." Indeed, the wise cowherd should, as long as the calf's flesh and blood remains, having left one or two teats, be one who milks leaving a remainder.
This one milks everything without leaving anything for the calf. The calf, who is to be nourished with milk, wastes away with thirst for milk, and being unable to stand, trembling, falls down in front of its mother and dies.
Seeing her little one, the mother, thinking, "My little one does not even obtain his own mother's milk to drink," because of sorrow for her little one, is not able to eat grass to her satisfaction, nor is she able to drink water. In her teats, the milk is cut off.
Thus his herd of cows also declines, and he is excluded from the five products of the cow as well.

"He is also one who milks without leaving any remainder."

They make the cows' drinking places: these are called "cow-fathers."
They lead the cows, taking them where they please: these are called "cow-leaders."
Not for excessive honoring." Indeed, the wise cowherd honors a bull of such a kind with excessive honoring. He gives choice food for the cows, he decorates it with fragrant five-finger marks, he places a garland on it, and he puts golden and silver coverings on its horns. At night, having lit a lamp, he lets it lie down under a cloth canopy.
But this one does not do even a single honor to it. The bulls, not obtaining excessive honoring, do not protect the herd of cows, they do not ward off danger.
Thus his herd of cows declines, and he is excluded from the five products of the cow.

Cow-fathers, cow-leaders, not for excessive honoring.

347. “And how, monks, does a bhikkhu (monk) have no knowledge of form (rūpa)? Here, monks, a bhikkhu does not understand as it actually is, that all form whatsoever is the four great elements (cattāri mahābhūtāni) and form derived from the four great elements (catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāyarūpa).” That is how, monks, a bhikkhu has no knowledge of form.

“And how, monks, is a bhikkhu unskilled in characteristics (lakkhana)? Here, monks, a bhikkhu does not understand as it actually is, that ‘a fool has action as his characteristic, a wise man has action as his characteristic.’" That is how, monks, a bhikkhu is unskilled in characteristics.

“And how, monks, does a bhikkhu fail to pick out flies’ eggs (āsāṭikaṃ hāretā)? Here, monks, a bhikkhu tolerates a sensual thought (kāmavitakka) that has arisen; he does not abandon it, remove it, do away with it, or annihilate it. When a thought of ill-will (byāpādavitakka) has arisen… when a thought of violence (vihiṃsāvitakka) has arisen… when evil unwholesome states (pāpake akusale dhamme) that have arisen again and again, he tolerates them; he does not abandon them, remove them, do away with them, or annihilate them." That is how, monks, a bhikkhu fails to pick out flies’ eggs.

“And how, monks, does a bhikkhu fail to dress wounds (vanaṃ paṭicchādetā)? Here, monks, having seen a form with the eye (cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā), a bhikkhu grasps at its signs (nimittaggāhī) and details (anubyañjanaggāhī). Even though, when he leaves the eye faculty (cakkhundriyaṃ) unguarded, evil unwholesome states of covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassā) might invade him, he does not practice the way of its restraint (saṃvarāya), he does not guard the eye faculty (cakkhundriyaṃ), he does not undertake the restraint of the eye faculty. Having heard a sound with the ear… smelled an odour with the nose… tasted a flavour with the tongue… touched a tangible with the body… cognized a mind-object with the mind, he grasps at its signs and details. Even though, when he leaves the mind faculty unguarded, evil unwholesome states of covetousness and grief might invade him, he does not practice the way of its restraint, he does not guard the mind faculty, he does not undertake the restraint of the mind faculty.” That is how, monks, a bhikkhu fails to dress wounds.

“And how, monks, does a bhikkhu fail to smoke out the sheds (dhūmaṃ kattā)? Here, monks, a bhikkhu does not extensively teach the Dharma (dhammaṃ) to others as he has heard and learned it.” That is how, monks, a bhikkhu fails to smoke out the sheds.

“And how, monks, does a bhikkhu not know the watering place (tittaṃ jānāti)? Here, monks, a bhikkhu does not go from time to time to those bhikkhus who have learned much, who are well versed in the tradition, who maintain the Dharma, the Discipline (vinaya), and the Codes (mātikā), and he does not enquire and ask questions of them thus: ‘How is this, venerable sir? What is its meaning?’ Those venerable ones do not reveal what is unrevealed, they do not clarify what is unclarified, and they do not dispel doubt about the many points that cause doubt." That is how, monks, a bhikkhu does not know the watering place.

“And how, monks, does a bhikkhu not know what it is to have drunk (pītaṃ jānāti)? Here, monks, when the Dharma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata is being taught, a bhikkhu does not gain inspiration in the meaning (attha), does not gain inspiration in the Dharma, does not gain gladness connected with the Dharma (dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ).” That is how, monks, a bhikkhu does not know what it is to have drunk.

“And how, monks, does a bhikkhu not know the road (vīthiṃ jānāti)? Here, monks, a bhikkhu does not understand the Noble Eightfold Path (ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ) as it actually is.” That is how, monks, a bhikkhu does not know the road.

“And how, monks, is a bhikkhu unskilled in pastures (gocarakusalo)? Here, monks, a bhikkhu does not understand the four foundations of mindfulness (cattāro satipaṭṭhāne) as they actually are.” That is how, monks, a bhikkhu is unskilled in pastures.

“And how, monks, does a bhikkhu milk dry (anavasesadohī)? Here, monks, faithful householders invite a bhikkhu and offer him robes (cīvara), alms food (piṇḍapāta), lodging (senāsana), and medicine as the requisites for the sick (gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārehi), but in this matter the bhikkhu does not know moderation in accepting them.” That is how, monks, a bhikkhu milks dry.

“And how, monks, does a bhikkhu show no extra veneration to those elder bhikkhus of long-standing who have long gone forth, the fathers and leaders of the Saṃgha?
Here, monks, a bhikkhu does not maintain bodily acts of loving-kindness (mettā kāyakamma) both openly and privately towards those elder bhikkhus who are senior, who have long been ordained, the fathers of the Saṅgha, the leaders of the Saṅgha; he does not maintain verbal acts of loving-kindness (mettā vacīkamma) towards them both openly and privately; he does not maintain mental acts of loving-kindness (mettā manokamma) towards them both openly and privately.

Thus, monks, a bhikkhu shows no extra veneration to those elder bhikkhus who are senior, who have long been ordained, the fathers and leaders of the Saṅgha.

Monks, a bhikkhu who possesses these eleven qualities is incapable of growth (vuddhi), increase (virūḷhi), and fulfilment (vepulla) in this Dhamma and Vinaya (teachings and discipline).

347. "Here" ( idhā) means in this teaching (imasmiṃ sāsane).

"He does not know form" (na rūpaññū hotī) means he does not know form as it has been described, namely, "the four great elements (cattāri mahābhūtāni) and the form derived from the four great elements (catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāyarūpa)," in two ways, either by enumeration (gaṇanato) or by origin (samuṭṭhānato).

He does not know by enumeration (gaṇanato) means he does not know that "the eye-sphere (cakkhāyatanaṃ), the ear-nose-tongue-body-sphere (sota-ghāna-jivhā-kāyāyatanaṃ), the form-sound-smell-taste-tangible-sphere (rūpa-sadda-gandha-rasa-phoṭṭhabbāyatanaṃ), the woman-faculty (itthindriyaṃ), the man-faculty (purisindriyaṃ), the life-faculty (jīvitindriyaṃ), bodily communication (kāyaviññatti), verbal communication (vacīviññatti), the space-element (ākāsadhātu), the water-element (āpodhātu), lightness of form (rūpassa lahutā), pliancy (mudutā), adaptability (kammaññatā), integration (upacayo), continuity (santati), decay (jaratā), impermanence of form (rūpassa aniccatā), and edible food (kabaḷīkāro āhāro)," these twenty-five categories of form have come in the Pāli.

Just as a cowherd does not know the form of cows by enumeration (gaṇanato gunnaṃ rūpaṃ na jānāti), so too is this monk.

Not knowing form by enumeration (gaṇanato rūpaṃ ajānanto), having grasped form (rūpaṃ pariggahetvā), having determined the formless (arūpaṃ vavatthapetvā), having grasped form and the formless (rūpārūpaṃ pariggahetvā), having considered the conditions (paccayaṃ sallakkhetvā), having imposed the characteristics (lakkhaṇaṃ āropetvā), he is not able to bring the meditation subject (kammaṭṭhānaṃ) to the summit (matthakaṃ pāpetuṃ na sakkoti).

Just as the herd of cows of that cowherd does not grow (yathā tassa gopālakassa gogaṇo na vaḍḍhati), so too in this teaching he does not grow in ethical conduct (sīla), concentration (samādhi), insight (vipassanā), path (magga), fruit (phala), and nirvāṇa (nibbānehi na vaḍḍhati). And just as that cowherd is excluded from the five products of the cow (pañcahi gorasehi paribāhiro hoti), so too is he excluded from the five aggregates of qualities (pañcahi dhammakkhandhehi paribāhiro hoti), namely, the aggregate of flawless ethical conduct (asekkhena sīlakkhandhena), flawless concentration (asekkhena samādhi), wisdom (paññā), liberation (vimutti), and the aggregate of the vision of the knowledge of liberation (vimuttiñāṇadassanakkhandhena).

By origin (samuṭṭhānato)…

He does not know means he does not know that "so much form has a single origin (ettakaṃ rūpaṃ ekasamuṭṭhānaṃ), so much has two origins (ettakaṃ dvisamuṭṭhānaṃ), so much has three origins (ettakaṃ tisamuṭṭhānaṃ), so much has four origins (ettakaṃ catusamuṭṭhānaṃ), so much does not originate from anywhere (ettakaṃ na kutocisamuṭṭhātī)."

Just as a cowherd does not know the form of cows by color (vaṇṇato gunnaṃ rūpaṃ na jānāti), so too is this monk.

Not knowing form by origin (samuṭṭhānato rūpaṃ ajānanto), having grasped form (rūpaṃ pariggahetvā), having determined the formless (arūpaṃ vavatthapetvā)…he is excluded.

He is not skilled in characteristics (na lakkhaṇakusalo hotī) means he does not know that "action has characteristics, a fool has characteristics, a wise person has characteristics," thus it is said, "skillful and unskillful action, the fool and the wise person have characteristics (kusalākusalaṃ kammaṃ paṇḍitabālalakkhaṇanti na jānāti)."

Not knowing thus, avoiding fools he does not associate with the wise (bāle vajjetvā paṇḍite na sevati), avoiding fools and not associating with the wise, he does not know what is allowable and unallowable (kappiyākappiyaṃ), skillful and unskillful (kusalākusalaṃ), blameworthy and blameless (*sāvajNot knowing, having become fixed on the worldly path, he is not able to bring about the supramundane… he is excluded.

He does not know the path. He is not skilled in the pasture: that cowherd, like [one who reckons] a five-day period as a seven-day period, does not understand the four foundations of mindfulness, "these are worldly, these are supramundane," according to reality.

Not knowing, having roamed his knowledge in subtle places, having become fixed on the worldly foundations of mindfulness, he is not able to bring about the supramundane… he is excluded.

He is not skilled in the pasture. He is an indiscriminate milker: not knowing the proper measure in receiving, he milks indiscriminately.

But in the section on exposition, they invite [him] to bring [offerings]: they invite [him] having brought [offerings]. Here, there are two kinds of bringing: bringing by speech and bringing of requisites. Bringing by speech means people, having gone to the monk, invite [him], "Speak, venerable sir, by which [there is] benefit." Bringing of requisites means having taken cloths, etc., or ghee, molasses, etc., having gone to the monk, they say, "Take, venerable sir, as much as [there is] benefit." There, the monk does not know the proper measure: the monk does not know the measure in those requisites - "the patron's resources should be known, the value of the gift should be known, one's own strength should be known" - not taking [requisites] with proper measure in the way that was said in the Rathavinīta, he takes all that they bring, this is the meaning. People, being remorseful, do not invite [him] again having brought [offerings]. He, being exhausted by the requisites, is not able to take up a meditation subject and develop it… he is excluded.

He is an indiscriminate milker. They invite [him] to bring [offerings]. Bringing by speech. Bringing of requisites. There, the monk does not know the proper measure. They are not those who honor with excessive honor: that cowherd, like a great bull, does not honor those elder monks with excessive honor by this loving-kindness of body-action, etc., both openly and in secret. Then the elders, [thinking], "these do not show respect to us," do not accept the new monks with the two acceptances, not with the acceptance of material things, with a robe, or a bowl, or what is connected with the bowl, or a dwelling place. Even when [they are] exhausted and withered, they do not care for [them]. They do not teach [them] the Pāḷi, or the commentary, or the arrangement of a Dhamma talk, or a secret text. The new monks, not obtaining these two acceptances entirely from the elders, are not able to be established in this Dispensation. Just as that cowherd's herd of cows does not grow, so sīla, etc., do not grow. And just as that cowherd is excluded from the five products of the cow, so they are excluded from the five aggregates of qualities. The bright side should be understood by connecting [it] in terms of the opposite of what was said in the dark side.

They are not those who honor with excessive honor.

The Mahāgopālakasuttavaṇṇanā is finished.

Idha, bhikkhave , gopālako rūpaññū hoti, lakkhaṇakusalo hoti, āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti, vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti, dhūmaṃ kattā hoti, titthaṃ jānāti, pītaṃ jānāti, vīthiṃ jānāti, gocarakusalo hoti, sāvasesadohī ca hoti. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu bhabbo imasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjituṃ. Katamehi ekādasahi? 348. "Bhikkhus, when a cowherd possesses eleven factors (ekādasahi aṅgehi), he is capable of keeping and rearing a herd of cattle.

What are the eleven? Here, bhikkhus, a cowherd has knowledge of form (rūpaññū), he is skilled in characteristics (lakkhaṇakusalo), he picks out flies’ eggs (āsāṭikaṃ hāretā), he dresses wounds (vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā), he smokes out the sheds (dhūmaṃ kattā), he knows the watering place (titthaṃ jānāti), he knows what it is to have drunk (pītaṃ jānāti), he knows the road (vīthiṃ jānāti), he is skilled in pastures (gocarakusalo), he does not milk dry (sāvasesadohī).

And he shows extra veneration to those bulls (usabhā) who are fathers (gopitaro) and leaders of the herd (gopariṇāyakā).

When a cowherd possesses these eleven factors (ekādasahi aṅgehi), he is capable of keeping and rearing a herd of cattle.

“So too, bhikkhus, when a bhikṣu possesses these eleven qualities (ekādasahi dhammehi), he is capable of growth (vuddhiṃ), increase (virūḷhiṃ), and fulfilment (vepullaṃ) in this Dhamma and Discipline (dhammavinaye).

What are the eleven? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikṣu has knowledge of form (rūpaññū), he is skilled in characteristics (lakkhaṇakusalo), he picks out flies’ eggs (āsāṭikaṃ hāretā), he dresses wounds (vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā), he smokes out the sheds (dhūmaṃ kattā), he knows the watering place (titthaṃ jānāti), he knows what it is to have drunk (pītaṃ jānāti), he knows the road (vīthiṃ jānāti), he is skilled in pastures (gocarakusalo), he does not milk dry (sāvasesadohī).

And he shows extra veneration to those elder bhikkhus (bhikkhū therā) of longstanding (rattaññū) who have long since gone forth (cirapabbajitā), the fathers (saṅghapitaro) and leaders of the Sangha (saṅghapariṇāyakā).

349. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu rūpaññū hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yaṃ kiñci rūpaṃ sabbaṃ rūpaṃ ‘cattāri mahābhūtāni , catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāyarūpa’nti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu rūpaññū hoti. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu lakkhaṇakusalo hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kammalakkhaṇo bālo, kammalakkhaṇo paṇḍitoti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu lakkhaṇakusalo hoti. ‘‘Kathañca , bhikkhave, bhikkhu āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu uppannaṃ kāmavitakkaṃ nādhivāseti, pajahati vinodeti byantī karoti anabhāvaṃ gameti. Uppannaṃ byāpādavitakkaṃ…pe… uppannaṃ vihiṃsāvitakkaṃ…pe… uppannuppanne pāpake akusale dhamme nādhivāseti, pajahati vinodeti byantī karoti anabhāvaṃ gameti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā na nimittaggāhī hoti nānubyañjanaggāhī. Yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ cakkhundriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ tassa saṃvarāya paṭipajjati, rakkhati cakkhundriyaṃ, cakkhundriye saṃvaraṃ āpajjati. Sotena saddaṃ sutvā…pe… ghānena gandhaṃ ghāyitvā…pe… jivhāya rasaṃ sāyitvā…pe… kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ phusitvā…pe… manasā dhammaṃ viññāya na nimittaggāhī hoti nānubyañjanaggāhī. Yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ manindriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ tassa saṃvarāya paṭipajjati, rakkhati manindriyaṃ, manindriye saṃvaraṃ āpajjati. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhūmaṃ kattā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yathāsutaṃ yathāpariyattaṃ dhammaṃ vitthārena paresaṃ desetā hoti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhūmaṃ kattā hoti. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu titthaṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū bahussutā āgatāgamā dhammadharā vinayadharā mātikādharā te kālena kālaṃ upasaṅkamitvā paripucchati, paripañhati – ‘idaṃ, bhante, kathaṃ? Imassa ko attho’ti? Tassa te āyasmanto avivaṭañceva vivaranti, anuttānīkatañca uttānī karonti, anekavihitesu ca kaṅkhāṭhānīyesu dhammesu kaṅkhaṃ paṭivinodenti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu titthaṃ jānāti. ‘‘Kathañca bhikkhave, bhikkhu pītaṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye desiyamāne labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu pītaṃ jānāti. ‘‘Kathañca , bhikkhave, bhikkhu vīthiṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vīthiṃ jānāti. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu gocarakusalo hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu cattāro satipaṭṭhāne yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu gocarakusalo hoti. ‘‘Kathañca bhikkhave, bhikkhu sāvasesadohī hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhuṃ saddhā gahapatikā abhihaṭṭhuṃ pavārenti cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārehi. Tatra bhikkhu mattaṃ jānāti paṭiggahaṇāya. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sāvasesadohī hoti. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā, te atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā tesu mettaṃ kāyakammaṃ paccupaṭṭhāpeti āvi ceva raho ca; mettaṃ vacīkammaṃ paccupaṭṭhāpeti āvi ceva raho ca; mettaṃ manokammaṃ paccupaṭṭhāpeti āvi ceva raho ca. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā te atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti. ‘‘Imehi kho, bhikkhave, ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu bhabbo imasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjitu’’nti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Mahāgopālakasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ tatiyaṃ.
“And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu have knowledge of form? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu understands as it actually is thus: ‘All material form of whatever kind consists of the four great elements (cattāri mahābhūtāni) and the material form derived from the four great elements (catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāyarūpa).’ That is how a bhikkhu has knowledge of form.

“How, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu skilled in characteristics? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu understands as it actually is thus: ‘A fool is characterized by their actions; a wise person is characterized by their actions.’ That is how a bhikkhu is skilled in characteristics.

“And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu pick out flies’ eggs? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not tolerate an arisen thought of sensual desire; he abandons it, dispels it, terminates it, and obliterates it. (He does not tolerate) an arisen thought of ill will… (He does not tolerate) an arisen thought of harming… (He does not tolerate) bad unwholesome states whenever they arise; he abandons them, dispels them, terminates them, and obliterates them. That is how a bhikkhu picks out flies’ eggs.

“And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu dress wounds? Here, bhikkhus, having seen a form with the eye, the bhikkhu does not grasp at the sign or the details. Since, if he were to dwell without restraint over the faculty of the eye, evil unwholesome states such as covetousness and grief might assail him, he practises restraint, guards the faculty of the eye, and achieves restraint over the faculty of the eye. Having heard a sound with the ear … having smelled an odour with the nose … having tasted a flavour with the tongue … having touched a tangible object with the body … having cognized a mind-object with the mind, the bhikkhu does not grasp at the sign or the details. Since, if he were to dwell without restraint over the faculty of the mind, evil unwholesome states such as covetousness and grief might assail him, he practises restraint, guards the faculty of the mind, and achieves restraint over the faculty of the mind. That is how a bhikkhu dresses wounds.

“How, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu smoke out the sheds? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu teaches the Dhamma in detail to others just as he has heard it and learned it. That is how a bhikkhu smokes out the sheds.

“How, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu know the watering place? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu approaches those bhikkhus who are learned, versed in the scriptures, observers of the Dhamma, observers of the Vinaya, observers of the Codes, and when from time to time one asks them questions, ‘How is this, venerable sir? What is the meaning of this?’ then those venerable ones reveal the unrevealed, explain the unexplained, and remove doubt about the many things that raise doubts. That is how a bhikkhu knows the watering place.

“How, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu know what it is to have drunk? Here, bhikkhus, when the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata is being taught, a bhikkhu gains inspiration in the meaning, gains inspiration in the Dhamma, gains gladness connected with the Dhamma. That is how a bhikkhu knows what it is to have drunk.

“How, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu understand the road? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu understands the Noble Eightfold Path (ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ) as it actually is. That is how a bhikkhu understands the road.

“How, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu skilled in pastures? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu understands the four foundations of mindfulness (cattāro satipaṭṭhāne) as they actually are. That is how a bhikkhu is skilled in pastures.

“How, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu not milk dry? Here, bhikkhus, when faithful householders invite a bhikkhu to take as much as he likes of robes, almsfood, resting places, and medicinal requisites, the bhikkhu knows moderation in accepting. That is how a bhikkhu does not milk dry.
“And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu show extra veneration to those elder bhikkhus of long-standing who have long gone forth, the fathers and leaders of the saṅgha? Here, a bhikkhu maintains bodily acts of loving-kindness (maitrī-kāyakarma) towards those elder bhikkhus both openly and privately; he maintains verbal acts of loving-kindness (maitrī-vacīkarma) towards them both openly and privately; he maintains mental acts of loving-kindness (maitrī-manokarma) towards them both openly and privately. That is how a bhikkhu shows extra veneration to those elder bhikkhus of long-standing who have long gone forth, the fathers and leaders of the saṅgha.

“When a bhikkhu possesses these eleven qualities (ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato), he is capable of growth (vṛddhi), increase (virūḷhi), and fulfilment (vepulla) in this dharma and discipline (dharma-vinaya).”

The Blessed One said this.

Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Mahāgopālaka Sutta is finished, the third.
