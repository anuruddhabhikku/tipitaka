Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ sunivattho hoti pattacīvaramādāya vesāliṃ piṇḍāya pavisitukāmo pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya… pavisitukāmo hoti (sī.). Disvāna bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ayaṃ, bhante, saccako nigaṇṭhaputto āgacchati bhassappavādako paṇḍitavādo sādhusammato bahujanassa. Eso kho, bhante, avaṇṇakāmo buddhassa, avaṇṇakāmo dhammassa, avaṇṇakāmo saṅghassa. Sādhu, bhante, bhagavā muhuttaṃ nisīdatu anukampaṃ upādāyā’’ti. Nisīdi bhagavā paññatte āsane. Atha kho saccako nigaṇṭhaputto yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi, sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Vesālī in the Great Wood in the Hall with the Peaked Roof.

Now on that occasion, when it was morning, the Blessed One had finished dressing and had taken his bowl and outer robe, desiring to go into Vesālī for alms.

Then, as Saccaka the Nigaṇṭha’s son was walking and wandering for exercise, he came to the Hall with the Peaked Roof in the Great Wood.

The venerable Ānanda saw Saccaka the Nigaṇṭha’s son coming in the distance.

Seeing the Blessed One, he said this: “Venerable sir, this is Saccaka, the son of the Nigaṇṭha, who is coming, a fluent speaker, a sophist, considered by many to be a good man.

He wants to discredit the Buddha, discredit the dharma (Dhamma), and discredit the sangha (Saṅgha).

It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would sit down for a moment out of compassion."

The Blessed One sat down on the seat that was prepared for him.

Then Saccaka the Nigaṇṭha’s son went up to the Blessed One and exchanged greetings with him. When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

Having sat down to one side, Saccaka the Nigaṇṭha’s son said this to the Blessed One:

Bhagavā evarūpe samaye rattadupaṭṭaṃ nivāsetvā kāyabandhanaṃ bandhitvā paṃsukūlacīvaraṃ ekaṃsaṃ pārupitvā gandhakuṭito nikkhamma bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto gandhakuṭipamukhe aṭṭhāsi. Taṃ sandhāya, – ‘‘ekaṃ samayanti ca tena kho pana samayenāti ca pubbaṇhasamaya’’nti ca vuttaṃ. Pavisitukāmoti piṇḍāya pavisissāmīti evaṃ katasanniṭṭhāno. Tenupasaṅkamīti kasmā upasaṅkamīti? Vādāropanajjhāsayena. Evaṃ kirassa ahosi – ‘‘pubbepāhaṃ apaṇḍitatāya sakalaṃ vesāliparisaṃ gahetvā samaṇassa gotamassa santikaṃ gantvā parisamajjhe maṅku jāto. 364. Thus I have heard: the Mahāsaccakasuttaṃ.

Therein, the word " ekaṃ samaya " (one time), " tena kho pana samayena " (at that time), and " pubbaṇhasamaya " (morning time) refer to the same time in three phrases.

For monks, after performing the customary observances, washing their face, taking their robes and bowl, worshiping the stupa, thinking “Which village shall I enter for alms?” is known as the time of standing in the windbreak of thoughts.

The Blessed One, at such a time, having dressed in a red upper robe, tied his waistband, put on a robe made of rags from a dust heap, went out from the scented chamber surrounded by a community of monks, and stood in front of the scented chamber.

In reference to that, it was said, " ekaṃ samayaṃ " (one time), " tena kho pana samayena " (at that time), and " pubbaṇhasamaya " (morning time).

"pavisitukāmo " (desiring to enter) means resolving to enter for alms.

"tenupasaṅkamī " (then he approached): Why did he approach? With the intention of raising a debate.

Thus it occurred to him: "Formerly, due to my lack of wisdom, I took the entire Vesālī assembly, went to the presence of the ascetic Gotama, and became embarrassed in the midst of the assembly.

Now, without doing so, I will go alone and raise a debate.

If I am able to defeat the ascetic Gotama, I will make known my doctrine and achieve victory.

If the ascetic Gotama's victory will be, no one will know, like a dance in the dark," having taken a riddle question, he approached with this intention of debating.

"anukampaṃ upādāyā " (having taken compassion) means with compassion towards Saccaka, the son of Nigaṇṭha.

Thus it occurred to the Elder: "While the Blessed One is sitting for a moment, he will obtain the sight of the Buddha and the hearing of the Dhamma.

That will lead to their welfare and happiness for a long time."

Therefore, having requested the Blessed One, having spread out the rag-robe fourfold, he said, "nisīdatu bhagavā " (may the Blessed One sit down).

"'Ānanda speaks with a reason,' thinking thus, the Blessed One sat down on the prepared seat.

"bhagavantaṃ etadavocā " (he said this to the Blessed One) means setting aside the question that he had come to ask, turning the subject aside, he spoke these words, "santi, bho Gotamā " (are there, good Gotamas?).

anukampaṃ upādāyā (having taken compassion), nisīdatu bhagavā (may the Blessed One sit down), bhagavantaṃ etadavocā (he said this to the Blessed One), santi, bho Gotamā (are there, good Gotamas?).

365. ‘‘Santi, bho gotama, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā kāyabhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā viharanti, no cittabhāvanaṃ. Phusanti hi te, bho gotama, sārīrikaṃ dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ. Bhūtapubbaṃ, bho gotama, sārīrikāya dukkhāya vedanāya phuṭṭhassa sato ūrukkhambhopi nāma bhavissati, hadayampi nāma phalissati, uṇhampi lohitaṃ mukhato uggamissati, ummādampi pāpuṇissati pāpuṇissanti (syā. kaṃ.) cittakkhepaṃ. Tassa kho etaṃ, bho gotama, kāyanvayaṃ cittaṃ hoti, kāyassa vasena vattati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Abhāvitattā cittassa. Santi pana, bho gotama, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā cittabhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā viharanti, no kāyabhāvanaṃ. Phusanti hi te, bho gotama, cetasikaṃ dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ. Bhūtapubbaṃ, bho gotama, cetasikāya dukkhāya vedanāya phuṭṭhassa sato ūrukkhambhopi nāma bhavissati, hadayampi nāma phalissati, uṇhampi lohitaṃ mukhato uggamissati, ummādampi pāpuṇissati cittakkhepaṃ. Tassa kho eso, bho gotama, cittanvayo kāyo hoti, cittassa vasena vattati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Abhāvitattā kāyassa . Tassa mayhaṃ, bho gotama, evaṃ hoti – ‘addhā bhoto gotamassa sāvakā cittabhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā viharanti, no kāyabhāvana’’’nti.
365. “Master Gotama, there are some ascetics and Brahmins who abide pursuing the practice of bodily development (kāyabhāvanā), but not mental development (cittabhāvanā). They are touched by bodily painful feeling (sārīrikaṃ dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ). In the past, when one was touched by bodily painful feeling, one’s thighs would become rigid (ūrukkhambha), one’s heart would burst, hot blood would gush from one’s mouth, and one would go mad (ummāda), go out of one’s mind (cittakkhepaṃ). So then the mind (cittaṃ) was subservient to the body (kāya), the body wielded mastery over it (kāyassa vasena vattati). What is the reason for that? Because the mind has not been developed. But there are some ascetics and Brahmins who abide pursuing development of mind (cittabhāvanā), but not development of body (kāyabhāvanā). They are touched by mental painful feeling (cetasikaṃ dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ). In the past, when one was touched by mental painful feeling, one’s thighs would become rigid, one’s heart would burst, hot blood would gush from one’s mouth, and one would go mad, go out of one’s mind. So then the body (kāyo) was subservient to the mind (cittaṃ), the mind wielded mastery over it (cittassa vasena vattati). What is the reason for that? Because the body has not been developed. It has occurred to me: ‘Surely Master Gotama’s disciples abide pursuing development of mind (cittabhāvanā), but not development of body (kāyabhāvanā).’”
365.Phusanti hi te, bho gotamāti te samaṇabrāhmaṇā sarīre uppannaṃ sārīrikaṃ dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ phusanti labhanti, anubhavantīti attho. Ūrukkhambhoti khambhakataūrubhāvo, ūruthaddhatāti attho. Vimhayatthavasena panettha bhavissatīti anāgatavacanaṃ kataṃ. Kāyanvayaṃ hotīti kāyānugataṃ hoti kāyassa vasavatti. Kāyabhāvanāti pana vipassanā vuccati, tāya cittavikkhepaṃ pāpuṇanto nāma natthi, iti nigaṇṭho asantaṃ abhūtaṃ yaṃ natthi, tadevāha. Cittabhāvanātipi samatho vuccati, samādhiyuttassa ca puggalassa ūrukkhambhādayo nāma natthi, iti nigaṇṭho idaṃ abhūtameva āha. Aṭṭhakathāyaṃ pana vuttaṃ – ‘‘yatheva ‘bhūtapubbanti vatvā ūrukkhambhopi nāma bhavissatī’tiādīni vadato anāgatarūpaṃ na sameti, tathā atthopi na sameti, asantaṃ abhūtaṃ yaṃ natthi, taṃ kathetī’’ti. No kāyabhāvananti pañcātapatappanādiṃ attakilamathānuyogaṃ sandhāyāha. Ayañhi tesaṃ kāyabhāvanā nāma. Kiṃ pana so disvā evamāha? So kira divādivassa vihāraṃ āgacchati, tasmiṃ kho pana samaye bhikkhū pattacīvaraṃ paṭisāmetvā attano attano rattiṭṭhānadivāṭṭhānesu paṭisallānaṃ upagacchanti. So te paṭisallīne disvā cittabhāvanāmattaṃ ete anuyuñjanti, kāyabhāvanā panetesaṃ natthīti maññamāno evamāha. No kāyabhāvana
365. “Phusanti hi te, bho Gotamā” means those śramaṇa and brāhmaṇa experience, obtain, and feel bodily suffering (śārīrikaṃ duḥkhaṃ vedanaṃ). This is the meaning.

"Ūrukkhambho" means having thighs that are like pillars (khambhakataūrubhāvo), having stiff thighs. This is the meaning.

Because of the wonderful meaning, here, "bhavissati" is said, it is a statement about the future (anāgatavacanaṃ kataṃ).

"Kāyanvayaṃ hoti" means being in accordance with the body (kāyānugataṃ). It is mastery over the body (kāyassa vasavatti).

"Kāyabhāvanā" However, it is called vipaśyanā. There is no distraction of the mind (cittavikkhepaṃ) through that. Thus, the nigaṇṭha speaks of what is unreal and untrue, which does not exist.

"Cittabhāvanā " is also called samatha. For a person endowed with samādhi, there are no such things as thigh-pillars and so on. Thus, the nigaṇṭha speaks of what is simply unreal.

In the Aṭṭhakathā, however, it was said: "Just as by saying 'bhūtapubbanti vatvā ūrukkhambhopi nāma bhavissati’ etc., the future form does not fit, so also the meaning does not fit. He speaks of what is unreal and untrue, which does not exist."

"No kāyabhāvana" means he speaks referring to self-mortification (attakilamathānuyogaṃ) such as the fivefold heating of oneself.

This is their practice of the body (kāyabhāvanā).

But why did he say this, having seen? He comes to the vihāra at the time of day. At that time, the bhikkhus, having put away their robes and bowls, go to their respective daytime and nighttime dwellings for seclusion (paṭisallānaṃ upagacchanti).

He, having seen them in seclusion, thinks that they are only engaged in mental cultivation (cittabhāvanāmattaṃ ete anuyuñjanti), and that there is no practice of the body (kāyabhāvanā panetesaṃ natthīti), and so he says this.

"No kāyabhāvana"

366. ‘‘Kinti pana te, aggivessana, kāyabhāvanā sutā’’ti? ‘‘Seyyathidaṃ – nando vaccho, kiso saṃkicco, makkhali gosālo – etehi, bho gotama, acelakā muttācārā hatthāpalekhanā naehibhaddantikā natiṭṭhabhaddantikā naehibhadantikā, natiṭṭhabhadantikā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī. ka.) na abhihaṭaṃ na uddissakataṃ na nimantanaṃ sādiyanti, te na kumbhimukhā paṭiggaṇhanti na kaḷopimukhā paṭiggaṇhanti na eḷakamantaraṃ na daṇḍamantaraṃ na musalamantaraṃ na dvinnaṃ bhuñjamānānaṃ na gabbhiniyā na pāyamānāya na purisantaragatāya na saṅkittīsu na yattha sā upaṭṭhito hoti na yattha makkhikā saṇḍasaṇḍacārinī , na macchaṃ na maṃsaṃ na suraṃ na merayaṃ na thusodakaṃ pivanti. Te ekāgārikā vā honti ekālopikā, dvāgārikā vā honti dvālopikā…pe… sattāgārikā vā honti sattālopikā. Ekissāpi dattiyā yāpenti, dvīhipi dattīhi yāpenti…pe… sattahipi dattīhi yāpenti. Ekāhikampi āhāraṃ āhārenti, dvīhikampi āhāraṃ āhārenti…pe… sattāhikampi āhāraṃ āhārenti. Iti evarūpaṃ addhamāsikampi pariyāyabhattabhojanānuyogamanuyuttā viharantī’’ti. ‘‘Kiṃ pana te, aggivessana, tāvatakeneva yāpentī’’ti? ‘‘No hidaṃ, bho gotama. Appekadā, bho gotama, uḷārāni uḷārāni khādanīyāni khādanti, uḷārāni uḷārāni bhojanāni bhuñjanti, uḷārāni uḷārāni sāyanīyāni sāyanti, uḷārāni uḷārāni pānāni pivanti. Te imaṃ kāyaṃ balaṃ gāhenti nāma, brūhenti nāma, medenti nāmā’’ti. ‘‘Yaṃ kho te, aggivessana, purimaṃ pahāya pacchā upacinanti, evaṃ imassa kāyassa ācayāpacayo hoti. Kinti pana te, aggivessana, cittabhāvanā sutā’’ti? Cittabhāvanāya kho saccako nigaṇṭhaputto bhagavatā puṭṭho samāno na sampāyāsi.
366. "Well then, Aggivessana, what sort of physical practices (kāyabhāvanā) are known to you?"

"For example, there are Nanda Vaccha, Kisa Sankicca, Makkhali Gosāla. These naked ascetics, rejecting conventions, licking their hands, not coming when asked, not stopping when asked, do not accept food brought or food specially made or an invitation to a meal; they receive nothing from a pot, from a bowl, across a threshold, across a stick, across a pestle, from two eating together, from a pregnant woman, from a woman giving suck, from a woman living with a man, from where food is advertised to be distributed, from where a dog is waiting, from where flies are buzzing; they eat no fish or meat and drink no rum or spirits or fermented rice-gruel. They keep to one house, to one morsel; they keep to two houses, to two morsels…they keep to seven houses, to seven morsels. They live on one saucerful a day, on two saucerfuls a day…on seven saucerfuls a day. They take food once a day, once every two days…once every seven days. Thus even up to once every fortnight, they dwell pursuing the practice of taking food at stated intervals."

"Well then, Aggivessana, do they live only on that much?"

"No, Gotama. Sometimes, Gotama, they consume excellent hard food, eat excellent soft food, taste excellent delicacies, drink excellent drinks. Thereby they again regain their strength (balaṃ), fortify themselves, and become fat."

"What they earlier abandoned, Aggivessana, they later gather together again. That is how there is increase (ācaya) and decrease (apacaya) of this body (kāyassa). Well then, Aggivessana, what sort of mental development (cittabhāvanā) is known to you?"

When Saccaka the Nigaṇṭha’s son was asked by the Blessed One about development of mind, he was unable to answer.
366. Atha naṃ bhagavā anuyuñjanto kinti pana te, aggivessana, kāyabhāvanā sutāti āha. So taṃ vitthārento seyyathidaṃ, nando vacchotiādimāha. Tattha nandoti tassa nāmaṃ. Vacchoti gottaṃ. Kisoti nāmaṃ. Saṃkiccoti gottaṃ. Makkhaligosālo heṭṭhā āgatova. Eteti ete tayo janā, te kira kiliṭṭhatapānaṃ matthakapattā ahesuṃ. Uḷārāni uḷārānīti paṇītāni paṇītāni. Gāhenti nāmāti balaṃ gaṇhāpenti nāma. Brūhentīti vaḍḍhenti. Medentīti jātamedaṃ karonti. Purimaṃ pahāyāti purimaṃ dukkarakāraṃ pahāya. Pacchā upacinantīti pacchā uḷārakhādanīyādīhi santappenti, vaḍḍhenti. Ācayāpacayo hotīti vaḍḍhi ca avaḍḍhi ca hoti, iti imassa kāyassa kālena vaḍḍhi, kālena parihānīti vaḍḍhiparihānimattameva paññāyati, kāyabhāvanā pana na paññāyatīti dīpetvā cittabhāvanaṃ pucchanto, ‘‘kinti pana te, aggivessana, cittabhāvanā sutā’’ti āha. Na sampāyāsīti sampādetvā kathetuṃ nāsakkhi, yathā taṃ bālaputhujjano.
Then, the Blessed One, questioning him, said, “So what body cultivation (kāyabhāvanā), Aggivessana, have you heard?”

He, elaborating on that, said, “That is to say, Nanda, Vaccha,” and so on.

There, Nanda means his name.

Vaccha means his lineage (gottaṃ).

Kisa means his name.

Saṃkicca means his lineage.

Makkhali Gosāla came down below.

Ete means these three people, they, it is said, were at the height of corrupted asceticism.

Uḷārāni uḷārānī means exquisite, exquisite.

Gāhenti nāmā means they cause strength (balaṃ) to be acquired, it is said.

Brūhentī means they increase.

Medentī means they make what has become fat (jātamedaṃ).

Purimaṃ pahāyā means having abandoned the former, having abandoned the former practice of austerity (dukkarakāraṃ).

Pacchā upacinantī means afterwards they satisfy and increase with exquisite food (uḷārakhādanīya) and so forth.

Ācayāpacayo hotī means there is increase (vaḍḍhi) and decrease (avaḍḍhi), thus with time there is increase of this body (kāyassa), and with time, decrease (parihānī), thus only the measure of increase and decrease is evident, but body cultivation (kāyabhāvanā) is not evident. Having shown this, asking about mind cultivation (cittabhāvanaṃ), he said, “So what mind cultivation (cittabhāvanā), Aggivessana, have you heard?”

Na sampāyāsī means he was not able to bring it about and tell it, like that childish ordinary person (bālaputhujjano).

367. Atha kho bhagavā saccakaṃ nigaṇṭhaputtaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘yāpi kho te esā, aggivessana, purimā kāyabhāvanā bhāsitā sāpi ariyassa vinaye no dhammikā kāyabhāvanā. Kāyabhāvanampi kāyabhāvanaṃ hi (sī. pī. ka.) kho tvaṃ, aggivessana, na aññāsi, kuto pana tvaṃ cittabhāvanaṃ jānissasi ? Api ca, aggivessana, yathā abhāvitakāyo ca hoti abhāvitacitto ca, bhāvitakāyo ca hoti bhāvitacitto ca. Taṃ suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi, bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bho’’ti kho saccako nigaṇṭhaputto bhagavato paccassosi. Bhagavā etadavoca –
Then, the Blessed One said this to Saccaka, the son of Nigaṇṭha: "Aggivessana, that former development of the body (kāyabhāvanā) you spoke of, that is not a righteous development of the body (kāyabhāvanā) in the Discipline of the Noble Ones.

Furthermore, Aggivessana, since you do not understand the development of the body (kāyabhāvanā), how will you understand the development of the mind (cittabhāvanā)? Moreover, Aggivessana, it is like this: one is undeveloped in body (abhāvitakāyo) and undeveloped in mind (abhāvitacitto), or one is developed in body (bhāvitakāyo) and developed in mind (bhāvitacitto).

Listen and pay close attention, I shall speak."

"Yes, sir (bho)," replied Saccaka, the son of Nigaṇṭha, to the Blessed One.

How so? At the moment of entering concentration, suffering is far away, and no small amount of pleasure descends. In this way, concentration is the opposite of suffering and close to pleasure.

Upamaṃ, bhante, karothāti. Yathā, mahārāja , karīsasahassamatte sālikkhette ekasālisīsato avasesasālīyeva bahū, evaṃ mayā kathitaguṇā appā, avasesā bahūti. Aparampi, bhante, upamaṃ karothāti. Yathā, mahārāja, mahāgaṅgāya oghapuṇṇāya sūcipāsaṃ sammukhaṃ kareyya, sūcipāsena gataudakaṃ appaṃ, sesaṃ bahu, evameva mayā kathitaguṇā appā, avasesā bahūti. Aparampi, bhante, upamaṃ karothāti. Idha, mahārāja, cātakasakuṇā nāma ākāse kīḷantā vicaranti. Khuddakā sā sakuṇajāti, kiṃ nu kho tassa sakuṇassa ākāse pakkhapasāraṇaṭṭhānaṃ bahu, avaseso ākāso appoti? Kiṃ, bhante, vadatha, appo tassa pakkhapasāraṇokāso, avasesova bahūti. Evameva, mahārāja, appakā mayā buddhaguṇā kathitā, avasesā bahū anantā appameyyāti. Sukathitaṃ, bhante, anantā buddhaguṇā ananteneva ākāsena upamitā. Pasannā mayaṃ ayyassa, anucchavikaṃ pana kātuṃ na sakkoma. Ayaṃ me duggatapaṇṇākāro imasmiṃ tambapaṇṇidīpe imaṃ tiyojanasatikaṃ rajjaṃ ayyassa demāti. Tumhehi, mahārāja, attano pasannākāro kato, mayaṃ pana amhākaṃ dinnaṃ rajjaṃ tumhākaṃyeva dema, dhammena samena rajjaṃ kārehi mahārājāti. 389. Āsajja āsajjā: striking and striking.

Upanītehī: having brought near, with words.

Vacanappathehī: with words.

Abhinanditvā anumoditvā: being pleased at heart, accepting, rejoicing, praising with speech, and approving.

The Blessed One spoke two sūtras to this Nigaṇṭha.

The first sūtra is one recitation section, this [next] is one and a half. Even after hearing one and a half recitation sections, this Nigaṇṭha neither attained clear understanding, nor went forth [as a monk], nor was established in the refuges.

Why did the Blessed One teach the dharma to him? For the sake of habituation in the future.

For the Blessed One sees, "Now, this one has no support. But after the passing away into complete nirvāṇa of me, after the lapse of two hundred years, the teaching will be established in Tambapaṇṇidīpa.

There, this one, having been born in a family home, when the time has come, having gone forth [as a monk], having grasped the three piṭakas, having increased insight, together with the paṭisambhidās, having attained arhatship, will become a great one whose defilements are destroyed, by the name of Kāḷabuddharakkhita."

Having seen this, he taught the dharma for the sake of habituation in the future.

And that one, in that very Tambapaṇṇidīpa, when the teaching was established, having passed away from the world of the gods, having been born in a minister's family in a village for alms in the Dakkhiṇagirivihāra, having gone forth [as a monk] in youth capable of going forth, having grasped the three piṭakas, the word of the Buddha, protecting the community, surrounded by a great community of monks, went to see [his] preceptor.

Then his preceptor, thinking, "I will rebuke the co-resident," having grasped the three piṭakas, the word of the Buddha, with that one who had come, having given [him] a face, he did not speak even a word.

He, at dawn, having arisen, went to the elder, saying, "Venerable sir, having done the work of tying a knot in me, having come to your presence, having given [me] a face, you did not speak even a word. What is my fault?"

The elder said, "You, friend Buddharakkhita, with just this much, do you make the notion, 'The duties of going forth have reached my peak'?"

What should I do, venerable sir? Having gladdened the community, having cut off proliferation, having gone to the Cetiyapabbatavihāra, do the śramaṇa-dharma!

He, having stood in the preceptor's instruction, together with the paṭisambhidās, having attained arhatship, being meritorious, being honored by the king, having become surrounded by a great community of monks, lived in the Cetiyapabbatavihāra.

At that time, Tissamahārāja, while doing the uposatha work, lived in the royal cave in Cetiyapabbata.

He gave a sign to the attendant monk of the elder, "When my lord answers a question or speaks dharma, then give me a sign."

The elder, on a certain day for hearing the dharma, surrounded by a community of monks, having ascended the Kaṇṭakacetiyangaṇa, having worshiped the cetiya, stood at the root of the Kāḷatimbarurukkha.

Then a certain alms-goer elder asked him a question in the Kāḷakārāmasuttanta.

The elder said, "Friend, is today the day for hearing the dharma?"

"Yes, venerable sir, it is the day for hearing the dharma."

"Then bring a seat. Having sat down right here, we will do the hearing of the dharma."

Then they prepared a seat at the root of the tree and gave [it to him].

The elder, having spoken the preliminary verses, began the Kāḷakārāmasutta.

That attendant youth gave the sign to the king.

The king arrived while the preliminary verses were not yet finished.

Having arrived, and having stood at the edge, with the appearance of a stranger, having heard the dharma for three watches of the night, at the time of the words "The Blessed One said this," he gave applause.

The elder, having known, asked, "When did you come, great king?"

"At the very time of the utterance of the preliminary verses, venerable sir."

"Difficult is that which you, great king, have done."

"This is not difficult, venerable sir. But if there was even one word of different intention, from the time when my lord's dharma talk was begun, may I not have ownership in a place even as small as the prick of a goad in Tambapaṇṇidīpa." Thus he swore an oath.
In that sūtra, the qualities of the Buddha are clearly explained. Therefore, the king asked: “Are these all the qualities of the Buddha, venerable sir, or are there others as well?”

[The Buddha replied:] "Mahārāja, what I have not spoken of is truly much, immeasurable."

[The king asked:] "Venerable sir, is it possible to give a simile?"

[The Buddha said:] “Mahārāja, just as in a rice field of a thousand karīsas, there are many more grains of rice than on a single rice stalk, so too, the qualities I have spoken of are few, while those remaining are many."

[The king asked:] "Venerable sir, is it possible to give another simile?"

[The Buddha said:] "Mahārāja, imagine someone were to make an opening with a needle near the great Ganges river, which is filled with floodwater. The water that passes through the needle's opening would be little, while the remaining [water] would be much. In the same way, the qualities I have spoken of are few, while those remaining are many."

[The king asked:] "Venerable sir, is it possible to give another simile?"

[The Buddha said:] "Here, Mahārāja, there are birds called cātakas that wander about playing in the sky. That species of bird is small. Now, is the space where that bird extends its wings in the sky large, or is the remaining sky small?"

[The king asked:] "What do you say, venerable sir? Is the space where it extends its wings small, while the remaining [sky] is very much?"

[The Buddha said:] “In the same way, Mahārāja, the qualities of the Buddha that I have spoken of are few, while those remaining are many, endless, and immeasurable.”

[The king said:] "Well spoken, venerable sir. The endless qualities of the Buddha are comparable to the endless sky."

"We are pleased with the venerable one, but we are unable to do what is fitting. This humble gift of leaves is [offered so that] he might rule this kingdom of three hundred yojanas in this Tambapaṇṇi island."

[The Buddha said:] “Mahārāja, you have shown your pleasure, but we will give back to you the kingdom you have given us. Rule the kingdom with righteousness and justice, Mahārāja!”
