91. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde. Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā tadahuposathe pannarase puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto abbhokāse nisinno hoti. Atha kho bhagavā tuṇhībhūtaṃ tuṇhībhūtaṃ bhikkhusaṅghaṃ anuviloketvā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘jāneyya nu kho, bhikkhave, asappuriso asappurisaṃ – ‘asappuriso ayaṃ bhava’’’nti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Sādhu, bhikkhave; aṭṭhānametaṃ, bhikkhave, anavakāso yaṃ asappuriso asappurisaṃ jāneyya – ‘asappuriso ayaṃ bhava’nti. Jāneyya pana, bhikkhave, asappuriso sappurisaṃ – ‘sappuriso ayaṃ bhava’’’nti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Sādhu, bhikkhave; etampi kho, bhikkhave, aṭṭhānaṃ anavakāso yaṃ asappuriso sappurisaṃ jāneyya – ‘sappuriso ayaṃ bhava’nti. Asappuriso, bhikkhave, assaddhammasamannāgato hoti, asappurisabhatti asappurisabhattī (sabbattha) hoti, asappurisacintī hoti, asappurisamantī hoti, asappurisavāco hoti, asappurisakammanto hoti, asappurisadiṭṭhi asappurisadiṭṭhī (sabbattha) hoti; asappurisadānaṃ deti’’. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, asappuriso assaddhammasamannāgato hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, asappurisassa ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā assaddhā ahirikā anottappino appassutā kusītā muṭṭhassatino duppaññā tyāssa mittā honti te sahāyā. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvatthī in the Eastern Park in Migāramātā’s mansion.

Then, on that day of the uposatha, on the fifteenth, on the full-moon night, the Blessed One was seated in the open air surrounded by the Community of monks.

Then, surveying the silent Sangha of bhikkhus, the Blessed One addressed them thus: “Bhikkhus, would an untrue man know of an untrue man: ‘This person is an untrue man’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“Good, bhikkhus. It is impossible, bhikkhus, it cannot be, that an untrue man should know of an untrue man: ‘This person is an untrue man.’

But would an untrue man know of a true man: ‘This person is a true man’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“Good, bhikkhus. It is impossible, bhikkhus, it cannot be, that an untrue man should know of a true man: ‘This person is a true man.’

Bhikkhus, an untrue man is possessed of bad qualities (asaddhamma-samannāgato); he associates as an untrue man (asappurisa-bhatti), he wills as an untrue man, he counsels as an untrue man, he speaks as an untrue man, he acts as an untrue man, he holds views as an untrue man; and he gives gifts as an untrue man.”

“And how, bhikkhus, is an untrue man possessed of bad qualities? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man has no faith (assaddho), no shame (ahiriko), no fear of wrongdoing (anottappī); he is unlearned (appassuto), lazy (kusīto), forgetful (muṭṭhassati), and unwise (duppañño).

That is how an untrue man is possessed of bad qualities.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man associate as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, those recluses and Brahmins who are without faith, without shame, without fear of wrong-doing, unlearned, lazy, forgetful, and unwise are the friends and companions of an untrue man.

That is how an untrue man associates as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man will as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man wills for his own affliction, for the affliction of others, and for the affliction of both.

That is how an untrue man wills as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man counsel as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man counsels for his own affliction, for the affliction of others, and for the affliction of both.

That is how an untrue man counsels as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man speak as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man is a liar, a slanderer, one who speaks harshly, and a gossip.

That is how an untrue man speaks as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man act as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man kills living beings (prāṇātipātī), takes what is not given (adinnādāyī), and misconducts himself in sensual pleasures (kāmesu-micchācārī).

That is how an untrue man acts as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man hold views as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man holds such a view as this: ‘There is nothing given (natthi dinnaṃ), nothing offered (natthi yiṭṭhaṃ), nothing sacrificed (natthi hutaṃ); there is no fruit or result of good and bad actions; no this world, no other world; no mother, no father; no beings who are reborn spontaneously; no good and virtuous recluses and Brahmins in the world who have realised for themselves by direct knowledge and declare this world and the other world.’
) The result of actions (kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko) is that there is nothing given, there is no this world (natthi ayaṃ loko), there is no other world (natthi paro loko), there is no mother (natthi mātā), there is no father (natthi pitā), there are no beings who are reborn spontaneously (natthi sattā opapātikā), there are no ascetics and Brahmins in the world (natthi loke samaṇabrāhmaṇā) who have reached the state of being rightly attained, who have themselves realised this world and the next world by direct knowledge (abhiññā) and declare it.

That is how, mendicants, an untrue man holds untrue views.

Furthermore, mendicants, how does an untrue man give a gift as an untrue man? Here, mendicants, an untrue man gives a gift carelessly, gives it not with his own hand, gives it without showing respect, gives what is to be discarded, gives it with the view that nothing will come of it.

That is how, mendicants, an untrue man gives gifts as an untrue man.

That untrue man, mendicants, is thus endowed with disbelief in the Dhamma, with untrue devotion, with untrue thoughts, with untrue counsel, with untrue speech, with untrue actions, and with untrue views; having given gifts in such a way, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he is reborn in such a destination of untrue men.

And what is the destination of untrue men? Either hell (nirayo) or the animal realm (tiracchānayoni).
91.Evaṃme sutanti cūḷapuṇṇamasuttaṃ. Tattha tuṇhībhūtaṃ tuṇhībhūtanti yaṃ yaṃ disaṃ anuviloketi, tattha tattha tuṇhībhūtameva. Anuviloketvāti pañcapasādapaṭimaṇḍitāni akkhīni ummīletvā tato tato viloketvā antamaso hatthakukkuccapādakukkuccānampi abhāvaṃ disvā. Asappurisoti pāpapuriso. No hetaṃ, bhanteti yasmā andho andhaṃ viya so taṃ jānituṃ na sakkoti, tasmā evamāhaṃsu. Eteneva nayena ito paresupi vāresu attho veditabbo. Assaddhasamannāgatoti pāpadhammasamannāgato. Asappurisabhattīti asappurisasevano. Asappurisacintīti asappurisacintāya cintako. Asappurisamantīti asappurisamantanaṃ mantetā. Asappurisavācoti asappurisavācaṃ bhāsitā. Asappurisakammantoti asappurisakammānaṃ kattā. Asappurisadiṭṭhīti asappurisadiṭṭhiyā samannāgato. Asappurisadānanti asappurisehi dātabbaṃ dānaṃ. Tyāssa mittāti te assa mittā. Attabyābādhāyapi cetetīti pāṇaṃ hanissāmi, adinnaṃ ādiyissāmi, micchā carissāmi, dasa akusalakammapathe samādāya vattissāmīti evaṃ attano dukkhatthāya cinteti. Parabyābādhāyāti yathā asuko asukaṃ pāṇaṃ hanti, asukassa santakaṃ adinnaṃ ādiyati, dasa akusalakammapathe samādāya vattati, evaṃ naṃ āṇāpessāmīti evaṃ parassa dukkhatthāya cinteti. Ubhayabyābādhāyāti ahaṃ asukañca asukañca gahetvā dasa akusalakammapathe samādāya vattissāmīti evaṃ ubhayadukkhatthāya cintetīti. Attabyābādhāyapi mantetītiādīsu ahaṃ dasa akusalakammapathe samādāya vattissāmīti mantento attabyābādhāya manteti nāma. Asukaṃ dasa akusalakammapathe samādapessāmīti mantento parabyābādhāya manteti nāma. Aññena saddhiṃ – ‘‘mayaṃ ubhopi ekato hutvā dasa akusalakammapathe samādāya vattissāmā’’ti mantento ubhayabyābādhāya manteti nāma. Attabyābādhāyapi mantetī Asakkaccaṃ dānaṃ detīti deyyadhammampi puggalampi na sakkaroti. Deyyadhammaṃ na sakkaroti nāma uttaṇḍulādidosasamannāgataṃ āhāraṃ deti, na pasannaṃ karoti. Puggalaṃ na sakkaroti nāma nisīdanaṭṭhānaṃ asammajjitvā yattha vā tattha vā nisīdāpetvā yaṃ vā taṃ vā ādhārakaṃ ṭhapetvā dānaṃ deti. Asahatthāti attano hatthena, na deti, dāsakammakārādīhi dāpeti. Acittikatvāti heṭṭhā vuttanayena deyyadhammepi puggalepi na cittīkāraṃ katvā deti. Apaviddhanti chaḍḍetukāmo hutvā vammike uragaṃ pakkhipanto viya deti. Anāgamanadiṭṭhikoti no phalapāṭikaṅkhī hutvā deti. Asakkaccaṃ dānaṃ detī Asahatthā Acittikatvā Apaviddha Anāgamanadiṭṭhiko Tatthaupapajjatīti na dānaṃ datvā niraye upapajjati. Yaṃ pana tena pāpaladdhikāya micchādassanaṃ gahitaṃ, tāya micchādiṭṭhiyā niraye upapajjati. Sukkapakkho vuttapaṭipakkhanayena veditabbo. Devamahattatāti chakāmāvacaradevā. Manussamahattatāti tiṇṇaṃ kulānaṃ sampatti. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānameva. Idaṃ pana suttaṃ suddhavaṭṭavaseneva kathitanti. Tattha upapajjatī Devamahattatā Manussamahattatā Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Cūḷapuṇṇamasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā. Paṭhamavaggavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
91. Thus have I heard: the Cūḷapuṇṇama Sutta.

There, "silent, silent," wherever he looks around, it is only silence. "Having looked around" means having opened the eyes adorned with the five sense-organs, having looked here and there, and having seen the absence of even hand-worrying and foot-worrying. "An unrighteous man" means a sinful man. "Certainly not, venerable sir," because just as a blind man cannot know a blind man, so they said thus. In this way, the meaning should be understood in the subsequent instances as well. "Endowed with lack of faith" means endowed with sinful qualities. "Devoted to unrighteous men" means associating with unrighteous men. "Thinking unrighteous thoughts" means thinking with unrighteous thoughts. "Counseling unrighteous counsel" means counseling unrighteous counsel. "Speaking unrighteous speech" means speaking unrighteous speech. "Doing unrighteous deeds" means doing unrighteous deeds. "Having unrighteous views" means endowed with unrighteous views. "Unrighteous giving" means giving what should be given to unrighteous men. "Those are his friends" means those are his friends. "He intends for his own harm" means he thinks for his own suffering, "I will kill a living being, I will take what is not given, I will behave wrongly, I will undertake and engage in the ten unwholesome courses of action." "For the harm of others" means he thinks for the suffering of others, "Just as so-and-so kills such-and-such a living being, takes what is not given belonging to so-and-so, undertakes and engages in the ten unwholesome courses of action, so I will command him." "For the harm of both" means he thinks for the suffering of both, "I will take such-and-such and undertake and engage in the ten unwholesome courses of action."

In "he intends for his own harm," etc., intending, "I will undertake and engage in the ten unwholesome courses of action," is called intending for his own harm. Intending, "I will induce so-and-so to undertake the ten unwholesome courses of action," is called intending for the harm of others. With another, intending, "We both, having become one, will undertake and engage in the ten unwholesome courses of action," is called intending for the harm of both.

"He gives a gift disrespectfully" means he does not respect either the gift-worthy object or the recipient. He does not respect the gift-worthy object, meaning he gives food with impurities such as coarse rice, and does not make it pleasing. He does not respect the recipient, meaning he does not sweep the seat, or having seated [the recipient] just anywhere, or having placed just any kind of support, he gives the gift. "Without his own hand" means not with his own hand, he has it given by slaves, servants, etc. "Without paying attention" means he gives without paying attention to either the gift-worthy object or the recipient, in the manner stated below. "Apaviddha" means wanting to throw it away, he gives as if throwing a snake into an anthill. "Having no expectation of return" means he gives without expecting any fruit.

"There, he is reborn" means he is not reborn in hell by giving a gift. But by that wrong view taken by him due to sinful acquisition, he is reborn in hell. The bright side should be understood in the manner opposite to what has been said.
devamahattatā means the gods of the six desire realms.

manussamahattatā means the prosperity of three families.

The rest is clear throughout.

This sutra, however, is said to be spoken purely in terms of the cycle.

There, upapajjati devamahattatā manussamahattatā.

"Bhikkhus, would a true man know a true man, thinking, ‘This person is a true man’?" "Yes, venerable sir." "Good, bhikkhus. It is possible, bhikkhus, that a true man would know a true man, thinking, ‘This person is a true man.’ But, bhikkhus, would a true man know an untrue man, thinking, ‘This person is an untrue man’?" "Yes, venerable sir." "Good, bhikkhus. It is possible, bhikkhus, that a true man would know an untrue man, thinking, ‘This person is an untrue man.’ A true man, bhikkhus, is possessed of good qualities; he associates as a true man, he wills as a true man, he counsels as a true man, he speaks as a true man, he acts as a true man, he holds views as a true man, and he gives gifts as a true man."

"And how, bhikkhus, is a true man possessed of good qualities? Here, bhikkhus, a true man has faith (saddhā), shame (hirī), and fear of wrongdoing (ottappa); he is learned (bahussuta), energetic (āraddhavīriya), mindful (upaṭṭhitasati), and wise (paññavā). That is how a true man is possessed of good qualities."

"And how, bhikkhus, does a true man associate as a true man? Here, bhikkhus, those ascetics and Brahmins who are faithful, have shame and fear of wrongdoing, are learned, energetic, mindful, and wise—these are his friends and companions. That is how a true man associates as a true man."

"And how, bhikkhus, does a true man will as a true man? Here, bhikkhus, a true man does not will for his own affliction, for the affliction of others, or for the affliction of both. That is how a true man wills as a true man."

"And how, bhikkhus, does a true man counsel as a true man? Here, bhikkhus, a true man does not counsel for his own affliction, for the affliction of others, or for the affliction of both. That is how a true man counsels as a true man."

"And how, bhikkhus, does a true man speak as a true man? Here, bhikkhus, a true man abstains from false speech (musāvāda), from malicious speech (pisuṇā vācā), from harsh speech (pharusā vācā), and from gossip (samphappalāpa). That is how a true man speaks as a true man."

"And how, bhikkhus, does a true man act as a true man? Here, bhikkhus, a true man abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā), abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā), abstains from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācāra). That is how a true man acts as a true man."

"And how, bhikkhus, does a true man hold views as a true man? Here, bhikkhus, a true man holds such a view as this: ‘There is what is given (dinna), what is offered (yiṭṭha), and what is sacrificed (huta); there is fruit and result of good and bad actions (sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko); there is this world (ayaṃ loko) and the other world (paro loko); there is mother (mātā) and father (pitā); there are beings who are reborn spontaneously (sattā opapātikā); there are good and virtuous recluses and brahmins in the world who have realised for themselves by direct knowledge (abhiññā) and declare this world and the other world.’ That is how a true man holds views as a true man."

"And how, bhikkhus, does a true man give gifts as a true man? Here, bhikkhus, a true man gives a gift carefully (sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ deti), gives it with his own hand (sahatthā dānaṃ deti), gives it showing respect (cittīkatvā dānaṃ deti), gives a valuable gift (anapaviṭṭhaṃ dānaṃ deti), gives it with the view that something will come of it (āgamanadiṭṭhiko dānaṃ deti). That is how a true man gives gifts as a true man."

"That true man - thus possessed of good qualities, who thus associates as a true man, wills as a true man, counsels as a true man, speaks as a true man, acts as a true man, holds views as a true man, and gives gifts as a true man - on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappears in the destination of true men. And what, bhikkhus, is the destination of true men? Greatness among the gods or greatness among human beings."

The Blessed One said this. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.
The Cūḷapuṇṇama Sutta, the tenth, is finished.

The Devadaha Section, the first, is finished.

Its summary:

Devadaha, five triads, kinti, sāma, sunakkhatta;

sappāya, gaṇa, gopaka, mahāpuṇṇa, and cūḷapuṇṇa.
