229. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati aññataraṃ purisaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘ehi tvaṃ, ambho purisa, yenāyasmā anuruddho tenupasaṅkama; upasaṅkamitvā mama vacanena āyasmato anuruddhassa pāde sirasā vandāhi vandāhi, evañca vadehi (sī. pī.) – ‘pañcakaṅgo, bhante, thapati āyasmato anuruddhassa pāde sirasā vandatī’ti; evañca vadehi evañca vadeti (sī. pī.) – ‘adhivāsetu kira, bhante, āyasmā anuruddho pañcakaṅgassa thapatissa svātanāya attacatuttho bhattaṃ; yena ca kira, bhante, āyasmā anuruddho pagevataraṃ āgaccheyya; pañcakaṅgo, bhante, thapati pañcakaṅgo thapati (sī. pī.) bahukicco bahukaraṇīyo rājakaraṇīyenā’’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho so puriso pañcakaṅgassa thapatissa paṭissutvā yenāyasmā anuruddho tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho so puriso āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘pañcakaṅgo, bhante, thapati āyasmato anuruddhassa pāde sirasā vandati, evañca vadeti – ‘adhivāsetu kira, bhante, āyasmā anuruddho pañcakaṅgassa thapatissa svātanāya attacatuttho bhattaṃ; yena ca kira, bhante, āyasmā anuruddho pagevataraṃ āgaccheyya; pañcakaṅgo, bhante, thapati bahukicco bahukaraṇīyo rājakaraṇīyenā’’’ti. Adhivāsesi kho āyasmā anuruddho tuṇhībhāvena.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Savatthi (sāvatthiyaṃ) in Jeta’s Grove (jetavane), Anathapindika’s (anāthapiṇḍikassa) monastery (ārāme).

Then the carpenter (thapati) Pancakanga (pañcakaṅgo) addressed a certain man (purisaṃ) thus: “Come, good man (ambho purisa), go to the venerable (āyasmā) Anuruddha (anuruddho), pay homage (vandāhi) in my name (vacanena) with your head (sirasā) at his feet (pāde), and say: ‘Venerable sir (bhante), the carpenter (thapati) Pancakanga (pañcakaṅgo) pays homage (vandati) with his head (sirasā) at the venerable (āyasmato) Anuruddha’s (anuruddhassa) feet (pāde)’; and say thus: ‘Venerable sir (bhante), let the venerable (āyasmā) Anuruddha (anuruddho), together with three others (attacatuttho), consent to accept tomorrow’s (svātanāya) meal (bhattaṃ) from the carpenter (thapatissa) Pancakanga (pañcakaṅgassa); and let the venerable (āyasmā) Anuruddha (anuruddho) arrive (āgaccheyya) punctually (pagevataraṃ), for the carpenter (thapati) Pancakanga (pañcakaṅgo), venerable sir (bhante), is very busy (bahukicco) and has much to do (bahukaraṇīyo) in the king’s service (rājakaraṇīyenā).’”

“Yes, sir (evaṃ, bhante),” that man (puriso) replied (paṭissutvā), and he went (upasaṅkami) to the venerable (āyasmā) Anuruddha (anuruddho). After paying homage (abhivādetvā) to the venerable (āyasmantaṃ) Anuruddha (anuruddhaṃ), he sat down (nisīdi) at one side (ekamantaṃ).

Having sat down (nisinno) at one side (ekamantaṃ), that man (puriso) said (avoca) to the venerable (āyasmantaṃ) Anuruddha (anuruddhaṃ): “The carpenter (thapati) Pancakanga (pañcakaṅgo), venerable sir (bhante), pays homage (vandati) with his head (sirasā) at the venerable (āyasmato) Anuruddha’s (anuruddhassa) feet (pāde), and says (vadeti) thus: ‘Venerable sir (bhante), let the venerable (āyasmā) Anuruddha (anuruddho), together with three others (attacatuttho), consent to accept tomorrow’s (svātanāya) meal (bhattaṃ) from the carpenter (thapatissa) Pancakanga (pañcakaṅgassa); and let the venerable (āyasmā) Anuruddha (anuruddho) arrive (āgaccheyya) punctually (pagevataraṃ), for the carpenter (thapati) Pancakanga (pañcakaṅgo), venerable sir (bhante), is very busy (bahukicco) and has much to do (bahukaraṇīyo) in the king’s service (rājakaraṇīyenā).’”

The venerable (āyasmā) Anuruddha (anuruddho) consented (adhivāsesi) in silence (tuṇhībhāvena).

230. Atha kho āyasmā anuruddho tassā rattiyā accayena pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena pañcakaṅgassa thapatissa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ paṇītena khādanīyena bhojanīyena sahatthā santappesi sampavāresi. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ bhuttāviṃ onītapattapāṇiṃ aññataraṃ nīcaṃ āsanaṃ gahetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho pañcakaṅgo thapati āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘Idha maṃ, bhante, therā bhikkhū upasaṅkamitvā evamāhaṃsu – ‘appamāṇaṃ, gahapati, cetovimuttiṃ bhāvehī’ti appamāṇā gahapati cetovimutti bhāvetabbāti (ka.). Ekacce therā evamāhaṃsu – ‘mahaggataṃ, gahapati, cetovimuttiṃ bhāvehī’ti. Yā cāyaṃ, bhante, appamāṇā cetovimutti yā ca mahaggatā cetovimutti – ime dhammā nānatthā ceva nānābyañjanā ca, udāhu ekatthā byañjanameva nāna’’nti? ‘‘Tena hi, gahapati, taṃ yevettha paṭibhātu. Apaṇṇakante ito bhavissatī’’ti. ‘‘Mayhaṃ kho, bhante, evaṃ hoti – ‘yā cāyaṃ appamāṇā cetovimutti yā ca mahaggatā cetovimutti ime dhammā ekatthā byañjanameva nāna’’’nti. ‘‘Yā cāyaṃ, gahapati, appamāṇā cetovimutti yā ca mahaggatā cetovimutti ime dhammā nānatthā ceva nānābyañjanā ca . Tadamināpetaṃ, gahapati, pariyāyena veditabbaṃ yathā ime dhammā nānatthā ceva nānābyañjanā ca’’. appamāṇā gahapati cetovimutti bhāvetabbāti (ka.) ‘‘Katamā ca, gahapati, appamāṇā cetovimutti? Idha, gahapati, bhikkhu mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ tathā tatiyaṃ tathā catutthaṃ; iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharati. Karuṇāsahagatena cetasā… muditāsahagatena cetasā… upekkhāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ tathā tatiyaṃ tathā catutthaṃ; iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ upekkhāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharati. Ayaṃ vuccati, gahapati, appamāṇā cetovimutti.
230. Then, as the night ended and it was morning, the venerable Anuruddha dressed, took his bowl and robe, and went to the residence of the carpenter Pañcakaṅga; having arrived, he sat down on the prepared seat.

Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga, with his own hands, served and satisfied the venerable Anuruddha with delicious food, both hard and soft.

Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga, after the venerable Anuruddha had finished eating and withdrawn his hand from the bowl, took a low seat and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, the carpenter Pañcakaṅga said to the venerable Anuruddha:

“Here, venerable sir, some elder bhikkhus came to me and said, ‘Householder, cultivate the immeasurable ( appamāṇaṃ) deliverance of mind ( ceto-vimuttiṃ) .’

Some elders said, ‘Householder, cultivate the exalted (mahaggataṃ) deliverance of mind ( ceto-vimuttiṃ).’

[Note: appamāṇā gahapati cetovimutti bhāvetabbāti (ka.)]

“Venerable sir, is this immeasurable deliverance of mind (appamāṇā ceto-vimutti) and this exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā ceto-vimutti) different in meaning and different in expression, or are they the same in meaning and different only in expression?”

“Then, householder, let that which occurs to you occur right here.

Afterwards it will be cleared up for you."

“Venerable sir, it seems to me that this immeasurable deliverance of mind (appamāṇā ceto-vimutti) and this exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā ceto-vimutti) are the same in meaning and different only in expression.”

“Householder, this immeasurable deliverance of mind (appamāṇā ceto-vimutti) and this exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā ceto-vimutti) are different in meaning and different in expression.

This should be understood by you through this method, householder, as to how these states are different in meaning and different in expression.”

[Note: appamāṇā gahapati cetovimutti bhāvetabbāti (ka.)]

“And what, householder, is the immeasurable deliverance of mind (appamāṇā ceto-vimutti)? Here, householder, a bhikkhu abides pervading one direction with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettā-sahagatena cetasā), likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth; thus above, below, across, everywhere, and to all as to himself, he abides pervading the entire world with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettā-sahagatena cetasā), vast, exalted (vipulena mahaggatena), immeasurable (appamāṇena), without hostility, and without ill will.

With a mind imbued with compassion (karuṇā-sahagatena cetasā)… with a mind imbued with appreciative joy (muditā-sahagatena cetasā)… with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhā-sahagatena cetasā) he abides pervading one direction, likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth; thus above, below, across, everywhere, and to all as to himself, he abides pervading the entire world with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhā-sahagatena cetasā), vast, exalted (vipulena mahaggatena), immeasurable (appamāṇena), without hostility, and without ill will.

This is called the immeasurable deliverance of mind (appamāṇā ceto-vimutti), householder.
230.Evaṃme sutanti anuruddhasuttaṃ. Tattha evamāhaṃsūti tassa upāsakassa aphāsukakālo ahosi, tadā upasaṅkamitvā evamāhaṃsu. Apaṇṇakanti avirādhitaṃ. Ekatthāti appamāṇāti vā mahaggatāti vā jhānameva cittekaggatāyeva evaṃ vuccatīti imaṃ sandhāya evamāha.
Thus.

"This I have heard" is from the Anuruddha Sutta.

There, "they said thus" means that it was an uncomfortable time for that lay follower; then, having approached, they said thus.

Apaṇṇaka means unimpeded.

Ekatthā means either immeasurable (appamāṇāti) or exalted (mahaggatāti); jhāna itself, concentration of mind, thus it is said, with this in mind, he said thus.

Here, householder (gahapati), a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the root of one tree, pervading it as exalted. This is called, householder (gahapati), the exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā cetovimutti). Here again, householder (gahapati), a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the roots of two or three trees, pervading it as exalted. This too is called, householder (gahapati), the exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā cetovimutti). Here again, householder (gahapati), a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one village (gāmakkhettaṃ), pervading it as exalted. This too is called, householder (gahapati), the exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā cetovimutti). Here again, householder (gahapati), a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three villages (gāmakkhettaṃ), pervading it as exalted. This too is called, householder (gahapati), the exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā cetovimutti). Here again, householder (gahapati), a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one major kingdom (mahārajjaṃ), pervading it as exalted. This too is called, householder (gahapati), the exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā cetovimutti). Here again, householder (gahapati), a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three major kingdoms (mahārajjaṃ), pervading it as exalted. This too is called, householder (gahapati), the exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā cetovimutti). Here again, householder (gahapati), a bhikkhu abides resolved upon the earth (pathaviṃ) as far as the ocean (samuddapariyantaṃ), pervading it as exalted. This too is called, householder (gahapati), the exalted deliverance of mind (mahaggatā cetovimutti). It is in this way, householder (gahapati), that it can be understood how these states are different in meaning (nānatthā) and different in name (nānābyañjanā)."

232. “There are, householder, these four kinds of arising in a state of existence (bhavūpapatti).

What are the four? Here, householder, someone abides resolved upon and pervading ‘limited radiance’ (parittābhā).

On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Limited Radiance (parittābhānaṃ devānaṃ).

Here again, householder, someone abides resolved upon and pervading ‘immeasurable radiance’ (appamāṇābhā).

On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Immeasurable Radiance (appamāṇābhānaṃ devānaṃ).

Here again, householder, someone abides resolved upon and pervading ‘defiled radiance’ (saṃkiliṭṭhābhā).

On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Defiled Radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhānaṃ devānaṃ).

Here again, householder, someone abides resolved upon and pervading ‘pure radiance’ (parisuddhābhā).

On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Pure Radiance (parisuddhābhānaṃ devānaṃ).

These, householder, are the four kinds of arising in a state of existence (bhavūpapatti).

“There is an occasion, householder, when those deities assemble in one place. When they have assembled in one place, a difference in their colour can be discerned but no difference in their radiance.

Just as, householder, if a man were to bring several oil-lamps into a house,

a difference in the flames of the lamps might be discerned but no difference in their radiance; so too, householder, there is an occasion when those deities assemble in one place… When they have assembled in one place, a difference in their colour can be discerned but no difference in their radiance.

“There is an occasion, householder, when those deities disperse from there. When they have dispersed, a difference in their colours can be discerned and also a difference in their radiance.

Just as, householder, if the man were to remove those several oil-lamps from that house,

a difference might be discerned in the flames of the lamps and also a difference in their radiance; so too, householder, there is an occasion when those deities disperse from there…and also a difference in their radiance.

“It does not occur to those deities: ‘This life of ours is permanent (nicca), everlasting (dhūva), and eternal (sassata)’; yet wherever those deities settle down (abhinivisanti), there they find delight (abhiramanti).

Just as, when flies are being carried along on a carrying-pole (kājena) or on a basket (piṭakena), it does not occur to them: ‘This life of ours is permanent (nicca), everlasting (dhūva), or eternal (sassata)’; yet wherever those flies settle down (abhinivisanti), there they find delight (abhiramanti); so too, householder, it does not occur to those deities: ‘This life of ours is permanent (nicca), everlasting (dhūva), or eternal (sassata)’; yet wherever they settle down (abhinivisanti), there they find delight (abhiramanti).”

232. Now, drawing from the attainment of great heights, while indicating the cause of rebirth, he says, "These four," and so forth.

Parittābhā means this enjoyment exists for one who knows, having pervaded [something]. However, the jhāna that is the cause of rebirth among the gods of Limited Radiance is spoken thus, having cultivated it.

This method applies to all cases.

The Limited Radiance might be of defiled radiance, or it might be of purified radiance; the Immeasurable Radiance might be of defiled radiance, or it might be of purified radiance.

How so? Either through being very intoxicated or through being deranged, having done the preliminary work on the kasiṇa, having produced the samāpatti, because the mastery that is accumulated in five ways and the opposing qualities are not well purified, having shaken a weak samāpatti, having remained in a jhāna of little quality, having made time [i.e., died], one is reborn among those of Limited Radiance; and his complexion is both limited and defiled.

However, having accumulated mastery in five ways, because the opposing qualities are well purified, having shaken a very pure samāpatti, having remained in a jhāna of good quality, having made time [i.e., died], one is reborn among those of Limited Radiance, and his complexion is both limited and purified.

Thus, the Limited Radiance might be of defiled radiance, or it might be of purified radiance.

However, having done extensive preliminary work on the kasiṇa, having produced the samāpatti, since the mastery is accumulated in five ways, all of that should be understood as just like before.

Thus, the Immeasurable Radiance might be of defiled radiance, or it might be of purified radiance.

The diversity of complexion (vaṇṇanānatta) means the diversity of the complexion of the body.

"And not the diversity of radiance" (no ca ābhānānatta) means the diversity in light is not known.

"The diversity of flames" (accinānatta) means the diversity of flames according to whether they are long or short, subtle or coarse.

The diversity of complexion, and not the diversity of radiance, and the diversity of flames.

"Wherever, wherever" (yattha yatthā) means wherever, wherever in gardens, celestial mansions, wishing trees, riverbanks, and the banks of lotus ponds.

"They settle down" (abhinivisantī) means they dwell.

"They delight" (abhiramantī) means they delight; they do not become bored.

"By means of porridge" (kājenā) means by whatever means, in porridges, rice, oil, molasses, fish, meat, and wooden hammers.

"Even with a hammer" is also a reading; this is the meaning.

"By means of a basket" (piṭakenā) means by means of a carrying basket.

"Just there, just there" (tattha tatthevā) means, having been brought from a place where ghee, honey, molasses, and the like are easily obtained to a place where salt, rotten fish, and the like are abundant, without producing the thought, "Formerly, our dwelling place was comfortable; there, we dwelt happily; here, either the salt afflicts us, or the stench of rotten fish produces a disease of the head," they delight just there, just there.

'Excellent, venerable Anuruddha! And I have a further question to ask.
Are all those radiant ones, venerable, deities of Defiled Radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhā), or are some of them deities of Pure Radiance (parisuddhābhā)?'

'By reason of the factor responsible for rebirth, friend Kaccana, some are deities of Defiled Radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhā), some deities of Pure Radiance (parisuddhābhā).'

'Venerable Anuruddha, what is the cause and reason why among those deities that have reappeared in a single order of gods, some are deities of Defiled Radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhā), some deities of Pure Radiance (parisuddhābhā)?'

“As to that, friend Kaccana, I shall give a simile.
For some wise men understand the meaning of a statement by means of a simile.

Suppose an oil-lamp is burning with impure oil and an impure wick; because of the impurity of its oil and its wick it burns dimly. So too, here a bhikkhu abides resolved upon and pervading an area with a defiled radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhā). His bodily inertia kāyaduṭṭhulla has not fully subsided, his sloth and torpor thinamiddha have not been fully eliminated, his restlessness and remorse uddhaccakukkucca have not been fully removed; because of this he meditates, as it were, dimly.
On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Defiled Radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhānaṃ devānaṃ).

Suppose an oil-lamp is burning with pure oil and a pure wick; because of the purity of its oil and its wick it does not burn dimly. So too, here a bhikkhu abides resolved upon and pervading an area with a pure radiance (parisuddhābhā).
His bodily inertia kāyaduṭṭhulla has fully subsided, his sloth and torpor thinamiddha have been fully eliminated, his restlessness and remorse uddhaccakukkucca have been fully removed; because of this he does not meditate, as it were, dimly. On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Pure Radiance (parisuddhābhānaṃ devānaṃ).

“This is the cause and reason, friend Kaccana, why among those deities that have reappeared in the same order of gods, some are deities of Defiled Radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhā), some deities of Pure Radiance (parisuddhābhā).”

235.Dīgharattaṃ kho meti thero kira pāramiyo pūrento isipabbajjaṃ pabbajitvā samāpattiṃ nibbattetvā nirantaraṃ tīṇi attabhāvasatāni brahmaloke paṭilabhi, taṃ sandhāyetaṃ āha. Vuttampi cetaṃ – ‘‘Avokiṇṇaṃ tīṇi sataṃ, yaṃ pabbajiṃ isipabbajjaṃ; Asaṅkhataṃ gavesanto, pubbe sañcaritaṃ mama’’nti. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Anuruddhasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
235. The Elder, while fulfilling the pāramīs, having gone forth into the ascetic life, having brought about attainment, uninterruptedly obtained three hundred existences in the Brahmā world, referring to that, he said this:

And this was said:

"Scattered, three hundred, into which I went forth into the ascetic life; seeking the unconditioned, formerly experienced by me."

The remainder is easily understood everywhere.

The Anuruddha Sutta Commentary of the Papañcasūdanī Majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathā is finished.
