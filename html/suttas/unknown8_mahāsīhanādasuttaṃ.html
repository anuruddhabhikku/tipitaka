Paragraph 381
Mūla (Source Text)
381. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā uruññāyaṃ ujuññāyaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) viharati kaṇṇakatthale migadāye. Atha kho acelo kassapo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho acelo kassapo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘sutaṃ metaṃ, bho gotama – ‘samaṇo gotamo sabbaṃ tapaṃ garahati, sabbaṃ tapassiṃ lūkhājīviṃ ekaṃsena upakkosati upavadatī’ti. Ye te, bho gotama, evamāhaṃsu – ‘samaṇo gotamo sabbaṃ tapaṃ garahati, sabbaṃ tapassiṃ lūkhājīviṃ ekaṃsena upakkosati upavadatī’ti, kacci te bhoto gotamassa vuttavādino, na ca bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ abhūtena abbhācikkhanti, dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākaronti, na ca koci sahadhammiko vādānuvādo gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchati? Anabbhakkhātukāmā hi mayaṃ bhavantaṃ gotama’’nti.
English Translation
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Uruññā, or Ujuññā (Sī., Syā., Kaṃ., Pī.) in the deer park of Kaṇṇakatthala.

Then the naked ascetic Kassapa approached the Blessed One, and exchanged greetings with him. After an exchange of friendly greetings and courtesies, he stood to one side.

Standing to one side, the naked ascetic Kassapa said to the Blessed One: "I have heard it said, Venerable Gotama, that 'The *śramaṇa* Gotama disparages all austerity (*tapas*), and entirely reviles and condemns all ascetics who lead a rough life.'"

"Now, concerning those who say this, Venerable Gotama—'The *śramaṇa* Gotama disparages all austerity (*tapas*), and entirely reviles and condemns all ascetics who lead a rough life'—are they stating what the Venerable Gotama has said, and not misrepresenting the Venerable Gotama with what is untrue? Do they explain the *dharma* in accordance with the *dharma*, and does no co-religionist's criticism reach a justifiable ground? For we do not wish to misrepresent the Venerable Gotama."
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
381.Evaṃme sutaṃ…pe… uruññāyaṃ viharatīti mahāsīhanādasuttaṃ. Tatrāyaṃ apubbapadavaṇṇanā. Uruññāyanti uruññāti tassa raṭṭhassapi nagarassapi etadeva nāmaṃ, bhagavā uruññānagaraṃ upanissāya viharati. Kaṇṇakatthale migadāyeti tassa nagarassa avidūre kaṇṇakatthalaṃ nāma eko ramaṇīyo bhūmibhāgo atthi. So migānaṃ abhayatthāya dinnattā ‘‘migadāyo’’ti vuccati, tasmiṃ kaṇṇakatthale migadāye. Aceloti naggaparibbājako. Kassapoti tassa nāmaṃ. Tapassinti tapanissitakaṃ. Lūkhājīvinti acelakamuttācārādivasena lūkho ājīvo assāti lūkhājīvī, taṃ lūkhājīviṃ. Upakkosatīti upaṇḍeti. Upavadatīti hīḷeti vambheti. Dhammassa ca anudhammaṃ byākarontīti bhotā gotamena vuttakāraṇassa anukāraṇaṃ kathenti. Sahadhammiko vādānuvādoti parehi vuttakāraṇena sakāraṇo hutvā tumhākaṃ vādo vā anuvādo vā viññūhi garahitabbaṃ, kāraṇaṃ koci appamattakopi kiṃ na āgacchati. Idaṃ vuttaṃ hoti, ‘‘kiṃ sabbākārenapi tava vāde gārayhaṃ kāraṇaṃ natthī’’ti. Anabbhakkhātukāmāti na abhūtena vattukāmā.
English Commentary
381. Thus have I heard…pe… the Great Lion's Roar Discourse, [which] he lived in Uruññā.
Herein is an explanation of unusual terms.
"Uruññāyaṃ" means Uruññā is the name of that country and city. The Blessed One lived depending on the city of Uruññā.
"Kaṇṇakatthale Migadāye" means not far from that city, there is a delightful place called Kaṇṇakatthala Migadāya.
Since it was given for the safety of the animals, it is called "Migadāya." [He was] in that Kaṇṇakatthala Migadāya.
"Aceloti" means a naked ascetic.
"Kassapoti" was his name.
"Tapassinti" means dependent on austerity.
"Lūkhājīvinti" means a rough livelihood, that is, he is one whose livelihood is rough because of being a naked ascetic and having unrestrained conduct, etc.; [therefore,] [he is called] a rough-liver, that rough-liver.
"Upakkosatīti" means he scorns.
"Upavadatīti" means he despises and reviles.
"Dhammassa ca anudhammaṃ byākarontīti" means they explain a reason similar to the reason spoken by the venerable Gotama.
"Sahadhammiko vādānuvādoti" means having a reason based on the reason spoken by others, your argument or counter-argument is blameworthy by the wise; why does even a small reason not come about?
"Idaṃ vuttaṃ hoti" means this was said: "In every way, is there no blameworthy reason in your argument?"
"Anabbhakkhātukāmāti" means they do not desire to speak what is untrue.
Paragraph 382
Mūla (Source Text)
382. ‘‘Ye te, kassapa, evamāhaṃsu – ‘samaṇo gotamo sabbaṃ tapaṃ garahati, sabbaṃ tapassiṃ lūkhājīviṃ ekaṃsena upakkosati upavadatī’ti, na me te vuttavādino, abbhācikkhanti ca pana maṃ te asatā abhūtena. Idhāhaṃ, kassapa, ekaccaṃ tapassiṃ lūkhājīviṃ passāmi dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapannaṃ. Idha panāhaṃ, kassapa, ekaccaṃ tapassiṃ lūkhājīviṃ passāmi dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannaṃ.
English Translation
“Kassapa, those who say: ‘The *śramaṇa* Gotama disparages all asceticism, he categorically reviles and denounces all ascetics who live a coarse life,’ they are not speaking according to what I have said, and they are misrepresenting me with what is false and untrue.

Here, Kassapa, I see one ascetic who lives a coarse life, and with the divine eye (*divyacakṣus*), which is purified and surpasses the human, I see him arising after death, at the breaking up of the body, in a place of misery (*apāya*), in a bad destination (*durgati*), in a place of ruin (*vinipāta*), in hell (*niraya*).

Again, Kassapa, I see one ascetic who lives a coarse life, and with the divine eye (*divyacakṣus*), which is purified and surpasses the human, I see him arising after death, at the breaking up of the body, in a good destination (*sugati*), in a heavenly world (*svarga loka*).
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
382.Ekaccaṃ tapassiṃ lūkhājīvintiādīsu idhekacco acelakapabbajjāditapanissitattā tapassī ‘‘lūkhena jīvitaṃ kappessāmī’’ti tiṇagomayādibhakkhanādīhi nānappakārehi attānaṃ kilameti, appapuññatāya ca sukhena jīvitavuttimeva na labhati, so tīṇi duccaritāni pūretvā niraye nibbattati. Aparo tādisaṃ tapanissitopi puññavā hoti, labhati lābhasakkāraṃ. So ‘‘na dāni mayā sadiso atthī’’ti attānaṃ ucce ṭhāne sambhāvetvā ‘‘bhiyyosomattāya lābhaṃ uppādessāmī’’ti anesanavasena tīṇi duccaritāni pūretvā niraye nibbattati. Ime dve sandhāya paṭhamanayo vutto. Aparo tapanissitako lūkhājīvī appapuñño hoti, na labhati sukhena jīvitavuttiṃ. So ‘‘mayhaṃ pubbepi akatapuññatāya sukhajīvikā nuppajjati , handadāni puññāni karomī’’ti tīṇi sucaritāni pūretvā sagge nibbattati. Aparo lūkhājīvī puññavā hoti, labhati sukhena jīvitavuttiṃ. So – ‘‘mayhaṃ pubbepi katapuññatāya sukhajīvikā uppajjatī’’ti cintetvā anesanaṃ pahāya tīṇi sucaritāni pūretvā sagge nibbattati. Ime dve sandhāya dutiyanayo vutto. Eko pana tapassī appadukkhavihārī hoti bāhirakācārayutto tāpaso vā channaparibbājako vā, appapuññatāya ca manāpe paccaye na labhati. So anesanavasena tīṇi duccaritāni pūretvā attānaṃ sukhe ṭhapetvā niraye nibbattati. Aparo puññavā hoti, so – ‘‘na dāni mayā sadiso atthī’’ti mānaṃ uppādetvā anesanavasena lābhasakkāraṃ vā uppādento micchādiṭṭhivasena – ‘‘sukho imissā paribbājikāya daharāya mudukāya lomasāya samphasso’’tiādīni cintetvā kāmesu pātabyataṃ vā āpajjanto tīṇi duccaritāni pūretvā niraye nibbattati. Ime dve sandhāya tatiyanayo vutto. Aparo pana appadukkhavihārī appapuñño hoti, so – ‘‘ahaṃ pubbepi akatapuññatāya sukhena jīvikaṃ na labhāmī’’ti tīṇi sucaritāni pūretvā sagge nibbattati. Aparo puññavā hoti, so – ‘‘pubbepāhaṃ katapuññatāya sukhaṃ labhāmi, idāni puññāni karissāmī’’ti tīṇi sucaritāni pūretvā sagge nibbattati. Ime dve sandhāya catutthanayo vutto. Idaṃ titthiyavasena āgataṃ, sāsanepi pana labbhati. Ekacco hi dhutaṅgasamādānavasena lūkhājīvī hoti, appapuññatāya vā sakalampi gāmaṃ vicaritvā udarapūraṃ na labhati. So – ‘‘paccaye uppādessāmī’’ti vejjakammādivasena vā anesanaṃ katvā, arahattaṃ vā paṭijānitvā, tīṇi vā kuhanavatthūni paṭisevitvā niraye nibbattati. Aparo ca tādisova puññavā hoti. So tāya puññasampattiyā mānaṃ janayitvā uppannaṃ lābhaṃ thāvaraṃ kattukāmo anesanavasena tīṇi duccaritāni pūretvā niraye uppajjati. Aparo samādinnadhutaṅgo appapuññova hoti, na labhati sukhena jīvitavuttiṃ. So – ‘‘pubbepāhaṃ akatapuññatāya kiñci na labhāmi, sace idāni anesanaṃ karissaṃ, āyatimpi dullabhasukho bhavissāmī’’ti tīṇi sucaritāni pūretvā arahattaṃ pattuṃ asakkonto sagge nibbattati. Aparo puññavā hoti, so – ‘‘pubbepāhaṃ katapuññatāya etarahi sukhito, idānipi puññaṃ karissāmī’’ti anesanaṃ pahāya tīṇi sucaritāni pūretvā arahattaṃ pattuṃ asakkonto sagge nibbattati.
English Commentary
382. In the category of ascetics who live a rough life, etc., here, one ascetic, due to relying on ascetic practices such as going forth as a naked ascetic, torments himself in various ways with food such as grass, cow dung, etc., and due to having little merit, does not obtain a livelihood with ease. He, having filled up three evil deeds, is reborn in hell.

Another, though relying on such asceticism, is meritorious and obtains gain and honor.

He, esteeming himself in a high position, thinking, "I will produce gain to a greater extent," having filled up three evil deeds by improper means, is reborn in hell.

With reference to these two, the first method is stated.

Another ascetic practicing austerity is unmeritorious, and does not obtain a livelihood with ease.

He, thinking, "Even in the past, I did not have a pleasant livelihood due to lack of merit, now I will perform meritorious deeds," having filled up three good deeds, is reborn in heaven.

Another ascetic who lives a rough life is meritorious, and obtains a livelihood with ease.

He, thinking, "Even in the past, I had a pleasant livelihood due to merit," having abandoned improper means, and having filled up three good deeds, is reborn in heaven.

With reference to these two, the second method is stated.

Moreover, one ascetic is one who dwells without suffering, endowed with external conduct, either a renunciate or a wandering ascetic, and due to lack of merit, does not obtain agreeable requisites.

He, having filled up three evil deeds by improper means, having established himself in pleasure, is reborn in hell.

Another is meritorious, he, generating pride, thinking, "Now there is no one like me," either producing gain and honor by improper means, or by wrong view - thinking, "Pleasant is the touch of this wandering female ascetic, young, tender, hairy," etc., or undergoing addiction to sensual pleasures, having filled up three evil deeds, is reborn in hell.

With reference to these two, the third method is stated.

Moreover, one who dwells without suffering is unmeritorious, he, thinking, "Even in the past, I did not obtain a livelihood with ease due to lack of merit," having filled up three good deeds, is reborn in heaven.

Another is meritorious, he, thinking, "In the past, I obtained happiness due to merit, now I will perform meritorious deeds," having filled up three good deeds, is reborn in heaven.

With reference to these two, the fourth method is stated.

This has come about through the way of the sectarians, but it is also obtained in the Dispensation.

One is indeed a rough-living person through undertaking ascetic practices, but due to lack of merit, even having wandered through the entire village, he does not obtain enough to fill his belly.

He - thinking, "I will produce requisites" - either having committed improper means through medical practices etc., or having falsely claimed to be an *arhat*, or having practiced three deceitful things, is reborn in hell.

Another is indeed just like that, meritorious.

He, having generated pride by that meritorious accomplishment, desiring to make the arisen gain permanent, having filled up three evil deeds by improper means, is reborn in hell.

Another, undertaking asceticism, is indeed unmeritorious, he does not obtain a livelihood with ease.

He - "In the past too, I did not obtain anything because of lack of merit. If I now use improper means, I will in the future experience little happiness," - having filled up three good deeds, and being unable to attain *arhat*-ship, is reborn in heaven.

Another is meritorious, he - "In the past too, I was happy due to meritorious actions. Now, too, I will do meritorious actions!" - abandoning improper means, having filled up three good deeds, and being unable to attain *arhat*-ship, is reborn in heaven.
Paragraph 383
Mūla (Source Text)
383. ‘‘Idhāhaṃ, kassapa, ekaccaṃ tapassiṃ appadukkhavihāriṃ passāmi dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapannaṃ. Idha panāhaṃ, kassapa, ekaccaṃ tapassiṃ appadukkhavihāriṃ passāmi dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannaṃ. Yohaṃ, kassapa, imesaṃ tapassīnaṃ evaṃ āgatiñca gatiñca cutiñca upapattiñca yathābhūtaṃ pajānāmi , sohaṃ kiṃ sabbaṃ tapaṃ garahissāmi, sabbaṃ vā tapassiṃ lūkhājīviṃ ekaṃsena upakkosissāmi upavadissāmi?
English Translation
383. "Here, Kassapa, I see with the divine eye ( *dibyena cakṣunā*), which is purified and surpasses the human, a certain ascetic who practices little austerity, arising after death, at the breaking up of the body, in a state of deprivation (*apāyaṃ*), in an unhappy destination (*duggatiṃ*), in perdition (*vinipātaṃ*), in hell (*nirayaṃ*).

Here again, Kassapa, I see with the divine eye, which is purified and surpasses the human, a certain ascetic who practices little austerity, arising after death, at the breaking up of the body, in a good destination (*sugatiṃ*), in a heavenly world (*saggaṃ lokaṃ*).

Since I understand as it truly is the coming (*āgatiṃ*), the going (*gatiṃ*), the deceasing (*cutiṃ*), and the arising (*upapattiṃ*) of these ascetics, how could I disapprove of all austerity (*tapaṃ*), and censure and blame all ascetics who lead a harsh life (*lūkhājīviṃ*) in every respect?"
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
383.Āgatiñcāti – ‘‘asukaṭṭhānato nāma ime āgatā’’ti evaṃ āgatiñca. Gatiñcāti idāni gantabbaṭṭhānañca. Cutiñcāti tato cavanañca. Upapattiñcāti tato cutānaṃ puna upapattiñca. Kiṃ sabbaṃ tapaṃ garahissāmīti – ‘‘kena kāraṇena garahissāmi, garahitabbameva hi mayaṃ garahāma, pasaṃsitabbaṃ pasaṃsāma, na bhaṇḍikaṃ karonto mahārajako viya dhotañca adhotañca ekato karomā’’ti dasseti. Idāni tamatthaṃ pakāsento – ‘‘santi kassapa eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā’’tiādimāha.
English Commentary
383.

*Āgatiñca* means "arrival," as in, "These have arrived from such and such a place; thus, *āgatiñca*."

*Gatiñca* now means the place to be gone to.

*Cutiñca* means the falling away from that.

*Upapattiñca* means the arising again after the falling away from that.

What, shall I censure all asceticism? He shows this by saying, "For what reason should I censure? Indeed, we censure what should be censured, we praise what should be praised. We do not act like a great dyer, making a mixed lot of what is washed and what is not washed."

Now, revealing that meaning, he said, "There are some ascetics and Brahmins, Kassapa..." and so on.
Paragraph 384
Mūla (Source Text)
384. ‘‘Santi, kassapa, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā paṇḍitā nipuṇā kataparappavādā vālavedhirūpā. Te bhindantā maññe caranti paññāgatena diṭṭhigatāni. Tehipi me saddhiṃ ekaccesu ṭhānesu sameti, ekaccesu ṭhānesu na sameti. Yaṃ te ekaccaṃ vadanti ‘sādhū’ti, mayampi taṃ ekaccaṃ vadema ‘sādhū’ti. Yaṃ te ekaccaṃ vadanti ‘na sādhū’ti, mayampi taṃ ekaccaṃ vadema ‘na sādhū’ti. Yaṃ te ekaccaṃ vadanti ‘sādhū’ti, mayaṃ taṃ ekaccaṃ vadema ‘na sādhū’ti. Yaṃ te ekaccaṃ vadanti ‘na sādhū’ti, mayaṃ taṃ ekaccaṃ vadema ‘sādhū’ti. ‘‘Yaṃ mayaṃ ekaccaṃ vadema ‘sādhū’ti, parepi taṃ ekaccaṃ vadanti ‘sādhū’ti. Yaṃ mayaṃ ekaccaṃ vadema ‘na sādhū’ti, parepi taṃ ekaccaṃ vadanti ‘na sādhū’ti. Yaṃ mayaṃ ekaccaṃ vadema ‘na sādhū’ti, pare taṃ ekaccaṃ vadanti ‘sādhū’ti. Yaṃ mayaṃ ekaccaṃ vadema ‘sādhū’ti, pare taṃ ekaccaṃ vadanti ‘na sādhū’ti.
English Translation
384.

“Kassapa, there are some ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇā) who are wise (paṇḍitā), skilled (nipuṇā), practiced in disputation (kataparappavādā), and appear like splitters of hairs (vālavedhirūpā).

They wander about, as it were, demolishing the views (diṭṭhigatāni) of others with their sharp wits (paññāgatena).

Sometimes their views accord with mine, sometimes they do not.

What they sometimes applaud (sādhū), we also sometimes applaud (sādhū).

What they sometimes do not applaud (na sādhū), we also sometimes do not applaud (na sādhū).

What they sometimes applaud (sādhū), we sometimes do not applaud (na sādhū).

What they sometimes do not applaud (na sādhū), we sometimes applaud (sādhū).

What we sometimes applaud (sādhū), others also sometimes applaud (sādhū).

What we sometimes do not applaud (na sādhū), others also sometimes do not applaud (na sādhū).

What we sometimes do not applaud (na sādhū), others sometimes applaud (sādhū).

What we sometimes applaud (sādhū), others sometimes do not applaud (na sādhū).
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
384.Yaṃ te ekaccanti pañcavidhaṃ sīlaṃ, tañhi loke na koci ‘‘na sādhū’’ti vadati. Puna yaṃ te ekaccanti pañcavidhaṃ veraṃ, taṃ na koci ‘‘sādhū’’ti vadati. Puna yaṃ te ekaccanti pañcadvāre asaṃvaraṃ, te kira – ‘‘cakkhu nāma na nirundhitabbaṃ, cakkhunā manāpaṃ rūpaṃ daṭṭhabba’’nti vadanti, esa nayo sotādīsu. Puna yaṃ te ekaccanti pañcadvāre saṃvaraṃ. Evaṃ paresaṃ vādena saha attano vādassa samānāsamānataṃ dassetvā idāni attano vādena saha paresaṃ vādassa samānāsamānataṃ dassento ‘‘yaṃ maya’’ntiādimāha. Tatrāpi pañcasīlādivaseneva attho veditabbo.
English Commentary
384.
Again, as for those who have the fivefold virtue (pañcavidhaṃ sīlaṃ) that one may embrace, no one in the world would say, "That is not good (sādhu)."
Again, as for those who have the fivefold enmity (pañcavidhaṃ veraṃ) that one may embrace, no one would say, "That is good (sādhu)."
Again, as for those who embrace lack of restraint (asaṃvaraṃ) at the five sense-doors (pañcadvāre), they say, "Indeed, the eye (cakkhu) should not be restrained (nirundhitabbaṃ); agreeable forms (manāpaṃ rūpaṃ) should be seen with the eye (cakkhunā)," this is their method concerning the ear (sota) and the others.
Again, as for those who embrace restraint (saṃvaraṃ) at the five sense-doors (pañcadvāre).
Thus, having shown the similarity and dissimilarity of one's own doctrine (vādassa) together with the doctrine (vādena) of others, now, showing the similarity and dissimilarity of the doctrine (vādassa) of others together with one's own doctrine (vādena), he begins by saying, "yaṃ maya."
There also, the meaning (attho) should be understood just as in the case of the five precepts (pañcasīla) and so on.
Paragraph 385
Mūla (Source Text)
385. ‘‘Tyāhaṃ upasaṅkamitvā evaṃ vadāmi – yesu no, āvuso, ṭhānesu na sameti, tiṭṭhantu tāni ṭhānāni. Yesu ṭhānesu sameti, tattha viññū samanuyuñjantaṃ samanugāhantaṃ samanubhāsantaṃ satthārā vā satthāraṃ saṅghena vā saṅghaṃ – ‘ye imesaṃ bhavataṃ dhammā akusalā akusalasaṅkhātā, sāvajjā sāvajjasaṅkhātā, asevitabbā asevitabbasaṅkhātā, na alamariyā na alamariyasaṅkhātā, kaṇhā kaṇhasaṅkhātā. Ko ime dhamme anavasesaṃ pahāya vattati, samaṇo vā gotamo, pare vā pana bhonto gaṇācariyā’ti?
English Translation
‘Then, approaching them, I say thus: “In those points where we do not agree, dear friends, let those points stand aside.

In those points where we agree, there the wise should question, investigate, and discuss with the teacher (*śāstṛ*) or the teacher's followers, or with the *saṃgha* or the *saṃgha*'s members, saying: ‘Those things that are unskillful (*akuśala*) and reckoned as unskillful, blameworthy (*sāvajja*) and reckoned as blameworthy, not to be cultivated (*asevitavyā*) and reckoned as not to be cultivated, not befitting a noble one (*analamariyā*) and reckoned as not befitting a noble one, dark (*kṛṣṇa*) and reckoned as dark—

Who is there who has completely abandoned these things and lives accordingly, the ascetic (*śramaṇa*) Gautama, or other venerable teachers (*gaṇācārya*)?’”
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)

There, *tapopakkama* means the beginning of austerity (tapa), that is, the actions of austerity.
*Sāmaññasaṅkhāta* means designated as the work of a *samaṇa* (ascetic).
*Brahmaññasaṅkhāta* means designated as the work of a *brāhmaṇa*.
*Acelaka* means *niccola*, that is, naked.
*Muttācāra* means behaving without restraint (*visaṭṭhācāra*), being without the customary behavior of noble sons in worldly matters, such as evacuating bowels and bladder: he evacuates his bowels, urinates, eats, and consumes [food] just as he is.
*Hatthāpalekhana* means that when food (*piṇḍa*) is in his hand, he licks his hand with his tongue; or, having defecated, he wipes his hand while regarding it as a stick.
"Come here, venerable sir (*bhante*), for the purpose of taking almsfood (*bhikkhā*)"—if he does not come when he is told this, he is a "non-comer-respectful one" (*na ehibhaddantiko*).
"In that case, stay here, venerable sir"—even when told this, if he does not stay, he is a "non-stayer-respectful one" (*natiṭṭhabhaddantiko*).
Even both of those, it seems, he does not do, [thinking,] "This statement will be done for me."
*Abhihaṭanti*: almsfood (*bhikkhā*) brought having taken [it] beforehand; *uddissakatanti*: almsfood announced thus, "This was done having designated you."
*Na nimantananti*: he does not approve of, nor does he accept, almsfood to which he was invited thus, "You should enter such-and-such a family or street or village."
*Nakumbhimukhāti*: he does not accept almsfood being given having drawn [it] out from a pot (*kumbhi*).
*Na kaḷopimukhāti*: *kaḷopīti* is a pot (*ukkhali*) or a dish (*pacchi*); he does not accept [almsfood] even from that.
Why? [He thinks,] "The pot and dish receive a blow with a ladle because of me."
*Na eḷakamantaranti*: he does not accept [almsfood] being given having made a door-bolt (*ummāra*) in between.
Why? "This one obtains inner actions because of me."
The same method applies in the case of staffs and pestles too.
*Dvinnanti*: among two eating, he does not accept from one who, having risen, is giving.
Why? "There is an obstruction to the morsel (*kabala*) of one."
*Na gabbhiniyātiādīsu*: moreover, regarding a pregnant woman (*gabbhinī*) etc., [he does not accept almsfood thinking,] "A child is exhausted in the belly of the pregnant woman.
There is an obstruction of milk for a nursing child; there is an obstruction of pleasure for one gone between men."
*Saṃkittīsūti*: at jointly agreed upon meals, it seems that, in times of famine, disciples of the naked ascetics, having gathered rice (*taṇḍula*) etc. from here and there for the sake of the naked ascetics, cook food.
An extreme naked ascetic does not accept even from that.
He does not accept almsfood brought without giving to a dog or a jackal (*sunakha*)—"I will obtain almsfood (*piṇḍa*)"—that is waiting nearby.
Why? "There is an obstruction to the almsfood (*piṇḍa*) of that one."
*Saṇḍasaṇḍacārinīti*: moving in a crowd, in a group; if, indeed, having seen a naked ascetic, people enter a food-hall [thinking,] "We will give almsfood to this one," and, with those entering, flies hidden in the mouths of pots (*kaḷopimukhādīsu*) fly up and move in crowds, he does not accept almsfood brought from that.
Why? "Because of me, there is an obstruction to the range of the flies."
*Thusodakanti*: *sovīraka* made from all collections of crops (*sabbasassasambhārehi*).
And here, indeed, drinking alcohol (*surā*) itself is blameworthy (*sāvajja*); but this one regards everything as blameworthy.
*Ekāgārika* means one who, having obtained almsfood in one house alone, turns back.
*Ekālopika* means one who lives on one morsel (*ālopa*) alone.

There, there, someone said to me, "A wandering ascetic": There, in Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak mountain, while I was dwelling, a wandering ascetic named Nigrodha.

"Adhijeguccha?" He asked a question concerning the undertaking of disgust for evil with vigor.

This was said referring to the fact that the Blessed One, sitting at Vulture Peak in the great monastery, having heard the conversation of Nigrodha the wandering ascetic, who was sitting in the park of the goddess Udumbarikā, and of Sandhāna the lay follower, by means of his divine faculty of hearing, came through the sky, sat down on the seat prepared in their presence, and answered the question asked by Nigrodha, "Adhijeguccha?"

"Paraṃ viya mattāyāti paramāya mattāya": "By a measure that is supreme," meaning by a measure that is very great; that is the meaning.

Who, except a blind fool, a follower of wrong views, would say, after hearing the Blessed One's Dharma, "Who is not delighted in mind?"

"I might obtain it." This is what he thought: "For a long time, indeed, has my self been exhausted by attaching myself to the side of non-deliverance, like one who, while turning over and over, thinks, 'I will bathe on the bank of a dry river,' or like one who pounds chaff, no purpose is accomplished."

Thinking, "Come, I will apply myself to yoga," he said.

Then the Blessed One, regarding the probation for those of other sects that has been prescribed in this section, for someone who was formerly of another sect, standing on the ground of a novice: "I, venerable sir, of such and such a name, formerly of another sect, desire ordination in this Dharma-vinaya; therefore, venerable sir, I ask the Saṃgha for four months of probation," etc. (Mahāva.)

(86) Having undertaken and observed it according to the rule, referring to that, he said, "Someone who, Kaśyapa, was formerly of another sect," etc.

Ahaṃ cattāri vassāni parivasissāmi, catunnaṃ vassānaṃ accayena āraddhacittā bhikkhū pabbājentu, upasampādentu bhikkhubhāvāyā’’ti. English Translation
“Kassapa, whoever, having formerly belonged to another sect, desires the going forth ( *pabbajjaṃ*) in this *Dhamma*-Discipline and desires the higher ordination ( *upasampadaṃ*), undergoes probation for four months. At the end of the four months, the monks, being satisfied in their minds, grant him the going forth and the higher ordination into the state of a *bhikkhu*.

Moreover, in this matter, there is a distinction of individuals known."

“Venerable sir, if those who formerly belonged to other sects desire the going forth ( *pabbajjaṃ*) in this *Dhamma*-Discipline and desire the higher ordination ( *upasampadaṃ*), and undergo probation for four months, and at the end of four months the monks, being satisfied in their minds, grant them the going forth and the higher ordination into the state of a *bhikkhu*, then I will undergo probation for four years, and at the end of four years, the monks, being satisfied in their minds, should grant me the going forth and the higher ordination into the state of a *bhikkhu*."

Then the naked Kassapa obtained the going forth ( *pabbajjaṃ*) in the presence of the Blessed One, and he obtained the higher ordination ( *upasampadaṃ*).

And the Venerable Kassapa, not long after his higher ordination ( *acirūpasampanno*), dwelling alone, secluded, diligent, ardent, and resolute, before long attained, by realizing it with his own super-knowledge ( *abhiññā*) here and now, that unsurpassed goal of the holy life ( *brahmacariyapariyosānaṃ*) for which young men of good family rightly go forth from home to homelessness:

'Destroyed is birth ( *khīṇā jāti*), the holy life has been lived ( *vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ*), what had to be done has been done ( *kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ*), there is no more coming to any state of being ( *nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti*)’ – thus he knew.

And the Venerable Kassapa became another one of the *arahants*.

405.
There, "going forth" ( *pabbajjā*) is spoken of merely due to the elegance of the words, for one obtains the going forth without having to observe the probationary period (*parivāsa*).

However, one seeking full ordination (*upasampadā*) should observe the probationary period by fulfilling the eight duties, such as not spending the night in a village, without undue delay.

"With aroused minds" means with minds gladdened by the fulfillment of the eight duties; this is the meaning in brief here.

But this probationary period for those of other views (*titthiya-parivāsa*) should be understood in detail according to the method stated in the *Pabbajjakkhandhaka* commentary of the *Samantapāsādikā* Vinaya.

Furthermore, "with me here" means, "also with me here."

"The distinctness of individuals is known" means the diversity of individuals is known.

“This individual is worthy of probation (*parivāsa*), this one is not worthy of probation" – this shows that this is evident to me.

Then Kassapa thought, "Indeed, the Buddha's teaching is wonderful! Here, having examined and scrutinized, they accept only what is fitting and reject what is unfitting." Then, with even greater enthusiasm for the going forth, he said, "If, venerable sir," and so on.

Then, the Blessed One, knowing his intense desire, said, "Kassapa is not deserving of probation." He addressed a certain monk, "Go, monk, bathe Kassapa, give him the going forth, and bring him here."

That monk did so, gave him the going forth, and came to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One seated him in the midst of the assembly and conferred full ordination (*upasampadā*).

Therefore, it was said, "Then the naked Kassapa received the going forth (*pabbajja*) in the presence of the Blessed One, and he obtained full ordination (*upasampadā*)."

"Recently fully ordained" means having been fully ordained, not long ago.

"Secluded" means secluded from sense objects, defilements, and sensual pleasures by body and also by mind.

"Diligent" means not abandoning mindfulness in the meditation subject (*kammaṭṭhāna*).

"Ardently striving" means ardently striving with the energy and heat characterized by bodily and mental exertion.

"Having a dedicated self" means having a mind directed without regard for body and life, having relinquished self-concern.

"For whose sake" means for whose benefit.

"Young men of good families" means young men of good conduct (*ācārakulaputtā*).

"Through right reason" means through the very cause, through reason itself.

*Tadanuttaraṃ* means that unsurpassed, that superior.

"The culmination of the holy life" means the fruit of *arhatship* (*arahattaphalaṃ*), which is the culmination of the holy life of the path (*magga-brahmacariya*).

Indeed, for that benefit, young men of good families go forth.

*Diṭṭhe va dhamme* means in this very existence, in this very lifetime.

*Sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā* means having realized directly by one's own wisdom, the meaning is having understood without dependence on others.

Having been fully ordained, he dwelt: having attained and accomplished, he dwelt. Thus dwelling, destruction of birth…pe…he realized.

In this way, after showing him the basis for reflection and completing the teaching with the pinnacle of *arhatship*, it was said, "A certain venerable Kassapa became one of the *arhats*."

There, "a certain one" means one.

"*Arhataṃ*" means among the *arhats*; the meaning here is that he was among the Blessed One's disciples, the *arhats*.
