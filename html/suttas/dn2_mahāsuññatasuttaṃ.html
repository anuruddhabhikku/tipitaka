185. Thus have I heard:

On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling in the land of the Sakyas (sakkesu), near Kapilavatthu (kapilavatthusmiṃ), in Nigrodha’s Park (nigrodhārāme).

Then, in the morning, the Blessed One (bhagavā) having dressed, taking his bowl (patta) and robe (cīvara), entered Kapilavatthu (kapilavatthuṃ) for alms (piṇḍāya).

When he had wandered for alms (piṇḍāya) in Kapilavatthu (kapilavatthusmiṃ) and had returned from his almsround (piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto), after his meal, he went to the dwelling (vihāro) of Kāḷakhemaka the Sakyan (kāḷakhemakassa sakkassa) for the day’s abiding (divāvihārāya).

Now on that occasion there were many resting places (sambahulāni senāsanāni) prepared (paññattāni) in Kāḷakhemaka the Sakyan’s (kāḷakhemakassa sakkassa) dwelling (vihāre).

When the Blessed One (bhagavā) saw this, there were many resting places (sambahulāni senāsanāni) prepared (paññattāni) in Kāḷakhemaka the Sakyan’s (kāḷakhemakassa sakkassa) dwelling (vihāre).

He thought: “There are many resting places (sambahulāni senāsanāni) prepared (paññattāni) in Kāḷakhemaka the Sakyan’s (kāḷakhemakassa sakkassa) dwelling (vihāre).

Do many monks (bhikkhū) live here (idha)?”

186. Now on that occasion the venerable Ānanda, along with many bhikkhus, was busy making robes at Ghata the Sakyan’s dwelling ( vihāra ).

Then, when it was evening, the Blessed One ( bhagavā ) rose from retreat and went to Ghata the Sakyan’s dwelling ( vihāra ); on arrival, he sat down on a seat laid out.

Having sat down, the Blessed One ( bhagavā ) addressed the venerable Ānanda, “Ānanda, many resting places (senāsanāni) have been prepared in Kalakhemaka’s Sakyan dwelling ( vihāra ).

Do many bhikkhus live here?” “Venerable Sir, many resting places (senāsanāni) have been prepared in Kalakhemaka’s Sakyan dwelling ( vihāra ).

Many bhikkhus are living here.

This is our time for making robes, venerable sir.”

“Ānanda, a bhikkhu does not shine by delighting in company, by taking delight in company, by devoting himself to delight in company; by delighting in society, by taking delight in society, by rejoicing in society.

Indeed, Ānanda, it is not possible that a bhikkhu who delights in company, takes delight in company, and devotes himself to delight in company, who delights in society, takes delight in society, and rejoices in society, will ever obtain at will, without trouble or difficulty, the bliss of renunciation ( nekkhammasukhaṃ ), the bliss of seclusion ( pavivekasukhaṃ ), the bliss of peace ( upasamasukhaṃ ), the bliss of enlightenment ( sambodhisukhaṃ ). [Note: sambodhisukhaṃ, cittekaggatāsukhaṃ (ka.) upari araṇavibhaṅgasutte pana sambodhisukhantveva dissati]

But it can be expected that when a bhikkhu lives alone, withdrawn from society, he will obtain at will, without trouble or difficulty, the bliss of renunciation ( nekkhammasukhaṃ ), the bliss of seclusion ( pavivekasukhaṃ ), the bliss of peace ( upasamasukhaṃ ), the bliss of enlightenment ( sambodhisukhaṃ ).

[Note: sambodhisukhaṃ, cittekaggatāsukhaṃ (ka.) upari araṇavibhaṅgasutte pana sambodhisukhantveva dissati ]

Indeed, Ānanda, it is not possible that a bhikkhu who delights in company, takes delight in company, and devotes himself to delight in company, who delights in society, takes delight in society, and rejoices in society, will ever enter upon and abide in either the deliverance of mind ( cetovimuttiṃ ) that is temporary and delectable or in the deliverance of mind ( cetovimuttiṃ ) that is perpetual and unshakeable.

But it can be expected that when a bhikkhu lives alone, withdrawn from society, he will enter upon and abide in the deliverance of mind ( cetovimuttiṃ ) that is temporary and delectable or in the deliverance of mind ( cetovimuttiṃ ) that is perpetual and unshakeable.

“I do not see even a single kind of form (rūpaṃ), Ananda, from the change and alteration of which there would not arise sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair in one who lusts for it and takes delight in it.

187. However, Ānanda, there is this dwelling (vihāra) discovered by the Tathāgata: to enter and abide in voidness (śūnyatā) internally by giving no attention to all signs (nimitta).

If, while the Tathāgata is abiding thus in this dwelling (vihāra), he is visited by bhikkhus or bhikkhunīs, by men or women lay followers (upāsaka and upāsikā), by kings (rājan) or kings’ ministers (rājamahāmattā), by other sectarians (tīrthika) or their disciples (tīrthikasāvakā), then with a mind leaning to seclusion (viveka), tending and inclining to seclusion, withdrawn, delighting in renunciation (naikṣammya), and altogether done with things that are the basis for taints (āsava), he invariably talks to them in a way concerned with dismissing them.

Therefore, Ānanda, if a bhikkhu should wish: ‘May I enter upon and abide in voidness (śūnyatā) internally,’ he should steady his mind (citta) internally, quiet it, bring it to singleness, and concentrate it.

“And how, Ānanda, does a bhikkhu steady his mind internally, quiet it, bring it to singleness, and concentrate it (samādahati)? Here, Ānanda, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna… the second jhāna…the third jhāna…the fourth jhāna. That is how, Ānanda, a bhikkhu steadies his mind internally, quiets it, brings it to singleness, and concentrates it.

He gives attention to voidness internally (ajjhattaṃ śūnyataṃ manasi karoti). While he is giving attention to voidness internally, his mind does not enter into voidness, does not acquire confidence, steadiness, and decision. When that is so, Ānanda, a bhikkhu understands thus: ‘While I am giving attention to voidness internally, my mind does not enter into voidness internally or acquire confidence, steadiness, and decision.’ Thus he clearly comprehends this.

He gives attention to voidness externally…He gives attention to voidness internally and externally…He gives attention to imperturbability (āneñja). While he is giving attention to imperturbability (āneñja), his mind does not enter into imperturbability (āneñja) or acquire confidence, steadiness, and decision. When that is so, Ānanda, a bhikkhu understands thus: ‘While I am giving attention to imperturbability, my mind does not enter into imperturbability or acquire confidence, steadiness, and decision.’ Thus he clearly comprehends this.

“Then, Ānanda, that bhikkhu should steady his mind internally, quiet it, bring it to singleness, and concentrate it on that same sign of concentration as before. He gives attention to voidness internally (ajjhattaṃ śūnyataṃ manasi karoti). While he is giving attention to voidness internally, his mind enters into voidness and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision. When that is so, Ānanda, a bhikkhu understands thus: ‘While I am giving attention to voidness internally, my mind enters into voidness internally and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.’ Thus he clearly comprehends this.

He gives attention to voidness externally…He gives attention to voidness internally and externally…He gives attention to imperturbability (āneñja). While he is giving attention to imperturbability (āneñja), his mind enters into imperturbability and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision. When that is so, Ānanda, a bhikkhu understands thus: ‘While I am giving attention to imperturbability, my mind enters into imperturbability and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.’ Thus he clearly comprehends this.

“189. And when a bhikkhu, Ānanda, abides thus, if his mind inclines to walking (caṅkama), he walks, thinking: ‘While I am walking thus, no evil unwholesome states of covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassa) will beset me.’

In this way he has full awareness of that.

And when a bhikkhu, Ānanda, abides thus, if his mind inclines to standing (ṭhāna), he stands, thinking: ‘While I am standing thus, no evil unwholesome states of covetousness and grief will beset me.’

In this way he has full awareness of that.

And when a bhikkhu, Ānanda, abides thus, if his mind inclines to sitting (nisajjā), he sits, thinking: ‘While I am sitting thus, no evil unwholesome states of covetousness and grief will beset me.’

In this way he has full awareness of that.

And when a bhikkhu, Ānanda, abides thus, if his mind inclines to lying down (sayana), he lies down, thinking: ‘While I am lying down thus, no evil unwholesome states of covetousness and grief will beset me.’

In this way he has full awareness of that.

“And when a bhikkhu, Ānanda, abides thus, if his mind inclines to talking (kathā), he resolves: ‘Such talk as is low, vulgar, coarse, ignoble, unbeneficial, and which does not lead to disenchantment (nibbidā), dispassion (virāga), cessation (nirodha), peace (upasama), direct knowledge (abhiññā), enlightenment (sambodha), and Nibbāna, that is, talk of kings (rājakathā), robbers (corakathā), ministers (mahāmattakathā), armies (senākathā), dangers (bhayakathā), battles (yuddhakathā), food (annakathā), drink (pānakathā), clothing (vatthakatha), beds (sayanakatha), garlands (mālākathā), perfumes (gandhakathā), relatives (ñātikathā), vehicles (yānakathā), villages (gāmakathā), towns (nigamakathā), cities (nagarakathā), countries (janapadakathā), women (itthikathā), heroes (surākathā), streets (visikhākathā), wells (kumbhaṭṭhānakathā), the dead (pubbapetakathā), trivialities (nānattakathā), the origin of the world (lokakkhāyikā), the origin of the sea (samuddakkhāyikā), talk of existence and non-existence: such talk I shall not utter.’

In this way he has full awareness of that.

196. Therefore, Ānanda, behave towards me with friendliness (mittavatāya), not with hostility (sapattavatāya). That will lead to your welfare (hitāya) and happiness (sukhāya) for a long time (dīgharattaṃ).

How, Ānanda, do disciples behave towards the Teacher with hostility (sapattavatāya), not with friendliness (mittavatāya)? Here, Ānanda, the Teacher teaches the Dharma to the disciples out of compassion, seeking their welfare (hitesī), taking compassion as his basis, [saying:] ‘This is for your welfare (hitāya), this is for your happiness (sukhāya)’. But those disciples do not want to hear, do not give ear, and do not apply their minds to understanding; they err and turn aside from the Teacher’s Dispensation (satthusāsanā). Thus, Ānanda, do disciples behave towards the Teacher with hostility (sapattavatāya), not with friendliness (mittavatāya).

How, Ānanda, do disciples behave towards the Teacher with friendliness (mittavatāya), not with hostility (sapattavatāya)? Here, Ānanda, the Teacher teaches the Dharma to the disciples out of compassion, seeking their welfare (hitesī), taking compassion as his basis, [saying:] ‘This is for your welfare (hitāya), this is for your happiness (sukhāya)’. And those disciples want to hear, they give ear, and they apply their minds to understanding; they do not err and turn aside from the Teacher’s Dispensation (satthusāsanā). Thus, Ānanda, do disciples behave towards the Teacher with friendliness (mittavatāya), not with hostility (sapattavatāya).

Therefore, Ānanda, behave towards me with friendliness (mittavatāya), not with hostility (sapattavatāya). That will lead to your welfare (hitāya) and happiness (sukhāya) for a long time (dīgharattaṃ). I shall not treat you as the potter treats the raw damp clay (āmake āmakamatte). Repeatedly restraining you, Ānanda, I shall speak; repeatedly admonishing you, Ānanda, I shall speak. The sound core (sāro) will stand the test.”

This was stated by the Blessed One (bhagavā). The venerable (āyasmā) Ānanda was satisfied and delighted (attamano abhinandīti) in the Blessed One’s words (bhāsitaṃ).

The Greater Discourse on Emptiness (mahāsuññatasuttaṃ) is finished, the second.
