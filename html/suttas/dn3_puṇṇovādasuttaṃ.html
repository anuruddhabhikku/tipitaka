Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo sāyanhasamayaṃ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā puṇṇo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘sādhu maṃ, bhante, bhagavā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadatu, yamahaṃ bhagavato dhammaṃ sutvā eko vūpakaṭṭho appamatto ātāpī pahitatto vihareyya’’nti. ‘‘Tena hi, puṇṇa, suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā puṇṇo bhagavato paccassosi. Bhagavā etadavoca –

<pb ed="T" n="3.0482"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Santi <pb ed="M" n="3.0312"/> ca kho, puṇṇa, cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṃhitā rajanīyā. <pb ed="V" n="3.0321"/> <pb ed="P" n="3.0268"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Iminā ca tvaṃ puṇṇa, mayā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadito katarasmiṃ janapade viharissasī’’ti? ‘‘Imināhaṃ, bhante, bhagavatā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadito, atthi sunāparanto nāma janapado, tatthāhaṃ viharissāmī’’ti.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Anāthapindika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Sāvatthi.

Then, when it was evening, the venerable Puṇṇa rose from meditation and went to the Blessed One. After paying homage to the Blessed One, he sat down at one side.

Having sat down to one side, the venerable Puṇṇa said to the Blessed One, “Venerable sir, it would be good if the Blessed One would give me brief advice, so that after I have listened to the Dharma from the Blessed One, I will come to dwell alone, in solitude, not carelessly but diligently, directing myself toward myself."

“Well then, Puṇṇa, listen and attend carefully to what I shall say.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Puṇṇa replied.

The Blessed One said this:

“Puṇṇa, there are forms cognizable by the eye that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust.

If a bhikkhu delights in them, welcomes them, and remains holding to them, delight arises in him.

With the arising of delight, Punna, there is the arising of suffering, I say.

“Puṇṇa, there are sounds cognizable by the ear… odours cognizable by the nose… flavours cognizable by the tongue… tangibles cognizable by the body… mind-objects cognizable by the mind that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust.

If a bhikkhu delights in them, welcomes them, and remains holding to them, delight arises in him.

With the arising of delight, Punna, there is the arising of suffering, I say.

“And again, Punna, there are forms cognizable by the eye that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust.

If a bhikkhu does not delight in them, welcome them, and remain holding to them, delight ceases in him.

With the cessation of delight, Punna, there is the cessation of suffering, I say.

“And again, Punna, there are sounds cognizable by the ear… odours cognizable by the nose… flavours cognizable by the tongue… tangibles cognizable by the body… mind-objects cognizable by the mind that are wished for, desired, agreeable, and likeable, connected with sensual desire and provocative of lust.

If a bhikkhu does not delight in them, welcome them, and remain holding to them, delight ceases in him.

With the cessation of delight, Punna, there is the cessation of suffering, I say.

“And with this condensed advice that I have given you, Punna, in which country will you dwell?”

“With this condensed advice that I have been given by the Blessed One, venerable sir, there is a country called Sunāparanta, there I will dwell.”

396. “Punna, the people of Sunaparanta are wild and rough. If they abuse and revile you, what will you think about that?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunaparanta abuse and revile me, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunaparanta are admirable, truly admirable, in that they do not give me a blow with the fist.’ Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Sublime One.”

“But, Punna, if the people of Sunaparanta do give you a blow with the fist, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunaparanta give me a blow with the fist, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunaparanta are admirable, truly admirable, in that they do not give me a blow with a clod.’ Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Sublime One.”

“But, Punna, if the people of Sunaparanta do give you a blow with a clod, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunaparanta do give me a blow with a clod, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunaparanta are admirable, truly admirable, in that they do not give me a blow with a stick.’ Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Sublime One.”

“But, Punna, if the people of Sunaparanta do give you a blow with a rod, what will you think about that?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunaparanta give me a blow with a rod, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunaparanta are admirable, truly admirable, in that they do not stab me with a knife.’ Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Sublime One.”

“But, Punna, if the people of Sunaparanta give you a blow with a knife, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunaparanta give me a blow with a knife, then I will think: ‘These people of Sunaparanta are admirable, truly admirable, in that they do not deprive me of life with a sharp knife.’ Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Sublime One.”

“But, Punna, if the people of Sunaparanta deprive you of life with a sharp knife, what will you think then?”

“Venerable sir, if the people of Sunaparanta deprive me of life with a sharp knife, then I will think: ‘There are disciples of the Blessed One who, being worried, ashamed, and disgusted with the body and with life, seek an executioner. But I have had my life deprived by the knife without seeking for it.’ Then I will think thus, Blessed One; then I will think thus, Sublime One.”

“Excellent, excellent, Punna! Possessing such self-control and peacefulness, you will be able to dwell in the Sunaparanta country. Now, Punna, it is time to do as you think fit.”

<p n="397" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">397</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā <pb ed="T" n="3.0485"/> padakkhiṇaṃ katvā senāsanaṃ saṃsāmetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena sunāparanto janapado tena <pb ed="M" n="3.0314"/> cārikaṃ pakkāmi. Anupubbena cārikaṃ caramāno yena sunāparanto janapado tadavasari. Tatra sudaṃ āyasmā puṇṇo sunāparantasmiṃ janapade viharati. Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo tenevantaravassena pañcamattāni upāsakasatāni paṭivedesi <note>paṭipādesi (sī. pī.), paṭidesesi (syā. kaṃ.)</note>, tenevantaravassena pañcamattāni upāsikasatāni paṭivedesi, tenevantaravassena tisso vijjā sacchākāsi. Atha kho āyasmā puṇṇo aparena samayena parinibbāyi.

<p rend="bodytext">Atha <pb ed="V" n="3.0323"/> kho sambahulā bhikkhū yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘yo so, bhante, puṇṇo nāma <pb ed="P" n="3.0270"/> kulaputto bhagavatā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadito so kālaṅkato. Tassa kā gati, ko abhisamparāyo’’ti? ‘‘Paṇḍito, bhikkhave, puṇṇo kulaputto paccapādi <note>saccavādī dhammavādī (ka.)</note> dhammassānudhammaṃ, na ca maṃ dhammādhikaraṇaṃ viheṭhesi. Parinibbuto, bhikkhave, puṇṇo kulaputto’’ti.

<p rend="centre">Puṇṇovādasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ tatiyaṃ.

Then, the venerable Puṇṇa, having delighted in and rejoiced at the Blessed One's words, rose from his seat, respectfully saluted the Blessed One, circumambulated him, put his dwelling in order, and, taking his bowl and robes, departed on a walking tour towards the Sunāparanta country.

Wandering by stages, he eventually arrived in the Sunāparanta country.

There, in the Sunāparanta country, the venerable Puṇṇa resided.

Then, during that rainy season, the venerable Puṇṇa established five hundred male lay followers and five hundred female lay followers; during that rainy season, he realized the three knowledges.

Then, at a later time, the venerable Puṇṇa attained final parinirvāṇa.

Then a number of monks went to the Blessed One, and having arrived, bowed down to him and sat to one side.

Sitting to one side, those monks said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, the clansman Puṇṇa, who was briefly instructed by the Blessed One, has passed away. What is his destiny, what is his future state?"

"Monks, the clansman Puṇṇa was wise; he practiced according to the Dharma, and did not trouble me with questions about the Dharma. The clansman Puṇṇa has attained final parinirvāṇa."

This the Blessed One said. Delighted, those monks rejoiced in the Blessed One's words.

The Puṇṇovāda Sutta, the third, is finished.
