473. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering among the Kosalans with a large community of monks.

At that time, a brāhmaṇī named Dhanañjānī, firmly trusting in the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Saṃgha, was living in Cañcalikappa.

Then the brāhmaṇī Dhanañjānī, having stumbled, exclaimed three times this solemn utterance: "Homage to that Blessed One, the Arhat, the perfectly enlightened Buddha! Homage to that Blessed One, the Arhat, the perfectly enlightened Buddha! Homage to that Blessed One, the Arhat, the perfectly enlightened Buddha!"

At that time, a youth named Saṅgārava, who was versed in the three Vedas, with their nighaṇḍu, keṭubha, and systems of akkhara, and was familiar with the itihāsa as a fifth subject, was living in Cañcalikappa; he was skilled in padaka, grammar, and lokāyata and the characteristics of a great man.

Saṅgārava the youth heard Dhanañjānī the brāhmaṇī speaking thus.

Having heard Dhanañjānī the brāhmaṇī, he said this: "This Dhanañjānī the brāhmaṇī is ruined! This Dhanañjānī the brāhmaṇī, while there are brāhmaṇas who maintain the Vedas (threefold knowledge), she will praise that shaveling ascetic!"

"Indeed, dear sir, you do not know the virtue and wisdom (śīlapaññāṇaṃ) of that Blessed One. If you knew the virtue and wisdom of that Blessed One, you would not think of abusing and reviling him."

"Then, lady, when the ascetic Gotama arrives at Cañcalikappa, you should inform me."

"Yes, dear sir," replied Dhanañjānī the brāhmaṇī to Saṅgārava the youth.

That brahman formerly, at the right time, invited brahmans, and offered them honor.

He, having come home, told that matter to the brāhmaṇī, saying, "If, lady, you were able to guard your face for one day, I would give alms to the brahmans for one day."

Give your alms-duty in a pleasant place; what is it to me here?

He invited the brahmans, had thin rice-gruel cooked, and having cleaned the house and set out seats, he seated the brahmans.

The brāhmaṇī, having put on a great cloak, taking a ladle and serving, spilling it on the edge of the cloth, without even making the notion, "I will serve the brahmans," suddenly recollecting the Teacher by force of habit, uttered an exclamation.

The brahmans, having heard the exclamation, saying, "This is one sided with the ascetic Gotama, we will not accept alms-duty from him," angered, threw away the food and departed.

The brahman – "Surely I told you beforehand, 'Guard your face for just this one day!' 474. “Yes, sir,” replied Saṅgārava, the student, to the brahmin Dhanañjānī, and he went to the Blessed One. Having approached, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One.

When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

Seated to one side, Saṅgārava, the student, said to the Blessed One: “There are some ascetics and Brahmins, Master Gotama, who claim to teach the fundamentals of the holy life (ādibrahmacariyaṃ) after having reached the consummation and perfection of direct knowledge (diṭṭhadhammābhiññāvosānapāramippattā).

So, Master Gotama, which of those ascetics and Brahmins are you, who claim to teach the fundamentals of the holy life after having reached the consummation and perfection of direct knowledge?" "Bharadvaja, I say that there is a diversity among those who have attained the consummation and perfection of direct knowledge and claim to teach the fundamentals of the holy life.

There are some ascetics and Brahmins, Bharadvaja, who are traditionalists (anussavikā).

They, on the basis of oral tradition (anussavena), claim to teach the fundamentals of the holy life after having reached the consummation and perfection of direct knowledge; such as the Brahmins of the Three Vedas (tevijjā).

There are some ascetics and Brahmins, Bharadvaja, who, entirely on the basis of mere faith (saddhāmattakena), claim to teach the fundamentals of the holy life after having reached the consummation and perfection of direct knowledge; such are the reasoners and investigators (takkī vīmaṃsī).

There are some ascetics and Brahmins, Bharadvaja, who, having directly known the Dharma for themselves among things not heard before, claim to teach the fundamentals of the holy life after having reached the consummation and perfection of direct knowledge.

“I, Bharadvaja, am one of those ascetics and Brahmins who, having directly known the Dharma for themselves among things not heard before, claim to teach the fundamentals of the holy life after having reached the consummation and perfection of direct knowledge.

As to how I am one of those ascetics and Brahmins, Bharadvaja, that may be understood in the following way: those ascetics and Brahmins who, having directly known the Dharma for themselves among things not heard before, claim to teach the fundamentals of the holy life after having reached the consummation and perfection of direct knowledge, I am one of them.

"Having attained the ultimate perfection of direct-knowledge-cessation" means having directly known, in this very existence, and having become those whose cessation is cessation, having said that we have attained nirvāṇa, which is the ultimate of all dharmas that is reckoned as perfection, they undertake the original Brahmā conduct, this is the meaning.

"Ādibrahmacariya" means the original, productive, and generative Brahmā conduct, thus they undertake, it is said.

"Takkī" means one who grasps at reasoning.

"Vīmaṃsī" means one who investigates, having caused the practice of wisdom to be observed, one who speaks thus.

"Tesāhamasmī" means I am one of those perfectly awakened Buddhas.

Tassa mayhaṃ bhāradvāja, etadahosi – ‘abhijānāmi kho panāhaṃ pitu sakkassa kammante sītāya jambucchāyāya nisinno vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharitā. Siyā nu kho eso maggo bodhāyā’ti? Tassa mayhaṃ, bhāradvāja, satānusāri viññāṇaṃ ahosi – ‘eseva maggo bodhāyā’ti. Tassa mayhaṃ, bhāradvāja, etadahosi – ‘kiṃ nu kho ahaṃ tassa sukhassa bhāyāmi yaṃ taṃ sukhaṃ aññatreva kāmehi aññatra akusalehi dhammehī’ti? Tassa mayhaṃ, bhāradvāja, etadahosi – ‘na kho ahaṃ tassa sukhassa bhāyāmi yaṃ taṃ sukhaṃ aññatreva kāmehi aññatra akusalehi dhammehī’ti.
“Then, Bhāradvāja, I thought: ‘Whatever recluses or brāhmaṇas in the past have experienced painful, racking, piercing feelings due to exertion, this is the utmost, there is none beyond this. And whatever recluses and brāhmaṇas in the future will experience painful, racking, piercing feelings due to exertion, this is the utmost, there is none beyond this. And whatever recluses and brāhmaṇas at present experience painful, racking, piercing feelings due to exertion, this is the utmost, there is none beyond this. But by this racking practice of austerities I have not attained any superhuman states, any distinction in knowledge and vision worthy of the noble ones. Could there be another path to awakening (bodhi)?’

Then, Bhāradvāja, I thought: ‘I recall that when my father the Sakyan was occupied, while I was sitting in the cool shade of a rose-apple tree (jambucchāyā), quite secluded from sensual pleasures (kāmehi), secluded from unwholesome states (akusalehi dhammehi), I entered upon and abided in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought (savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ), with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion (vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ). Could this be the path to awakening (bodhi)?’ Then, Bhāradvāja, there arose in me a consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) that was in accordance with memory (satānusāri): ‘This is the path to awakening (bodhi)!’

Then, Bhāradvāja, I thought: ‘Am I afraid of that pleasure since it has nothing to do with sensual pleasures (kāmehi) and unwholesome states (akusalehi dhammehi)?’ Then, Bhāradvāja, I thought: ‘I am not afraid of that pleasure since it has nothing to do with sensual pleasures (kāmehi) and unwholesome states (akusalehi dhammehi).’"

483. ‘‘Tassa mayhaṃ, bhāradvāja, etadahosi – ‘na kho taṃ sukaraṃ sukhaṃ adhigantuṃ evaṃ adhimattakasimānaṃ pattakāyena. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ oḷārikaṃ āhāraṃ āhāreyyaṃ odanakummāsa’nti. So kho ahaṃ, bhāradvāja, oḷārikaṃ āhāraṃ āhāresiṃ odanakummāsaṃ. Tena kho pana maṃ, bhāradvāja, samayena pañcavaggiyā bhikkhū paccupaṭṭhitā honti – ‘yaṃ kho samaṇo gotamo dhammaṃ adhigamissati taṃ no ārocessatī’ti. Yato kho ahaṃ, bhāradvāja, oḷārikaṃ āhāraṃ āhāresiṃ odanakummāsaṃ, atha me te pañcavaggiyā bhikkhū nibbijja pakkamiṃsu – ‘bāhulliko samaṇo gotamo padhānavibbhanto āvatto bāhullāyā’ti. ‘‘So kho ahaṃ, bhāradvāja, oḷārikaṃ āhāraṃ āhāretvā balaṃ gahetvā vivicceva kāmehi…pe… paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja vihāsiṃ. Vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ… tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ… catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja vihāsiṃ. ‘‘So evaṃ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte pubbenivāsānussatiñāṇāya cittaṃ abhininnāmesiṃ. So anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarāmi, seyyathidaṃ – ekampi jātiṃ dvepi jātiyo…pe… iti sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarāmi. Ayaṃ kho me, bhāradvāja, rattiyā paṭhame yāme paṭhamā vijjā adhigatā, avijjā vihatā, vijjā uppannā; tamo vihato, āloko uppanno; yathā taṃ appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato.
483. Then it occurred to me, Bhāradvāja: ‘It is not easy to attain that pleasure with a body so excessively emaciated. What if I were to take solid food, boiled rice and porridge?’

Then, Bhāradvāja, I took solid food, boiled rice and porridge.

Now at that time, Bhāradvāja, five bhikkhus were waiting upon me, thinking: ‘If the ascetic Gotama attains the Dharma, he will inform us.’

But when I took solid food, boiled rice and porridge, the five bhikkhus were disgusted and left me, thinking: ‘The ascetic Gotama now lives luxuriously; he has given up his striving and reverted to luxury.’

Then, Bhāradvāja, having taken solid food and gained strength, quite secluded from sense pleasures… I entered upon and abided in the first jhāna (first absorption).

With the stilling of applied and sustained thought, with internal tranquility and oneness of mind, I entered upon and abided in the second jhāna (second absorption), which is without applied and sustained thought, and has rapture and pleasure born of concentration… the third jhāna (third absorption)… the fourth jhāna (fourth absorption).

When my mind was thus concentrated, purified, cleansed, without blemish, rid of imperfection, malleable, wieldy, steady, and attained to imperturbability, I directed it to the knowledge of the recollection of past lives (pūrva-nivāsa-anusmṛti-jñāna).

I recollected my manifold past lives, that is, one birth, two births… Thus with their aspects and particulars I recollected my manifold past lives.

This was the first true knowledge attained by me in the first watch of the night, Bhāradvāja: ignorance was banished and true knowledge arose, darkness was banished and light arose, as happens in one who abides diligent, ardent, and resolute.
484
484. ‘‘So evaṃ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte sattānaṃ cutūpapātañāṇāya cittaṃ abhininnāmesiṃ. So dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passāmi cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāmi…pe… ayaṃ kho me, bhāradvāja, rattiyā majjhime yāme dutiyā vijjā adhigatā, avijjā vihatā, vijjā uppannā; tamo vihato, āloko uppanno; yathā taṃ appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato. ‘‘So evaṃ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte āsavānaṃ khayañāṇāya cittaṃ abhininnāmesiṃ. So ‘idaṃ dukkha’nti yathābhūtaṃ abbhaññāsiṃ, ‘ayaṃ dukkhasamudayo’ti yathābhūtaṃ abbhaññāsiṃ, ‘ayaṃ dukkhanirodho’ti yathābhūtaṃ abbhaññāsiṃ, ‘ayaṃ dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā’ti yathābhūtaṃ abbhaññāsiṃ; ‘ime āsavā’ti yathābhūtaṃ abbhaññāsiṃ, ‘ayaṃ āsavasamudayo’ti yathābhūtaṃ abbhaññāsiṃ, ‘ayaṃ āsavanirodho’ti yathābhūtaṃ abbhaññāsiṃ, ‘ayaṃ āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā’ti yathābhūtaṃ abbhaññāsiṃ. Tassa me evaṃ jānato evaṃ passato kāmāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccittha, bhavāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccittha, avijjāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccittha. Vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ ahosi. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti abbhaññāsiṃ. Ayaṃ kho me, bhāradvāja, rattiyā pacchime yāme tatiyā vijjā adhigatā, avijjā vihatā, vijjā uppannā; tamo vihato, āloko uppanno; yathā taṃ appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato’’ti.
484. Thus, when the mind was concentrated, purified, cleansed, stainless, free from defilements, supple, wieldy, steady, and attained to imperturbability, I directed my mind to the knowledge of the passing away and reappearance of beings.

Thus, with the divine eye ( dibbena cakkhunā) which is purified and surpasses the human, I saw beings passing away and reappearing, inferior and superior, fair and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate, and I understood how beings pass on according to their actions. "This was the second true knowledge ( dutiyā vijjā) attained by me in the middle watch ( majjhime yāme) of the night ( rattiyā). Ignorance ( avijjā) was banished and true knowledge ( vijjā) arose, darkness ( tamo) was banished and light ( āloko) arose, as happens in one who abides diligent ( appamattassa), ardent ( ātāpino), and resolute ( pahitattassa).

"When the mind was thus concentrated, purified, cleansed, stainless, free from defilements, supple, wieldy, steady, and attained to imperturbability, I directed it to the knowledge of the destruction of the taints ( āsavānaṃ khayañāṇāya).

I knew according to reality: 'This is suffering ( idaṃ dukkhaṃ)'; 'This is the origin of suffering ( ayaṃ dukkhasamudayo)'; 'This is the cessation of suffering ( ayaṃ dukkhanirodho)'; 'This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering ( ayaṃ dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā)'; I knew according to reality: 'These are the corruptions ( ime āsavā)'; 'This is the origin of the corruptions ( ayaṃ āsavasamudayo)'; 'This is the end of the corruptions ( ayaṃ āsavanirodho)'; 'This is the path leading to the end of the corruptions ( ayaṃ āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā)'.

When I knew and saw thus, my mind was liberated from the taint of sensual desire ( kāmāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccittha), from the taint of being ( bhavāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccittha), and from the taint of ignorance ( avijjāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccittha).

When it was liberated, there came the knowledge: 'It is liberated ( vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ ahosi)'.

'Destroyed is birth ( khīṇā jāti), the spiritual life has been fulfilled ( vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done ( kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being ( nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti abbhaññāsiṃ)'.

"This was the third knowledge ( tatiyā vijjā) I attained in the last watch ( pacchime yāme) of the night ( rattiyā). Ignorance ( avijjā) has been destroyed; knowledge ( vijjā) has arisen; darkness ( tamo) has been destroyed; light ( āloko) has arisen–as happens in one who remains heedful ( appamattassa), ardent ( ātāpino), & resolute ( pahitattassa).’"

Evaṃ vutte, saṅgāravo māṇavo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘aṭṭhitavataṃ aṭṭhita vata (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) bhoto gotamassa padhānaṃ ahosi, sappurisavataṃ sappurisa vata (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) bhoto gotamassa padhānaṃ ahosi; yathā taṃ arahato sammāsambuddhassa. Kiṃ nu kho, bho gotama, atthi devā’’ti adhidevāti (ka.) evaṃ sabbesu ‘atthi devā’tipadesu? ‘‘Ṭhānaso metaṃ kho panetaṃ (syā. kaṃ. ka.), bhāradvāja, viditaṃ yadidaṃ – adhidevā’’ti atthi devāti (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.), atidevāti (?) evaṃ sabbesu ‘adhidevā’tipadesu. ‘‘Kiṃ nu kho, bho gotama, ‘atthi devā’ti puṭṭho samāno ‘ṭhānaso metaṃ, bhāradvāja , viditaṃ yadidaṃ adhidevā’ti vadesi. Nanu, bho gotama, evaṃ sante tucchā musā hotī’’ti? ‘‘‘Atthi devā’ti, bhāradvāja, puṭṭho samāno ‘atthi devā’ti yo vadeyya, ‘ṭhānaso me viditā’ti ṭhānaso viditā me viditāti (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.), ṭhānaso me viditā atidevāti (?) yo vadeyya; atha khvettha viññunā purisena ekaṃsena niṭṭhaṃ gantabbaṃ gantuṃ (ka.), gantuṃ vā (syā. kaṃ.) yadidaṃ – ‘atthi devā’’’ti. ‘‘Kissa pana me bhavaṃ gotamo ādikeneva na byākāsī’’ti gotamo ādikeneva byākāsīti (ka.), gotamo atthi devāti na byākāsīti (?)? ‘‘Uccena sammataṃ kho etaṃ, bhāradvāja, lokasmiṃ yadidaṃ – ‘atthi devā’’’ti.
When this was said, the brahmin student Saṅgārava said to the Blessed One: “Master Gotama’s striving was unfaltering, Master Gotama’s striving was that of a true man, as it should be for an arhat, a Fully Enlightened One.

But how is it, Master Gotama, are there gods?”

“This is a matter about which Master Gotama can be trusted, Bhāradvāja, it is known that there are superior gods.”

“But how is it, Master Gotama, that when asked, ‘Are there gods?’ you reply, ‘This is a matter about which I can be trusted, Bhāradvāja, it is known that there are superior gods’?”

“Surely, Master Gotama, in this case, it is empty and false?”

“If, Bhāradvāja, when asked, ‘Are there gods?’ someone should say, ‘I know for certain that there are gods,’ or ‘I know for certain that there are superior gods,’ then a discerning person should come to a firm conclusion in one respect, namely: ‘There are gods.’”

“But why didn’t Master Gotama answer me in the first way?”

“Because, Bhāradvāja, it is widely accepted in the world that ‘there are gods.’”

Atthidevātipuṭṭho samānoti idaṃ māṇavo ‘‘sammāsambuddho ajānantova pakāsesī’’ti saññāya āha. Evaṃ santeti tumhākaṃ ajānanabhāve sante. Tucchaṃ musā hotīti tumhākaṃ kathā aphalā nipphalā hoti. Evaṃ māṇavo bhagavantaṃ musāvādena niggaṇhāti nāma. Viññunā purisenāti paṇḍitena manussena. Tvaṃ pana aviññutāya mayā byākatampi na jānāsīti dīpeti. Uccena sammatanti uccena saddena sammataṃ pākaṭaṃ lokasmiṃ. Adhidevāti susudārakāpi hi devā nāma honti, deviyo nāma honti devā pana adhidevā nāma, loke devo devīti laddhanāmehi manussehi adhikāti attho. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Saṅgāravasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā. Pañcamavaggavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā. Majjhimapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā niṭṭhitā.
485. "Aṭṭhitavata" means aṭṭhitatapaṃ, association with the term padhānapada, as well as sappurisapada.

idañhi vuttaṃ hoti – It was said, "Bhoto Gotamassa aṭṭhitapadhānavataṃ ahosi, sappurisapadhānavataṃ ahosīti."

"Atthi," "Devāti," "Puṭṭho samāno" means, the youth (māṇavo) said this with the understanding (saññāya), "The perfectly enlightened Buddha (sammāsambuddho) reveals (pakāsesī) even without knowing (ajānantova)."

"Evaṃ sante" means, while ignorance (ajānanabhāve) exists for you (tumhākaṃ).

"Tucchaṃ musā hotī" means, your (tumhākaṃ) speech (kathā) is fruitless (aphalā), unproductive (nipphalā).

Evaṃ māṇavo bhagavantaṃ musāvādena niggaṇhāti nāma. Thus, the youth refutes the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ) with false speech (musāvādena).

"Viññunā purisenā" means, by a wise (paṇḍitena) man (manussena).

tvaṃ pana aviññutāya mayā byākatampi na jānāsīti dīpeti. It reveals that you do not know even what was explained by me due to ignorance (aviññutāya).

"Uccena sammata" means sammataṃ pākaṭaṃ lokasmiṃ with a loud (uccena) sound (saddena), well-known (pākaṭaṃ) in the world (lokasmiṃ).

"Adhidevā" means, even very young girls (susudārakāpi) are called gods (devā), there are goddesses (deviyo) called gods (devā), but gods (devā) are called super-gods (adhidevā), in the world (loke), the meaning (attho) is more (adhikāti) than men (manussehi) who have obtained the names (laddhanāmehi) "god (devo)," "goddess (devī)."

sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. The rest is clear throughout.

Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya – cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantīti – evamevaṃ bhotā gotamena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Master Gotama has made the Dhamma clear in many ways, as though he were turning upright what had been overthrown, revealing what was hidden, showing the way to one who was lost, or holding up a lamp in the dark for those with eyesight to see forms. I go to Master Gotama for refuge and to the Dhamma and to the bhikkhu Saṅgha. May the Reverend Gotama accept me as a lay follower who has taken refuge from this day onwards while life lasts.”
