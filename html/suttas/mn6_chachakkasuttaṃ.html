420. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘dhammaṃ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāsessāmi, yadidaṃ – cha chakkāni. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Cha ajjhattikāni āyatanāni veditabbāni, cha bāhirāni āyatanāni veditabbāni, cha viññāṇakāyā veditabbā, cha phassakāyā veditabbā, cha vedanākāyā veditabbā, cha taṇhākāyā veditabbā.
Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling near Sāavatthī (sāvatthiyaṃ) in Jeta’s Grove (jetavane), Anāthapindika’s Park (anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).
There the Fortunate One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: “Monks (bhikkhavo)!”
“Venerable sir (bhadante),” those monks (bhikkhū) replied to the Fortunate One (bhagavato).
The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this: “Monks (bhikkhave), I will teach you the Dhamma (dhammaṃ) admirable in the beginning (ādikalyāṇaṃ), admirable in the middle (majjhekalyāṇaṃ), admirable in the end (pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ), with its meaning (sātthaṃ) and with its expression (sabyañjanaṃ), entirely complete (kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ), surpassingly pure (parisuddhaṃ); I will expound the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ), in other words, the six sextets (cha chakkāni).
Listen to that (taṃ), pay close attention (sādhukaṃ manasi karotha); I will speak (bhāsissāmīti).”
“As you say, lord (evaṃ bhante),” those monks (bhikkhū) replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).
The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this:

“The six internal sense bases (cha ajjhattikāni āyatanāni) should be known (veditabbāni), the six external sense bases (cha bāhirāni āyatanāni) should be known (veditabbāni), the six classes of consciousness (cha viññāṇakāyā) should be known (veditabbā), the six classes of contact (cha phassakāyā) should be known (veditabbā), the six classes of feeling (cha vedanākāyā) should be known (veditabbā), the six classes of craving (cha taṇhākāyā) should be known (veditabbā).
420.Evaṃme sutanti chachakkasuttaṃ. Tattha ādikalyāṇantiādimhi kalyāṇaṃ bhaddakaṃ niddosaṃ katvā desessāmi. Majjhapariyosānesupi eseva nayo. Iti bhagavā ariyavaṃsaṃ navahi, mahāsatipaṭṭhānaṃ sattahi, mahāassapuraṃ sattahiyeva padehi thomesi. Idaṃ pana suttaṃ navahi padehi thomesi. Veditabbānīti sahavipassanena maggena jānitabbāni. Manāyatanena tebhūmakacittameva kathitaṃ, dhammāyatanena bahiddhā tebhūmakadhammā ca, manoviññāṇena ṭhapetvā dve pañcaviññāṇāni sesaṃ bāvīsatividhaṃ lokiyavipākacittaṃ. Phassavedanā yathāvuttavipākaviññāṇasampayuttāva. Taṇhāti vipākavedanāpaccayā javanakkhaṇe uppannataṇhā. Veditabbānī Taṇhā
420. evaṃ me suta, the Chachakkasutta.

There, ādikalyāṇa means: I will teach [the Dhamma] making the beginning good, auspicious, and without fault. The same method applies to the middle and the end. Thus, the Blessed One praised the Ariyavaṃsa with nine, the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna with seven, the Mahāassapura with seven words. But he praised this sutta with nine words.

veditabbānī means: they should be known with the path together with insight. With manāyatana, only the three-plane consciousness is spoken of; with dhammāyatana, the external three-plane dhammas as well; with manoviññāṇa, leaving aside the two five consciousnesses, the remaining twenty-two kinds of mundane resultant consciousness. Contact and feeling are only associated with the aforementioned resultant consciousness. taṇhā means: craving that has arisen in the moment of impulsion due to resultant feeling.

Idaṃ paṭhamaṃ chakkaṃ. ‘‘‘Cha bāhirāni āyatanāni veditabbānī’ti – iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ . Kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? Rūpāyatanaṃ, saddāyatanaṃ, gandhāyatanaṃ, rasāyatanaṃ, phoṭṭhabbāyatanaṃ, dhammāyatanaṃ. ‘Cha bāhirāni āyatanāni veditabbānī’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ. Idaṃ dutiyaṃ chakkaṃ. ‘‘‘Cha viññāṇakāyā veditabbā’ti – iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ. Kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? Cakkhuñca paṭicca rūpe ca uppajjati cakkhuviññāṇaṃ, tiṇṇaṃ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā; sotañca paṭicca sadde ca uppajjati sotaviññāṇaṃ, tiṇṇaṃ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā; ghānañca paṭicca gandhe ca uppajjati ghānaviññāṇaṃ, tiṇṇaṃ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā; jivhañca paṭicca rase ca uppajjati jivhāviññāṇaṃ, tiṇṇaṃ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā; kāyañca paṭicca phoṭṭhabbe ca uppajjati kāyaviññāṇaṃ, tiṇṇaṃ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā; manañca paṭicca dhamme ca uppajjati manoviññāṇaṃ, tiṇṇaṃ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā. ‘Cha vedanākāyā veditabbā’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ. 421. “‘The six internal sense-spheres (ajjhattikāni āyatanāni) should be known.’ Thus was it said.

And in reference to what was it said? The eye sense-sphere (cakkhāyatanaṃ), the ear sense-sphere (sotāyatanaṃ), the nose sense-sphere (ghānāyatanaṃ), the tongue sense-sphere (jivhāyatanaṃ), the body sense-sphere (kāyāyatanaṃ), and the mind sense-sphere (manāyatanaṃ).

‘The six internal sense-spheres should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said.

This is the first sextet.

‘‘‘The six external sense-spheres (bāhirāni āyatanāni) should be known.’ Thus was it said.

And in reference to what was it said? The form sense-sphere (rūpāyatanaṃ), the sound sense-sphere (saddāyatanaṃ), the smell sense-sphere (gandhāyatanaṃ), the taste sense-sphere (rasāyatanaṃ), the touch sense-sphere (phoṭṭhabbāyatanaṃ), and the mental object sense-sphere (dhammāyatanaṃ).

‘The six external sense-spheres should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said.

This is the second sextet.

‘‘‘The six aggregates of consciousness (viññāṇakāyā) should be known.’ Thus was it said.

And in reference to what was it said? Dependent on the eye (cakkhu) and forms (rūpe), eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) arises; dependent on the ear (sota) and sounds (sadde), ear-consciousness (sotaviññāṇaṃ) arises; dependent on the nose (ghāna) and smells (gandhe), nose-consciousness (ghānaviññāṇaṃ) arises; dependent on the tongue (jivha) and tastes (rase), tongue-consciousness (jivhāviññāṇaṃ) arises; dependent on the body (kāya) and tactile objects (phoṭṭhabbe), body-consciousness (kāyaviññāṇaṃ) arises; dependent on the mind (mana) and mental objects (dhamme), mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇaṃ) arises.

‘The six aggregates of consciousness should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said.

This is the third sextet.

‘‘‘The six aggregates of contact (phassakāyā) should be known.’ Thus was it said.

And in reference to what was it said? Dependent on the eye (cakkhu) and forms (rūpe), eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact (phasso); dependent on the ear (sota) and sounds (sadde), ear-consciousness (sotaviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact; dependent on the nose (ghāna) and smells (gandhe), nose-consciousness (ghānaviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact; dependent on the tongue (jivha) and tastes (rase), tongue-consciousness (jivhāviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact; dependent on the body (kāya) and tactile objects (phoṭṭhabbe), body-consciousness (kāyaviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact; dependent on the mind (mana) and mental objects (dhamme), mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact.

‘The six aggregates of contact should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said.

This is the fourth sextet.

‘‘‘The six aggregates of feeling (vedanākāyā) should be known.’ Thus was it said.

And in reference to what was it said? Dependent on the eye (cakkhu) and forms (rūpe), eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact (phasso), with contact as condition there is feeling (vedanā); dependent on the ear (sota) and sounds (sadde), ear-consciousness (sotaviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact, with contact as condition there is feeling; dependent on the nose (ghāna) and smells (gandhe), nose-consciousness (ghānaviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact, with contact as condition there is feeling; dependent on the tongue (jivha) and tastes (rase), tongue-consciousness (jivhāviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact, with contact as condition there is feeling; dependent on the body (kāya) and tactile objects (phoṭṭhabbe), body-consciousness (kāyaviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact, with contact as condition there is feeling; dependent on the mind (mana) and mental objects (dhamme), mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact, with contact as condition there is feeling.

‘The six aggregates of feeling should be known.’ Thus was it said. And in reference to this was it said.

This is the fifth sextet.

‘‘‘The six aggregates of craving (taṇhākāyā) should be known.’ Thus was it said.

And in reference to what was it said? Dependent on the eye (cakkhu) and forms (rūpe), eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact (phasso), with contact as condition there is feeling (vedanā), with feeling as condition there is craving (taṇhā); dependent on the ear (sota) and sounds (sadde), ear-consciousness (sotaviññāṇaṃ) arises…pe… dependent on the nose (ghāna) and smells (gandhe), nose-consciousness (ghānaviññāṇaṃ) arises… dependent on the tongue (jivha) and tastes (rase), tongue-consciousness (jivhāviññāṇaṃ) arises… dependent on the body (kāya) and tactile objects (phoṭṭhabbe), body-consciousness (kāyaviññāṇaṃ) arises… dependent on the mind (mana) and mental objects (dhamme), mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇaṃ) arises, the meeting of the three is contact, with contact as condition there is feeling, with feeling as condition there is craving.

‘The six aggregates of craving should be known.’ Thus was it said.
This is the sixth sextet.

422. “If anyone were to say, ‘The eye (cakkhu) is the self (attā)’, that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of the eye (cakkhussa) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (attā) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘The eye (cakkhu) is the self (attā)’. Thus, the eye (cakkhu) is not-self (anattā).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Forms (rūpā) are the self (attā)’, that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of forms (rūpānaṃ) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (attā) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Forms (rūpā) are the self (attā)’. Thus, the eye (cakkhu) is not-self (anattā) and forms (rūpā) are not-self (anattā).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Consciousness at the eye (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) is the self (attā)’, that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of consciousness at the eye (cakkhuviññāṇassa) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (attā) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Consciousness at the eye (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) is the self (attā)’. Thus, the eye (cakkhu) is not-self (anattā), forms (rūpā) are not-self (anattā), and consciousness at the eye (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) is not-self (anattā).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Contact at the eye (cakkhusamphasso) is the self (attā)’, that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of contact at the eye (cakkhusamphassassa) are discerned.
And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (attā) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Contact at the eye (cakkhusamphasso) is the self (attā)’. Thus, the eye (cakkhu) is not-self (anattā), forms (rūpā) are not-self (anattā), consciousness at the eye (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) is not-self (anattā), and contact at the eye (cakkhusamphasso) is not-self (anattā).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Feeling (vedanā) is the self (attā)’, that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of feeling (vedanāya) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (attā) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Feeling (vedanā) is the self (attā)’. Thus, the eye (cakkhu) is not-self (anattā), forms (rūpā) are not-self (anattā), consciousness at the eye (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) is not-self (anattā), contact at the eye (cakkhusamphasso) is not-self (anattā), and feeling (vedanā) is not-self (anattā).

“If anyone were to say, ‘Craving (taṇhā) is the self (attā)’, that wouldn’t be tenable. The arising and falling away of craving (taṇhāya) are discerned. And when its arising and falling away are discerned, it would follow that ‘My self (attā) arises and falls away.’ That’s why it wouldn’t be tenable if anyone were to say, ‘Craving (taṇhā) is the self (attā)’. Thus, the eye (cakkhu) is not-self (anattā), forms (rūpā) are not-self (anattā), consciousness at the eye (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) is not-self (anattā), contact at the eye (cakkhusamphasso) is not-self (anattā), feeling (vedanā) is not-self (anattā), and craving (taṇhā) is not-self (anattā).
422.Cakkhu attāti pāṭiyekko anusandhi. Heṭṭhā kathitānañhi dvinnaṃ saccānaṃ anattabhāvadassanatthaṃ ayaṃ desanā āraddhā. Tattha na upapajjatīti na yujjati. Vetīti vigacchati nirujjhati.

Having shown thus the unfolding ( vivaṭṭaṃ dassetvā), now, in order to show again the round (of rebirth) by way of the three proclivities (anusaya), he says, “Cakkhuñca, bhikkhave” etc. There, "abhinandatī" etc. are stated only in terms of craving (taṇhā) and views (diṭṭhi). "Anusetī" means it is not abandoned (appahīno hoti). "Dukkhassā" means of the round of suffering (vaṭṭadukkha) and the suffering of defilements (kilesadukkha).

426. ‘‘Cakkhuñca , bhikkhave, paṭicca rūpe ca uppajjati cakkhuviññāṇaṃ, tiṇṇaṃ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā uppajjati vedayitaṃ sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā. So sukhāya vedanāya phuṭṭho samāno nābhinandati nābhivadati nājjhosāya tiṭṭhati. Tassa rāgānusayo nānuseti. Dukkhāya vedanāya phuṭṭho samāno na socati na kilamati paridevati na urattāḷiṃ kandati na sammohaṃ āpajjati. Tassa paṭighānusayo nānuseti. Adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya phuṭṭho samāno tassā vedanāya samudayañca atthaṅgamañca assādañca ādīnavañca nissaraṇañca yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Tassa avijjānusayo nānuseti. So sukhāya vedanāya phuṭṭho samāno nābhinandati nābhivadati nājjhosāya tiṭṭhati. Tassa rāgānusayo nānuseti. Dukkhāya vedanāya phuṭṭho samāno na socati na kilamati na paridevati na urattāḷiṃ kandati na sammohaṃ āpajjati. Tassa paṭighānusayo nānuseti. Adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya phuṭṭho samāno tassā vedanāya samudayañca atthaṅgamañca assādañca ādīnavañca nissaraṇañca yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Tassa avijjānusayo nānuseti. So vata, bhikkhave, sukhāya vedanāya rāgānusayaṃ pahāya dukkhāya vedanāya paṭighānusayaṃ paṭivinodetvā adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya avijjānusayaṃ samūhanitvā avijjaṃ pahāya vijjaṃ uppādetvā diṭṭheva dhamme dukkhassantakaro bhavissatīti – ṭhānametaṃ vijjati.
426. "Monks, dependent on the eye and forms, eye-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; conditioned by contact, there arises feeling, be it pleasant, painful, or neither-painful-nor-pleasant.

When touched by a pleasant feeling, one does not delight in it, welcome it, or remain attached to it. One's underlying tendency to passion does not lie within.

When touched by a painful feeling, one does not grieve, lament, or despair; one does not beat one's breast and become distraught. One's underlying tendency to aversion does not lie within.

When touched by a feeling that is neither pleasant nor painful, one understands as it really is the arising, passing away, allure, danger, and escape from that feeling. One's underlying tendency to ignorance does not lie within.

Monks, it is indeed possible that a person, having abandoned the underlying tendency to passion for pleasant feeling, having eliminated the underlying tendency to aversion for painful feeling, having uprooted the underlying tendency to ignorance for neither-pleasant-nor-painful feeling, having abandoned ignorance and generated knowledge, will put an end to suffering in this very life."

"Monks, dependent on the ear and sounds, ear-consciousness arises...pe...".

"Monks, dependent on the nose and smells, nose-consciousness arises...pe...".

"Monks, dependent on the tongue and tastes, tongue-consciousness arises...pe...".

"Monks, dependent on the body and tactile objects, body-consciousness arises...pe...".

"Monks, dependent on the mind and mental objects, mind-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; conditioned by contact, there arises feeling, be it pleasant, painful, or neither-painful-nor-pleasant.

When touched by a pleasant feeling, one does not delight in it, welcome it, or remain attached to it. One's underlying tendency to passion does not lie within.

When touched by a painful feeling, one does not grieve, lament, or despair; one does not beat one's breast and become distraught. One's underlying tendency to aversion does not lie within.

When touched by a feeling that is neither pleasant nor painful, one understands as it really is the arising, passing away, allure, danger, and escape from that feeling. One's underlying tendency to ignorance does not lie within.

Monks, it is indeed possible that a person, having abandoned the underlying tendency to passion for pleasant feeling, having eliminated the underlying tendency to aversion for painful feeling, having uprooted the underlying tendency to ignorance for neither-pleasant-nor-painful feeling, having abandoned ignorance and generated knowledge, will put an end to suffering in this very life."
426. Evaṃ tiṇṇaṃ anusayānaṃ vasena vaṭṭaṃ kathetvā idāni tesaṃ paṭikkhepavasena vivaṭṭaṃ dassento puna cakkhuñcātiādimāha. Avijjaṃ pahāyāti vaṭṭamūlikaṃ avijjaṃ pajahitvā. Vijjanti arahattamaggavijjaṃ uppādetvā.
Thus, having spoken of the cycle (vaṭṭaṃ) in terms of the three latent tendencies (tiṇṇaṃ anusayānaṃ vasena), now, showing the reversal (vivaṭṭaṃ) in terms of their rejection (paṭikkhepavasena), he says again, beginning with "the eye" (cakkhuñcātiādimāha).

"Having abandoned ignorance (avijjaṃ pahāyā)," means having abandoned ignorance (avijjaṃ pajahitvā) which is the root of the cycle (vaṭṭamūlikaṃ).

"Knowledge (vijja)," means having produced the knowledge of the path of Arhatship (arahattamaggavijjaṃ uppādetvā).

427. ‘‘Evaṃ passaṃ, bhikkhave, sutavā ariyasāvako cakkhusmiṃ cakkhusmimpi (syā. kaṃ.) evamitaresupi nibbindati, rūpesu nibbindati, cakkhuviññāṇe nibbindati, cakkhusamphasse nibbindati, vedanāya nibbindati, taṇhāya nibbindati. Sotasmiṃ nibbindati, saddesu nibbindati…pe… ghānasmiṃ nibbindati, gandhesu nibbindati… jivhāya nibbindati, rasesu nibbindati… kāyasmiṃ nibbindati, phoṭṭhabbesu nibbindati… manasmiṃ nibbindati, dhammesu nibbindati, manoviññāṇe nibbindati, manosamphasse nibbindati, vedanāya nibbindati, taṇhāya nibbindati. Nibbindaṃ virajjati , virāgā vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānātī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Imasmiṃ kho pana veyyākaraṇasmiṃ bhaññamāne saṭṭhimattānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ anupādāya āsavehi cittāni vimucciṃsūti. Chachakkasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ.
“Seeing thus, bhikkhus, a well-instructed noble disciple grows disenchanted with the eye ( cakkhusmiṃ nibbindati), disenchanted with forms (rūpesu nibbindati), disenchanted with visual consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇe nibbindati), disenchanted with visual contact (cakkhusamphasse nibbindati), disenchanted with feeling (vedanāya nibbindati), disenchanted with craving (taṇhāya nibbindati). He grows disenchanted with the ear, disenchanted with sounds…pe… He grows disenchanted with the nose, disenchanted with odors… He grows disenchanted with the tongue, disenchanted with tastes… He grows disenchanted with the body, disenchanted with tactile sensations… He grows disenchanted with the mind, disenchanted with mental phenomena, disenchanted with mental consciousness, disenchanted with mental contact, disenchanted with feeling, disenchanted with craving. Disenchanted, he becomes dispassionate (nibbindaṃ virajjati); through dispassion, he is liberated (virāgā vimuccati). When liberated, there is knowledge that he is liberated (vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti). He understands: ‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāti)’”.

This the Blessed One said. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement (attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti). And while this explanation was being given, the hearts of sixty monks, through non-clinging, were fully released from the taints (imasmiṃ kho pana veyyākaraṇasmiṃ bhaññamāne saṭṭhimattānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ anupādāya āsavehi cittāni vimucciṃsūti).

The Sixth Discourse on the Six Sextets is finished.
427.Ṭhānametaṃ vijjatīti ettakeneva kathāmaggena vaṭṭavivaṭṭavasena desanaṃ matthakaṃ pāpetvā puna tadeva sampiṇḍetvā dassento evaṃ passaṃ, bhikkhavetiādimāha. Saṭṭhimattānaṃ bhikkhūnanti ettha anacchariyametaṃ, yaṃ sayameva tathāgate desente saṭṭhi bhikkhū arahattaṃ pattā. Imañhi suttaṃ dhammasenāpatimhi kathentepi saṭṭhi bhikkhū arahattaṃ pattā, mahāmoggallāne kathentepi, asītimahātheresu kathentesupi pattā eva. Etampi anacchariyaṃ. Mahābhiññappattā hi te sāvakā. Aparabhāge pana tambapaṇṇidīpe māleyyadevatthero nāma heṭṭhā lohapāsāde idaṃ suttaṃ kathesi. Tadāpi saṭṭhi bhikkhū arahattaṃ pattā. Yathā ca lohapāsāde, evaṃ thero mahāmaṇḍapepi idaṃ suttaṃ kathesi. Mahāvihārā nikkhamitvā cetiyapabbataṃ gato, tatthāpi kathesi. Tato sākiyavaṃsavihāre, kūṭālivihāre, antarasobbhe, muttaṅgaṇe, vātakapabbate, pācinagharake, dīghavāpiyaṃ, lokandare, nomaṇḍalatale kathesi. Tesupi ṭhānesu saṭṭhi saṭṭhi bhikkhū arahattaṃ pattā. Tato nikkhamitvā pana thero cittalapabbataṃ gato. Tadā ca cittalapabbatavihāre atirekasaṭṭhivasso mahāthero, pokkharaṇiyaṃ kuruvakatitthaṃ nāma paṭicchannaṭṭhānaṃ atthi, tattha thero nhāyissāmīti otiṇṇo. Devatthero tassa santikaṃ gantvā nhāpemi, bhanteti āha. Thero paṭisanthāreneva – ‘‘māleyyadevo nāma atthīti vadanti, so ayaṃ bhavissatī’’ti ñatvā tvaṃ devoti āha. Āma, bhanteti. Saṭṭhivassaddhānaṃ me, āvuso, koci sarīraṃ hatthena phusituṃ nāma na labhi, tvaṃ pana nhāpehīti uttaritvā tīre nisīdi. Thero sabbampi hatthapādādiparikammaṃ katvā mahātheraṃ nhāpesi. Taṃ divasañca dhammassavanadivaso hoti. Atha mahāthero – ‘‘deva amhākaṃ dhammadānaṃ dātuṃ vaṭṭatī’’ti āha. Thero sādhu, bhanteti sampaṭicchi. Atthaṅgate sūriye dhammassavanaṃ ghosesuṃ. Atikkantasaṭṭhivassāva saṭṭhi mahātherā dhammassavanatthaṃ agamaṃsu. Devatthero sarabhāṇāvasāne imaṃ suttaṃ ārabhi, suttantapariyosāne ca saṭṭhi mahātherā arahattaṃ pāpuṇiṃsu. Tato tissamahāvihāraṃ gantvā kathesi, tasmimpi saṭṭhi therā. Tato nāgamahāvihāre kāḷakacchagāme kathesi, tasmimpi saṭṭhi therā. Tato kalyāṇiṃ gantvā tattha cātuddase heṭṭhāpāsāde kathesi, tasmimpi saṭṭhi therā. Uposathadivase uparipāsāde kathesi, tasmimpi saṭṭhi therāti evaṃ devatthereyeva idaṃ suttaṃ kathente saṭṭhiṭṭhānesu saṭṭhi saṭṭhi janā arahattaṃ pattā. Ambilakāḷakavihāre pana tipiṭakacūḷanāgatthere imaṃ suttaṃ kathente manussaparisā gāvutaṃ ahosi, devaparisā yojanikā. Suttapariyosāne sahassabhikkhū arahattaṃ pattā, devesu pana tato tato ekekova puthujjano ahosīti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Chachakkasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
427. "This is possible (ṭhānametaṃ vijjatī)"—in this way, having brought the teaching to its peak by way of revolving and reverting the discourse, and again showing it by condensing it, [the Buddha] said beginning with, "Seeing thus, bhikkhus (evaṃ passaṃ, bhikkhave)."

"Sixty monks (saṭṭhimattānaṃ bhikkhūna)"—here, this is not astonishing that sixty monks attained arahatship while the Tathāgata himself was teaching. Indeed, even when this sutta was being recited by the General of the Dhamma, sixty monks attained arahatship; even when recited by Mahāmoggallāna, even when recited by the eighty great elders, they attained [arahatship]. This too is not astonishing. For those disciples had attained great supernormal knowledges (mahābhiññappattā hi te sāvakā).

However, later, on the island of Tambapaṇṇi, an elder named Māleyyadeva recited this sutta below the Lohapāsāda. At that time too, sixty monks attained arahatship. Just as at the Lohapāsāda, so too the elder recited this sutta in the great pavilion. Having departed from the Mahāvihāra, he went to Cetiyapabbata, and there too he recited it. Then at Sākiyavaṃsavihāra, Kūṭālīvihāra, Antarasobbha, Muttaṅgaṇa, Vātakapabbata, Pācinagharaka, Dīghavāpiya, Lokandara, Nomaṇḍalatala, he recited it. In those places too, sixty monks, sixty monks attained arahatship. Then, having departed, the elder went to Cittalapabbata.

At that time, in the Cittalapabbata Vihāra, there was a great elder who was more than sixty years old. In the pokkharaṇi, there was a secluded place called Kuruvakatittha. The elder, thinking "I will bathe," descended there. Devatthera went to his presence and said, "May I bathe you, venerable sir (nhāpemi, bhante)?" The elder, just by way of cordiality, knowing, "They say there is one named Māleyyadeva, this will be him," said, "Are you a deva (tvaṃ deva)?" "Yes, venerable sir (āma, bhante)," [he replied]. "For sixty years, friend (āvuso), no one has been able to touch my body with their hand, but you, bathe me," he replied, and sat down on the bank.

The elder, having done all the services such as hand and foot massage, bathed the great elder. That day was the day for hearing the Dhamma. Then the great elder said, "Deva, it is fitting to give us the gift of the Dhamma (dhammadānaṃ dātuṃ vaṭṭatī)." The elder agreed, "Good, venerable sir (sādhu, bhante)." When the sun had set, they announced the hearing of the Dhamma. Sixty great elders, who had lived beyond sixty years, came for the purpose of hearing the Dhamma. Devatthera began this sutta at the end of the recitation, and at the conclusion of the sutta, sixty great elders attained arahatship. Then, having gone to Tissamahāvihāra, he recited it; at that place too, sixty elders [attained arahatship]. Then at Nāgamahāvihāra in Kāḷakacchagāma, he recited it; at that place too, sixty elders [attained arahatship]. Then, having gone to Kalyāṇi, there he recited it in the lower palace on the fourteenth; at that place too, sixty elders [attained arahatship]. On the uposatha day, he recited it in the upper palace; at that place too, sixty elders [attained arahatship]. Thus, while Devatthera was reciting this sutta, in sixty places, sixty people, sixty people attained arahatship.

However, at Ambilakāḷakavihāra, when the Tipiṭaka Cūḷanāgatthera was reciting this sutta, the assembly of humans was a gāvuta, the assembly of devas was a yojana. At the conclusion of the sutta, a thousand monks attained arahatship; but among the devas, from here and there, only one ordinary person (puthujjano) remained.

The commentary on the Chachakkasutta is finished, from the Papañcasūdanī, the commentary to the Majjhimanikāya.
