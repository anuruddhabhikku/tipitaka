<p n="229" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">229</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0284"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0185"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati aññataraṃ purisaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘ehi tvaṃ, ambho purisa, yenāyasmā anuruddho tenupasaṅkama; upasaṅkamitvā mama vacanena āyasmato anuruddhassa <pb ed="P" n="3.0145"/> pāde sirasā vandāhi <note>vandāhi, evañca vadehi (sī. pī.)</note> – ‘pañcakaṅgo, bhante, thapati āyasmato anuruddhassa pāde sirasā vandatī’ti; evañca vadehi <note>evañca vadeti (sī. pī.)</note> – ‘adhivāsetu kira, bhante, āyasmā anuruddho pañcakaṅgassa thapatissa svātanāya attacatuttho bhattaṃ; yena ca kira, bhante, āyasmā anuruddho pagevataraṃ āgaccheyya; pañcakaṅgo, bhante, thapati <note>pañcakaṅgo thapati (sī. pī.)</note> bahukicco bahukaraṇīyo rājakaraṇīyenā’’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho so puriso pañcakaṅgassa thapatissa paṭissutvā yenāyasmā anuruddho tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho so puriso āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘pañcakaṅgo, bhante, thapati āyasmato anuruddhassa pāde sirasā vandati, evañca vadeti – ‘adhivāsetu kira, bhante, āyasmā anuruddho pañcakaṅgassa thapatissa svātanāya attacatuttho bhattaṃ; yena ca kira, bhante, āyasmā anuruddho pagevataraṃ āgaccheyya; pañcakaṅgo, bhante, thapati bahukicco bahukaraṇīyo rājakaraṇīyenā’’’ti. Adhivāsesi kho <pb ed="T" n="3.0285"/> āyasmā anuruddho tuṇhībhāvena.

Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthi in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park.

Then the carpenter Pañcakanga addressed a certain man thus: “Come, good man, go to the venerable Anuruddha, pay homage in my name with your head at his feet, and say: ‘Venerable sir, the carpenter Pañcakanga pays homage with his head at the venerable Anuruddha’s feet’; and say: ‘Venerable sir, let the venerable Anuruddha with three others consent to accept tomorrow’s meal from the carpenter Pañcakanga; and let the venerable Anuruddha arrive punctually as the carpenter Pañcakanga is very busy and has much to do on royal business.’”

“Yes, sir,” that man replied, and he went to the venerable Anuruddha. After paying homage to the venerable Anuruddha, he sat down at one side.

Seated at one side, that man said to the venerable Anuruddha: “Venerable sir, the carpenter Pañcakanga pays homage with his head at the venerable Anuruddha’s feet, and says: ‘Venerable sir, let the venerable Anuruddha with three others consent to accept tomorrow’s meal from the carpenter Pañcakanga; and let the venerable Anuruddha arrive punctually as the carpenter Pañcakanga is very busy and has much to do on royal business.’”

The venerable Anuruddha consented in silence.

Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ paṇītena khādanīyena bhojanīyena sahatthā santappesi sampavāresi. Atha kho pañcakaṅgo thapati āyasmantaṃ anuruddhaṃ bhuttāviṃ onītapattapāṇiṃ aññataraṃ nīcaṃ āsanaṃ gahetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Idha maṃ, bhante, therā bhikkhū upasaṅkamitvā evamāhaṃsu – ‘appamāṇaṃ, gahapati, cetovimuttiṃ <pb ed="V" n="3.0186"/> bhāvehī’ti <note>appamāṇā gahapati cetovimutti bhāvetabbāti (ka.)</note>. Ekacce therā evamāhaṃsu – ‘mahaggataṃ, gahapati, cetovimuttiṃ bhāvehī’ti. Yā cāyaṃ, bhante, appamāṇā cetovimutti yā ca mahaggatā cetovimutti – ime dhammā nānatthā ceva nānābyañjanā <pb ed="P" n="3.0146"/> ca, udāhu ekatthā byañjanameva nāna’’nti? ‘‘Tena hi, gahapati, taṃ yevettha paṭibhātu. Apaṇṇakante ito bhavissatī’’ti. ‘‘Mayhaṃ kho, bhante, evaṃ hoti – ‘yā cāyaṃ appamāṇā cetovimutti yā ca mahaggatā cetovimutti ime dhammā ekatthā byañjanameva nāna’’’nti. ‘‘Yā cāyaṃ, gahapati, appamāṇā cetovimutti yā ca mahaggatā cetovimutti ime dhammā nānatthā ceva nānābyañjanā ca <pb ed="T" n="3.0286"/>. Tadamināpetaṃ, gahapati, pariyāyena veditabbaṃ yathā ime dhammā nānatthā ceva nānābyañjanā ca’’.

<pb ed="V" n="3.0186"/> <note>appamāṇā gahapati cetovimutti bhāvetabbāti (ka.)</note> <pb ed="P" n="3.0146"/> <pb ed="T" n="3.0286"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katamā ca, gahapati, appamāṇā cetovimutti? Idha, gahapati, bhikkhu mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ tathā tatiyaṃ tathā catutthaṃ; iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharati. Karuṇāsahagatena cetasā… muditāsahagatena cetasā… upekkhāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ tathā tatiyaṃ tathā catutthaṃ; iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ upekkhāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharati. Ayaṃ vuccati, gahapati, appamāṇā cetovimutti.

Then, venerable Anuruddha, when the night had ended, in the morning, having dressed, taking his bowl and robe, went to the dwelling of the carpenter Pañcakaṅga; having approached, he sat down on the seat that was prepared.

Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga, with his own hands, served and satisfied the venerable Anuruddha with delicious food, both hard and soft.

Then the carpenter Pañcakaṅga, when the venerable Anuruddha had finished eating and had removed his hand from his bowl, taking a low seat, sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, the carpenter Pañcakaṅga said to the venerable Anuruddha:

“Here, venerable sir, elder monks came to me and said: ‘Householder, develop the immeasurable deliverance of mind’."

Some elders said thus: ‘Householder, develop the exalted deliverance of mind.’

“Venerable sir, the immeasurable deliverance of mind and the exalted deliverance of mind – are these states different in meaning and different in expression, or are they the same in meaning and different only in expression?”

“Then, householder, let whatever occurs to you occur right here.

Afterwards it will be cleared up for you.”

“Venerable sir, it occurs to me thus: ‘The immeasurable deliverance of mind and the exalted deliverance of mind, these states are the same in meaning and different only in expression.’”

“Householder, the immeasurable deliverance of mind and the exalted deliverance of mind - these states are different in meaning and different in expression.

And it should be understood in this way, householder, how these states are different in meaning and different in expression.”

“And what, householder, is the immeasurable deliverance of mind? Here, householder, a bhikkhu abides pervading one direction with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettā), likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth; thus above, below, across, everywhere, identifying himself with all, he abides pervading the entire world with a mind imbued with loving-kindness, vast, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, and without ill-will.

…with a mind imbued with compassion (karuṇā)… with a mind imbued with sympathetic joy (muditā)… with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhā), he abides pervading one direction, likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth; thus above, below, across, everywhere, identifying himself with all, he abides pervading the entire world with a mind imbued with equanimity, vast, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility, and without ill-will.

This, householder, is called the immeasurable deliverance of mind (appamāṇā cetovimutti).

231. “And what, householder (gahapati), is the exalted (mahaggata) deliverance of mind (cetovimutti)? Here, a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the root of one tree (ekaṃ rukkhamūlaṃ), pervading it as exalted (mahaggatanti pharitvā adhimuccitvā viharati). This is called the exalted deliverance of mind.

Here again, householder, a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the roots of two or three trees (dve vā tīṇi vā rukkhamūlāni), pervading it as exalted. This too is called the exalted deliverance of mind.

Here again, householder, a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one village (ekaṃ gāmakkhettaṃ), pervading it as exalted. This too is called the exalted deliverance of mind.

Here again, householder, a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three villages (dve vā tīṇi vā gāmakkhettāni), pervading it as exalted. This too is called the exalted deliverance of mind.

Here again, householder, a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one major kingdom (ekaṃ mahārajjaṃ), pervading it as exalted. This too is called the exalted deliverance of mind.

Here again, householder, a bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three major kingdoms (dve vā tīṇi vā mahārajjāni), pervading it as exalted. This too is called the exalted deliverance of mind.

Here again, householder, a bhikkhu abides resolved upon the earth as far as the sea extends (samuddapariyantaṃ pathaviṃ), pervading it as exalted. This too is called the exalted deliverance of mind.

It is in this way, householder, that it can be understood how these states are different in meaning (nānā atthā) and different in name (nānā byañjanā)."

232. “There are, householder, these four kinds of reappearance in a future state of being. What are the four? Here, householder, someone abides resolved upon and pervading ‘limited radiance’ (parittābhā). On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Limited Radiance (parittābhānaṃ devānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjati). Here, householder, someone abides resolved upon and pervading ‘immeasurable radiance’ (appamāṇābhā). On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Immeasurable Radiance (appamāṇābhānaṃ devānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjati). Here, householder, someone abides resolved upon and pervading ‘defiled radiance’ (saṃkiliṭṭhābhā). On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Defiled Radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhānaṃ devānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjati). Here, householder, someone abides resolved upon and pervading ‘pure radiance’ (parisuddhābhā). On the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Pure Radiance (parisuddhābhānaṃ devānaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjati). These, householder, are the four kinds of reappearance in a future state of being.

“There is an occasion, householder, when those deities assemble in one place. When they have assembled in one place, a difference in their colour can be discerned but no difference in their radiance. Just as, if a man were to bring several oil-lamps into a house, a difference in the flames of the lamps might be discerned but no difference in their radiance; so too, householder, there is an occasion when those deities assemble in one place; when they have assembled in one place, a difference in their colour can be discerned but no difference in their radiance.

“There is an occasion, householder, when those deities disperse from there. When they have dispersed, a difference in their colours can be discerned and also a difference in their radiance. Just as, if the man were to remove those several oil-lamps from that house, a difference might be discerned in the flames of the lamps and also a difference in their radiance; so too, householder, there is an occasion when those deities disperse from there; when they have dispersed, a difference in their colours can be discerned and also a difference in their radiance.

“It does not occur to those deities: ‘This life of ours is permanent, everlasting, and eternal; yet wherever those deities settle down, there they find delight. Just as, when flies are being carried along on a carrying-pole or on a basket, it does not occur to them: ‘This life of ours is permanent, everlasting, or eternal; yet wherever those flies settle down, there they find delight; so too, it does not occur to those deities…yet wherever they settle down, there they find delight.”

233.

Thus spoken, the venerable Sabhiya Kaccāna (Kaccāyana [in Sī.]) said this to the venerable Anuruddha: "Well said, venerable Anuruddha! But I have a further question to ask. Are all those radiant ones deities of Limited Radiance (parittābhā), or are some of them deities of Immeasurable Radiance (appamāṇābhā)?"

"Indeed, friend Kaccāna, some deities there are of Limited Radiance, and some deities there are of Immeasurable Radiance."

"Venerable Anuruddha, what is the cause and reason why among those deities who have reappeared in a single order of gods, some are deities of Limited Radiance, and some deities of Immeasurable Radiance?"

"Then, friend Kaccāna, I will ask you about that very matter. Answer as you see fit. What do you think, friend Kaccāna, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the root of one tree, pervading it as mahaggata, and another bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the roots of two or three trees, pervading it as mahaggata - which of these two types of mental development is more mahaggata?"

"Venerable sir, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the roots of two or three trees, pervading it as mahaggata – this one is more mahaggata of these two types of mental development."

"What do you think, friend Kaccāna, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the roots of two or three trees, pervading it as mahaggata, and another bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one village, pervading it as mahaggata - which of these two types of mental development is more mahaggata?"

"Venerable sir, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one village, pervading it as mahaggata – this one is more mahaggata of these two types of mental development."

"What do you think, friend Kaccāna, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one village, pervading it as mahaggata, and another bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three villages, pervading it as mahaggata - which of these two types of mental development is more mahaggata?"

"Venerable sir, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three villages, pervading it as mahaggata – this one is more mahaggata of these two types of mental development."

"What do you think, friend Kaccāna, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three villages, pervading it as mahaggata, and another bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one major kingdom (mahārajja), pervading it as mahaggata - which of these two types of mental development is more mahaggata?"

"Venerable sir, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one major kingdom (mahārajja), pervading it as mahaggata – this one is more mahaggata of these two types of mental development."

"What do you think, friend Kaccāna, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of one major kingdom (mahārajja), pervading it as mahaggata, and another bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three major kingdoms (mahārajja), pervading it as mahaggata - which of these two types of mental development is more mahaggata?"

"Venerable sir, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three major kingdoms (mahārajja), pervading it as mahaggata – this one is more mahaggata of these two types of mental development."

"What do you think, friend Kaccāna, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of two or three major kingdoms (mahārajja), pervading it as mahaggata, and another bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the earth bounded by the ocean (samuddapariyantaṃ pathaviṃ), pervading it as mahaggata - which of these two types of mental development is more mahaggata?"

"Venerable sir, when one bhikkhu abides resolved upon an area the size of the earth bounded by the ocean (samuddapariyantaṃ pathaviṃ), pervading it as mahaggata – this one is more mahaggata of these two types of mental development."

"That, friend Kaccāna, is the cause, that is the reason, why among those deities who have reappeared in a single order of gods, some are deities of Limited Radiance (parittābhā), and some deities are of Immeasurable Radiance (appamāṇābhā)."

234. “Excellent, venerable Anuruddha! But I have a further question to ask here. Are all those radiant ones deities of Defiled Radiance, or are some of them deities of Pure Radiance?”

“By reason of the factor responsible for rebirth, friend Kaccana, some are deities of Defiled Radiance, some deities of Pure Radiance.”

“Venerable Anuruddha, what is the cause and reason why among those deities that have reappeared in a single order of gods, some are deities of Defiled Radiance, some deities of Pure Radiance?”

“As to that, friend Kaccana, I shall give you a simile, for some wise men understand the meaning of a statement by means of a simile. Suppose an oil-lamp is burning with impure oil and an impure wick; because of the impurity of its oil and its wick it burns dimly. So too, here a bhikkhu abides resolved upon and pervading an area with a defiled radiance (saṃkiliṭṭhābhā). His bodily inertia has not fully subsided, his sloth and torpor have not been fully eliminated, his restlessness and remorse have not been fully removed; because of this he meditates, as it were, dimly. With the breakup of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Defiled Radiance.

“Suppose an oil-lamp is burning with pure oil and a pure wick; because of the purity of its oil and its wick it does not burn dimly. So too, here a bhikkhu abides resolved upon and pervading an area with a pure radiance (parisuddhābhā). His bodily inertia has fully subsided, his sloth and torpor have been fully eliminated, his restlessness and remorse have been fully removed; because of this he does not meditate dimly. With the breakup of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of the gods of Pure Radiance.

“This is the cause and reason, friend Kaccana, why among those deities that have reappeared in the same order of gods, some are deities of Defiled Radiance, some deities of Pure Radiance.”

Thus it was said: The venerable Sābhiya Kaccāna said this to the venerable Anuruddha: “Excellent, venerable Anuruddha! Venerable Anuruddha does not say: ‘Thus have I heard,’ or ‘Thus it should be.’ But rather, venerable Anuruddha speaks thus: ‘These deities are such, and those deities are such.’ It occurs to me, venerable sir, that the venerable Anuruddha certainly has associated with those deities, talked with them, and held conversations with them.”

“Certainly, friend Kaccāna, your words are offensive and discourteous, but still I will answer you. For a long time, friend Kaccāna, I have associated with those deities, talked with them, and held conversations with them.”

Thus it was said: The venerable Sābhiya Kaccāna said this to the carpenter Pañcakaṅga: “It is a gain for you, householder, a great gain for you, that you have abandoned your state of doubt and have had the opportunity to hear this discourse on the Dharma.”

The Anuruddha Sutta, the seventh, is finished.
