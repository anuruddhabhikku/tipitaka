473. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘yebhuyyena, bhikkhave, sattā evaṃkāmā evaṃchandā evaṃadhippāyā – ‘aho vata aniṭṭhā akantā amanāpā dhammā parihāyeyyuṃ, iṭṭhā kantā manāpā dhammā abhivaḍḍheyyu’nti. Tesaṃ, bhikkhave, sattānaṃ evaṃkāmānaṃ evaṃchandānaṃ evaṃadhippāyānaṃ aniṭṭhā akantā amanāpā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti, iṭṭhā kantā manāpā dhammā parihāyanti. Tatra tumhe, bhikkhave, kaṃ hetuṃ paccethā’’ti? ‘‘Bhagavaṃmūlakā no, bhante, dhammā, bhagavaṃnettikā, bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā. Sādhu vata, bhante, bhagavantaññeva paṭibhātu etassa bhāsitassa attho; bhagavato sutvā bhikkhū dhāressantī’’ti. ‘‘Tena hi, bhikkhave, suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha, bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. 473. Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Śrāvastī (sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: “Monks (bhikkhavo)!”

“Venerable sir (bhadante),” those monks (bhikkhū) replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this: “Bhikkhus (bhikkhave), for the most part beings (sattā) have this wish (evaṃkāmā), desire (evaṃchandā), and longing (evaṃadhippāyā): ‘If only unwished for (aniṭṭhā), undesired (akantā), disagreeable things (amanāpā dhammā) would diminish (parihāyeyyuṃ), and wished for (iṭṭhā), desired (kantā), agreeable things (manāpā dhammā) would increase (abhivaḍḍheyyuṃ’nti)!’

Yet although beings (sattānaṃ), bhikkhus (bhikkhave), have this wish (evaṃkāmānaṃ), desire (evaṃchandānaṃ), and longing (evaṃadhippāyānaṃ), unwished for (aniṭṭhā), undesired (akantā), disagreeable things (amanāpā dhammā) increase (abhivaḍḍhanti) for them, and wished for (iṭṭhā), desired (kantā), agreeable things (manāpā dhammā) diminish (parihāyanti).

Now, bhikkhus (bhikkhave), what do you think is the reason (hetuṃ) for that?”

“Venerable sir (bhante), our teachings (dhammā) are rooted in the Blessed One (bhagavaṃmūlakā), guided by the Blessed One (bhagavaṃnettikā), have the Blessed One as their resort (bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā). It would be good (sādhu vata), venerable sir (bhante), if the Blessed One (bhagavantaññeva) would explain the meaning (attho) of these words (bhāsitassa). Having heard it from the Blessed One (bhagavato sutvā), the bhikkhus (bhikkhū) will remember it (dhāressantī’ti).”

“Then listen (suṇātha), bhikkhus (bhikkhave), and attend carefully (sādhukaṃ manasi karotha). I shall speak (bhāsissāmī’ti).”

“As you say, lord (evaṃ, bhante),” those monks (bhikkhū) responded to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this:
473.Evaṃme suttanti mahādhammasamādānasuttaṃ. Tattha evaṃkāmāti evaṃicchā. Evaṃchandāti evaṃajjhāsayā. Evaṃadhippāyāti evaṃladdhikā. Tatrāti tasmiṃ aniṭṭhavaḍḍhane ceva iṭṭhaparihāne ca. Bhagavaṃmūlakāti bhagavā mūlaṃ etesanti bhagavaṃmūlakā. Idaṃ vuttaṃ hoti – ime, bhante, amhākaṃ dhammā pubbe kassapasammāsambuddhena uppāditā, tasmiṃ parinibbute ekaṃ buddhantaraṃ añño samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā ime dhamme uppādetuṃ samattho nāma nāhosi, bhagavatā pana no ime dhammā uppāditā. Bhagavantañhi nissāya mayaṃ ime dhamme ājānāma paṭivijjhāmāti evaṃ bhagavaṃmūlakā no, bhante, dhammāti. Bhagavaṃnettikāti bhagavā hi dhammānaṃ netā vinetā anunetāti. Yathāsabhāvato pāṭiyekkaṃ pāṭiyekkaṃ nāmaṃ gahetvā dassitā dhammā bhagavaṃnettikā nāma honti. Bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇāti catubhūmakā dhammā sabbaññutaññāṇassa āpāthaṃ āgacchamānā bhagavati paṭisaranti nāmāti bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā. Paṭisarantīti osaranti samosaranti. Apica mahābodhimaṇḍe nisinnassa bhagavato paṭivedhavasena phasso āgacchati, ahaṃ bhagavā kinnāmoti? Tvaṃ phusanaṭṭhena phasso nāma. Vedanā, saññā, saṅkhārā, viññāṇaṃ āgacchati. Ahaṃ bhagavā kinnāmanti? Tvaṃ vijānanaṭṭhena viññāṇaṃ nāmāti evaṃ catubhūmakadhammānaṃ yathāsabhāvato pāṭiyekkaṃ pāṭiyekkaṃ nāmaṃ gaṇhanto bhagavā dhamme paṭisaratītipi bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā. Bhagavantaññeva paṭibhātūti bhagavatoyeva etassa bhāsitassa attho upaṭṭhātu, tumheyeva no kathetvā dethāti attho.
473.
Thus, "my sutta," means the Mahādhammasamādānasutta.

There, evaṃkāmā means evaṃicchā (having such a desire).

Evaṃchandā means evaṃajjhāsayā (having such an aspiration).

Evaṃadhippāyā means evaṃladdhikā (having such an aim).

Tatrā means in that un-wished increase and in the wished decrease.

Bhagavaṃmūlakā means the Blessed One is the root of these, thus bhagavaṃmūlakā.

This was said - These, venerable sir, are the dharmas produced by the former Kassapasammāsambuddha. When he had completely passed away, for one Buddha-interval, no other samaṇa or brāhmaṇa was able to produce these dharmas. But these dharmas were produced for us by the Blessed One.

Because we understand and penetrate these dharmas relying on the Blessed One, thus we are bhagavaṃmūlakā, venerable sir, regarding the dharmas.

Bhagavaṃnettikā means the Blessed One is the leader, trainer, and subduer of the dharmas.

According to their own nature, taking each and every name, the dharmas shown are called bhagavaṃnettikā.

Bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā means the four-level dharmas, coming into the range of the knowledge of omniscience, resort to the Blessed One, thus bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā.

Paṭisarantīti means they approach, they completely approach.

Moreover, for the Blessed One seated at the great bodhimaṇḍa, contact (phassa) arises by way of penetration: "What am I called, Blessed One?" "You are called contact (phassa) by way of the function of touching."

Feeling (vedanā), perception (saññā), volitional formations (saṅkhārā), consciousness (viññāṇa) arise.

"What am I called, Blessed One?" "You are called consciousness (viññāṇa) by way of the function of cognizing." Thus, the Blessed One, taking each and every name of the four-level dharmas according to their own nature, resorts to the dharmas; therefore, also bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā.

Bhagavantaññeva paṭibhātū means may the meaning of this speech occur to the Blessed One alone; the meaning is, "May you alone explain and give it to us."

474. ‘‘Idha, bhikkhave, assutavā puthujjano, ariyānaṃ adassāvī ariyadhammassa akovido ariyadhamme avinīto, sappurisānaṃ adassāvī sappurisadhammassa akovido sappurisadhamme avinīto, sevitabbe dhamme na jānāti asevitabbe dhamme na jānāti, bhajitabbe dhamme na jānāti abhajitabbe dhamme na jānāti. So sevitabbe dhamme ajānanto asevitabbe dhamme ajānanto, bhajitabbe dhamme ajānanto abhajitabbe dhamme ajānanto, asevitabbe dhamme sevati sevitabbe dhamme na sevati, abhajitabbe dhamme bhajati bhajitabbe dhamme na bhajati. Tassa asevitabbe dhamme sevato sevitabbe dhamme asevato, abhajitabbe dhamme bhajato bhajitabbe dhamme abhajato aniṭṭhā akantā amanāpā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti, iṭṭhā kantā manāpā dhammā parihāyanti. Taṃ kissa hetu? Evañhetaṃ, bhikkhave, hoti yathā taṃ aviddasuno. ‘‘Sutavā ca kho, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako, ariyānaṃ dassāvī ariyadhammassa kovido ariyadhamme suvinīto, sappurisānaṃ dassāvī sappurisadhammassa kovido sappurisadhamme suvinīto, sevitabbe dhamme jānāti asevitabbe dhamme jānāti, bhajitabbe dhamme jānāti abhajitabbe dhamme jānāti. So sevitabbe dhamme jānanto asevitabbe dhamme jānanto, bhajitabbe dhamme jānanto abhajitabbe dhamme jānanto, asevitabbe dhamme na sevati sevitabbe dhamme sevati, abhajitabbe dhamme na bhajati bhajitabbe dhamme bhajati. Tassa asevitabbe dhamme asevato sevitabbe dhamme sevato, abhajitabbe dhamme abhajato bhajitabbe dhamme bhajato, aniṭṭhā akantā amanāpā dhammā parihāyanti, iṭṭhā kantā manāpā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti. Taṃ kissa hetu? Evañhetaṃ, bhikkhave, hoti yathā taṃ viddasuno.
Here, bhikkhus, an untaught ordinary person who has no regard for noble ones (ariyas) and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, who has no regard for true men and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, does not know what things should be cultivated and what things should not be cultivated, he does not know what things should be followed and what things should not be followed.

Not knowing this, he cultivates things that should not be cultivated and does not cultivate things that should be cultivated, he follows things that should not be followed and does not follow things that should be followed.

It is because he does this that unwished for, undesired, disagreeable things increase for him and wished for, desired, agreeable things diminish.

What is the reason? That is what happens to one who does not see.

The well-taught noble disciple (ariyasāvaka) who has regard for noble ones (ariyas) and is skilled and disciplined in their Dhamma, who has regard for true men and is skilled and disciplined in their Dhamma, knows what things should be cultivated and what things should not be cultivated, he knows what things should be followed and what things should not be followed.

Knowing this, he cultivates things that should be cultivated and does not cultivate things that should not be cultivated, he follows things that should be followed and does not follow things that should not be followed.

It is because he does this that unwished for, undesired, disagreeable things diminish for him and wished for, desired, agreeable things increase.

What is the reason? That is what happens to one who sees.

475. ‘‘Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānāni. Katamāni cattāri? Atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannadukkhañceva āyatiñca dukkhavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannasukhaṃ āyatiṃ dukkhavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannadukkhaṃ āyatiṃ sukhavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannasukhañceva āyatiñca sukhavipākaṃ.
“Monks, there are these four ways of undertaking things ( dhammasamādānāni).

Which four? There is a way of undertaking things that is presently painful and ripens in the future as pain ( paccuppannadukkhañceva āyatiñca dukkhavipākaṃ). There is a way of undertaking things that is presently pleasant and ripens in the future as pain ( paccuppannasukhaṃ āyatiṃ dukkhavipākaṃ). There is a way of undertaking things that is presently painful and ripens in the future as pleasure ( paccuppannadukkhaṃ āyatiṃ sukhavipākaṃ). There is a way of undertaking things that is presently pleasant and ripens in the future as pleasure ( paccuppannasukhañceva āyatiñca sukhavipākaṃ)."
475.Atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānanti purimasutte uppaṭipāṭiākārena mātikā ṭhapitā , idha pana yathādhammaraseneva satthā mātikaṃ ṭhapesi. Tattha dhammasamādānanti pāṇātipātādīnaṃ dhammānaṃ gahaṇaṃ.

atthi, bhikkhave, dhammasamādānanti, in the previous sūtra, the outline (mātikā) was established in reverse order. Here, however, the Teacher established the outline according to the flavor of the Dharma itself.
There, dhammasamādānanti means the acceptance (grahaṇaṃ) of dharmas such as taking life (pāṇātipāta) and so forth.

476. “Now, bhikkhus, one who is ignorant, not knowing this way of undertaking things that is painful now and ripens in the future as pain ( duḥkhavipākaṃ), does not understand it as it actually is thus: ‘This way of undertaking things is painful now and ripens in the future as pain.’

478. What, bhikkhus, is the way of undertaking things that is painful now and ripens in the future as pain? Here, bhikkhus, someone in pain and grief kills living beings (pāṇātipātī), and he experiences pain and grief that have killing of living beings as condition. This is called the way of undertaking things (dhammasamādānaṃ) that is painful now (paccuppannadukkhañceva) and ripens in the future as pain (āyatiñca dukkhavipākaṃ).

Here, "accompanied by suffering" means that these three—wrong conduct (micchācāra), covetousness (abhijjhā), and wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi)—cause painful sensations due to the two kinds of intention: prior intention and subsequent intention.

Furthermore, conclusive intention (sanniṭṭhāpakacetanā) is associated with either pleasure (sukhasampayuttā) or equanimity (upekkhāsampayuttā).

The remaining seven, starting with killing living beings (pāṇātipātādayo), also cause painful sensations due to these three intentions.

This was stated in reference to "accompanied by suffering, accompanied by sadness" (sahāpi dukkhena sahāpi domanassenā).

Here, it should be understood that sadness (domanassa) itself is suffering (dukkha).

Even physical suffering (kāyikaṃ dukkhaṃ) befalls one who undertakes a search (pariyeṭṭhiṃ), in the prior and subsequent stages.

“Suppose, monks, there were a bronze cup of beverage possessing a good colour (varṇa), smell (gandha), and taste (rasa).

But it was mixed with poison (viṣa).

A man would come along wanting to live (jīvitukāma), not wanting to die (amaritukāma), desiring happiness (sukhakāma) and averse to suffering (duḥkhappaṭikūla).

And they told him: ‘Good man (ambho purisa), this bronze cup of beverage possesses a good colour (varṇa), smell (gandha), and taste (rasa).

But it is mixed with poison (viṣa). Drink from it if you want;

as you drink from it, its colour (varṇa), smell (gandha), and taste (rasa) will agree with you, but after drinking from it, you will come to death (maraṇa) or deadly suffering (maraṇamattaṃ dukkha).’

Then he drank from it without reflecting (appaṭisaṅkhāya), and did not relinquish it.

As he drank from it, its colour (varṇa), smell (gandha), and taste (rasa) agreed with him, but after drinking from it, he came to death (maraṇa) or deadly suffering (maraṇamattaṃ dukkha).

Similar to that, I say, monks, is this way of undertaking things (dhammasamādāna) that is pleasant now (paccuppannasukha) and ripens in the future as pain (āyatiṃ dukkhavipākaṃ).
483.Āpānīyakaṃsoti āpānīyassa madhurapānakassa bharitakaṃso. Vaṇṇasampannoti pānakavaṇṇādīhi sampannavaṇṇo, kaṃse pakkhittapānakavasena pānakakaṃsopi evaṃ vutto. Chādessatīti tañhi halāhalavisaṃ yattha yattha pakkhittaṃ hoti, tassa tasseva rasaṃ deti. Tena vuttaṃ ‘‘chādessatī’’ti.
483. āpānīyakaṃso means a bharitakaṃsa (filled vessel) of sweet drink, āpānīya.

vaṇṇasampanno means having an excellent color due to the color of the drink, etc.; because of the drink placed in the vessel, the drink-vessel is also spoken of thus.

chādessatī -- just as the halāhalavisaṃ (deadly poison), wherever it is placed, gives its own taste, therefore it is said "chādessatī."

484. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, pūtimuttaṃ nānābhesajjehi saṃsaṭṭhaṃ. Atha puriso āgaccheyya paṇḍukarogī. Tamenaṃ evaṃ vadeyyuṃ – ‘ambho purisa, idaṃ pūtimuttaṃ nānābhesajjehi saṃsaṭṭhaṃ, sace ākaṅkhasi piva. Tassa te pivatohi kho nacchādessati vaṇṇenapi gandhenapi rasenapi, pivitvā ca pana sukhī bhavissasī’ti. So taṃ paṭisaṅkhāya piveyya, nappaṭinissajjeyya. Tassa taṃ pivatohi kho nacchādeyya vaṇṇenapi gandhenapi rasenapi, pivitvā ca pana sukhī assa. Tathūpamāhaṃ, bhikkhave, imaṃ dhammasamādānaṃ vadāmi, yamidaṃ dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannadukkhaṃ āyatiṃ sukhavipākaṃ.
484. Suppose, bhikkhus, there was fermented urine (pūtimuttaṃ) mixed with various medicines (nānābhesajjehi saṃsaṭṭhaṃ).

Then a man came sick with jaundice (paṇḍukarogī).

They might say to him, 'Good man, this fermented urine (pūtimuttaṃ) is mixed with various medicines (nānābhesajjehi saṃsaṭṭhaṃ). Drink from it if you want.

As you drink from it, its color, smell (gandhena), and taste (rasena) will not agree with you, but after drinking from it, you will be well.'

Then he drank from it after reflecting (paṭisaṅkhāya), and did not relinquish it.

As he drank from it, its color, smell (gandhena), and taste (rasena) did not agree with him, but after drinking from it, he became well.

Similarly, bhikkhus, I speak of this undertaking of things (dhammasamādānaṃ) that is painful now and ripens in the future as pleasure (paccuppannadukkhaṃ āyatiṃ sukhavipākaṃ).
484.Pūtimuttanti muttameva. Yathā hi manussabhāvo suvaṇṇavaṇṇo pūtikāyotveva, tadahujātāpi galocilatā pūtilatātveva vuccati. Evaṃ taṅkhaṇaṃ gahitaṃ taruṇampi muttaṃ pūtimuttameva. Nānābhesajjehīti harītakāmalakādīhi nānosadhehi. Sukhī assāti arogo suvaṇṇavaṇṇo sukhī bhaveyya.
484.
pūtimuttanti muttameva.
Just as the human state (manussabhāvo) [can be] golden-colored (suvaṇṇavaṇṇo) even as a putrid body (pūtikāyotveva), so too galocilatā, though born from that, is called pūtilatātveva.
Thus, that which is taken at that moment, even fresh (taruṇampi) muttam, is truly pūtimuttameva.
nānābhesajjehīti with various medicines, such as harītakāmalakādīhi, with various remedies (nānosadhehi).
sukhī assāti may [one] be happy (arogo sukhī bhaveyya), healthy and golden-colored (suvaṇṇavaṇṇo).

485. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, dadhi ca madhu ca sappi ca phāṇitañca ekajjhaṃ saṃsaṭṭhaṃ. Atha puriso āgaccheyya lohitapakkhandiko. Tamenaṃ evaṃ vadeyyuṃ – ‘ambho purisa, idaṃ dadhiṃ ca madhuṃ ca sappiṃ ca phāṇitañca ekajjhaṃ saṃsaṭṭhaṃ, sace ākaṅkhasi piva. Tassa te pivato ceva chādessati vaṇṇenapi gandhenapi rasenapi, pivitvā ca pana sukhī bhavissasī’ti. So taṃ paṭisaṅkhāya piveyya, nappaṭinissajjeyya. Tassa taṃ pivato ceva chādeyya vaṇṇenapi gandhenapi rasenapi, pivitvā ca pana sukhī assa. Tathūpamāhaṃ, bhikkhave, imaṃ dhammasamādānaṃ vadāmi, yamidaṃ dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannasukhañceva āyatiñca sukhavipākaṃ.
485. Suppose, mendicants, there were curd (dadhi), honey (madhu), ghee (sarpi), and molasses (phāṇita) mixed together.

Then a man with dysentery (lohitapakkhandiko) came.

They might say to him, ‘Good man (ambho purisa), this is curd (dadhi), honey (madhu), ghee (sarpi), and molasses (phāṇita) mixed together. Drink from it if you want.

As you drink from it, its colour, smell, and taste will agree with you, and after drinking from it you will be well.’

Then he drank from it after reflecting, and did not relinquish it.

As he drank from it, its colour, smell, and taste agreed with him, and after drinking from it, he became well.

Similar to that, I say, is this undertaking of the Dhamma (dhammasamādāna), this undertaking of the Dhamma (dhammasamādāna) which is pleasant in the present and ripens in the future as pleasure.
485.Dadhi ca madhu cāti suparisuddhaṃ dadhi ca sumadhuraṃ madhu ca. Ekajjhaṃ saṃsaṭṭhanti ekato katvā missitaṃ āluḷitaṃ. Tassa tanti tassa taṃ catumadhurabhesajjaṃ pivato rucceyya. Idañca yaṃ bhagandarasaṃsaṭṭhaṃ lohitaṃ pakkhandati, na tassa bhesajjaṃ, āhāraṃ thambhetvā maggaṃ avalañjaṃ karoti. Yaṃ pana pittasaṃsaṭṭhaṃ lohitaṃ, tassetaṃ bhesajjaṃ sītalakiriyāya pariyattabhūtaṃ.

486. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, vassānaṃ pacchime māse saradasamaye viddhe vigatavalāhake deve ādicco nabhaṃ abbhussakkamāno sabbaṃ ākāsagataṃ tamagataṃ abhivihacca bhāsate ca tapate ca virocate ca; evameva kho, bhikkhave, yamidaṃ dhammasamādānaṃ paccuppannasukhañceva āyatiñca sukhavipākaṃ tadaññe puthusamaṇabrāhmaṇaparappavāde abhivihacca bhāsate ca tapate ca virocate cā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Mahādhammasamādānasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ.
“Just as, monks, in the last month of the rainy season, in autumn, when the sky is clear and cloudless, the sun rises up into the sky, dispelling all the darkness in space, and shines, blazes, and radiates; so too, monks, this undertaking of the Dharma, which is pleasant in the present and ripens as pleasure in the future, dispels, shines, blazes, and radiates over any other doctrines of ordinary ascetics and Brahmins.”

This the Blessed One said. Elated, those monks delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Great Discourse on the Undertaking of the Dharma is finished, the sixth.
486.Viddheti ubbiddhe. Meghavigamena dūrībhūteti attho. Vigatavalāhaketi apagatameghe, deveti ākāse. Ākāsagataṃ tamagatanti ākāsagataṃ tamaṃ. Puthusamaṇabrāhmaṇaparappavādeti puthūnaṃ samaṇabrāhmaṇasaṅkhātānaṃ paresaṃ vāde. Abhivihaccāti abhihantvā. Bhāsate ca tapate ca virocate cāti saradakāle majjhanhikasamaye ādiccova obhāsaṃ muñcati tapati vijjotatīti. Idaṃ pana suttaṃ devatānaṃ ativiya piyaṃ manāpaṃ. Tatridaṃ vatthu – dakkhiṇadisāyaṃ kira hatthibhogajanapade saṅgaravihāro nāma atthi. Tassa bhojanasāladvāre saṅgararukkhe adhivatthā devatā rattibhāge ekassa daharassa sarabhaññavasena idaṃ suttaṃ osārentassa sutvā sādhukāraṃ adāsi. Daharo kiṃ esoti āha. Ahaṃ, bhante, imasmiṃ rukkhe adhivatthā devatāti. Kasmiṃ devate pasannāsi, kiṃ sadde, udāhu sutteti? Saddo nāma, bhante, yassa kassaci hotiyeva, sutte pasannāmhi. Satthārā jetavane nisīditvā kathitadivase ca ajja ca ekabyañjanepi nānaṃ natthīti. Assosi tvaṃ devate satthārā kathitadivaseti? Āma, bhante. Kattha ṭhitā assosīti? Jetavanaṃ, bhante, gatāmhi, mahesakkhāsu pana devatāsu āgacchantīsu tattha okāsaṃ alabhitvā idheva ṭhatvā assosinti. Ettha ṭhitāya sakkā sutthu saddo sotunti? Tvaṃ pana, bhante, mayhaṃ saddaṃ suṇasīti? Āma devateti. Dakkhiṇakaṇṇapasse nisīditvā kathanakālo viya, bhante, hotīti. Kiṃ pana devate satthu rūpaṃ passasīti? Satthā mameva oloketīti maññamānā saṇṭhātuṃ na sakkomi, bhanteti. Visesaṃ pana nibbattetuṃ asakkhittha devateti. Devatā tattheva antaradhāyi. Taṃ divasaṃ kiresa devaputto sotāpattiphale patiṭṭhito. Evamidaṃ suttaṃ devatānaṃ piyaṃ manāpaṃ. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Mahādhammasamādānasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.

Viddheti ubbiddhe.
The meaning is, "having become distant" through the disappearance of the clouds.
Vigatavalāhaketi, [means] apagatameghe, [namely:] in the sky (ākāse).
Ākāsagataṃ tamagatanti: ākāsagataṃ tamaṃ.
Puthusamaṇabrāhmaṇaparappavādeti: in the speech of others, who are numerous, reckoned as ascetics and Brahmins.
Abhivihaccāti: having struck.
Bhāsate ca tapate ca virocate cāti: in the autumn season, at midday, just like the sun, it releases light, burns, and shines brightly.
Now, this sūtra is exceedingly dear and pleasing to the gods (devatānaṃ).
In that regard, this is the story: in the southern direction, in the country of Hatthibhoga, there was a monastery called Saṅgara.
At the door of its refectory, a deity (devatā) dwelling in the Saṅgara tree, in the night, having heard this sūtra being recited by a certain young man in the manner of reciting the sarabhañña, gave approval.
He said, "Young man, what is this?"
"Venerable sir, I am a deity (devatā) dwelling in this tree."
"In what are you pleased, O deity (devate)? In the sound or in the sūtra?" "Sound, venerable sir, is just whatever it may be for anyone, [but] in the sūtra I am pleased."
"Venerable sir, there is no difference, even in a single consonant, between what the Teacher spoke when sitting in Jeta's Grove and what was spoken today."
"O deity (devate), did you hear what the Teacher spoke?" "Yes, venerable sir."
"Where were you standing when you heard it?" "Venerable sir, I had gone to Jeta's Grove, but since powerful deities (devatāsu) were coming, not obtaining space there, I stood right here and heard it."
"Is it possible to hear the sound well while standing here?" "Venerable sir, do you hear my sound?" "Yes, O deity (devateti)."
"It is just as if the time for speaking were when [you are] sitting beside [my] right ear, venerable sir."
"O deity (devate), do you see the Teacher's form?" "Thinking, 'The Teacher is looking right at me,' I am not able to remain still, venerable sir."
"O deity (devateti), were you not able to produce distinction?"
The deity (devatā) disappeared right there.
It is said that on that day, that divine son (devaputto) was established in the fruit of stream-entry (sotāpattiphale).
In this way, this sūtra is dear and pleasing to the gods (devatānaṃ).
The rest is clear throughout.
