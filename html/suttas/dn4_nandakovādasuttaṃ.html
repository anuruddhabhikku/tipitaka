Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Savatthi in Jeta’s Grove, Anathapindika’s Park.

Then Mahapajapati Gotami together with five hundred bhikkhunis went to the Blessed One. Having approached, she paid homage to the Blessed One and stood at one side. Standing at one side, Mahapajapati Gotami said this to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, let the Blessed One advise the bhikkhunis; let the Blessed One instruct the bhikkhunis; let the Blessed One give the bhikkhunis a talk on the Dhamma.”

Now on that occasion the elder bhikkhus were taking turns in advising the bhikkhunis. The venerable Nandaka did not want to advise the bhikkhunis in turn. Then the Blessed One addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Ānanda, whose turn is it today to advise the bhikkhunis in turn?” “Venerable sir, it has been arranged for all to advise the bhikkhunis in turn. This venerable Nandaka does not want to advise the bhikkhunis in turn.”

Then the Blessed One addressed the venerable Nandaka: “Advise the bhikkhunis, Nandaka. Instruct the bhikkhunis, Nandaka. Give the bhikkhunis a talk on the Dhamma, brahmin.” “Even so, venerable sir,” the venerable Nandaka replied to the Blessed One. Then, early in the morning, having dressed himself and taken his bowl and robe, he entered Savatthi for alms. Having wandered for alms in Savatthi and returned from his alms round after his meal, he went with a companion to the Rajaka Park. The bhikkhunis saw the venerable Nandaka coming in the distance. Having seen him, they prepared a seat and set out water for (washing) the feet. The venerable Nandaka sat down on the prepared seat. Sitting, he washed his feet. Those bhikkhunis paid homage to the venerable Nandaka and sat down at one side. Sitting at one side, the venerable Nandaka said this to those bhikkhunis: “Sisters, this talk will be in the form of questions. There, when you understand, you should say: ‘We understand’; when you do not understand, you should say: ‘We do not understand.’ Or if there is doubt or uncertainty, I myself should be asked there: ‘Venerable sir, how is this? What is the meaning of this?’” “Even with just this much, venerable sir, we are pleased and delighted with the noble Nandaka for inviting us.”

399. “Sisters, what do you think? Is the eye permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But that which is impermanent, (is) that unpleasant or pleasant?” “Unpleasant, venerable sir.”

“Is what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change, fit to be regarded thus: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“Sisters, what do you think? Is the ear permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, venerable sir…pe… Is the nose permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, venerable sir”… “Is the tongue permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, venerable sir”… “Is the body permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, venerable sir”… “Is the mind permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But that which is impermanent, (is) that unpleasant or pleasant?” “Unpleasant, venerable sir.”

“Is what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change, fit to be regarded thus: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for that?” “Because we have already seen this well with proper wisdom as it really is: ‘Thus, these six internal sense bases are impermanent.’”

“Good, good, sisters! So it is, sisters, it is for a noble disciple who sees this as it actually is with proper wisdom.”

402. Sisters, suppose an oil-lamp is burning: its oil is impermanent and subject to change, its wick is impermanent and subject to change, its flame is impermanent and subject to change, and its radiance is impermanent and subject to change.

Now would anyone be speaking rightly who spoke thus: ‘While this oil-lamp is burning, its oil, wick, and flame are impermanent and subject to change, but its radiance is permanent, everlasting, eternal, not subject to change’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for this?” “Because, venerable sir, while that oil-lamp is burning, its oil, wick, and flame are impermanent and subject to change, so its radiance must be impermanent and subject to change.”

“So too, sisters, would anyone be speaking rightly who spoke thus: ‘These six internal bases (ajjhattikā āyatanā) are impermanent and subject to change; but the pleasant (sukha), painful (dukkha), or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukha) feeling (vedanā) that one experiences in dependence upon the six internal bases is permanent, everlasting (sassataṃ), eternal, not subject to change’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for this?” “Because each feeling (vedanā) arises in dependence upon its corresponding condition (paccayaṃ paṭicca),

and with the cessation (nirodhā) of its corresponding condition, the feeling (vedanā) ceases.”

“Good, good, sisters! So it is with a noble disciple (ariyasāvakassa) who sees this as it actually is with proper wisdom (sammappaññāya passato).”

403. “Sisters, suppose a great tree is standing possessed of heartwood: its root (mūla) is impermanent (anicca) and subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ), its trunk (kandha) is impermanent and subject to change, its branches and foliage are impermanent and subject to change, and its shadow is impermanent and subject to change.

Now would anyone be speaking rightly who spoke thus: ‘The root of this great tree standing possessed of heartwood is impermanent and subject to change, its trunk is impermanent and subject to change, its branches and foliage are impermanent and subject to change, but its shadow is permanent (niccā), everlasting (dhuvā), eternal (sassatā), not subject to change (avipariṇāmadhammā)’?”

“No, indeed, Lord.”

“What is the cause of that?”

“Because, venerable sir, the root, trunk, branches, and foliage of this great tree standing possessed of heartwood are impermanent and subject to change, so its shadow must be impermanent and subject to change.”

“So too, sisters, would anyone be speaking rightly who spoke thus: ‘These six external bases are impermanent, but the pleasant (sukhaṃ), painful (dukkhaṃ), or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukhaṃ) feeling that one experiences in dependence upon the six external bases is permanent, everlasting, eternal, not subject to change’?”

Yañca kho cha ajjhattike āyatane <pb ed="T" n="3.0499"/> paṭicca paṭisaṃvedeti sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā taṃ niccaṃ dhuvaṃ sassataṃ avipariṇāmadhamma’nti; sammā nu kho so, bhaginiyo, vadamāno vadeyyā’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Taṃ kissa hetu’’? ‘‘Tajjaṃ tajjaṃ, bhante, paccayaṃ paṭicca tajjā tajjā vedanā uppajjanti. Tajjassa tajjassa paccayassa nirodhā tajjā tajjā vedanā nirujjhantī’’ti. ‘‘Sādhu sādhu, bhaginiyo! Evañhetaṃ, bhaginiyo, hoti ariyasāvakassa yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya passato’’.

“Sisters, suppose an oil-lamp is burning: its oil is impermanent and subject to change, its wick is impermanent and subject to change, its flame is impermanent and subject to change, and its radiance is impermanent and subject to change.

Now would anyone be speaking rightly who spoke thus: ‘While this oil-lamp is burning, its oil, wick, and flame are impermanent and subject to change, but its radiance is permanent, everlasting, eternal, not subject to change’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for that?”

“Because, venerable sir, while that oil-lamp is burning, its oil, wick, and flame are impermanent and subject to change, then what to say of its radiance being impermanent and changeable?”

“So too, sisters, would anyone be speaking rightly who spoke thus: ‘These six internal bases (ajjhattikā āyatanā) are impermanent and subject to change, but the pleasant (sukha), painful (dukkha), or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukhaṃ) feeling that one experiences in dependence upon the six internal bases is permanent, everlasting, eternal, not subject to change’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for that?”

“Because each feeling (vedanā) arises in dependence upon its corresponding condition (paccayaṃ), and with the cessation (nirodhā) of its corresponding condition, the feeling ceases.”

“Good, good, sisters! So it is with a noble disciple (ariyasāvakassa) who sees this as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ) with proper wisdom (sammappaññāya).”

<p n="412" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">412</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhaginiyo, mahato rukkhassa tiṭṭhato sāravato mūlampi aniccaṃ vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, khandhopi anicco vipariṇāmadhammo, sākhāpalāsampi aniccaṃ vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, chāyāpi aniccā vipariṇāmadhammā. Yañca kho bāhire āyatane paṭicca paṭisaṃvedeti sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā taṃ niccaṃ dhuvaṃ <pb ed="V" n="3.0332"/> sassataṃ avipariṇāmadhamma’nti; sammā nu kho so, bhaginiyo, vadamāno vadeyyā’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Taṃ kissa hetu’’? ‘‘Tajjaṃ tajjaṃ, bhante, paccayaṃ paṭicca tajjā tajjā vedanā uppajjanti. Tajjassa tajjassa paccayassa nirodhā tajjā tajjā vedanā nirujjhantī’’ti. ‘‘Sādhu sādhu, bhaginiyo! Evañhetaṃ, bhaginiyo, hoti ariyasāvakassa yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya passato’’.

Sisters, suppose a great tree is standing possessed of heartwood: its root is impermanent and subject to change, its trunk is impermanent and subject to change, its branches and foliage are impermanent and subject to change, and its shadow is impermanent and subject to change.

Now would anyone be speaking rightly who spoke thus: ‘The root, trunk, branches, and foliage of this great tree standing possessed of heartwood are impermanent and subject to change, but its shadow is permanent, everlasting, eternal, not subject to change’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for that?” “Because, venerable sir, the root, trunk, branches, and foliage of this great tree standing possessed of heartwood are impermanent and subject to change, so its shadow must be impermanent and subject to change.”

“Just so, sisters, would anyone be speaking rightly who spoke thus: ‘These six external sense-spheres (bāhirā āyatanā) are impermanent, but the pleasant (sukhaṃ), painful (dukkhaṃ), or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukhaṃ) feeling (vedanā) that one experiences in dependence upon the six external bases is permanent, everlasting, eternal, not subject to change’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for that?” “Because each feeling (vedanā) arises in dependence upon its corresponding condition (paccayaṃ), and with the cessation of its corresponding condition, the feeling ceases.”

“Good, good, sisters! So it is with a noble disciple (ariyasāvaka) who sees this as it actually is with proper wisdom (sammappaññāya).”

<p n="413" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">413</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhaginiyo, dakkho goghātako vā goghātakantevāsī vā gāviṃ vadhitvā tiṇhena govikantanena gāviṃ saṅkanteyya anupahacca <pb ed="M" n="3.0323"/> antaraṃ maṃsakāyaṃ anupahacca bāhiraṃ cammakāyaṃ. Yaṃ yadeva tattha antarā vilimaṃsaṃ antarā nhāru antarā bandhanaṃ taṃ tadeva tiṇhena govikantanena sañchindeyya saṅkanteyya sampakanteyya samparikanteyya. Sañchinditvā saṅkantitvā sampakantitvā samparikantitvā vidhunitvā bāhiraṃ cammakāyaṃ teneva cammena taṃ gāviṃ paṭicchādetvā evaṃ vadeyya – ‘tathevāyaṃ gāvī saṃyuttā imināva cammenā’ti; sammā nu kho so, bhaginiyo, vadamāno vadeyyā’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Taṃ kissa hetu’’? ‘‘Amu hi, bhante, dakkho goghātako vā goghātakantevāsī vā gāviṃ vadhitvā tiṇhena govikantanena gāviṃ saṅkanteyya anupahacca antaraṃ maṃsakāyaṃ anupahacca bāhiraṃ cammakāyaṃ. Yaṃ yadeva tattha antarā vilimaṃsaṃ antarā <pb ed="T" n="3.0501"/> nhāru antarā bandhanaṃ taṃ tadeva tiṇhena govikantanena sañchindeyya saṅkanteyya sampakanteyya samparikanteyya. Sañchinditvā saṅkantitvā sampakantitvā samparikantitvā vidhunitvā bāhiraṃ cammakāyaṃ teneva cammena taṃ gāviṃ paṭicchādetvā kiñcāpi so evaṃ vadeyya – ‘tathevāyaṃ gāvī saṃyuttā imināva cammenā’ti; atha kho sā gāvī visaṃyuttā teneva cammenā’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Upamā kho me ayaṃ, bhaginiyo, katā atthassa viññāpanāya ayamevettha attho. ‘Antarā maṃsakāyo’ti kho, bhaginiyo, channetaṃ ajjhattikānaṃ āyatanānaṃ adhivacanaṃ; ‘bāhiro cammakāyo’ti kho, bhaginiyo, channetaṃ bāhirānaṃ āyatanānaṃ adhivacanaṃ; ‘antarā vilimaṃsaṃ antarā nhāru antarā bandhana’nti kho, bhaginiyo, nandīrāgassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ; ‘tiṇhaṃ govikantana’nti kho, bhaginiyo, ariyāyetaṃ paññāya adhivacanaṃ; yāyaṃ ariyā paññā antarā kilesaṃ antarā saṃyojanaṃ antarā bandhanaṃ sañchindati saṅkantati sampakantati samparikantati.

“Sisters, suppose a skilled butcher or a butcher’s apprentice were to kill a cow and, with a sharp butcher’s knife, would sever the cow without harming the flesh on the inside, and without harming the hide on the outside; and whatever was right there—the flesh stuck to the inside, the sinew on the inside, the ligaments on the inside—he would cut, sever, and carve all that away with the sharp butcher’s knife; and having cut, severed, carved, and completely carved away the outer hide, he would cover the cow again with that same hide and say, ‘This cow is joined to this hide just as it was before.’ Would he be speaking rightly, sisters, if he were to say that?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for that?”

“Because, venerable sir, if that skilled butcher or his apprentice were to kill a cow and, with a sharp butcher’s knife, would sever the cow without harming the flesh on the inside, and without harming the hide on the outside; and whatever was right there—the flesh stuck to the inside, the sinew on the inside, the ligaments on the inside—he would cut, sever, and carve all that away with the sharp butcher’s knife; and having cut, severed, carved, and completely carved away the outer hide, even though he covers the cow again with that same hide and says: ‘This cow is joined to this hide just as it was before,’ that cow would still be disjoined from that hide.”

“Sisters, I have given this simile in order to convey a meaning. This is the meaning here: ‘The inner mass of flesh (antaraṃ maṃsakāya)’ is a term for the six internal sense bases (ajjhattikānaṃ āyatanānaṃ). ‘The outer hide (bāhiro cammakāyo)’ is a term for the six external sense bases (bāhirānaṃ āyatanānaṃ). ‘The inner flesh stuck [to the inside], the sinew [on the inside], and the ligaments (antarā vilimaṃsaṃ antarā nhāru antarā bandhana)’ is a term for delight and lust (nandīrāgassa). ‘The sharp butcher’s knife (tiṇhaṃ govikantana)’ is a term for noble wisdom (ariyāya paññāya)—the noble wisdom by which one cuts, severs, carves, and completely carves away the inner defilements (kilesaṃ), fetters (saṃyojanaṃ), and bonds (bandhanaṃ).”

414. “Sisters, there are these seven factors of enlightenment through the development and cultivation of which a bhikkhu, by realizing for himself with direct knowledge, here and now enters upon and abides in the deliverance of mind and deliverance by wisdom that are taintless with the destruction of the taints.

Which seven? Here, sisters, a bhikkhu develops the mindfulness enlightenment factor (satisambojjhaṅgaṃ), which is supported by seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, and ripens in relinquishment. He develops the investigation-of-states enlightenment factor…the energy enlightenment factor…the rapture enlightenment factor, the tranquility enlightenment factor, the concentration enlightenment factor (samādhisambojjhaṅgaṃ), the equanimity enlightenment factor (upekkhāsambojjhaṅgaṃ), which is supported by seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, and ripens in relinquishment.

These, sisters, are the seven factors of enlightenment through the development and cultivation of which a bhikkhu, by realizing for himself with direct knowledge, here and now enters upon and abides in the deliverance of mind and deliverance by wisdom that are taintless with the destruction of the taints.”

415. Then venerable Nandaka, having advised the bhikkhunis with this advice, sent them off, saying: “Go, sisters, it is time.”

Then the bhikkhunis, having delighted and rejoiced in the venerable Nandaka’s words, rose from their seats, and after paying homage to the venerable Nandaka, departed keeping him on their right (pradakṣiṇa). They went to the Blessed One (bhagavā), and after paying homage to him, stood at one side.

The Blessed One (bhagavā) told the bhikkhunis standing to one side: “Go, bhikkhunis, it is time.”

Then the bhikkhunis paid homage to the Blessed One (bhagavā) and departed keeping him on their right (pradakṣiṇa).

Then, soon after those bhikkhunis had left, the Blessed One (bhagavā) addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus, just as on the Uposatha day of the fifteenth there is no doubt or uncertainty in many people’s minds as to whether the moon is waning or full, but rather it is clear that the moon is full; so too, bhikkhus, those bhikkhunis are uplifted and have fulfilled their intentions through Nandaka’s teaching of the dharma.

Among those five hundred bhikkhunis, bhikkhus, the last bhikkhunī attained the state of sotāpanna (stream-enterer), being no longer subject to falling (into the lower realms) (avinipātadhamma), with a fixed destiny (niyatā) ending in Final Awakening (sambodhiparāyanā).”

The Blessed One (bhagavā) spoke thus.

Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) statement.

The Nandakovada Sutta, the fourth, is finished.
