Then, in a certain place beside the main road, the Blessed One smiled. Then the venerable Ānanda thought: “What now is the reason, what is the cause, for the Blessed One’s smile? The Tathāgatas do not reveal a smile without a reason.” Then the venerable Ānanda arranged his upper robe on one shoulder, and extending his hands in reverential salutation towards the Blessed One, he asked him: “Venerable sir, what is the reason, what is the cause, for the Blessed One’s smile? The Tathāgatas do not reveal a smile without a reason.”

282. Thus have I heard: the Ghaṭikāra Sutta.

There, "sitaṃ pātvākāsī" means: While going along the great road, looking at a certain spot of ground, thinking, "Have I ever lived in this place while practicing the cariya?", he saw—

"In the time of the Buddha Kassapa, in this place, there was a town named Vegaḷiṅga; then I was a young man named Jotipāla; my friend was a potter named Ghaṭikāra; with him I did a good deed here, unrevealed and hidden from the bhikkhu-saṅgha. Come, let me reveal it to the bhikkhu-saṅgha!"—Having descended from the road, standing in a certain place, he performed the miracle of the white [light], showing the tips of his teeth, he smiled a gentle smile.

Just as worldly people, striking their chests, laugh, saying "Where? Where?", not so do Buddhas; but the laughter of the Buddhas is only an expression of joy.

And this laughter occurs with thirteen minds accompanied by joy.

There, a worldly person laughs with eight minds: four from unwholesome [roots], and four from wholesome [roots] pertaining to the desire realm; a trainee laughs with six minds, having removed the two connected with wrong view from the unwholesome; a khīṇāsava laughs with five minds: four caused-rooted active minds and one uncaused-rooted active mind.

Even among those, when a strong object comes into the range [of perception], they laugh with two minds associated with knowledge; when the object is weak, they laugh with three minds: two dual-rooted minds and one uncaused mind.

But in this place, the action-rooted mind-consciousness-element accompanied by joy produced in the Blessed One only an expression of joy in laughter.

This laughter, even if it was very slight, became apparent to the Elder.

How [was that]? For at that time, from the four teeth of the tathāgata, rays of light the size of a great palm trunk, shining like lightning flashes with stars, arose, circumambulating the head three times, and then disappeared into the tips of the teeth themselves.

By that sign, the venerable Ānanda, even though he was walking behind the Blessed One, knew that there had been a manifestation of light.

"Bhagavantaṃ etadavocā" means: "Here, the Blessed One Kassapa advised the bhikkhu-saṅgha, he gave a declaration of the Four Noble Truths; I shall produce pleasure for the Blessed One to sit here; thus, this part of the earth will have been enjoyed by two Buddhas, and the great multitude, having worshiped [it] with garlands of flowers and the like, having made [it] a place for a cetiya, attending [to it], will be devoted to the path to heaven," thinking this, he spoke this speech beginning with "Then indeed, venerable sir."

283. Muṇḍakena samaṇakenā means muṇḍaṃ muṇḍoti, samaṇaṃ vā samaṇoti is fit to be said, but this one, because of immature knowledge (aparipakkañāṇattā), reviling only with the force of the usage learned in a Brahmin family (brāhmaṇakule uggahitavohāravaseneva), says thus (evamāha).

Sottisināni means katasottiṃ for the purpose of bathing (sinānatthāya).

Sotti namely kuruvindapāsāṇacuṇṇāni, having bound (bandhitvā) with lac (lākhāya), is called kataguḷikakalāpakā, referring to which (yaṃ sandhāya) – “Then (tena kho pana) at one time (samayena) the group-of-six monks (chabbaggiyā bhikkhū) bathe (nahāyantī) with kuruvindaka powder” – is said (vuttaṃ) (Cūḷava.
243).

Having taken (gahetvā) that (taṃ) at both ends (ubhosu antesu), they rub (ghaṃsanti) the body (sarīraṃ).

Evaṃ sammā means just as (yathā) even now (etarahipi) people (manussā), when said (vuttā) "Let us go to worship (cetiyavandanāya gacchāma) at the caitya, let us go for the purpose of hearing the Dharma (dhammassavanatthāya gacchāma)," do not make an effort (ussāhaṃ na karonti), but when said (vuttā) "Let us go for the purpose of seeing dance performances (naṭasamajjādidassanatthāya gacchāma)," they accept (sampaṭicchanti) with a single word (ekavacanena), just so (tatheva) when sinhāyitunti is said (vutte), accepting (sampaṭicchanto) with a single word (ekavacanena) he says thus (evamāha).

291. ‘“On one occasion, great king, I was staying at the village near the market town called Vebhalinga.

At that time, my hut leaked.

Then I addressed the monks thus: “Go, monks, and find out if there is any grass at the potter Ghaṭikāra’s house.”

When this was said, great king, those monks told me, “There is no grass at the potter Ghaṭikāra’s house, but there is a grass roof on his workshop.”

“Go, monks, and remove the grass from the potter Ghaṭikāra’s workshop.”

Then those monks did so.

Then the potter Ghaṭikāra’s parents asked the monks: “Who is removing the grass from the workshop?” “Sister, the hut of the Blessed One, the worthy, fully awakened Buddha, Kashyapa is leaking.”

“Take it, venerable sirs, take it and bless you!”

Then the potter Ghaṭikāra went to his parents and asked: “Who has removed the grass from the roof?” “Monks, dear, the hut of the Blessed One, the worthy, fully awakened Buddha, Kashyapa, is leaking.”

Then the potter Ghaṭikāra thought: “It is a gain for me, it is a great gain for me that the Blessed One Kashyapa, the worthy, fully awakened Buddha, relies on me thus!”

And the rapture and happiness never left the potter Ghaṭikāra for a half-month or his parents for a week.

Then that house remained three whole months with the sky for a roof, and yet no rain came in.

Such is the potter Ghaṭikāra.’

‘It is a gain for the potter Ghaṭikāra, venerable sir, it is a great gain for the potter Ghaṭikāra that the Blessed One relies on him thus.’"

“Take, O venerable one, take [it], O auspicious-faced ones!” Our son, when asked, “Where have you gone?” replies, “To the presence of the Ten-Powered One.” Where, indeed, does he go? He does not even know the sheltering place of the Teacher.” Thus, with delighted hearts, [and] regarding [their] sons as offenders in [this] matter, they spoke thus.

“For three months there was a covering of the sky” means that the Blessed One, after one month had passed of the four months of the rains, had grass brought. Therefore, he spoke thus.
Here the meaning of the word is this: the sky is a covering for it, therefore, "a sky-covering."

“It did not rain divinely” means that it merely did not over-rain. Just as in this case, even a single drop of water did not over-rain in the interior of the well-sheltered water-place. Thus, as in the interior of a house with a dense covering, neither wind nor heat caused discomfort; by nature, there was only seasonal comfort.

Even when that town was abandoned in the latter part, that place was still without rain.

People, while doing work, put cloths there while the gods rained.
As long as the aeon lasts, that place will be just like that.

And that is not through the miraculous power of the tathāgata, but through the perfection of the qualities of those [beings] themselves.

For to them [occurred the thought], “Where might one not obtain a fully enlightened Buddha? He has made a dwelling for two blind men!” Due to that cause, sadness did not arise. [But] “The foremost individual in the world with the gods is causing grass to be brought to our dwelling and is covering the gandhakuṭi!” So a considerable joy arose for them.

Thus, it should be understood that this miracle occurred through the very perfection of the qualities of those [beings].

“For three months there was a covering of the sky" [means] "a sky-covering."

Thinking that "food will be provided for twenty thousand monks for three months," the king sent that much.
Why did he refuse, saying, "alaṃ me raññova hotū (enough of the king)?" Because he had attained the state of desiring little.
Thus it occurred to him: "I have never been seen by the king before, how could he send [this]?"
Then he thought: "The teacher has gone to Vārāṇasī; indeed, while being requested for the rainy season residence by the king, he announced to him that I am pledged [to serve him], and he told the story of my virtues. The gain obtained through the story of virtues is like that obtained by a dancer having danced by an actor, or like that obtained by a singer having sung by a singer. What [is the use] to me of this, having done work, I am able to attend to my mother and father, and even to the perfectly enlightened Buddha."
