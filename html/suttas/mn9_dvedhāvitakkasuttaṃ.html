206. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Pubbeva me, bhikkhave, sambodhā anabhisambuddhassa bodhisattasseva sato etadahosi – ‘yaṃnūnāhaṃ dvidhā katvā dvidhā katvā vitakke vihareyya’nti. So kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, yo cāyaṃ kāmavitakko yo ca byāpādavitakko yo ca vihiṃsāvitakko – imaṃ ekaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ ; yo cāyaṃ nekkhammavitakko yo ca abyāpādavitakko yo ca avihiṃsāvitakko – imaṃ dutiyaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ.
Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Anāthapindika’s monastery (anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme), in the Jeta Grove (jetavane), near Śrāvastī (sāvatthiyaṃ).

There the Fortunate One (bhagavā) addressed the monks (bhikkhū), saying: “Monks (bhikkhavo)!”

“Venerable sir (bhadante),” those monks (bhikkhū) replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) said this:

“Even before my enlightenment (sambodhā), while I was still an unenlightened Bodhisattva (anabhisambuddhassa bodhisattasseva sato), it occurred to me: ‘What if I were to divide my thoughts (vitakke) into two categories, thinking in two ways (dvidhā katvā dvidhā katvā), and abide in them (vihareyya)’nti."

"So I, monks (bhikkhave), made thinking imbued with sensuality (kāmavitakka), thinking imbued with ill will (byāpādavitakka), & thinking imbued with harmfulness (vihiṃsāvitakka) one sort (imaṃ ekaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ); and thinking imbued with renunciation (nekkhammavitakka), thinking imbued with non-ill will (abyāpādavitakka), & thinking imbued with harmlessness (avihiṃsāvitakka) another sort (imaṃ dutiyaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ)."
206.Me sutanti dvedhāvitakkasuttaṃ. Tattha dvidhā katvā dvidhā katvāti dve dve bhāge katvā. Kāmavitakkoti kāmapaṭisaṃyutto vitakko. Byāpādavitakkoti byāpādapaṭisaṃyutto vitakko. Vihiṃsāvitakkoti vihiṃsāpaṭisaṃyutto vitakko. Ekaṃ bhāganti ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā oḷāriko vā sukhumo vā sabbo pāyaṃ vitakko akusalapakkhikoyevāti tayopi kāmabyāpādavihiṃsāvitakke ekaṃ koṭṭhāsamakāsiṃ. Kāmehi nissaṭo nekkhammapaṭisaṃyutto vitakko nekkhammavitakko nāma, so yāva paṭhamajjhānā vaṭṭati. Abyāpādapaṭisaṃyutto vitakko abyāpādavitakko, so mettāpubbabhāgato paṭṭhāya yāva paṭhamajjhānā vaṭṭati. Avihiṃsāpaṭisaṃyutto vitakko avihiṃsāvitakko, so karuṇāpubbabhāgato paṭṭhāya yāva paṭhamajjhānā vaṭṭati. Dutiyaṃ bhāganti sabbopāyaṃ kusalapakkhikoyevāti dutiyaṃ koṭṭhāsamakāsiṃ. Iminā bodhisattassa vitakkaniggahaṇakālo kathito. Bodhisattassa hi chabbassāni padhānaṃ padahantassa nekkhammavitakkādayo puñjapuñjā mahānadiyaṃ oghā viya pavattiṃsu. Satisammosena pana sahasā kāmavitakkādayo uppajjitvā kusalavāraṃ pacchinditvā sayaṃ akusalajavanavārā hutvā tiṭṭhanti. Tato bodhisatto cintesi – ‘‘mayhaṃ ime kāmavitakkādayo kusalavāraṃ pacchinditvā tiṭṭhanti, handāhaṃ ime vitakke dve bhāge katvā viharāmī’’ti kāmavitakkādayo akusalapakkhikāti ekaṃ bhāgaṃ karoti nekkhammavitakkādayo kusalapakkhikāti ekaṃ. Atha puna cintesi – ‘‘akusalapakkhato āgataṃ vitakkaṃ mantena kaṇhasappaṃ uppīḷetvā gaṇhanto viya amittaṃ gīvāya akkamanto viya ca niggahessāmi, nāssa vaḍḍhituṃ dassāmi. Kusalapakkhato āgataṃ vitakkaṃ meghasamaye meghaṃ viya sukhette sālakalyāṇipotakaṃ viya ca sīghaṃ vaḍḍhessāmī’’ti. So tathā katvā akusalavitakke niggaṇhi, kusalavitakke vaḍḍhesi. Evaṃ iminā bodhisattassa vitakkaniggahaṇanakālo kathitoti veditabbo.
206. Me sutanti Dvedhāvitakkasuttaṃ.

There, dvidhā katvā dvidhā katvāti means having made two two parts.

Kāmavitakkoti means thought connected with desire (kāma).

Byāpādavitakkoti means thought connected with ill-will (byāpāda).

Vihiṃsāvitakkoti means thought connected with harming (vihiṃsā).

Ekaṃ bhāganti means all those thoughts whether internal or external, gross or subtle are on the side of the unwholesome. Thus, he made the three, namely, thoughts of desire, ill-will, and harming, into one division.

Thought disconnected from desires, connected with renunciation (nekkhammapaṭisaṃyutto), is called nekkhammavitakko, it occurs until the first jhāna.

Thought connected with non-ill-will (abyāpādapaṭisaṃyutto) is abyāpādavitakko, it occurs from the preliminary stage of loving-kindness (mettā) up to the first jhāna.

Thought connected with non-harming (avihiṃsāpaṭisaṃyutto) is avihiṃsāvitakko, it occurs from the preliminary stage of compassion (karuṇā) up to the first jhāna.

Dutiyaṃ bhāganti means all those thoughts are on the side of the wholesome. Thus, he made a second division.

By this, the time of the Bodhisattva's suppression of thoughts is described.

For the Bodhisattva, while making effort for six years, thoughts of renunciation (nekkhammavitakka) and the like arose in heaps like floods in a great river.

But due to forgetfulness, thoughts of desire (kāmavitakka) and the like arose suddenly, cutting off the wholesome turn, and themselves stood as turns of unwholesome impulsion.

Then the Bodhisattva thought: "These thoughts of desire (kāmavitakka) and the like of mine cut off the wholesome turn and stand; come, let me divide these thoughts into two parts and abide." Thus, he made one part of thoughts of desire (kāmavitakka) and the like, which are on the side of the unwholesome, and one of thoughts of renunciation (nekkhammavitakka) and the like, which are on the side of the wholesome.

Then he thought again: "I will suppress the thought that comes from the unwholesome side as if grasping a black snake with a mantra, or as if attacking an enemy on the neck. I will not allow it to grow.

I will quickly increase the thought that comes from the wholesome side like a cloud in the rainy season, or like a young sālaka calf in a pleasant field."

So, having done thus, he suppressed the unwholesome thoughts and increased the wholesome thoughts.

Thus, it should be understood that by this the time of the Bodhisattva's suppression of thoughts is described.

207. ‘‘Tassa mayhaṃ, bhikkhave, evaṃ appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato uppajjati kāmavitakko. So evaṃ pajānāmi – ‘uppanno kho me ayaṃ kāmavitakko. So ca kho attabyābādhāyapi saṃvattati, parabyābādhāyapi saṃvattati, ubhayabyābādhāyapi saṃvattati, paññānirodhiko vighātapakkhiko anibbānasaṃvattaniko’ anibbānasaṃvattaniko’’ti (?). ‘Attabyābādhāya saṃvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṃ gacchati; ‘parabyābādhāya saṃvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṃ gacchati; ‘ubhayabyābādhāya saṃvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṃ gacchati; ‘paññānirodhiko vighātapakkhiko anibbānasaṃvattaniko’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṃ gacchati. So kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, uppannuppannaṃ kāmavitakkaṃ pajahameva atītakālikakiriyāpadāniyeva vinodameva atītakālikakiriyāpadāniyeva byantameva byanteva (sī. syā. pī.) naṃ akāsiṃ.
Bhikkhus, while I was living thus, diligent, ardent, and resolute, the thought of sensual pleasure (kāmavitakka) arose in me. I discerned that ‘Thinking imbued with sensuality has arisen in me; and that leads to my own affliction (attabyābādhāya) or to the affliction of others (parabyābādhāya) or to the affliction of both (ubhayabyābādhāya). It obstructs discernment (paññānirodhiko), promotes vexation (vighātapakkhiko), & does not lead to Unbinding (anibbānasaṃvattaniko).’ “As I noticed that it leads to my own affliction, it subsided. As I noticed that it leads to the affliction of others…to the affliction of both…it obstructs discernment, promotes vexation, & does not lead to Unbinding, it subsided. Bhikkhus, I abandoned, dispelled, and eliminated each and every thought of sensual pleasure that arose.
207. Idāni yathāssa te vitakkā uppajjiṃsu, yathā ca ne niggahesi, taṃ dassento tassa mayhaṃ, bhikkhavetiādimāha. Tattha appamattassāti satiyā avippavāse ṭhitassa. Ātāpinoti ātāpavīriyavantassa. Pahitattassāti pesitacittassa. Uppajjati kāmavitakkoti bodhisattassa chabbassāni padhānaṃ padahato rajjasukhaṃ vā ārabbha, pāsāde vā nāṭakāni vā orodhe vā kiñcideva vā sampattiṃ ārabbha kāmavitakko nāma na uppannapubbo. Dukkarakārikāya panassa āhārūpacchedena adhimattakasimānaṃ pattassa etadahosi – ‘‘na sakkā āhārūpacchedena visesaṃ nibbattetuṃ, yaṃnūnāhaṃ oḷārikaṃ āhāraṃ āhāreyya’’nti. So uruvelaṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi. Manussā – ‘‘mahāpuriso pubbe āharitvā dinnampi na gaṇhi, addhāssa idāni manoratho matthakaṃ patto, tasmā sayameva āgato’’ti paṇītapaṇītaṃ āhāraṃ upahariṃsu. Bodhisattassa attabhāvo nacirasseva pākatiko ahosi. Jarājiṇṇattabhāvo hi sappāyabhojanaṃ labhitvāpi pākatiko na hoti. Bodhisatto pana daharo. Tenassa sappāyabhojanaṃ bhuñjato attabhāvo na cirasseva pākatiko jāto, vippasannāni indriyāni, parisuddho chavivaṇṇo, samuggatatārāgaṇaṃ viya nabhaṃ paripuṇṇadvattiṃsamahāpurisalakkhaṇappaṭimaṇḍitasarīraṃ ahosi. So taṃ oloketvā ‘‘tāva kilanto nāma attabhāvo evaṃ paṭipākatiko jāto’’ti cintetvā attano paññāmahantatāya evaṃ parittakampi vitakkaṃ gahetvā kāmavitakkoti akāsi. Paṇṇasālāya purato nisinno camarapasadagavayarohitamigādike magagaṇe manuññasaddaravane moravanakukkuṭādike pakkhigaṇe nīluppalakumudakamalādisañchannāni pallalāni nānākusumasañchannaviṭapā vanarājiyo maṇikkhandhanimmalajalapavāhañca nadiṃ nerañjaraṃ passati. Tassa evaṃ hoti ‘‘sobhanā vatime migajātā pakkhigaṇā pallalāni vanarājiyo nadī nerañjarā’’ti. So tampi evaṃ parittakaṃ vitakkaṃ gahetvā kāmavitakkamakāsi, tenāha ‘‘uppajjati kāmavitakko’’ti. Attabyābādhāyapīti attadukkhāyapi. Esevanayo sabbattha. Kiṃ pana mahāsattassa ubhayadukkhāya saṃvattanakavitakko nāma atthīti? Natthi. Apariññāyaṃ ṭhitassa pana vitakko yāva ubhayabyābādhāya saṃvattatīti etāni tīṇi nāmāni labhati, tasmā evamāha. Paññānirodhikoti anuppannāya lokiyalokuttarāya paññāya uppajjituṃ na deti, lokiyapaññaṃ pana aṭṭhasamāpattipañcābhiññāvasena uppannampi samucchinditvā khipatīti paññānirodhiko. Vighātapakkhikoti dukkhakoṭṭhāsiko. Asaṅkhataṃ nibbānaṃ nāma, taṃ paccakkhaṃ kātuṃ na detīti anibbānasaṃvattaniko. Abbhatthaṃ gacchatīti khayaṃ natthibhāvaṃ gacchati. Udakapupphuḷako viya nirujjhati. Pajahamevāti pajahimeva. Vinodamevāti nīharimeva. Byantameva naṃ akāsinti vigatantaṃ nissesaṃ parivaṭumaṃ paricchinnameva naṃ akāsiṃ. Attabyābādhāyapī Paññānirodhiko paññānirodhiko. Vighātapakkhiko anibbānasaṃvattaniko. Abbhatthaṃ gacchatī Pajahamevā Vinodamevā Byantameva naṃ akāsi
207. Now, showing how those thoughts arose in him, and how he suppressed them, he said, "To me, monks," and so forth.

There, appamattassā means one established in mindfulness, in avippavāsa.

Ātāpino means one possessing the energy of zeal.

Pahitattassā means one whose mind is directed.

Uppajjati kāmavitakko means, for the Bodhisattva, for six years while practicing striving, a thought of sensual desire had never arisen, even in relation to royal bliss, or in a palace, or to plays, or in the harem, or to any kind of prosperity.

To him who had attained the extreme of emaciation by the practice of difficult asceticism, by the cutting off of food, this occurred: "It is not possible to bring about a distinction by the cutting off of food. What if I were to partake of coarse food?"

He entered Uruvelā for alms.

People [thought]: "The great man formerly did not accept even what was given after it had been brought. Surely now his wish has reached its peak, therefore he has come of his own accord," and they offered him choice food.

The Bodhisattva's body soon became natural.

For the state of being aged and decrepit does not become natural even by obtaining suitable food.

The Bodhisattva, however, was young.

Because of that, for him enjoying suitable food, his body soon became natural, his senses were very clear, the color of his skin was pure, his body was adorned with the complete thirty-two marks of a great man, like the sky with a collection of risen stars.

Having looked at it, thinking, "So great is the exhaustion, the body has become so very restored," and taking even this slight thought into account by the greatness of his wisdom, he made it a thought of sensual desire.

Sitting in front of the leaf hut, he sees herds of deer such as camaras, pasadas, gavayas, rohitas, and migas, flocks of birds such as peacocks, forest fowl, and cuckoos in a delightful, sounding forest, ponds covered with blue lotuses, water lilies, and lotuses, rows of forests with branches covered with various flowers, and the river Nerañjarā with a flow of water that is like a pure jewel.

To him it occurs, "How beautiful are these species of deer, flocks of birds, ponds, rows of forests, and the river Nerañjarā!"

Taking even that slight thought into account in this way, he made it a thought of sensual desire, therefore he said, "A thought of sensual desire arises."

Attabyābādhāyapī means even for self-affliction, even for self-suffering.

This is the method everywhere.

But what is a thought connected with suffering for both to the great being? It does not exist.

But a thought for one established in non-discernment extends even to affliction for both, so he obtains these three names, therefore he said thus.

Paññānirodhiko means he does not allow worldly and supramundane wisdom that has not arisen to arise, but having completely cut off and cast away even the worldly wisdom that has arisen by way of the eight attainments and the five superknowledges, he is paññānirodhiko.

Vighātapakkhiko means one belonging to the category of suffering.

Asaṅkhataṃ nibbānaṃ is called unconditioned liberation, because it does not allow the realization of that directly, he is anibbānasaṃvattaniko.

Abbhatthaṃ gacchatī means it goes to destruction, to non-existence.

It ceases like a water bubble.

Pajahamevā means just abandon it.

Vinodamevā means just remove it.

Byantameva naṃ akāsi means having destroyed it, utterly, completely, perfectly, he made it just that.

Attabyābādhāyapī Paññānirodhiko *Paññānirodhiko.
Vighātapakkhiko Anibbānasaṃvattaniko.*
He went to the place of destruction, indeed, he drove away, removed, and completely destroyed the cause (of suffering).

208. ‘‘Tassa mayhaṃ, bhikkhave, evaṃ appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato uppajjati byāpādavitakko…pe… uppajjati vihiṃsāvitakko. So evaṃ pajānāmi – ‘uppanno kho me ayaṃ vihiṃsāvitakko. So ca kho attabyābādhāyapi saṃvattati, parabyābādhāyapi saṃvattati, ubhayabyābādhāyapi saṃvattati, paññānirodhiko vighātapakkhiko anibbānasaṃvattaniko’. ‘Attabyābādhāya saṃvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṃ gacchati; ‘parabyābādhāya saṃvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṃ gacchati; ‘ubhayabyābādhāya saṃvattatī’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṃ gacchati; ‘paññānirodhiko vighātapakkhiko anibbānasaṃvattaniko’tipi me, bhikkhave, paṭisañcikkhato abbhatthaṃ gacchati. So kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, uppannuppannaṃ vihiṃsāvitakkaṃ pajahameva vinodameva byantameva naṃ akāsiṃ. ‘‘Yaññadeva, bhikkhave, bhikkhu bahulamanuvitakketi anuvicāreti, tathā tathā nati hoti cetaso. Kāmavitakkaṃ ce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu bahulamanuvitakketi anuvicāreti, pahāsi nekkhammavitakkaṃ, kāmavitakkaṃ bahulamakāsi, tassa taṃ kāmavitakkāya cittaṃ namati. Byāpādavitakkaṃ ce, bhikkhave…pe… vihiṃsāvitakkaṃ ce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu bahulamanuvitakketi anuvicāreti, pahāsi avihiṃsāvitakkaṃ, vihiṃsāvitakkaṃ bahulamakāsi, tassa taṃ vihiṃsāvitakkāya cittaṃ namati. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, vassānaṃ pacchime māse saradasamaye kiṭṭhasambādhe gopālako gāvo rakkheyya. So tā gāvo tato tato daṇḍena ākoṭeyya paṭikoṭeyya sannirundheyya sannivāreyya. Taṃ kissa hetu? Passati hi so, bhikkhave, gopālako tatonidānaṃ vadhaṃ vā bandhanaṃ vā jāniṃ vā garahaṃ vā. Evameva kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, addasaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ādīnavaṃ okāraṃ saṃkilesaṃ, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ nekkhamme ānisaṃsaṃ vodānapakkhaṃ.
208. Monks, when I remained thus heedful, ardent, & resolute, thinking imbued with ill will arose in me… and… thinking imbued with harmfulness arose.

I discerned that ‘Thinking imbued with harmfulness has arisen in me;

and that leads to my own affliction or to the affliction of others or to the affliction of both. It obstructs discernment, promotes vexation, & does not lead to Unbinding.’

“As I noticed that 'it leads to my own affliction,' it subsided. As I noticed that 'it leads to the affliction of others'... to the affliction of both…'it obstructs discernment, promotes vexation, & does not lead to Unbinding,' it subsided.

Whenever thinking imbued with harmfulness had arisen, I simply abandoned it, dispelled it, wiped it out of existence.

Whatever a monk keeps pursuing with his thinking & pondering, that becomes the inclination of his awareness.

If a monk keeps pursuing thinking imbued with sensuality, abandoning thinking imbued with renunciation (nekkhammavitakka), his mind is bent by that thinking imbued with sensuality (kāmavitakka).

If a monk keeps pursuing thinking imbued with ill will …harmfulness, abandoning thinking imbued with harmlessness, his mind is bent by that thinking imbued with harmfulness.

Just as in the last month of the Rains, in the autumn season when the crops are ripening, a cowherd would look after his cows:

He would tap & poke & check & curb them with a stick on this side & that.

Why is that? Because he foresees flogging or imprisonment or a fine or public censure arising from that if he let his cows wander into the crops.

In the same way I foresaw in unskillful qualities drawbacks, degradation, & defilement (saṃkilesa), and I foresaw in skillful qualities rewards related to renunciation (nekkhamma) & promoting cleansing (vodānapakkha).
208.Byāpādavitakkoti na bodhisattassa parūpaghātappaṭisaṃyutto nāma vitakko citte uppajjati, athassa ativassaaccuṇhaatisītādīni pana paṭicca cittavipariṇāmabhāvo hoti, taṃ sandhāya ‘‘byāpādavitakko’’ti āha. Vihiṃsāvitakkoti na mahāsattassa paresaṃ dukkhuppādanappaṭisaṃyutto vitakko uppajjati, citte pana uddhatākāro anekaggatākāro hoti, taṃ gahetvā vihiṃsāvitakkamakāsi. Paṇṇasālādvāre nisinno hi sīhabyagghādike vāḷamige sūkarādayo khuddamige vihiṃsante passati. Atha bodhisatto imasmimpi nāma akutobhaye araññe imesaṃ tiracchānagatānaṃ paccatthikā uppajjanti, balavanto dubbale khādanti, balavantakhāditā vattantīti kāruññaṃ uppādeti. Aññepi biḷārādayo kukkuṭamūsikādīni khādante passati, gāmaṃ piṇḍāya paviṭṭho manusse rājakammikehi upaddute vadhabandhādīni anubhavante attano kasivaṇijjādīni kammāni katvā jīvituṃ na labhantīti kāruññaṃ uppādeti, taṃ sandhāya ‘‘uppajjati vihiṃsāvitakko’’ti āha. Tathā tathāti tena tena ākārena. Idaṃ vuttaṃ hoti – kāmavitakkādīsu yaṃ yaṃ vitakketi, yaṃ yaṃ vitakkaṃ pavatteti, tena tene cassākārena kāmavitakkādibhāvo cetaso na hi hotīti. Pahāsi nekkhammavitakkanti nekkhammavitakkaṃ pajahati. Bahulamakāsīti bahulaṃ karoti. Tassa taṃ kāmavitakkāya cittanti tassa taṃ cittaṃ kāmavitakkatthāya. Yathā kāmavitakkasampayuttaṃ hoti, evamevaṃ namatīti attho. Sesapadesupi eseva nayo. Idāni atthadīpikaṃ upamaṃ dassento seyyathāpī tiādimāha. Tattha kiṭṭhasambādheti sassasambādhe. Ākoṭeyyāti ujukaṃ piṭṭhiyaṃ pahareyya. Paṭikoṭeyyāti tiriyaṃ phāsukāsu pahareyya. Sannirundheyyāti āvaritvā tiṭṭheyya. Sannivāreyyāti ito cito ca gantuṃ na dadeyya . Tatonidānanti tena kāraṇena, evaṃ arakkhitānaṃ gunnaṃ paresaṃ sassakhādanakāraṇenāti attho. Bālo hi gopāloko evaṃ gāvo arakkhamāno ‘‘ayaṃ amhākaṃ bhattavetanaṃ khādati, ujuṃ gāvo rakkhitumpi na sakkoti, kulehi saddhiṃ veraṃ gaṇhāpetī’’ti gosāmikānampi santikā vadhādīni pāpuṇāti, kiṭṭhasāmikānampi. 215. “Suppose, monks, that in a forest glade there was a large low-lying marsh.

A large herd of deer would live in dependence on it.

Then some man would appear who did not desire their benefit, did not desire their welfare, and did not desire their rest from bondage (ayogakkhemakāmo).

He would close off the safe, restful path that led to their rapture, and would open up a false path, set out a male decoy (okacaraṃ), and place a female decoy (okacārikaṃ).

And thus, monks, that large herd of deer would eventually fall into ruin and disaster.

Then suppose that a certain man were to appear to that same large herd of deer, desiring their benefit, desiring their welfare, desiring their rest from bondage (yogakkhemakāmo).

He would open up the safe, restful path that led to their rapture, would close off the false path, take away the male decoy (okacaraṃ), and destroy the female decoy (okacārikaṃ).

And thus, monks, that large herd of deer would eventually come into growth, increase, & abundance.

‘‘Monks, I have made this simile to explain the meaning.

The meaning is this: ‘The large, low-lying marsh’ stands for sensuality (kāmānaṃ etaṃ adhivacanaṃ).

‘The large herd of deer’ stands for beings (sattānaṃ etaṃ adhivacanaṃ).

‘The man not desiring their benefit, not desiring their welfare, not desiring their rest from bondage’ stands for Māra, the Evil One (mārassetaṃ pāpimato adhivacanaṃ).

‘The false path’ stands for the eightfold wrong path (aṭṭhaṅgikassetaṃ micchāmaggassa adhivacanaṃ), i. e. , wrong view (micchādiṭṭhiyā), wrong resolve (micchāsaṅkappassa), wrong speech (micchāvācāya), wrong action (micchākammantassa), wrong livelihood (micchāājīvassa), wrong effort (micchāvāyāmassa), wrong mindfulness (micchāsatiyā), & wrong concentration (micchāsamādhissa).

‘The male decoy’ stands for passion & delight (nandīrāgassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ).

‘The female decoy’ stands for ignorance (avijjāyetaṃ adhivacanaṃ).

‘The man desiring their benefit, desiring their welfare, desiring their rest from bondage’ stands for the Tathāgata, the Worthy One, the Rightly Self-awakened One (tathāgatassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ arahato sammāsambuddhassa).

‘The safe, restful path that led to their rapture’ stands for the noble eightfold path (ariyassetaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikassa maggassa adhivacanaṃ), i. e. , right view (sammādiṭṭhiyā), right resolve (sammāsaṅkappassa), right speech (sammāvācāya), right action (sammākammantassa), right livelihood (sammāājīvassa), right effort (sammāvāyāmassa), right mindfulness (sammāsatiyā), & right concentration (sammāsamādhissa).

“So, monks, I have opened up the safe, restful path, closed off the false path, removed the male decoy, and destroyed the female.

Whatever should be done, bhikkhus, by a compassionate teacher out of compassion for his disciples, desiring their welfare, that I have done for you.

Over there are the roots of trees; over there, empty dwellings. Practice jhāna, monks. Don’t be heedless. Don’t later fall into regret.

This is our instruction to you.”

Thus spoke the Lord.

Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Dvedhāvitakka Sutta, the ninth is finished.

215. “Seyyathāpīti” what does this mean here? This is an appropriate connection: the Blessed One, showing compassion toward beings and also the perfection of his own state of teacher, began this teaching.

There, araññe means in the wilderness (aṭavī).

Pavane means in a forest grove (vanasaṇḍa).

In reality, these two are one and the same; however, the second is a synonym for the first.

Ayogakkhemakāmo means not wanting safety (khema) and a state free of fear (nirbhayaṭṭhānaṃ) from the four types of yoga, but wanting only fear.

Sovatthiko means bringing about well-being (suvatthibhāvāvaho).

Pītigamanīyo means leading to satisfaction (tuṭṭhiṃ gamanīyo).

Alternatively, there is the reading “pītagamanīyo.”

Pidaheyyā means would cover with branches and so forth (sākhādīhi thakeyya).

Vivareyyā means would make an open mouth (visadamukhaṃ katvā) and would make it open (vivaṭaṃ kareyya).

Kummagga means an impassable path (amaggaṃ) obstructed by water, forests, mountains, and so forth (udakavanapabbatādīhi sanniruddhaṃ).

Odaheyya okacara means would place a deer that roams about in their habitat (tesaṃ oke caramānaṃ viya) in one place (ekasmiṃ ṭhāne ṭhapeyya).

Okacārika means a deer tied with a long rope (dīgharajjuyā bandhitaṃyeva migiṃ).

A deer hunter, after going to the forest, the dwelling place (vasanaṭṭhānaṃ) of deer, having observed that “they live here, they go out by this path, they roam here, they drink here, they enter by this path,” closes the path, opens an impassable path (kummaggaṃ vivaritvā), and placing a decoy deer (okacaraṃ) and a deer tied to a snare (okacārikaṃ), he himself stands in a concealed place, taking up a knife (sattiṃ gahetvā tiṭṭhati).

Then, at evening time (sāyanhasamaye), deer, fearless, after roaming in the forest, drinking water, playing with their young, coming near to their dwelling place (vasanaṭṭhānasantikaṃ āgantvā), seeing the decoy deer and the deer tied to a snare, enter without any suspicion, thinking, “Our friends have come!” Seeing the path closed, they go along the impassable path, thinking, “This is not the path; this will be the path!”

The deer hunter does nothing at first, but when they have entered, he strikes the very last one swiftly.

That one is startled; then, all of them, startled, looking in front, thinking, “Danger has arisen!” seeing the path obstructed by water, or by a forest, or by a mountain, and unable to enter the dense forest like a chain of fingers with both sides, they begin to go out by the same path by which they entered.

The hunter, knowing their state of turning back (nivattanabhāvaṃ ñatvā), from the beginning onwards, kills thirty or forty deer.

With this in mind, it was said: “Indeed, monks, in another time, that great herd of deer would meet with misfortune and disaster (anayabyasanaṃ āpajjeyyā).”

“This is a designation for delight in pleasure (nandīrāga), this is a designation for ignorance (avijjā),” here, because these beings, being ignorant due to ignorance, having bound themselves with delight in pleasure, having approached forms, etc. (rūpārammaṇādīni upanītā), obtain killing by the wheel of suffering (vaṭṭadukkhasattiyā ghātaṃ labhanti), therefore, the Blessed One, having made the decoy deer (okacaraṃ) “delight in pleasure (nandīrāga),” and the deer tied to a snare (okacārikaṃ) “ignorance (avijjā),” taught (dassesi).

A deer hunter, one time, having wiped their bodies with the breaking of branches (sākhābhaṅgena sarīraṃ puñchitvā), having removed the human smell, having placed a decoy deer in one place, having released a deer tied to a snare together with a rope, having concealed himself, taking up a knife, stands near to the decoy deer (okacarassa santike tiṭṭhati), the deer tied to a snare goes towards the roaming ground of the herd of deer (migagaṇassa caraṇaṭṭhānābhimukhī gacchati).

Seeing that, the deer lift up their heads and stand; that one also lifts up its head and stands; they take it to be a pasture, thinking, “This is of our own kind.”

That one also, as if eating grass, draws near gradually.

The leader of the herd, the wild deer (āraññiko yūthapatimigo), obtaining the scent of that one, sends away his own mate (sakabhariyaṃ vissajjetvā), and is facing towards that one.

For beings, only new things are pleasing.

The deer tied to a snare, not giving the very near presence to the wild deer, as if facing towards that one, after going back from behind (pacchato paṭikkamitvā), goes near to the decoy deer, wherever the rope is caught, there, after striking with its hoof, it releases it (khurena paharitvā moceti), the wild deer, seeing the decoy deer, being infatuated by the deer tied to a snare (okacārikāya sammatto hutvā), being jealous toward the decoy deer (okacare usūyaṃ katvā), lowering its back, shaking its head, stands, at that moment, even while licking the knife with its tongue, it does not know “what is this?” (kiṃ eta’’nti na jānāti), the decoy deer also... It is easy to strike that deer with the upper part of the knife; it lowers its back.

It is easy to strike it with the lower part of the knife; it raises its heart.

Then, the hunter, after striking the wild deer with the knife, after killing it right there, taking the meat, goes away.

Just so, just as that deer, being infatuated by the deer tied to a snare, being jealous towards the decoy deer, even while licking the knife with its tongue, does not know anything, so too, these beings, being infatuated by ignorance, being blinded, not knowing anything, after approaching delight in pleasure in forms, etc. (rūpādīsu nandīrāgaṃ upagamma), obtain killing by the wheel of suffering (vaṭṭadukkhasattiyā vadhaṃ labhanti), therefore, the Blessed One, having made the decoy deer “delight in pleasure,” and the deer tied to a snare “ignorance,” taught.
iti kho, bhikkhave, vivaṭo mayā khemo maggoti O monks, thus, the secure path is opened by me.

iti kho, bhikkhave, mayā imesaṃ sattānaṃ hitacaraṇena sammāsambodhiṃ patvā ahaṃ buddhosmīti tuṇhībhūtena anisīditvā dhammacakkappavattanato paṭṭhāya dhammaṃ desentena vivaṭo khemo ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, pihito kummaggo, O monks, thus, by me, having attained perfect awakening through beneficial conduct for these beings, I am the Buddha, the secure, noble eightfold path is opened by me who, being silent, did not sit down, but from the turning of the wheel of the Dharma onwards, taught the Dharma; the wrong path is closed off.

aññātakoṇḍaññādīnaṃ bhabbapuggalānaṃ ūhato okacaro nandīrāgo dvedhā chetvā pātito, nāsitā okacārikā avijjā sabbena sabbaṃ samugghātitāti attano hitūpacāraṃ dassesi. The delight and passion that roam in the house, inferred from the suitable individuals such as Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya, has been cut in two and cast down, ignorance that wanders in the house is destroyed, all is completely uprooted: thus he showed his beneficial service to himself.

sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti. The rest is clear throughout.
