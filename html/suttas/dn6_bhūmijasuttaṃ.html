<p n="223" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">223</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0273"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0178"/><pb ed="P" n="3.0138"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe. Atha kho āyasmā bhūmijo pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena jayasenassa rājakumārassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Atha kho jayaseno rājakumāro yenāyasmā bhūmijo tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā bhūmijena saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho jayaseno rājakumāro āyasmantaṃ bhūmijaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘santi, bho bhūmija, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā <pb ed="M" n="3.0178"/> evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘āsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā <note>carati, abhabbo (sī. pī.) evamuparipi ekavacaneneva dissati</note> phalassa adhigamāya; anāsañcepi <note>āsañca anāsañca cepi (aṭṭha.)</note> karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāyā’ti. Idha bhoto bhūmijassa satthā kiṃvādī <note>kiṃvādī kiṃdiṭṭhī (syā. kaṃ. ka.)</note> kimakkhāyī’’ti? ‘‘Na kho metaṃ, rājakumāra, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ. Ṭhānañca kho etaṃ vijjati yaṃ bhagavā evaṃ byākareyya – ‘āsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya <pb ed="T" n="3.0274"/>; anāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya. Āsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāya <pb ed="P" n="3.0139"/>; anāsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāyā’ti. Na kho me taṃ, rājakumāra, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ. Ṭhānañca kho etaṃ vijjati yaṃ bhagavā evaṃ byākareyyā’’ti. ‘‘Sace kho bhoto bhūmijassa satthā evaṃvādī <note>evaṃvādī evaṃdiṭṭhī (syā. kaṃ. ka.)</note> evamakkhāyī, addhā bhoto bhūmijassa satthā sabbesaṃyeva puthusamaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ muddhānaṃ <note>buddhānaṃ (ka.) muddhānantimuddhaṃ, matthakanti attho</note> maññe āhacca tiṭṭhatī’’ti <pb ed="V" n="3.0179"/>. Atha kho jayaseno rājakumāro āyasmantaṃ bhūmijaṃ sakeneva thālipākena parivisi.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rajagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary.

Then, when it was morning, the venerable Bhumija dressed, and taking his bowl and outer robe, went to the house of Prince Jayasena and sat down on a seat made ready.

Then Prince Jayasena went to the venerable Bhumija and exchanged greetings with him.

When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

Then Prince Jayasena, sitting to one side, said to the venerable Bhumija: “Master Bhumija, there are some recluses and Brahmins who make such assertions and hold such views as this: ‘If one makes an aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ), one is unable to procure any fruit; if one makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life, one is still unable to procure any fruit; if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life, one is still unable to procure any fruit; if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life, one is still unable to procure any fruit.’ What does Master Bhumija's teacher say, what does he proclaim in this matter?”

“I have not heard and learned that from the Blessed One’s own lips, prince. But it is possible that the Blessed One might say this: ‘If one makes an aspiration and one leads the holy life unwisely (ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ), one is unable to procure any fruit; if one makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life unwisely, one is still unable to procure any fruit; if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life unwisely, one is still unable to procure any fruit; if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life unwisely, one is still unable to procure any fruit. If one makes an aspiration and one leads the holy life wisely (yoniso brahmacariyaṃ), one is able to procure fruit; if one makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life wisely, one is still able to procure fruit; if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life wisely, one is still able to procure fruit; if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life wisely, one is still able to procure fruit.’ I have not heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s own lips, prince, but it is possible that the Blessed One would answer as I have stated.”

“If that is what Master Bhumija's teacher says, what he proclaims, then I think Master Bhumija's teacher stands accused above all other recluses and Brahmins.”

Then Prince Jayasena served the venerable Bhumija from his own dish of milk rice (thālipāka).

<p n="224" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">224</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Atha kho āyasmā bhūmijo pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Evaṃ vutte ahaṃ, bhante, jayasenaṃ rājakumāraṃ etadavocaṃ – ‘na kho me taṃ, rājakumāra, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ. Ṭhānañca kho etaṃ vijjati yaṃ bhagavā evaṃ byākareyya – āsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; anāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā ayoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, abhabbā phalassa adhigamāya. Āsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāya; anāsañcepi karitvā…pe… āsañca anāsañcepi karitvā…pe… nevāsaṃ nānāsañcepi karitvā yoniso brahmacariyaṃ caranti, bhabbā phalassa adhigamāyāti. Na kho me taṃ, rājakumāra, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ. Ṭhānañca kho etaṃ vijjati yaṃ bhagavā evaṃ byākareyyā’ti. ‘Sace bhoto bhūmijassa satthā evaṃvādī evamakkhāyī, addhā bhoto bhūmijassa satthā sabbesaṃyeva puthusamaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ muddhānaṃ maññe āhacca tiṭṭhatī’ti. ‘Kaccāhaṃ, bhante, evaṃ puṭṭho evaṃ byākaramāno vuttavādī ceva bhagavato homi, na ca bhagavantaṃ abhūtena abbhācikkhāmi, dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākaromi, na ca koci sahadhammiko vādānuvādo gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchatī’’’ti?

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Taggha <pb ed="V" n="3.0180"/><pb ed="P" n="3.0140"/> tvaṃ, bhūmija, evaṃ puṭṭho evaṃ byākaramāno vuttavādī ceva me hosi, na ca maṃ abhūtena abbhācikkhasi, dhammassa cānudhammaṃ byākarosi, na ca koci sahadhammiko vādānuvādo gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Ayoni <pb ed="T" n="3.0277"/> hesā, bhūmija, phalassa adhigamāya.

Then, venerable Bhumija, having returned from his alms round after his meal, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, venerable Bhumija told the Blessed One: "Here, venerable sir, having dressed in the morning, taking my bowl and robe, I approached Jayasena the prince's dwelling. Having approached, I sat down on the seat that was arranged.

Then, venerable sir, Jayasena the prince approached me; having approached, he exchanged greetings with me.

Having concluded the courteous and amiable talk, he sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, venerable sir, Jayasena the prince told me: 'Master Bhumija, there are some ascetics and brāhmaṇas who assert such views as this: "If one makes an aspiration and leads the holy life (brahmacariya), one is unable to procure any fruit; if one makes no aspiration… if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and leads the holy life (brahmacariya), one is unable to procure any fruit; if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and leads the holy life (brahmacariya), one is unable to procure any fruit."

"What does Master Bhumija's teacher say and declare in this regard?" When this was said, venerable sir, I told Jayasena the prince: "Prince, I have not heard and learned this from the Blessed One's own lips.

But it is possible that the Blessed One might say this: "If one makes an aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariya) unwisely, one is unable to procure any fruit; if one makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariya) unwisely, one is unable to procure any fruit; if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariya) unwisely, one is unable to procure any fruit; if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariya) unwisely, one is unable to procure any fruit.

However, if one makes an aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariya) wisely, one is able to procure fruit; if one makes no aspiration… if one both makes an aspiration and makes no aspiration… if one neither makes an aspiration nor makes no aspiration and one leads the holy life (brahmacariya) wisely, one is able to procure fruit."

Prince, I have not heard and learned this from the Blessed One's own lips.

But it is possible that the Blessed One would answer as I have stated.'

'If Master Bhumija's teacher speaks thus, if he declares thus, then it certainly seems that venerable Bhumija's teacher stands ahead of all the ordinary ascetics and brāhmaṇas.'

'Venerable sir, when asked such a question and answering thus, am I a speaker of what the Blessed One has said, and not misrepresenting the Blessed One with what is contrary to fact, explaining in accordance with the dharma (dhamma) in such a way that nothing which provides a ground for censure can be legitimately deduced from my assertion?'"

"Surely, Bhumija, when you were asked such a question and answered thus, you said what has been said by me and did not misrepresent me with what is contrary to fact. You explained in accordance with the dharma (dhamma) in such a way that nothing which provides a ground for censure can be legitimately deduced from your assertion.

Whatever ascetics or brāhmaṇas, Bhumija, have wrong view, wrong intention, wrong speech, wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, and wrong concentration, if they make an aspiration and they lead the holy life (brahmacariya), they are unable to procure any fruit; if they make no aspiration and they lead the holy life (brahmacariya), they are unable to procure any fruit; if they both make an aspiration and make no aspiration and they lead the holy life (brahmacariya), they are unable to procure any fruit; if they neither make an aspiration nor make no aspiration and they lead the holy life (brahmacariya), they are unable to procure any fruit.

What is the reason for that? Because that wrong path is not a proper method for procuring fruit.

226. “Suppose, Bhūmija, a man needing butter (navanīta), seeking butter, wandering in search of butter, were to pour water into a churn (kalasa) and churn it with a churning-stick (mattha).
Then, if he made an aspiration and poured water into a churn and churned it with a churning stick, he would still be unable to procure any butter; if he made no aspiration…and so on…if he both made an aspiration and made no aspiration…and so on…if he neither made an aspiration nor made no aspiration and acted thus, he would still be unable to procure any butter.
What is the reason for that? It is because it is unwise, Bhūmija, to try and procure butter in that way.
So too, Bhūmija, whatever recluses (śramaṇa) and Brahmins (brāhmaṇa) have wrong view…and so on… wrong concentration (micchāsamādhino), if they make an aspiration and they lead the holy life (brahmacariya), they are unable to procure any fruit (phala); if they make no aspiration…and so on…if they both make an aspiration and make no aspiration…and so on…if they neither make an aspiration nor make no aspiration and lead the holy life, they are still unable to procure any fruit.
What is the reason for that? It is because it is unwise, Bhūmija, to try and procure fruit in that way.

“Suppose, Bhūmija, a man needing fire (agni), seeking fire, wandering in search of fire, were to take an upper fire-stick (uttarāraṇi) and rub a dry sapless piece of wood with it.

What is the reason for that? It is because it is unwise, Bhūmija, to try and procure fruit in that way.
Whatever recluses and Brahmins have right view (sammādiṭṭhino), right intention (sammāsaṅkappā), right speech (sammāvācā), right action (sammākammantā), right livelihood (sammāājīvā), right effort (sammāvāyāmā), right mindfulness (sammāsatī), and right concentration (sammāsamādhino), if they make an aspiration and they lead the holy life, they are able to procure fruit; if they make no aspiration and they lead the holy life, they are still able to procure fruit; if they both make an aspiration and make no aspiration and they lead the holy life, they are still able to procure fruit; if they neither make an aspiration nor make no aspiration and they lead the holy life, they are still able to procure fruit.

228. “Suppose, Bhūmija, a man needing butter ( navanīta), seeking butter, wandering in search of butter, were to pour curd (dadhi) into a churn and churn it with a churning-stick (mattha).

“Bhūmija, if these four similes had occurred to you with reference to Prince Jayasena, he would have spontaneously acquired confidence in you, and being confident, would have shown his confidence to you.”

“But how could these four similes, unheard of before and beyond expectation, have occurred to me with reference to Prince Jayasena, reverend Sir, as they have to the Blessed One?”

Thus spoke the Lord.

The venerable Bhūmija was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Bhūmijasutta, the sixth, is ended.
