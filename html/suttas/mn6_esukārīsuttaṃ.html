436. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho esukārī brāhmaṇo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho esukārī brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, catasso pāricariyā paññapenti – brāhmaṇassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti, khattiyassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti, vessassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti, suddassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti. Tatridaṃ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā brāhmaṇassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti – ‘brāhmaṇo vā brāhmaṇaṃ paricareyya, khattiyo vā brāhmaṇaṃ paricareyya, vesso vā brāhmaṇaṃ paricareyya, suddo vā brāhmaṇaṃ paricareyyā’ti. Idaṃ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā brāhmaṇassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti. Tatridaṃ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā khattiyassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti – ‘khattiyo vā khattiyaṃ paricareyya, vesso vā khattiyaṃ paricareyya, suddo vā khattiyaṃ paricareyyā’ti. Idaṃ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā khattiyassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti. Tatridaṃ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā vessassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti – ‘vesso vā vessaṃ paricareyya, suddo vā vessaṃ paricareyyā’ti. Idaṃ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā vessassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti . Tatridaṃ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā suddassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti – ‘suddova suddaṃ paricareyya. Ko panañño suddaṃ paricarissatī’ti? Idaṃ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā suddassa pāricariyaṃ paññapenti. Brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, imā catasso pāricariyā paññapenti. Idha bhavaṃ gotamo kimāhā’’ti?
436.

Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī), in the Jeta Grove (Jetavana), Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park (Anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

Then the brāhmaṇa (Brahmin) Esukārī approached the Blessed One and exchanged greetings with him. Having concluded their greetings and cordial talk, he sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, the brāhmaṇa Esukārī said to the Blessed One: " Brāhmaṇas, Master Gotama, prescribe four levels of service (pāricariyā): the level of service to a brāhmaṇa, the level of service to a khattiya (noble), the level of service to a vessa (merchant), and the level of service to a sudda (worker).

Therein, Master Gotama, brāhmaṇas prescribe this as the level of service towards a brāhmaṇa: 'A brāhmaṇa may serve a brāhmaṇa, a khattiya may serve a brāhmaṇa, a vessa may serve a brāhmaṇa, and a sudda may serve a brāhmaṇa.'

That, Master Gotama, is the level of service towards a brāhmaṇa that brāhmaṇas prescribe.

Therein, Master Gotama, brāhmaṇas prescribe this as the level of service towards a khattiya: 'A khattiya may serve a khattiya, a vessa may serve a khattiya, and a sudda may serve a khattiya.'

That, Master Gotama, is the level of service towards a khattiya that brāhmaṇas prescribe.

Therein, Master Gotama, brāhmaṇas prescribe this as the level of service towards a vessa: 'A vessa may serve a vessa, and a sudda may serve a vessa.'

That, Master Gotama, is the level of service towards a vessa that brāhmaṇas prescribe.

Therein, Master Gotama, brāhmaṇas prescribe this as the level of service towards a sudda: 'Only a sudda may serve a sudda.

Who else would serve a sudda?' That, Master Gotama, is the level of service towards a sudda that brāhmaṇas prescribe.

Brāhmaṇas, Master Gotama, prescribe these four levels of service.

What does Master Gotama say about this?"

437. ‘‘Kiṃ pana, brāhmaṇa, sabbo loko brāhmaṇānaṃ etadabbhanujānāti – ‘imā catasso pāricariyā paññapentū’’’ti paññapentīti (sī. ka.)? ‘‘No hidaṃ, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, brāhmaṇa, puriso daliddo daḷiddo (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) assako anāḷhiyo. Tassa akāmassa bilaṃ olaggeyyuṃ – ‘idaṃ te, ambho purisa, maṃsaṃ khāditabbaṃ, mūlañca anuppadātabba’nti. Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, brāhmaṇā appaṭiññāya tesaṃ samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ, atha ca panimā catasso pāricariyā paññapenti. Nāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘sabbaṃ paricaritabba’nti vadāmi; nāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘sabbaṃ na paricaritabba’nti vadāmi. Yaṃ hissa, brāhmaṇa, paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, nāhaṃ taṃ ‘paricaritabba’nti vadāmi; yañca khvāssa, brāhmaṇa, paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo tamahaṃ ‘paricaritabba’nti vadāmi. Khattiyaṃ cepi, brāhmaṇa, evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘yaṃ vā te paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, yaṃ vā te paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo; kamettha paricareyyāsī’ti, khattiyopi hi, brāhmaṇa , sammā byākaramāno evaṃ byākareyya – ‘yañhi me paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, nāhaṃ taṃ paricareyyaṃ; yañca kho me paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo tamahaṃ paricareyya’nti. Brāhmaṇaṃ cepi, brāhmaṇa…pe… vessaṃ cepi, brāhmaṇa…pe… suddaṃ cepi, brāhmaṇa, evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘yaṃ vā te paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, yaṃ vā te paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo; kamettha paricareyyāsī’ti, suddopi hi, brāhmaṇa, sammā byākaramāno evaṃ byākareyya – ‘yañhi me paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, nāhaṃ taṃ paricareyyaṃ; yañca kho me paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo tamahaṃ paricareyya’nti. Nāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uccākulīnatā seyyaṃso’ti vadāmi, na panāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uccākulīnatā pāpiyaṃso’ti vadāmi; nāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uḷāravaṇṇatā seyyaṃso’ti vadāmi, na panāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uḷāravaṇṇatā pāpiyaṃso’ti vadāmi; nāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uḷārabhogatā seyyaṃso’ti vadāmi, na panāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uḷārabhogatā pāpiyaṃso’ti vadāmi.
437. “Well, brahmin, has all the world authorised the brāhmaṇas to prescribe these four levels of service (catasso pāricariyā)?” “No, Master Gotama.” “Just as, brahmin, a man is poor, wretched, and destitute. If they were to force a cut of meat upon that unwilling man and tell him: ‘Good man, you must eat this meat and pay for it.’ So too, brahmin, without the consent of those other śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas, the brāhmaṇas nevertheless prescribe those four levels of service (catasso pāricariyā). I do not say, brahmin, that all are to be served (sabbaṃ paricaritabba), nor do I say that none are to be served (sabbaṃ na paricaritabba). For if, when serving someone, one becomes worse and not better because of that service, then I say that he should not be served (paricaritabba). And if, when serving someone, one becomes better and not worse because of that service, then I say that he should be served (paricaritabba). “If they were to ask a noble thus: ‘Which of these should you serve - one in whose service you become worse and not better when serving him, or one in whose service you become better and not worse when serving him?’ Answering rightly, a noble would answer thus: ‘I should not serve the one in whose service I become worse and not better when serving him; I should serve the one in whose service I become better and not worse when serving him.’ “If they were to ask a brāhmaṇa…to ask a merchant…to ask a worker…answering rightly, a worker would answer thus: ‘I should not serve the one in whose service I become worse and not better when serving him; I should serve the one in whose service I become better and not worse when serving him.’ “I do not say, brahmin, that one is better because one is from an aristocratic family (uccākulīnatā), nor do I say that one is worse because one is from an aristocratic family (uccākulīnatā). I do not say that one is better because one is of great beauty (uḷāravaṇṇatā), nor do I say that one is worse because one is of great beauty (uḷāravaṇṇatā). I do not say that one is better because one is of great wealth (uḷārabhogatā), nor do I say that one is worse because one is of great wealth (uḷārabhogatā).
437.Evaṃme sutanti esukārīsuttaṃ. Tattha bilaṃ olaggeyyunti koṭṭhāsaṃ laggāpeyyuṃ, iminā satthadhammaṃ nāma dasseti. Satthavāho kira mahākantārapaṭipanno antarāmagge goṇe mate maṃsaṃ gahetvā sabbesaṃ satthikānaṃ ‘‘idaṃ khāditvā ettakaṃ mūlaṃ dātabba’’nti koṭṭhāsaṃ olaggeti, goṇamaṃsaṃ nāma khādantāpi atthi akhādantāpi, mūlaṃ dātuṃ sakkontāpi asakkontāpi. Satthavāho yena mūlena goṇo gahito, tassa nikkhamanatthaṃ sabbesaṃ balakkārena koṭṭhāsaṃ datvā mūlaṃ gaṇhāti, ayaṃ satthadhammo. Evamevaṃ brāhmaṇāpi lokassa paṭiññaṃ aggahetvā attanova dhammatāya catasso pāricariyā paññapentīti dassetuṃ evameva khotiādimāha. Pāpiyo assāti pāpaṃ assa. Seyyo assāti hitaṃ assa. Atha vā pāpiyoti pāpako lāmako attabhāvo assa. Seyyoti seṭṭho uttamo. Seyyaṃsoti seyyo. Uccākulīnatāti uccākulīnattena seyyo. Pāpiyaṃsoti pāpiyo. Uccākulīnatā ca dvīsu kulesu vaḍḍheti khattiyakule brāhmaṇakule ca, uḷāravaṇṇatā tīsu. Vessopi hi uḷāravaṇṇo hoti. Uḷārabhogatā catūsupi. Suddopi hi antamaso caṇḍālopi uḷārabhogo hotiyeva.
437. Thus, "I have heard" is the Esukārī Sutta.

There, "they would hang up a portion" means they would fix a share. By this, he shows what is called "the doctrine of the caravan."

A caravan leader, it seems, having set out on a great desert journey, when an ox dies on the way, takes the meat and hangs up a share for all the members of the caravan, saying, "Having eaten this, such and such a price must be paid." There are those who eat the ox meat and those who do not eat it, those who are able to pay the price and those who are not able to pay it.

The caravan leader, having forcibly given a share to all and taken the price for which the ox was taken, for the purpose of release, this is the doctrine of the caravan.

Just as the Brahmins, without accepting a pledge from the world, propound four services according to their own nature, to show this, he said, "Just so, indeed," and so forth.

"Worse would be" means evil would be.

"Better would be" means benefit would be.

Or, "worse" means an evil, wretched state of existence would be.

"Better" means the best, the highest.

"Of better lineage" means better by high lineage.

"Of worse lineage" means worse.

High lineage increases in two families, in the Khattiya family and in the Brahmin family, and excellent complexion in three.

Even a Vessa has an excellent complexion.

Excellent prosperity is in all four.

Even a Sudda, even a Caṇḍāla, has excellent prosperity.

438. ‘‘Uccākulīnopi hi, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco pāṇātipātī hoti, adinnādāyī hoti, kāmesumicchācārī hoti, musāvādī hoti, pisuṇāvāco hoti, pharusāvāco hoti, samphappalāpī hoti, abhijjhālu hoti , byāpannacitto hoti, micchādiṭṭhi hoti. Tasmā ‘na uccākulīnatā seyyaṃso’ti vadāmi. Uccākulīnopi hi, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti, adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti, kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato hoti, musāvādā paṭivirato hoti, pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti, pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti, samphappalāpā paṭivirato hoti, anabhijjhālu hoti, abyāpannacitto hoti, sammādiṭṭhi hoti. Tasmā ‘na uccākulīnatā pāpiyaṃso’ti vadāmi.
“438. Furthermore, brahmin, here some person of high birth may be one who kills living beings (pāṇātipātī), takes what is not given (adinnādāyī), engages in sexual misconduct (kāmesumicchācārī), speaks falsely (musāvādī), speaks malicious words (pisuṇāvāco), speaks harsh words (pharusāvāco), engages in idle chatter (samphappalāpī), is covetous (abhijjhālu), has a mind of ill will (byāpannacitto), and holds wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi).

Therefore, I do not say that being of high birth (uccākulīnatā) is superior.

Furthermore, brahmin, here some person of high birth may be one who abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato), abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato), abstains from sexual misconduct (kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato), abstains from false speech (musāvādā paṭivirato), abstains from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato), abstains from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato), abstains from idle chatter (samphappalāpā paṭivirato), is uncovetous (anabhijjhālu), has a mind without ill will (abyāpannacitto), and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi).

Therefore, I do not say that being of high birth (uccākulīnatā) is inferior.

439. ‘‘Uḷāravaṇṇopi hi, brāhmaṇa…pe… uḷārabhogopi hi, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco pāṇātipātī hoti…pe… micchādiṭṭhi hoti. Tasmā ‘na uḷārabhogatā seyyaṃso’ti vadāmi. Uḷārabhogopi hi, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti. Tasmā ‘na uḷārabhogatā pāpiyaṃso’ti vadāmi. Nāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘sabbaṃ paricaritabba’nti vadāmi, na panāhaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ‘sabbaṃ na paricaritabba’nti vadāmi. Yaṃ hissa, brāhmaṇa, paricarato pāricariyāhetu saddhā vaḍḍhati, sīlaṃ vaḍḍhati, sutaṃ vaḍḍhati, cāgo vaḍḍhati, paññā vaḍḍhati, tamahaṃ ‘paricaritabba’nti (vadāmi. Yaṃ hissa, brāhmaṇa, paricarato pāricariyāhetu na saddhā vaḍḍhati, na sīlaṃ vaḍḍhati, na sutaṃ vaḍḍhati, na cāgo vaḍḍhati, na paññā vaḍḍhati, nāhaṃ taṃ ‘paricaritabba’nti) ( ) etthantare pāṭho sī. syā. kaṃ. pī. potthakesu natthi vadāmī’’ti.
“Here, brahmin, one of great beauty…one of great wealth may kill living beings…and hold wrong view.

Therefore I say that one is not better because one is of great wealth.

But also, brahmin, one of great beauty…of great wealth may abstain from killing living beings…and hold right view.

Therefore I do not say that one is worse because one is of great wealth.

“I do not say, brahmin, that all are to be served, nor do I say that none are to be served.

For if, when serving someone, one’s faith (saddhā), virtue (sīla), learning (suta), generosity (cāga), and wisdom (prajñā) increase in his service, then I say that he should be served.”

For if, when serving someone, one’s faith does not increase, nor virtue, nor learning, nor generosity, nor wisdom, then I do not say that he should be served.

440. Evaṃ vutte, esukārī brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, cattāri dhanāni paññapenti – brāhmaṇassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti, khattiyassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti, vessassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti, suddassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti. Tatridaṃ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā brāhmaṇassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti bhikkhācariyaṃ; bhikkhācariyañca pana brāhmaṇo sandhanaṃ atimaññamāno akiccakārī hoti gopova adinnaṃ ādiyamānoti. Idaṃ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā brāhmaṇassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti. Tatridaṃ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā khattiyassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti dhanukalāpaṃ; dhanukalāpañca pana khattiyo sandhanaṃ atimaññamāno akiccakārī hoti gopova adinnaṃ ādiyamānoti. Idaṃ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā khattiyassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti. Tatridaṃ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā vessassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti kasigorakkhaṃ; kasigorakkhañca pana vesso sandhanaṃ atimaññamāno akiccakārī hoti gopova adinnaṃ ādiyamānoti. Idaṃ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā vessassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti. Tatridaṃ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā suddassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti asitabyābhaṅgiṃ; asitabyābhaṅgiñca pana suddo sandhanaṃ atimaññamāno akiccakārī hoti gopova adinnaṃ ādiyamānoti. Idaṃ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā suddassa sandhanaṃ paññapenti. Brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, imāni cattāri dhanāni paññapenti. Idha bhavaṃ gotamo kimāhā’’ti?
When this was said, the brāhmaṇa Esukārī said this to the Blessed One: "Master Gotama, the brāhmaṇas describe four kinds of wealth (dhanāni). They describe the wealth of a brāhmaṇa, the wealth of a kṣatriya, the wealth of a vaiśya, and the wealth of a śūdra.

"And therein, Master Gotama, the brāhmaṇas describe the wealth (sandhanaṃ) of a brāhmaṇa as the practice of begging for alms (bhikkhācariyaṃ); but a brāhmaṇa who despises that wealth (sandhanaṃ), the practice of begging for alms (bhikkhācariyaṃ), is an idler, like a herdsman who takes what is not given. That, Master Gotama, is what the brāhmaṇas describe as the wealth (sandhanaṃ) of a brāhmaṇa.

"And therein, Master Gotama, the brāhmaṇas describe the wealth (sandhanaṃ) of a kṣatriya as the bow and quiver (dhanukalāpaṃ); but a kṣatriya who despises that wealth (sandhanaṃ), the bow and quiver (dhanukalāpaṃ), is an idler, like a herdsman who takes what is not given. That, Master Gotama, is what the brāhmaṇas describe as the wealth (sandhanaṃ) of a kṣatriya.

"And therein, Master Gotama, the brāhmaṇas describe the wealth (sandhanaṃ) of a vaiśya as farming and cattle-raising (kasigorakkhaṃ); but a vaiśya who despises that wealth (sandhanaṃ), farming and cattle-raising (kasigorakkhaṃ), is an idler, like a herdsman who takes what is not given. That, Master Gotama, is what the brāhmaṇas describe as the wealth (sandhanaṃ) of a vaiśya.

"And therein, Master Gotama, the brāhmaṇas describe the wealth (sandhanaṃ) of a śūdra as the sickle and carrying pole (asitabyābhaṅgiṃ); but a śūdra who despises that wealth (sandhanaṃ), the sickle and carrying pole (asitabyābhaṅgiṃ), is an idler, like a herdsman who takes what is not given. That, Master Gotama, is what the brāhmaṇas describe as the wealth (sandhanaṃ) of a śūdra.

"Master Gotama, these are the four kinds of wealth (dhanāni) that the brāhmaṇas describe. What does Master Gotama say about this?"
440.Bhikkhācariyanti koṭidhanenapi hi brāhmaṇena bhikkhā caritabbāva, porāṇakabrāhmaṇā asītikoṭidhanāpi ekavelaṃ bhikkhaṃ caranti. Kasmā? Duggatakāle carantānaṃ idāni bhikkhaṃ carituṃ āraddhāti garahā na bhavissatīti. Atimaññamānoti yo bhikkhācariyavaṃsaṃ haritvā sattajīvakasikammavaṇijjādīhi jīvikaṃ kappeti, ayaṃ atimaññati nāma. Gopo vāti yathā gopako attanā rakkhitabbaṃ bhaṇḍaṃ thenento akiccakārī hoti, evanti attho. Iminā nayena sabbavāresu attho veditabbo. Asitabyābhaṅginti tiṇalāyanaasitañceva kājañca. Anussaratoti yattha jāto, tasmiṃ porāṇe mātāpettike kulavaṃse anussariyamāneti attho. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Esukārīsuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
440. Bhikkhācariya: Even a brāhmaṇa with wealth of a koṭi should still go begging for alms. Ancient brāhmaṇas with wealth of eighty koṭis would go for alms once a day. Why? So that when they go begging in times of poverty, there will be no criticism that they have only started begging now.

Atimaññamāno: This is someone who abandons the tradition of going for alms and makes a living through the crafts, trades, commerce, etc., that sustain life. This is what is called atimaññati.

Gopo vā: Just as a herdsman who steals goods that he should protect is one who does what should not be done; this is the meaning. In this way, the meaning should be understood in all instances.

Asitabyābhaṅgi: Both the asita made of tiṇalāyana and the kāja.

Anussarato: That is, remembering the ancient maternal and paternal family lineage in which he was born; this is the meaning.

The rest is clear throughout.

The commentary on the Esukārī Sutta in the Papañcasūdanī, the commentary to the Majjhimanikāya, is complete.

441. ‘‘Kiṃ pana, brāhmaṇa, sabbo loko brāhmaṇānaṃ etadabbhanujānāti – ‘imāni cattāri dhanāni paññapentū’’’ti? ‘‘No hidaṃ, bho gotama’’. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, brāhmaṇa, puriso daliddo assako anāḷhiyo. Tassa akāmassa bilaṃ olaggeyyuṃ – ‘idaṃ te, ambho purisa, maṃsaṃ khāditabbaṃ, mūlañca anuppadātabba’nti. Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, brāhmaṇā appaṭiññāya tesaṃ samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ, atha ca panimāni cattāri dhanāni paññapenti. Ariyaṃ kho ahaṃ, brāhmaṇa, lokuttaraṃ dhammaṃ purisassa sandhanaṃ paññapemi. Porāṇaṃ kho panassa mātāpettikaṃ kulavaṃsaṃ anussarato yattha yattheva attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti tena teneva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati. Khattiyakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘khattiyo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; brāhmaṇakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘brāhmaṇo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; vessakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘vesso’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; suddakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘suddo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati. Seyyathāpi, brāhmaṇa, yaṃyadeva paccayaṃ paṭicca aggi jalati tena teneva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati. Kaṭṭhañce paṭicca aggi jalati ‘kaṭṭhaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; sakalikañce paṭicca aggi jalati ‘sakalikaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; tiṇañce paṭicca aggi jalati ‘tiṇaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; gomayañce paṭicca aggi jalati ‘gomayaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati. Evameva kho ahaṃ, brāhmaṇa, ariyaṃ lokuttaraṃ dhammaṃ purisassa sandhanaṃ paññapemi. Porāṇaṃ kho panassa mātāpettikaṃ kulavaṃsaṃ anussarato yattha yattheva attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti tena teneva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati. ‘‘Khattiyakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘khattiyo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; brāhmaṇakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘brāhmaṇo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; vessakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘vesso’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati; suddakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘suddo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati. ‘‘Khattiyakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa, agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti, adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti, abrahmacariyā paṭivirato hoti, musāvādā paṭivirato hoti, pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti, pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti, samphappalāpā paṭivirato hoti, anabhijjhālu hoti, abyāpannacitto hoti, sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ. ‘‘Brāhmaṇakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa, agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ. ‘‘Vessakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa, agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ. ‘‘Suddakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa, agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ.
“Well, brahmin, has all the world authorised the brāhmaṇas to prescribe these four types of wealth?” “No, Gotama.”

“Just as, brahmin, they were to force a cut of meat upon a poor, resourceless, and helpless man and tell him: ‘Good man, you must eat this meat and pay for it’; so too, brahmin, without the consent of those other recluses and brāhmaṇas, the brāhmaṇas nevertheless prescribe these four types of wealth. I, brahmin, declare the noble supramundane Dhamma as a person’s own wealth (ariyaṃ kho ahaṃ, brāhmaṇa, lokuttaraṃ dhammaṃ purisassa sandhanaṃ paññapemi). But recollecting his ancient maternal and paternal family lineage, he is reckoned according to wherever he is reborn. If he is reborn in a clan of nobles (khattiyakule), he is reckoned as a noble (khattiyo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati); if he is reborn in a clan of brāhmaṇas, he is reckoned as a brāhmaṇa (brāhmaṇakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘brāhmaṇo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati); if he is reborn in a clan of merchants (vessakule), he is reckoned as a merchant (vesso’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati); if he is reborn in a clan of workers (suddakule), he is reckoned as a worker (suddo’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati). Just as fire is reckoned by the particular condition dependent on which it burns – when fire burns dependent on logs, it is reckoned as a log fire (kaṭṭhaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati); when fire burns dependent on faggots, it is reckoned as a faggot fire (sakalikaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati); when fire burns dependent on grass, it is reckoned as a grass fire (tiṇaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati); when fire burns dependent on cowdung, it is reckoned as a cowdung fire (gomayaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati) - so too, brahmin, I declare the noble supramundane Dhamma as a person’s own wealth. But recollecting his ancient maternal and paternal family lineage, he is reckoned according to wherever he is reborn.

“If he is reborn in a clan of nobles, he is reckoned as a noble; if he is reborn in a clan of brāhmaṇas, he is reckoned as a brāhmaṇa; if he is reborn in a clan of merchants, he is reckoned as a merchant; if he is reborn in a clan of workers, he is reckoned as a worker.

“If, brahmin, anyone from a clan of nobles goes forth from the home life into homelessness, and after encountering the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, he abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti), from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti), from incelibacy (abrahmacariyā paṭivirato hoti), from false speech (musāvādā paṭivirato hoti), from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti), from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti), and from gossip (samphappalāpā paṭivirato hoti), and is uncovetous (anabhijjhālu hoti), has a mind without ill will (abyāpannacitto hoti), and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi hoti), he is one who is accomplishing the true way, the Dhamma that is wholesome (ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ).

“If, brahmin, anyone from a clan of brāhmaṇas goes forth from the home life into homelessness, and after encountering the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, he abstains from killing living beings…and holds right view, he is one who is accomplishing the true way, the Dhamma that is wholesome.

“If, brahmin, anyone from a clan of merchants goes forth from the home life into homelessness, and after encountering the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, he abstains from killing living beings…and holds right view, he is one who is accomplishing the true way, the Dhamma that is wholesome.

“If anyone from a clan of workers goes forth from the home life into homelessness, and after encountering the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, he abstains from killing living beings…and holds right view, he is one who is accomplishing the true way, the Dhamma that is wholesome.
442
442. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, brāhmaṇa, brāhmaṇova nu kho pahoti asmiṃ padese averaṃ abyābajjhaṃ mettacittaṃ bhāvetuṃ, no khattiyo no vesso no suddo’’ti? ‘‘No hidaṃ, bho gotama. Khattiyopi hi, bho gotama, pahoti asmiṃ padese averaṃ abyābajjhaṃ mettacittaṃ bhāvetuṃ; brāhmaṇopi hi, bho gotama… vessopi hi, bho gotama… suddopi hi, bho gotama… sabbepi hi, bho gotama, cattāro vaṇṇā pahonti asmiṃ padese averaṃ abyābajjhaṃ mettacittaṃ bhāvetu’’nti. ‘‘Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, khattiyakulā cepi agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ. ‘‘Brāhmaṇakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa… vessakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa… suddakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa, agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ.
442. “What do you think, brahmin? Is only a brahmin capable of developing a mind of loving-kindness ( mettacittaṃ) towards a certain region, without hostility (averaṃ) and without ill will (abyābajjhaṃ), and not a noble (khattiyo), or a merchant (vesso), or a worker (suddo)?”

“No, indeed, sir (bho), Gotama. Whether it be a noble (khattiyo), sir (bho), Gotama, they are capable of developing a mind of loving-kindness (mettacittaṃ) towards a certain region, without hostility (averaṃ) and without ill will (abyābajjhaṃ); or a brahmin (brāhmaṇo), sir (bho), Gotama… or a merchant (vesso), sir (bho), Gotama… or a worker (suddo), sir (bho), Gotama… indeed all four castes (cattāro vaṇṇā), sir (bho), Gotama, are capable of developing a mind of loving-kindness (mettacittaṃ) towards a certain region, without hostility (averaṃ) and without ill will (abyābajjhaṃ).”

“So too, brahmin, if anyone from a clan of nobles (khattiyakulā) goes forth from the home life (agārasmā) into homelessness (anagāriyaṃ), and after encountering the Dhamma and Discipline (dhammavinayaṃ) proclaimed by the Tathāgata, he abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti)…and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), he is one who is accomplishing (ārādhako hoti) the true way (ñāyaṃ), the Dhamma (dhammaṃ) that is wholesome (kusalaṃ).

“If, brahmin, anyone from a clan of brahmins (brāhmaṇakulā)… or a clan of merchants (vessakulā), brahmin… or a clan of workers (suddakulā), brahmin, goes forth from the home life (agārasmā) into homelessness (anagāriyaṃ), and after encountering the Dhamma and Discipline (dhammavinayaṃ) proclaimed by the Tathāgata, he abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti)…and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), he is one who is accomplishing (ārādhako hoti) the true way (ñāyaṃ), the Dhamma (dhammaṃ) that is wholesome (kusalaṃ).

443. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, brāhmaṇa, brāhmaṇova nu kho pahoti sottisināniṃ ādāya nadiṃ gantvā rajojallaṃ pavāhetuṃ, no khattiyo no vesso no suddo’’ti? ‘‘No hidaṃ, bho gotama . Khattiyopi hi, bho gotama, pahoti sottisināniṃ ādāya nadiṃ gantvā rajojallaṃ pavāhetuṃ; brāhmaṇopi hi, bho gotama… vessopi hi, bho gotama … suddopi hi, bho gotama… sabbepi hi, bho gotama, cattāro vaṇṇā pahonti sottisināniṃ ādāya nadiṃ gantvā rajojallaṃ pavāhetu’’nti. ‘‘Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, khattiyakulā cepi agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ. ‘‘Brāhmaṇakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa… vessakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa… suddakulā cepi , brāhmaṇa, agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ.
“What do you think, brahmin? Is only a brāhmaṇa capable of taking a loofah and bath powder, going to the river, and washing off dust and dirt, and not a kṣatriya, or a vaiśya, or a śūdra?”

“Whether it be a kṣatriya, a brāhmaṇa, a vaiśya, or a śūdra — all four varṇas are capable of taking a loofah and bath powder, going to the river, and washing off dust and dirt.”

“So too, brahmin, if anyone from a clan of kṣatriyas goes forth from the home life into homelessness, and after encountering the dharma-vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata, he abstains from killing living beings…and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), he is one who is accomplishing the true way (ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ).”

“If, brahmin, anyone from a clan of brāhmaṇas, a clan of vaisyas, or a clan of śūdras goes forth from the home life into homelessness, and after encountering the dharma-vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata, he abstains from killing living beings…and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), he is one who is accomplishing the true way (ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ).”

444. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, brāhmaṇa, idha rājā khattiyo muddhāvasitto nānājaccānaṃ purisānaṃ purisasataṃ sannipāteyya – ‘āyantu bhonto ye tattha khattiyakulā brāhmaṇakulā rājaññakulā uppannā sākassa vā sālassa vā salaḷassa vā candanassa vā padumakassa vā uttarāraṇiṃ ādāya aggiṃ abhinibbattentu, tejo pātukarontu; āyantu pana bhonto ye tattha caṇḍālakulā nesādakulā venakulā rathakārakulā pukkusakulā uppannā sāpānadoṇiyā vā sūkaradoṇiyā vā rajakadoṇiyā vā eraṇḍakaṭṭhassa vā uttarāraṇiṃ ādāya aggiṃ abhinibbattentu, tejo pātukarontū’’’ti? ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, brāhmaṇa, yo evaṃ nu kho so khattiyakulā brāhmaṇakulā rājaññakulā uppannehi sākassa vā sālassa vā salaḷassa vā candanassa vā padumakassa vā uttarāraṇiṃ ādāya aggi abhinibbatto tejo pātukato so eva nu khvāssa aggi accimā ceva vaṇṇavā ca pabhassaro ca tena ca sakkā agginā aggikaraṇīyaṃ kātuṃ; yo pana so caṇḍālakulā nesādakulā venakulā rathakārakulā pukkusakulā uppannehi sāpānadoṇiyā vā sūkaradoṇiyā vā rajakadoṇiyā vā eraṇḍakaṭṭhassa vā uttarāraṇiṃ ādāya aggi abhinibbatto tejo pātukato svāssa aggi na ceva accimā na ca vaṇṇavā na ca pabhassaro na ca tena sakkā agginā aggikaraṇīyaṃ kātu’’nti? ‘‘No hidaṃ, bho gotama. Yopi hi so, bho gotama, khattiyakulā brāhmaṇakulā rājaññakulā uppannehi sākassa vā sālassa vā salaḷassa vā candanassa vā padumakassa vā uttarāraṇiṃ ādāya aggi abhinibbatto tejo pātukato svāssa aggi accimā ceva vaṇṇavā ca pabhassaro ca tena ca sakkā agginā aggikaraṇīyaṃ kātuṃ; yopi so caṇḍālakulā nesādakulā venakulā rathakārakulā pukkusakulā uppannehi sāpānadoṇiyā vā sūkaradoṇiyā vā rajakadoṇiyā vā eraṇḍakaṭṭhassa vā uttarāraṇiṃ ādāya aggi abhinibbatto tejo pātukato svāssa aggi accimā ceva vaṇṇavā ca pabhassaro ca tena ca sakkā agginā aggikaraṇīyaṃ kātuṃ. Sabbopi hi, bho gotama, aggi accimā ceva vaṇṇavā ca pabhassaro ca sabbenapi sakkā agginā aggikaraṇīyaṃ kātu’’nti. ‘‘Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, khattiyakulā cepi agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti…pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusalaṃ. Brāhmaṇakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa… vessakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa… suddakulā cepi, brāhmaṇa, agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito hoti, so ca tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ āgamma pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti, adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti, abrahmacariyā paṭivirato hoti, musāvādā paṭivirato hoti, pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti, pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti, samphappalāpā paṭivirato hoti, anabhijjhālu hoti, abyāpannacitto hoti, sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ārādhako hoti ñāyaṃ dhammaṃ kusala’’nti. Evaṃ vutte, esukārī brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama, abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama…pe… upāsakaṃ maṃ bhavaṃ gotamo dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gata’’nti. Esukārīsuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ.
“What do you think, brahmin? Suppose a noble king, a head-anointed one, were to assemble here a hundred men of different births, saying: ‘Let those who are born in noble families ( kṣatriyakulā ), Brahmin families ( brāhmaṇakulā ), or royal families ( rājaññakulā )—whether from teak, sāla, salaḷa, sandalwood candana, or padmaka wood—bring an upper fire-stick (uttarāraṇiṃ) and kindle a fire, produce heat. But let those who are born in outcaste (caṇḍāla) families, hunter (nesāda) families, veṇa families, chariot-maker (rathakāra) families, or pukkusa families—whether using a dog’s feeding bowl, a pig’s feeding bowl, a dyer’s bowl, or an castor-oil plant wood—bring an upper fire-stick (uttarāraṇiṃ) and kindle a fire, produce heat’?”

“What do you think, brahmin? When a fire is kindled and heat produced by those born in noble families ( kṣatriyakulā ), Brahmin families ( brāhmaṇakulā ), or royal families ( rājaññakulā )—whether from teak, sāla, salaḷa, sandalwood (candana), or padmaka wood—would that fire alone have a flame, color, and radiance, and only that fire could be used for the purposes of fire? And when a fire is kindled and heat produced by those born in outcaste (caṇḍāla) families, hunter (nesāda) families, veṇa families, chariot-maker (rathakāra) families, or pukkusa families—whether using a dog’s feeding bowl, a pig’s feeding bowl, a dyer’s bowl, or castor-oil plant wood—would that fire not have a flame, color, and radiance, and could that fire not be used for the purposes of fire?”

“No, Master Gotama.”

“So too, Master Gotama, when a fire is kindled and heat produced by those born in noble families (khattiyakulā), Brahmin families (brāhmaṇakulā), or royal families (rājaññakulā)—whether from teak, sāla, salaḷa, sandalwood (candana), or padmaka wood—that fire would have a flame, color, and radiance, and could be used for the purposes of fire. And when a fire is kindled and heat produced by those born in outcaste (caṇḍāla) families, hunter (nesāda) families, veṇa families, chariot-maker (rathakāra) families, or pukkusa families—whether using a dog’s feeding bowl, a pig’s feeding bowl, a dyer’s bowl, or castor-oil plant wood—that fire too would have a flame, color, and radiance, and could be used for the purposes of fire. For all fire, Master Gotama, has a flame, color, and radiance, and all fire can be used for the purposes of fire.”

“Just so, brahmin, if anyone from a clan of nobles (khattiyakulā) goes forth from the home life into homelessness, and after encountering the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the tathāgata , he abstains from killing living beings…and holds right view, he is one who is accomplishing the true way, the Dhamma that is wholesome. If anyone from a clan of Brahmins (brāhmaṇakulā), or merchants (vessakulā) or workers (śuddakulā) goes forth from the home life into homelessness, and after encountering the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the tathāgata , he abstains from killing living beings, from taking what is not given, from celibacy, from false speech, from malicious speech, from harsh speech, and from gossip, and is uncovetous, has a mind without ill will, and holds right view, he is one who is accomplishing the true way, the Dhamma that is wholesome.”

When this was said, the Brahmin Esukari said to the Blessed One (bhagavant): “Magnificent, Master Gotama! Magnificent, Master Gotama! …From today let Master Gotama remember me as a lay follower who has gone to him for refuge for life.”

The Esukari Sutta, the sixth, is finished.
