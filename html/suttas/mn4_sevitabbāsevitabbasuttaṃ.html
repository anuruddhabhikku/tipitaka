Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Sāvatthi.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks (bhikkhus), saying: “Bhikkhus!”

“Venerable sir (bhadante),” they replied to the Fortunate One.

The Blessed One said this: “Bhikkhus, I shall teach you a discourse on what should be cultivated and what should not be cultivated (sevitabbāsevitabbaṃ vo, bhikkhave, dhammapariyāyaṃ desessāmi).

Listen & pay close attention. I will speak.”

“As you say, lord (evaṃ, bhante),” the monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Bhikkhus, I say that bodily conduct (kāyasamācāra) is of two kinds (duvidhena): to be cultivated (sevitabbaṃpi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbaṃpi); and that bodily conduct is either the one or the other (tañca aññamaññaṃ kāyasamācāraṃ).

Verbal conduct (vacīsamācāra) is of two kinds (duvidhena), I say: to be cultivated (sevitabbaṃpi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbaṃpi); and verbal conduct is either the one or the other (tañca aññamaññaṃ vacīsamācāraṃ).

Mental conduct (manosamācāra) is of two kinds (duvidhena), I say: to be cultivated (sevitabbaṃpi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbaṃpi); and mental conduct is either the one or the other (tañca aññamaññaṃ manosamācāraṃ).

Inclination of mind (cittuppāda) is of two kinds (duvidhena), I say: to be cultivated (sevitabbaṃpi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbaṃpi); and inclination’ of mind is either the one or the other (tañca aññamaññaṃ cittuppādaṃ).

The acquisition of perception (saññāpaṭilābha) is of two kinds (duvidhena), I say: to be cultivated (sevitabbaṃpi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbaṃpi); and the acquisition of perception is either the one or the other (tañca aññamaññaṃ saññāpaṭilābhaṃ).

The acquisition of view (diṭṭhipaṭilābha) is of two kinds (duvidhena), I say: to be cultivated (sevitabbaṃpi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbaṃpi); and the acquisition of view is either the one or the other (tañca aññamaññaṃ diṭṭhipaṭilābhaṃ).

The acquisition of individuality (attabhāvapaṭilābha) is of two kinds (duvidhena), I say: to be cultivated (sevitabbaṃpi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbaṃpi); and the acquisition of individuality is either the one or the other.”

When this was said, the venerable Sāriputta said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, I understand the detailed meaning of the Blessed One’s utterance, which he has spoken in brief without expounding the detailed meaning, to be thus:

Thus. This I have heard: The Sevitabbāsevitabbasuttaṃ (The Discourse on What Should and Should Not Be Cultivated).

Therein, "bodily conduct that is to be cultivated and bodily conduct that is not to be cultivated" means: I say that one kind of bodily conduct should be cultivated, and another kind should not be cultivated. One should cultivate what should be cultivated by some method, not what should not be cultivated; what should not be cultivated or what should be cultivated—this I do not say, is the meaning.

The same method applies in the case of verbal conduct, and so on (vacīsamācārādīsu eseva nayo).

Thus, the Blessed One, with seven categories (sattahi padehi), having established the matrix (mātikaṃ) and without elaborately analyzing it, completed the teaching (desanaṃ niṭṭhāpesi).

Why? For the purpose of giving an opportunity to the venerable Śāriputta (sāriputtattherassa okāsakaraṇatthaṃ).

111. “And what kind of bodily conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states (akuśalā dharmā) to increase and wholesome states (kuśalā dharmā) to diminish in one who cultivates it? Here, venerable sir, someone kills living beings (prāṇātipātī); he is murderous, bloody-handed, given to blows and violence, merciless to living beings. He takes what is not given (adinnādāyī); he takes by way of theft the wealth and property of others in the village or in the forest. He misconducts himself in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārī); he has intercourse with such women as are protected by their mother, father, mother and father, brother, sister, or relatives, who have a husband, who are protected by law, and even with those who are garlanded in token of betrothal. Such bodily conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to increase and wholesome states to diminish in one who cultivates it.

“And what kind of bodily conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to diminish and wholesome states to increase in one who cultivates it? Here, venerable sir, someone, abandoning the taking of life (prāṇātipātaṃ pahāya), abstains from taking life (prāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti); he has laid aside the stick and the sword, is scrupulous, kind, and dwells full of compassion for all living beings. Abandoning the taking of what is not given (adinnādānaṃ pahāya), he abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti); he does not take by way of theft the wealth and property of others in the village or in the forest. Abandoning misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācāraṃ pahāya), he abstains from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato hoti); he does not have intercourse with women who are protected by their mother, father, mother and father, brother, sister, or relatives, who have a husband, who are protected by law, or with those who are garlanded in token of betrothal. Such bodily conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to diminish and wholesome states to increase in one who cultivates it.

“Bhikkhus, bodily conduct (kāyasamācāraṃ) is of two kinds, I say: to be cultivated (sevitabbampi) and not to be cultivated (asevitabbampi); and bodily conduct (kāyasamācāraṃ) is either the one or the other’—so it was with reference to this that it was said by the Blessed One.

But such verbal conduct as causes unwholesome states (akuśalā dharmā) to diminish and wholesome states (kuśalā dharmā) to increase in one who cultivates it should be cultivated (sevitabbo).

112. “And what kind of verbal conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to increase and wholesome states to diminish? Here, venerable sir, someone is a liar; when summoned to a court, or to a meeting, or to his relatives’ presence, or to his guild, or to the royal family’s presence, and questioned as a witness thus: ‘So, good man, tell what you know,’ not knowing, he says, ‘I know,’ or knowing, he says, ‘I do not know’; not seeing, he says, ‘I see,’ or seeing, he says, ‘I do not see’— in this way, for his own ends ( attahetu ) , or for another’s ends ( parahetu ), or for some trifling worldly end ( āmisakiñcikkhahetu ), he speaks falsehood in full awareness. He speaks maliciously; having heard something here, he repeats it elsewhere in order to divide those people from these; or having heard something elsewhere, he repeats it to these people in order to divide these people from those; thus he is one who causes division among those who are united, or one who encourages those who are divided; he delights in discord, rejoices in discord, enjoys discord, a speaker of words that create discord; he speaks harsh speech; that speech is thorny, rough, hard, bitter, offensive to others, connected with anger, conducive to lack of concentration; he speaks such speech; he is a gossip; he speaks untruthfully, he speaks what is devoid of benefit, he speaks what is contrary to the Dhamma, he speaks what is contrary to the discipline; he speaks speech that is without a foundation, at the wrong time, without reason, boundless, and connected with what is not beneficial— such verbal conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to increase and wholesome states to diminish."

“And what kind of verbal conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to diminish and wholesome states to increase? Here, venerable sir, someone, abandoning false speech, abstains from false speech; when summoned to a court, or to a meeting, or to his relatives’ presence, or to his guild, or to the royal family’s presence, and questioned as a witness thus: ‘So, good man, tell what you know,’ not knowing, he says, ‘I do not know,’ or knowing, he says, ‘I know’; not seeing, he says, ‘I do not see,’ or seeing, he says, ‘I see’— in this way, for his own ends ( attahetu ), or for another’s ends ( parahetu ), or for some trifling worldly end ( āmisakiñcikkhahetu ), he does not speak falsehood in full awareness. Abandoning malicious speech, he abstains from malicious speech; having heard something here, he does not repeat it elsewhere in order to divide those people from these; or having heard something elsewhere, he does not repeat it to these people in order to divide these people from those; thus he is one who reunites those who are divided, a promoter of friendships, who enjoys concord, rejoices in concord, delights in concord, a speaker of words that promote concord. Abandoning harsh speech, he abstains from harsh speech; he speaks such speech as is blameless, pleasing to the ear, agreeable, reaching the heart, polite, liked by many people, and pleasing to many people. Abandoning gossip, he abstains from gossip; he speaks at the right time, he speaks truthfully, he speaks what is connected with benefit, he speaks what is connected with the dharma ( dhammavādī ), he speaks what is connected with the discipline; he speaks speech that has a foundation, at the right time, with reason, bounded, and connected with benefit— such verbal conduct, venerable sir, causes unwholesome states to diminish and wholesome states to increase."

“I, monks, describe verbal conduct as twofold: to be cultivated and not to be cultivated; and verbal conduct is either the one or the other”— it was with reference to this that this was said by the Blessed One.

“And what was it with reference to that this was said? Such mental conduct as causes unwholesome states to increase and wholesome states to diminish should not be cultivated; but such mental conduct as causes unwholesome states to diminish and wholesome states to increase should be cultivated."

Apariniṭṭhitabhāvāyā means for the sake of the uncompleted state of existence (bhavānaṃ apariniṭṭhitabhāvāya).

Thus [are] the Stream-enterer (sotāpanna), Once-returner (sakadāgāmi), and Non-returner (anāgāmino).
Let it be so with ordinary people and so on; how does a Non-returner (anāgāmī) produce (abhinibbatteti) a self-nature with suffering (sabyābajjhaṃ attabhāvaṃ)? And how do unwholesome qualities (akusalā dhammā) increase (abhivaḍḍhanti) for him, and wholesome qualities (kusalā dhammā) decline (parihāyanti)?
Even a Non-returner (anāgāmīpi), having been born (nibbatto) in the Pure Abodes (suddhāvāse), looks at the garden, the celestial mansions, and the wish-fulfilling trees (uyyānavimānakapparukkhe oloketvā) and exclaims, “Oh, happiness! Oh, happiness!” (‘‘aho sukhaṃ aho sukha’’nti udānaṃ udāneti). The Non-returner's (anāgāmino) desire for existence (bhavalobho), craving for existence (bhavataṇhā) are not abandoned (appahīnāva honti). Because of his craving that is not abandoned (tassa appahīnataṇhatāya), unwholesome qualities (akusalā) increase (vaḍḍhanti), wholesome qualities (kusalā) decline (parihāyanti), he produces (abhinibbatteti) a self-nature with suffering (sadukkhameva attabhāvaṃ), and it should be understood that he is in a state of uncompleted existence (apariniṭṭhitabhavoyeva hotīti veditabbo).

Abyābajjha means without suffering (a-dukkhaṃ).
This too should be understood in terms of four types of people (ayampi catunnaṃ janānaṃ vasena veditabbo).
For even an ordinary person (yo hi puthujjanopi) who is able (sakkoti) to complete existence (bhavaṃ pariniṭṭhāpetuṃ) with that self-nature (tenattabhāvena), does not take (na gaṇhāti) rebirth (puna paṭisandhiṃ), from the moment of the taking of rebirth (tassa paṭisandhiggahaṇato paṭṭhāya) unwholesome qualities (akusalā) decline (parihāyanti), wholesome qualities (kusalāyeva) increase (vaḍḍhanti), and he produces (nibbatteti) a self-nature without suffering (adukkhameva attabhāvaṃ), and he is truly in a state of completed existence (pariniṭṭhitabhavoyeva nāma hoti).
Thus [are] the Stream-enterer (sotāpanna), Once-returner (sakadāgāmi), and Non-returner (anāgāmino).
Let it be so with Stream-enterers and so on; how does an ordinary person (puthujjano) produce (nibbatteti) a self-nature without suffering (abyābajjhaattabhāvaṃ)? And how do the decline of unwholesome qualities and so on (akusalaparihāniādīni) occur (honti) for him?
Even an ordinary person (puthujjanopi) who is in his final existence (pacchimabhaviko) is capable (samattho hoti) of completing existence (bhavaṃ pariniṭṭhāpetuṃ) with that self-nature (tenattabhāvena).
For him, even though Aṅgulimāla was killing a thousand less one person (aṅgulimālassa viya ekenūnapāṇasahassaṃ ghātentassāpi), his self-nature (attabhāvo) was truly without suffering (abyābajjhoyeva nāma), he truly completed existence (bhavaṃ pariniṭṭhāpetiyeva nāma).
Only unwholesome [qualities] decline (akusalameva hāyati), and truly insight (vipassanaṃ eva) causes [him] to take a womb (gabbhaṃ gaṇhāpeti nāma).
