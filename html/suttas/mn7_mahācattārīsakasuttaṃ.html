136. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘ariyaṃ vo, bhikkhave, sammāsamādhiṃ desessāmi saupanisaṃ saparikkhāraṃ. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Katamo ca, bhikkhave, ariyo sammāsamādhi saupaniso saparikkhāro? Seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi, sammāsaṅkappo, sammāvācā, sammākammanto, sammāājīvo, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati; yā kho, bhikkhave, imehi sattahaṅgehi cittassa ekaggatā parikkhatā – ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, ariyo sammāsamādhi saupaniso itipi, saparikkhāro itipi. Tatra, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti. Kathañca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti? Micchādiṭṭhiṃ ‘micchādiṭṭhī’ti pajānāti, sammādiṭṭhiṃ ‘sammādiṭṭhī’ti pajānāti – sāssa hoti sammādiṭṭhi. ‘‘Katamā ca, bhikkhave, micchādiṭṭhi? ‘Natthi dinnaṃ, natthi yiṭṭhaṃ, natthi hutaṃ, natthi sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko, natthi ayaṃ loko, natthi paro loko, natthi mātā, natthi pitā, natthi sattā opapātikā, natthi loke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sammaggatā sammāpaṭipannā ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī’ti – ayaṃ, bhikkhave, micchādiṭṭhi. ‘‘Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi? Sammādiṭṭhiṃpahaṃ sammādiṭṭhimahaṃ (ka.) evaṃ sammāsaṅkappaṃpahaṃkyādīsupi, bhikkhave, dvāyaṃ dvayaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) ṭīkā oloketabbā vadāmi – atthi, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi sāsavā puññabhāgiyā upadhivepakkā; atthi, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi ariyā anāsavā lokuttarā maggaṅgā. Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi sāsavā puññabhāgiyā upadhivepakkā ? ‘Atthi dinnaṃ, atthi yiṭṭhaṃ, atthi hutaṃ, atthi sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko, atthi ayaṃ loko, atthi paro loko, atthi mātā, atthi pitā, atthi sattā opapātikā, atthi loke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sammaggatā sammāpaṭipannā ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī’ti – ayaṃ, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi sāsavā puññabhāgiyā upadhivepakkā. sammādiṭṭhimahaṃ (ka.) evaṃ sammāsaṅkappaṃpahaṃkyādīsupi dvayaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) ṭīkā oloketabbā ‘‘Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi ariyā anāsavā lokuttarā maggaṅgā? Yā kho, bhikkhave, ariyacittassa anāsavacittassa ariyamaggasamaṅgino ariyamaggaṃ bhāvayato paññā paññindriyaṃ paññābalaṃ dhammavicayasambojjhaṅgo sammādiṭṭhi maggaṅgaṃ maggaṅgā (sī. pī.) – ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi ariyā anāsavā lokuttarā maggaṅgā. So micchādiṭṭhiyā pahānāya vāyamati, sammādiṭṭhiyā, upasampadāya, svāssa svāyaṃ (ka.) hoti sammāvāyāmo. So sato micchādiṭṭhiṃ pajahati, sato sammādiṭṭhiṃ upasampajja viharati, sāssa sāyaṃ (ka.) hoti sammāsati. Itiyime itime (sī.), itissime (syā. kaṃ. pī.) tayo dhammā sammādiṭṭhiṃ anuparidhāvanti anuparivattanti, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati. maggaṅgā (sī. pī.) svāyaṃ (ka.) sāyaṃ (ka.) itime (sī.), itissime (syā. kaṃ. pī.)
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: “Bhikkhus!”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied.

The Blessed One said this: “Bhikkhus, I will teach you noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites.

Listen to that and attend closely, I will speak.”

“As you say, lord,” the monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“And what, bhikkhus, is noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites? That is, right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right intention (sammāsaṅkappo), right speech (sammāvācā), right action (sammākammanto), right livelihood (sammāājīvo), right effort (sammāvāyāmo), and right mindfulness (sammāsati); that unification of mind equipped with these seven factors, bhikkhus, is called noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites.”

Therein, bhikkhus, right view comes first.

And how, bhikkhus, does right view come first? One understands wrong view as wrong view, and right view as right view: that is one's right view.

“And what, bhikkhus, is wrong view? ‘There is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed; there is no fruit or result of good and bad actions; there is no this world, no other world; there is no mother, no father; there are no beings who are reborn spontaneously; there are in the world no recluses or brahmins who are truly advanced & practice rightly, who proclaim this world & the next after having directly known & realized it for themselves.’ This, bhikkhus, is wrong view.

“And what, bhikkhus, is right view? Right view, I say, is twofold: there is right view that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment; and there is right view that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

And what, bhikkhus, is right view that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment? ‘There is what is given, what is offered, what is sacrificed. There are fruits & results of good & bad actions. There is this world & the next world. There is mother & father. There are spontaneously reborn beings; there are brahmans & contemplatives who, faring rightly & practicing rightly, proclaim this world & the next after having directly known & realized it for themselves.’ This, bhikkhus, is right view that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment.

“And what, bhikkhus, is right view that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path? The wisdom, the faculty of wisdom, the power of wisdom, the investigation-of-states enlightenment factor, the path factor of right view in one whose mind is noble, whose mind is taintless, who possesses the noble path and is developing the noble path: this is right view that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

For the abandoning of wrong view one makes an effort, for the entering upon right view, that is one’s right effort.

Mindfully one abandons wrong view, mindfully one enters upon and abides in right view: that is one’s right mindfulness.
There is right mindfulness.

Thus, these three states run and circle around right view, that is, right view (sammā-diṭṭhi), right effort (sammā-vāyāmo), and right mindfulness (sammā-sati).
136.Evaṃme sutanti mahācattārīsakasuttaṃ. Tattha ariyanti niddosaṃ lokuttaraṃ, niddosañhi ‘‘ariya’’nti vuccati. Sammāsamādhinti maggasamādhiṃ. Saupanisanti sapaccayaṃ. Saparikkhāranti saparivāraṃ. Parikkhatāti parivāritā. Sammādiṭṭhipubbaṅgamā hotīti dvidhā sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti purecārikā vipassanāsammādiṭṭhi ca maggasammādiṭṭhi ca. Vipassanāsammādiṭṭhi tebhūmakasaṅkhāre aniccādivasena parivīmaṃsati; maggasammādiṭṭhi pana parivīmaṃsanapariyosāne bhūmiladdhaṃ vaṭṭaṃ samugghāṭayamānā vūpasamayamānā sītudakaghaṭasahassaṃ matthake āsiñcamānā viya uppajjati. Yathā hi khettaṃ kurumāno kassako paṭhamaṃ araññe rukkhe chindati, pacchā aggiṃ deti, so aggi paṭhamaṃ chinne rukkhe anavasese jhāpeti, evameva vipassanāsammādiṭṭhi paṭhamaṃ aniccādivasena saṅkhāre vīmaṃsati, maggasammādiṭṭhi tāya vīmaṃsanatthaṃ saṅkhāre puna appavattivasena samugghāṭayamānā uppajjati, sā duvidhāpi idha adhippetā. Parikkhatā Sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hotī Micchādiṭṭhīti pajānātīti micchādiṭṭhiṃ aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ anattāti lakkhaṇapaṭivedhena ārammaṇato pajānāti, sammādiṭṭhiṃ kiccato asammohato pajānāti. Sāssa hoti sammādiṭṭhīti sā evaṃ pajānanā assa sammādiṭṭhi nāma hoti. Micchādiṭṭhīti pajānātī Sāssa hoti sammādiṭṭhī Dvāyaṃ vadāmīti dvayaṃ vadāmi, duvidhakoṭṭhāsaṃ vadāmīti attho. Puññabhāgiyāti puññakoṭṭhāsabhūtā. Upadhivepakkāti upadhisaṅkhātassa vipākassa dāyikā. Dvāyaṃ vadāmī Puññabhāgiyā Upadhivepakkā Paññā paññindriyantiādīsu vibhajitvā vibhajitvā amatadvāraṃ paññapeti dassetīti paññā. Tasmiṃ atthe indattaṃ karotīti paññindriyaṃ. Avijjāya na kampatīti paññābalaṃ. Bojjhaṅgappattā hutvā catusaccadhamme vicinātīti dhammavicayasambojjhaṅgo. Maggasampattiyā pasaṭṭhā sobhanā diṭṭhīti sammādiṭṭhi. Ariyamaggassa aṅganti maggaṅgaṃ. Soti so bhikkhu. Pahānāyāti pajahanatthāya. Upasampadāyāti paṭilābhatthāya. Sammāvāyāmoti niyyāniko kusalavāyāmo. Satoti satiyā samannāgato hutvā. Anuparidhāvanti anuparivattantīti sahajātā ca purejātā ca hutvā parivārenti. Ettha hi sammāvāyāmo ca sammāsati ca lokuttarasammādiṭṭhiṃ sahajātā parivārenti rājānaṃ viya ekarathe ṭhitā asiggāhachattaggāhā. Vipassanāsammādiṭṭhi pana purejātā hutvā parivāreti rathassa purato pattikādayo viya. Dutiyapabbato paṭṭhāya pana sammāsaṅkappādīnaṃ tayopi sahajātaparivārāva hontīti veditabbā. Paññā paññindriya paññā paññindriyaṃ paññābalaṃ dhammavicayasambojjhaṅgo sammādiṭṭhi maggaṅgaṃ. So Pahānāyā Upasampadāyā Sammāvāyāmo Sato Anuparidhāvanti anuparivattantī
136. "Thus I have heard" is [the title of] the Mahācattārīsaka Sutta.

There, ariya means faultless, transcendent; for "ariya" is said to be faultless.

Sammāsamādhi means the samādhi of the path.

Saupanisa means with conditions (sapaccayaṃ).

Saparikkhāra means with retinue (saparivāraṃ).

Parikkhatā means surrounded (parivāritā).

"Sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti" means right view is foremost in two ways: as the preliminary and as the leader, namely insight right view (vipassanāsammādiṭṭhi) and path right view (maggasammādiṭṭhi).

Insight right view examines conditioned things of the triple world as impermanent, etc.; path right view, however, at the end of the examination, as the fruition of the [noble] level, eradicates the round [of rebirth], pacifying [suffering], arising like [someone] pouring a thousand pots of cool water on the head.

Just as a farmer preparing a field first cuts down trees in the forest and afterwards sets fire [to them], so the fire first completely burns the cut-down trees, just so insight right view first examines conditioned things as impermanent, etc. Path right view arises eradicating conditioned things for the sake of that examination, as non-occurring again; both of these are intended here.

Parikkhatā sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti

"Micchādiṭṭhīti pajānāti" means he understands wrong view in terms of its characteristic penetration as impermanent, suffering, not-self in its object; he understands right view as non-delusion in its function.

"Sāssa hoti sammādiṭṭhī" means that this understanding of his is called right view.

Micchādiṭṭhīti pajānāti sāssa hoti sammādiṭṭhī

"Dvāyaṃ vadāmī" means I speak of two, I speak of a twofold division, is the meaning.

"Puññabhāgiyā" means belonging to the division of merit.

"Upadhivepakkā" means productive of the result consisting of renewed existence (upadhi).

Dvāyaṃ vadāmī puññabhāgiyā upadhivepakkā

"Paññā paññindriya" and so forth, paññā means that having divided and divided, it makes known and shows the door to the Deathless.

Paññindriyaṃ means that it performs the function of sovereignty in that connection.

Paññābalaṃ means it does not tremble at ignorance.

Dhammavicayasambojjhaṅgo means having attained the enlightenment factor, it investigates the four noble truths.

Sammādiṭṭhi means the good view that is excellent through attainment of the path.

Maggaṅgaṃ means a factor of the noble path.

So means that bhikkhu.

Pahānāyā means for the sake of abandoning.

Upasampadāyā means for the sake of obtaining.

Sammāvāyāmo means effort that is the cause of emergence, wholesome effort.

Sato means having come possessed of mindfulness.

Anuparidhāvanti anuparivattantī means having arisen together and previously, they surround.

Here, right effort and right mindfulness surround the transcendent right view, having arisen together, like axe-bearers and umbrella-bearers standing in the same chariot as the king.

Insight right view, however, surrounds, having arisen previously, like foot soldiers and so forth in front of the chariot.

From the second section onwards, it should be understood that the three, beginning with right thought, are those that have arisen together as a retinue.

Paññā paññindriya paññā paññindriyaṃ paññābalaṃ dhammavicayasambojjhaṅgo sammādiṭṭhi maggaṅgaṃ.
That is, for abandoning and fully attaining, right effort and mindfulness run around and circulate.

137. ‘‘Tatra, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti. Kathañca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti? Micchāsaṅkappaṃ ‘micchāsaṅkappo’ti pajānāti, sammāsaṅkappaṃ ‘sammāsaṅkappo’ti pajānāti, sāssa hoti sammādiṭṭhi . ‘‘Katamo ca, bhikkhave, micchāsaṅkappo? Kāmasaṅkappo, byāpādasaṅkappo, vihiṃsāsaṅkappo – ayaṃ, bhikkhave, micchāsaṅkappo. ‘‘Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo? Sammāsaṅkappaṃpahaṃ, bhikkhave, dvāyaṃ vadāmi – atthi, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo sāsavo puññabhāgiyo upadhivepakko; atthi, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo ariyo anāsavo lokuttaro maggaṅgo. Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo sāsavo puññabhāgiyo upadhivepakko? Nekkhammasaṅkappo, abyāpādasaṅkappo, avihiṃsāsaṅkappo – ‘ayaṃ, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo sāsavo puññabhāgiyo upadhivepakko’’’. ‘‘Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo ariyo anāsavo lokuttaro maggaṅgo? Yo kho, bhikkhave, ariyacittassa anāsavacittassa ariyamaggasamaṅgino ariyamaggaṃ bhāvayato takko vitakko saṅkappo appanā byappanā cetaso abhiniropanā vacīsaṅkhāro – ayaṃ, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo ariyo anāsavo lokuttaro maggaṅgo. So micchāsaṅkappassa pahānāya vāyamati, sammāsaṅkappassa upasampadāya, svāssa hoti sammāvāyāmo. So sato micchāsaṅkappaṃ pajahati, sato sammāsaṅkappaṃ upasampajja viharati; sāssa hoti sammāsati. Itiyime tayo dhammā sammāsaṅkappaṃ anuparidhāvanti anuparivattanti, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati.
137. "Therein, bhikkhus, right view (sammāditthi) comes first.

And how, bhikkhus, does right view come first? One understands wrong intention (micchāsaṅkappa) as 'wrong intention,' and right intention (sammāsaṅkappa) as 'right intention.' This is one's right view.

"And what, bhikkhus, is wrong intention? It is the intention of sensual pleasure (kāmasaṅkappa), the intention of ill-will (byāpādasaṅkappa), and the intention of cruelty (vihiṃsāsaṅkappa) - this, bhikkhus, is wrong intention.

"And what, bhikkhus, is right intention? Right intention, I say, bhikkhus, is twofold: there is right intention that is affected by taints (sāsava), partaking of merit (puññabhāgiya), ripening on the side of attachment (upadhivepakka); and there is right intention that is noble (ariya), taintless (anāsava), supramundane (lokuttara), a factor of the path (maggaṅga).

"And what, bhikkhus, is right intention that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment? It is the intention of renunciation (nekkhammasaṅkappa), the intention of non-ill-will (abyāpādasaṅkappa), and the intention of non-cruelty (avihiṃsāsaṅkappa) - this, bhikkhus, is right intention that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment."

"And what, bhikkhus, is right intention that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path? The thinking (takka), thought (vitakka), intention (saṅkappa), mental absorption (appanā), mental fixity (byappanā), directing of mind (cetaso abhiniropanā), verbal formation (vacīsaṅkhāra) in one whose mind is noble, whose mind is taintless, who possesses the noble path and is developing the noble path: this, bhikkhus, is right intention that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

One makes an effort (vāyamati) to abandon wrong intention and to enter upon right intention: this is one’s right effort (sammāvāyāma).

Mindfully (sato) one abandons wrong intention, mindfully one enters upon and abides in right intention: this is one’s right mindfulness (sammāsati).

Thus these three states run and circle around right intention, that is, right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right effort (sammāvāyāma), and right mindfulness (sammāsati).
137.Micchāsaṅkappoti pajānātīti micchāsaṅkappaṃ aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ anattāti lakkhaṇapaṭivedhena ārammaṇato pajānāti sammāsaṅkappaṃ kiccato asammohato pajānāti. Ito aparesu sammāvācādīsupi evameva yojanā veditabbā. Kāmasaṅkappādayo dvedhāvitakkasutte (ma. ni. 1.206) vuttāyeva. Takkotiādīsu takkanavasena takko. Sveva ca upasaggena padaṃ vaḍḍhetvā vitakkoti vutto, sveva saṅkappanavasena saṅkappo. Ekaggo hutvā ārammaṇe appetīti appanā. Upasaggena pana padaṃ vaḍḍhetvā byappanāti vuttaṃ. Cetaso abhiniropanāti cittassa abhiniropanā. Vitakkasmiñhi sati vitakko ārammaṇe cittaṃ abhiniropeti vitakke pana asati attanoyeva dhammatāya cittaṃ ārammaṇaṃ abhiruhati jātisampanno abhiññātapuriso viya rājagehaṃ. Anabhiññātassa hi paṭihārena vā dovārikena vā attho hoti, abhiññātaṃ jātisampannaṃ sabbe rājarājamahāmattā jānantīti attanova dhammatāya nikkhamati ceva pavisati ca, evaṃsampadamidaṃ veditabbaṃ. Vācaṃ saṅkharotīti vacīsaṅkhāro. Ettha ca lokiyavitakko vācaṃ saṅkharoti, na lokuttaro. Kiñcāpi na saṅkharoti, vacīsaṅkhārotveva ca panassa nāmaṃ hoti. Sammāsaṅkappaṃ anuparidhāvantīti lokuttarasammāsaṅkappaṃ parivārenti. Ettha ca tayopi nekkhammasaṅkappādayo pubbabhāge nānācittesu labbhanti, maggakkhaṇe pana tiṇṇampi kāmasaṅkappādīnañca padacchedaṃ samugghātaṃ karonto maggaṅgaṃ pūrayamāno ekova sammāsaṅkappo uppajjitvā nekkhammasaṅkappādivasena tīṇi nāmāni labhati. Parato sammāvācādīsupi eseva nayo. Takko takko vitakko saṅkappo appanā byappanā Cetaso abhiniropanā vacīsaṅkhāro Sammāsaṅkappaṃ anuparidhāvantī
137. “He understands ‘wrong intention ( micchāsaṅkappa)’": he understands wrong intention as impermanent (anicca), suffering (dukkha), and not-self (anatta) by way of the penetration of characteristics, from the perspective of the object. He understands right intention (sammāsaṅkappa) from the perspective of its function, as non-delusion.

The same method should be understood as applying to the other [factors of the path] beginning with right speech (sammāvācā).

Volitional thought regarding sense pleasures (kāmasaṅkappa) and so forth have already been explained in the Two Kinds of Thinking Discourse (Majjhima Nikāya 1.206).

In "thinking (takko)" and so forth, takko [is named] due to the nature of thinking. That same [word] is called vitakko by increasing the word with a prefix. That same is saṅkappo due to the nature of intention. Appanā: [the mind] becomes one-pointed and applies [itself] to the object. By increasing the word with a prefix, it is called byappanā. Cetaso abhiniropanā: the application of the mind. When there is volitional thought (vitakka), that volitional thought applies the mind to the object. But when there is no volitional thought, the mind rises to the object by its very nature, like a well-born man known [to the palace guards] going to the royal palace. For a man who is not known, there is need of either a pass or a doorkeeper. But all the kings, royal ministers, and great ministers know a well-born man, and he goes out and enters by his very nature. This analogy should be understood. Vacīsaṅkhāro: that which fabricates speech. Here, mundane volitional thought (lokiyavitakka) fabricates speech, not supramundane [volitional thought] (lokuttara). Although it does not fabricate, it is still named verbal fabrication (vacīsaṅkhāro). “They attend to right intention (sammāsaṅkappa)": they attend upon supramundane right intention (lokuttarasammāsaṅkappa). Here, these three, namely, volitional thought regarding renunciation (nekkhammasaṅkappa) and so forth, are obtained in various minds in the preliminary stage. But at the moment of the path, while making a word-break and extirpation of the three, namely, volitional thought regarding sense pleasures (kāmasaṅkappa) and so forth, and while fulfilling the path factor, only one right intention (sammāsaṅkappa) arises and obtains three names by way of being volitional thought regarding renunciation (nekkhammasaṅkappa) and so forth. Hereafter, the same method applies to right speech (sammāvācā) and so forth.

Takko, takko, vitakko, saṅkappo, appanā, byappanā, cetaso abhiniropanā, vacīsaṅkhāro, sammāsaṅkappaṃ anuparidhāvantī.

sammādiṭṭhissa, Monks, micchādiṭṭhi nijjiṇṇā hotīti, those who recite the remainder of the Nikāyas say that the fruit is spoken of, but the Middle reciters say that the path is spoken of in the place where the ten objects of annihilation have come.
Therein, right view ( samyagdṛṣṭi) should be understood in the sense of seeing, right knowledge ( samyagjñāna) in the sense of the cause of knowing, and right liberation ( samyagvimukti) in the sense of being devoted to that.
vīsati kusalapakkhāti, the ten beginning with right view (samyagdṛṣṭi), "because of right view, many wholesome qualities ( kuśalā dhammā) are spoken of in this way, thus there are twenty wholesome factors.

mahācattārīsakoti, because of the great result (mahāvipāka), the forty qualities ( dhammānaṃ) of the great wholesome and unwholesome factors are manifested, thus it is called great forty.

Therein, "one understands wrong view as wrong view," the insight right view ( vipaśyanāsamyagdṛṣṭi) is spoken of in this way.
"There is giving," the right view of the ownership of karma is spoken of in this way.
"For one with right view (samyagdṛṣṭi), monks, right intention arises," here the right view of the path and the right view of the fruit are both spoken of.
"Right knowledge arises."
Here, the right view of retrospection is spoken of.

143. ‘‘Yo hi koci, bhikkhave, samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā imaṃ mahācattārīsakaṃ dhammapariyāyaṃ garahitabbaṃ paṭikkositabbaṃ maññeyya tassa diṭṭheva dhamme dasasahadhammikā vādānuvādā gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchanti – sammādiṭṭhiṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati, ye ca micchādiṭṭhī samaṇabrāhmaṇā te bhoto pujjā, te bhoto pāsaṃsā; sammāsaṅkappaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati , ye ca micchāsaṅkappā samaṇabrāhmaṇā te bhoto pujjā, te bhoto pāsaṃsā; sammāvācaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati…pe… sammākammantaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāājīvaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāvāyāmaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāsatiṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāsamādhiṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāñāṇaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati … sammāvimuttiṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati, ye ca micchāvimuttī samaṇabrāhmaṇā te bhoto pujjā, te bhoto pāsaṃsā. Yepi te, bhikkhave, ahesuṃ okkalā vassabhaññā vayabhiññā (ka.) saṃ. ni. 3.62; a. ni. 4.30 passitabbaṃ ahetuvādā akiriyavādā natthikavādā tepi mahācattārīsakaṃ dhammapariyāyaṃ na garahitabbaṃ napaṭikkositabbaṃ amaññiṃsu maññeyyuṃ (ka.). Taṃ kissa hetu? Nindābyārosaupārambhabhayā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Mahācattārīsakasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ sattamaṃ.
“Bhikkhus, if any recluse or brāhmaṇa thinks that this Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty should be censured and rejected, then there are ten legitimate deductions from his assertions that would provide grounds for censuring him here and now. If that worthy one censures right view (sammādiṭṭhiṃ), then he would honour and praise those recluses and brāhmaṇas who are of wrong view (micchādiṭṭhī). If that worthy one censures right intention (sammāsaṅkappaṃ), then he would honour and praise those recluses and brāhmaṇas who are of wrong intention. If that worthy one censures right speech (sammāvācaṃ)… If that worthy one censures right action (sammākammantaṃ)… If that worthy one censures right livelihood (sammāājīvaṃ)… If that worthy one censures right effort (sammāvāyāmaṃ)… If that worthy one censures right mindfulness (sammāsatiṃ)… If that worthy one censures right concentration (sammāsamādhiṃ)… If that worthy one censures right knowledge (sammāñāṇaṃ)… If that worthy one censures right liberation (sammāvimuttiṃ), then he would honour and praise those recluses and brāhmaṇas who are of wrong liberation.

"Bhikkhus, if any recluse or brāhmaṇa thinks that this Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty should be censured and rejected, then these are ten legitimate deductions from his assertions that would provide grounds for censuring him here and now.

Those recluses and brāhmaṇas of Okkala and Vassabhañña, who held to the doctrine of no-cause (ahetuvādā), the doctrine of no-action (akiriyavādā), and the annihilationist doctrine (natthikavādā), did not think that this Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty should be censured or rejected. What is the reason for that? [They feared] the danger of blame, reproach, and disparagement."

This is what the Buddha said. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Great Forty Discourse, the seventh [sutta], is finished.

If one reviles sammādiṭṭhi, it is called wrong view. Even if one says, "This is beautiful," it is called wrong view. Even if one says, "This is not beautiful," one is reviling sammādiṭṭhi.

okkalā means inhabitants of the Okkala region.

vassabhaññā means Vassa and Bhañña, these two people.

ahetuvādā means those who claim "There is no cause, there is no condition for the purification of beings," and so on.

akiriyavādā means those who deny action by saying, "By doing so there is no evil," and so on.

natthikavādā means those who claim, "There is no giving," and so on.

They were fixed on these three views.

How is there a fixed consequence in these?

Someone who, having taken up such a view, sits contemplating and pondering day and night, for him, "There is no cause, there is no condition. By doing so there is no evil, there is no giving. At the breakup of the body, one is annihilated," in that object wrong mindfulness is established, the mind becomes one-pointed, impulsive moments occur.

In the first impulsive moment there is effort, likewise in the second and so on.

In the seventh, even for Buddhas, there is effortlessness, irreversibility, similar to an ariṭṭhakaṇḍaka.

There, someone might espouse one view, another two, another three. But whether one espouses one, two, or three views, one adopts a wrong view with fixed consequences. One has met an obstruction to the path to heaven and an obstruction to the path to liberation.

It is impossible for him to reach heaven immediately following this present existence, much less to reach liberation. This being has become a stump in the round of existence, a watchman of the earth. Generally, one like this does not emerge from existence.

The vassabhaññās were also like this.

nindābyārosaupārambhabhayā means fear of blame, fear of abuse, fear of censure, this is the meaning.

The rest is clear throughout.
