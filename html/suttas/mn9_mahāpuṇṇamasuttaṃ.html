Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvathtī in the Eastern Park in Migāramātā’s mansion.

Now on that occasion—the uposatha day of the fifteenth, the full-moon night—the Blessed One was seated in the open surrounded by the saṃgha of bhikkhus.

Then a certain bhikkhu rose from his seat, arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, raised his joined hands in reverential salutation (añjali) towards the Blessed One, and said to him: “Venerable sir (bhante), I would ask the Blessed One about a certain point, if the Blessed One would grant me the favor of answering my question.”

“Well then, bhikkhu, sit down in your own seat and ask whatever you wish.”

85. Thus have I heard: the Mahāpuṇṇama Sutta.

Therein, tadahū means in that [time], in that day is the meaning.

Upavasanti etthāti uposatho: Because one dwells near, therefore, it is uposatha.

Upavasantīti: the meaning is that they dwell having approached with virtue (śīla) or non-eating (anasanena).

Here is the explanation of the meaning: “Come, friend Kappina, we will go to the uposatha,” etc., the pātimokkha recitation is the uposatha.

"The uposatha observed by Visākhe is complete with eight factors," etc. (A.N. 8.53): virtue (śīla).

"For the pure one there is always ease, for the pure one there is always the uposatha," etc. (M.N. 1.79): fasting (upavāso).

"Uposatha is the name of the Nāga king," etc. (D.N. 2.246): description (paññatti).

"Monks, monasteries are not to be found on that uposatha day,” etc. (Mahāva. 181): the day for observing the uposatha.

Here also, that same is intended.

That, however, is threefold by the distinction of the eighth, fourteenth, and fifteenth [days of the lunar cycle].

Therefore, to prevent the other two, it is said "pannarase," on the fifteenth.

Therefore, it was said, "upavasanti etthāti uposatho," because one dwells near, therefore, it is uposatha.

Because the full moon is complete, it is called puṇṇā.

Mā—this is said of the moon (cando). Therefore, because it is full, it is puṇṇamā.

Thus, in these two words, puṇṇāya puṇṇamāyāti, the meaning should be understood.

Desa means reason (kāraṇaṃ).

"Tena hi tvaṃ bhikkhu sake āsane nisīditvā pucchā," therefore, monk, sit on your own seat and ask—why did the Blessed One cause him to sit down without speaking to him while he was standing?

It is said that a certain monk, a saṅghathera of sixty-odd leading monks, taking sixty monks, dwells in the forest. They, having taken a meditation subject (kammaṭṭhānaṃ) near him, strive and endeavor.

They grasp the great elements (mahābhūtāni) and the derived forms (upādārūpāni), they grasp the vipassanā [mindfulness] with name and form as condition and characteristic as object.

Then, having come to the teacher's presence, having bowed and sat down, the elder asks about the great elements, grasping, and so on.

They tell everything, but when asked about the path and the fruit, they are not able to explain.

Then the elder thought, "Among those near me, there is no decline in instruction (ovādassa parihāni natthi), and these ones dwell having aroused energy (āraddhavīriyā viharanti).

Even for the time it takes a chicken to drink water, there is no negligence in their actions.

Even so, they are not able to produce the paths and the fruits.

I do not know the disposition (ajjhāsayaṃ) of these ones. These will be those who can be guided by the Buddha (buddhaveneyyā ete bhavissanti).

But when asked as if not knowing, while speaking, he brings a reason from here and there, making it clear, he speaks.
Someone, however, even not knowing, asks as if knowing.
What will the Elder do with such a speech? But it should be understood that he asks as if not knowing, even though he knows.

Then that bhikkhu, sitting on his own seat, said this to the Blessed One: “Are these, venerable sir, the five aggregates affected by clinging; that is, the form aggregate affected by clinging (rūpupādānakkhandha), the feeling aggregate affected by clinging (vedanupādānakkhandha), the perception aggregate affected by clinging (saññupādānakkhandha), the formations aggregate affected by clinging (saṅkhārupādānakkhandha), and the consciousness aggregate affected by clinging (viññāṇupādānakkhandha)?” “These, bhikkhu, are the five aggregates affected by clinging; that is, the form aggregate affected by clinging, the feeling aggregate affected by clinging, the perception aggregate affected by clinging, the formations aggregate affected by clinging, and the consciousness aggregate affected by clinging.”

“Good, venerable sir,” that bhikkhu, delighting and rejoicing in the Blessed One’s words, asked the Blessed One a further question: “But, venerable sir, what is the root of these five aggregates affected by clinging?” “These five aggregates affected by clinging are rooted in desire (chandamūlakā),” said the Blessed One. “Is that very clinging the same as these five aggregates affected by clinging, venerable sir, or is clinging something apart from the five aggregates affected by clinging?” “Bhikkhu, that clinging is neither the same as these five aggregates affected by clinging, nor is clinging something apart from the five aggregates affected by clinging. Rather, bhikkhu, the desire and lust (chandarāgo) in the five aggregates affected by clinging, that is the clinging there.”

“But, venerable sir, could there be diversity in the desire and lust regarding the five aggregates affected by clinging?” “There could be, bhikkhu,” the Blessed One said. “Here, bhikkhu, it occurs to someone: ‘May my form be thus in the future (evaṃrūpo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānaṃ), may my feeling be thus in the future, may my perception be thus in the future, may my formations be thus in the future, may my consciousness be thus in the future.’ Thus, bhikkhu, there could be diversity in the desire and lust regarding the five aggregates affected by clinging.”

“But to what extent, venerable sir, does the term ‘aggregate’ (khandhādhivacanaṃ) apply to the aggregates (khandhānaṃ)?” “Bhikkhu, any kind of form (rūpaṃ) whatsoever – whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the form aggregate (rūpakkhandho). Any kind of feeling (vedanā) whatsoever – whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the feeling aggregate (vedanākkhandho). Any kind of perception (saññā) whatsoever… whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the perception aggregate (saññākkhandho). Any kind of formations (saṅkhārā) whatsoever – whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the formations aggregate (saṅkhārakkhandho). Any kind of consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) whatsoever – whether past, future, or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near – this is the consciousness aggregate (viññāṇakkhandho). It is to this extent, bhikkhu, that the term ‘aggregate’ applies to the aggregates.”

“What, venerable sir, is the cause (hetu) and condition (paccayo) for the manifestation (paññāpanāya) of the form aggregate (rūpakkhandhassa)? What is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the feeling aggregate (vedanākkhandhassa)? What is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the perception aggregate (saññākkhandhassa)? What is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the formations aggregate (saṅkhārakkhandhassa)? What is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the consciousness aggregate (viññāṇakkhandhassa)?”

“The four great elements (cattāro mahābhūtā), bhikkhu, are the cause and condition for the manifestation of the form aggregate. Contact (phasso) is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the feeling aggregate. Contact is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the perception aggregate. Contact is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the formations aggregate. Mentality-materiality (nāmarūpaṃ), bhikkhu, is the cause and condition for the manifestation of the consciousness aggregate.”

87. ‘‘Kathaṃ pana, bhante, sakkāyadiṭṭhi hotī’’ti? ‘‘Idha, bhikkhu, assutavā puthujjano ariyānaṃ adassāvī ariyadhammassa akovido ariyadhamme avinīto sappurisānaṃ adassāvī sappurisadhammassa akovido sappurisadhamme avinīto rūpaṃ attato samanupassati rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā rūpaṃ rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ; vedanaṃ attato samanupassati vedanāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā vedanaṃ vedanāya vā attānaṃ; saññaṃ attato samanupassati saññāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā saññaṃ saññāya vā attānaṃ; saṅkhāre attato samanupassati saṅkhāravantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā saṅkhāre saṅkhāresu vā attānaṃ; viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā viññāṇaṃ viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ. Evaṃ kho , bhikkhu, sakkāyadiṭṭhi hotī’’ti. ‘‘Kathaṃ pana, bhante, sakkāyadiṭṭhi na hotī’’ti? ‘‘Idha, bhikkhu, sutavā ariyasāvako ariyānaṃ dassāvī ariyadhammassa kovido ariyadhamme suvinīto sappurisānaṃ dassāvī sappurisadhammassa kovido sappurisadhamme suvinīto na rūpaṃ attato samanupassati na rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā rūpaṃ na rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ; na vedanaṃ attato samanupassati na vedanāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā vedanaṃ na vedanāya vā attānaṃ; na saññaṃ attato samanupassati na saññāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā saññaṃ na saññāya vā attānaṃ; na saṅkhāre attato samanupassati na saṅkhāravantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā saṅkhāre na saṅkhāresu vā attānaṃ; na viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati na viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ na attani vā viññāṇaṃ na viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, sakkāyadiṭṭhi na hotī’’ti.
How, venerable sir, does identity view come to be?”

“Here, bhikkhu, an untaught ordinary person ( puthujjana), who has no regard for noble ones (ariyānaṃ adassāvī) and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma (ariyadhamme avinīto), who has no regard for true men (sappurisānaṃ adassāvī) and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, regards form (rūpa) as self (āttato), or self as possessed of form (rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ), or form as in self (attani vā rūpaṃ), or self as in form (rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ). He regards feeling (vedanā) as self, or self as possessed of feeling (vedanāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ), or feeling as in self (attani vā vedanaṃ), or self as in feeling (vedanāya vā attānaṃ). He regards perception (saññaṃ) as self, or self as possessed of perception (saññāvantaṃ vā attānaṃ), or perception as in self (attani vā saññaṃ), or self as in perception (saññāya vā attānaṃ). He regards formations (saṅkhāre) as self, or self as possessed of formations (saṅkhāravantaṃ vā attānaṃ), or formations as in self (attani vā saṅkhāre), or self as in formations (saṅkhāresu vā attānaṃ). He regards consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) as self, or self as possessed of consciousness (viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ), or consciousness as in self (attani vā viññāṇaṃ), or self as in consciousness (viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ). That is how, bhikkhu, identity view (sakkāyadiṭṭhi) comes to be.”

“How, venerable sir, does identity view not come to be?”

“Here, bhikkhu, a well-taught noble disciple (ariyasāvako), who has regard for noble ones (ariyānaṃ dassāvī) and is skilled in their Dhamma (ariyadhammassa kovido) and well-disciplined in their Dhamma (ariyadhamme suvinīto), who has regard for true men (sappurisānaṃ dassāvī) and is skilled in their Dhamma and well-disciplined in their Dhamma, does not regard form (rūpaṃ) as self (attato), or self as possessed of form, or form as in self, or self as in form. He does not regard feeling as self, or self as possessed of feeling, or feeling as in self, or self as in feeling. He does not regard perception as self, or self as possessed of perception, or perception as in self, or self as in perception. He does not regard formations as self, or self as possessed of formations, or formations as in self, or self as in formations. He does not regard consciousness as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness. That is how, bhikkhu, identity view does not come to be.”
87.Kathaṃ pana, bhanteti idaṃ kittakena nu khoti vaṭṭaṃ pucchanto evamāha. Sakkāyadiṭṭhi na hotīti idaṃ vivaṭṭaṃ pucchanto evamāha.
How, then, bhante—asking in what way, indeed, Venerable One, Vatta said thus.

"There is no view of self"—asking clearly, he said thus.

88. ‘‘Ko nu kho, bhante, rūpe assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇaṃ? Ko vedanāya assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇaṃ? Ko saññāya assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇaṃ? Ko saṅkhāresu assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇaṃ? Ko viññāṇe assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇa’’nti? ‘‘Yaṃ kho, bhikkhu, rūpaṃ paṭicca uppajjati sukhaṃ somanassaṃ, ayaṃ rūpe assādo. Yaṃ rūpaṃ aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, ayaṃ rūpe ādīnavo. Yo rūpe chandarāgavinayo chandarāgappahānaṃ, idaṃ rūpe nissaraṇaṃ. Yaṃ kho yañca (syā. kaṃ.), bhikkhu, vedanaṃ paṭicca… saññaṃ paṭicca… saṅkhāre paṭicca… viññāṇaṃ paṭicca uppajjati sukhaṃ somanassaṃ, ayaṃ viññāṇe assādo. Yaṃ viññāṇaṃ aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, ayaṃ viññāṇe ādīnavo. Yo viññāṇe chandarāgavinayo chandarāgappahānaṃ, idaṃ viññāṇe nissaraṇa’’nti.
88. "But what, venerable sir, is the gratification (assāda), what is the danger (ādīnava), and what is the escape (nissaraṇaṃ) in the case of form (rūpa)? What is the gratification, what is the danger, and what is the escape in the case of feeling (vedanā)? What is the gratification, what is the danger, and what is the escape in the case of perception (saṃjñā)? What is the gratification, what is the danger, and what is the escape in the case of volitional formations (saṅkhāresu)? What is the gratification, what is the danger, and what is the escape in the case of consciousness (vijñāṇa)?"

"The pleasure (sukhaṃ) and joy (somanassaṃ) that arise in dependence on form, this is the gratification in the case of form. Form is impermanent (aniccaṃ), suffering (dukkhaṃ), and subject to change (vipariṇāmadhammaṃ) - this is the danger in the case of form. The removal of desire and lust (chandarāgavinayo), the abandonment of desire and lust (chandarāgappahānaṃ) for form - this is the escape in the case of form.

The pleasure and joy that arise in dependence on feeling…in dependence on perception…in dependence on volitional formations…in dependence on consciousness - this is the gratification in the case of consciousness. Consciousness is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change - this is the danger in the case of consciousness. The removal and abandonment of desire and lust for consciousness - this is the escape in the case of consciousness."
88.Ayaṃrūpe assādoti iminā pariññāpaṭivedho ceva dukkhasaccañca kathitaṃ. Ayaṃ rūpe ādīnavoti iminā pahānapaṭivedho ceva samudayasaccañca. Idaṃ rūpe nissaraṇanti iminā sacchikiriyāpaṭivedho ceva nirodhasaccañca. Ye imesu tīsu ṭhānesu sammādiṭṭhiādayo dhammā, ayaṃ bhāvanāpaṭivedho maggasaccaṃ. Sesapadesupi eseva nayo.
This [is] "enjoyment in form (rūpe assādo)"; by this, the penetration of full understanding (pariññāpaṭivedho) and the truth of suffering (dukkhasacca) are spoken of.

This [is] "danger in form (rūpe ādīnavo)"; by this, the penetration of abandoning (pahānapaṭivedho) and the truth of origin (samudayasacca) [are spoken of].

This [is] "escape from form (rūpe nissaraṇa)"; by this, the penetration of realization (sacchikiriyāpaṭivedho) and the truth of cessation (nirodhasacca) [are spoken of].

Those things, such as right view (sammādiṭṭhi), in these three places, this [is] the penetration of development (bhāvanāpaṭivedho), the truth of the path (maggasaccaṃ).

And so with the other clauses (sesapadesupi eseva nayo).

89. ‘‘Kathaṃ pana, bhante, jānato kathaṃ passato imasmiñca saviññāṇake kāye bahiddhā ca sabbanimittesu ahaṃkāramamaṃkāramānānusayā na hontī’’ti? ‘‘Yaṃ kiñci, bhikkhu, rūpaṃ – atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā yaṃ dūre santike vā – sabbaṃ rūpaṃ ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti – evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya passati. Yā kāci vedanā… yā kāci saññā… ye keci saṅkhārā… yaṃ kiñci viññāṇaṃ – atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā yaṃ dūre santike vā – sabbaṃ viññāṇaṃ ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti – evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya passati. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhu, jānato evaṃ passato imasmiñca saviññāṇake kāye bahiddhā ca sabbanimittesu ahaṃkāramamaṃkāramānānusayā na hontī’’ti.
89. “But how, venerable sir, when one knows, how when one sees, do I-making, mine-making, and the underlying tendency to conceit not occur in regard to this body with its consciousness and in regard to all external signs?”

“Any kind of form (rūpa) whatsoever, bhikkhu, whether past (atītā), future (anāgata), or present (paccuppanna); internal (ajjhattaṃ) or external (bahiddhā); gross (oḷārikaṃ) or subtle (sukhumaṃ); inferior (hīnaṃ) or superior (paṇītaṃ); far (dūre) or near (santike): all form (rūpa)—‘This is not mine (netaṃ mama), this I am not (nesohamasmi), this is not my self (na meso attā)’—one sees all form as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ) with proper wisdom (sammappaññāya).

Any kind of feeling (vedanā) whatsoever… Any kind of perception (saññā) whatsoever… Any kind of volitional formations (saṅkhārā) whatsoever… Any kind of consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) whatsoever, whether past, future, or present; internal or external; gross or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near: all consciousness—‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self’—one sees all consciousness as it actually is with proper wisdom.

“When one knows thus, bhikkhu, when one sees thus, then in regard to this body with consciousness and in regard to all external signs, I-making (ahaṃkāra), mine-making (mamaṃkāra), and the underlying tendency to conceit (mānānusayā) no longer occur within.”
89.Bahiddhāti parassa saviññāṇake kāye. Sabbanimittesūti iminā pana anindriyabaddhampi saṅgaṇhāti. ‘‘Saviññāṇake kāye’’ti vacanena vā attano ca parassa ca kāyo gahitova, bahiddhā ca sabbanimittaggahaṇena anindriyabaddhaṃ gaṇhāti.

bahiddhā: in the body of another that possesses consciousness ( saviññāṇake kāye).
sabbanimittesū: by this, it also includes that which is not bound by the senses ( anindriyabaddhampi).
‘‘Because of the expression, saviññāṇake kāye, the body of oneself and of another is taken, and bahiddhā, through the taking of all signs (sabbanimittaggahaṇena), it takes that which is not bound by the senses (anindriyabaddhaṃ).

90. Atha kho aññatarassa bhikkhuno evaṃ cetaso parivitakko udapādi – ‘‘iti kira, bho, rūpaṃ anattā, vedanā anattā, saññā anattā, saṅkhārā anattā, viññāṇaṃ anattā; anattakatāni kammāni kamattānaṃ kathamattānaṃ (saṃ. ni. 3.82) phusissantī’’ti? Atha kho bhagavā tassa bhikkhuno cetasā cetoparivitakkamaññāya bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, bhikkhave, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco moghapuriso avidvā avijjāgato taṇhādhipateyyena cetasā satthu sāsanaṃ atidhāvitabbaṃ maññeyya – ‘iti kira, bho, rūpaṃ anattā, vedanā anattā, saññā anattā, saṅkhārā anattā, viññāṇaṃ anattā; anattakatāni kammāni kamattānaṃ phusissantī’ti. Paṭivinītā paṭicca vinītā (sī. pī.), paṭipucchāmi vinītā (syā. kaṃ.) kho me tumhe, bhikkhave , tatra tatra dhammesu’’. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññatha, bhikkhave, rūpaṃ niccaṃ vā aniccaṃ vā’’ti? ‘‘Aniccaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Yaṃ panāniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vā taṃ sukhaṃ vā’’ti? ‘‘Dukkhaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Yaṃ panāniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, kallaṃ nu taṃ samanupassituṃ – ‘etaṃ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā’’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ , bhante’’. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññatha, bhikkhave, vedanā… saññā… saṅkhārā… viññāṇaṃ niccaṃ vā aniccaṃ vā’’ti? ‘‘Aniccaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Yaṃ panāniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vā taṃ sukhaṃ vā’’ti? ‘‘Dukkhaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Yaṃ panāniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, kallaṃ nu taṃ samanupassituṃ – ‘etaṃ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā’’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Tasmātiha, bhikkhave, yaṃ kiñci rūpaṃ atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā yaṃ dūre santike vā sabbaṃ rūpaṃ – ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya daṭṭhabbaṃ. Yā kāci vedanā… yā kāci saññā… ye keci saṅkhārā… yaṃ kiñci viññāṇaṃ atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā yaṃ dūre santike vā sabbaṃ viññāṇaṃ – ‘netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā’ti evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya daṭṭhabbaṃ. Evaṃ passaṃ, bhikkhave, sutavā ariyasāvako rūpasmimpi nibbindati, vedanāyapi nibbindati, saññāyapi nibbindati, saṅkhāresupi nibbindati, viññāṇasmimpi nibbindati; nibbindaṃ virajjati , virāgā vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānātī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Imasmiñca pana veyyākaraṇasmiṃ bhaññamāne saṭṭhimattānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ anupādāya āsavehi cittāni vimucciṃsūti. Mahāpuṇṇamasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ navamaṃ.
90. Then, in the mind of a certain bhikkhu (monk) this thought arose: “So, it seems, material form (rūpa) is not self (anattā), feeling (vedanā) is not self, perception (saññā) is not self, formations (saṅkhārā) are not self, consciousness (viññāṇa) is not self. What self, then, will actions (kamma) done by the not-self affect?”

Then the Blessed One (bhagavā), knowing in his mind the thought in the mind of that bhikkhu, addressed the bhikkhus thus: “It is possible, bhikkhus, that some misguided man here, obtuse and ignorant, with his mind dominated by craving, might think that he can outstrip the Teacher’s Dispensation thus: ‘So, it seems, material form (rūpa) is not self (anattā)…consciousness (viññāṇa) is not self. What self, then, will actions (kamma) done by the not-self affect?’”

“Am I one whose doubts have been dispelled, O bhikkhus, concerning those things in that very place?”

“What do you think, bhikkhus, is material form (rūpa) permanent (nicca) or impermanent (anicca)?” “Impermanent (anicca), Lord (bhante)”.

“But that which is impermanent (anicca), is that unpleasant (dukkha) or pleasant (sukha)?” “Unpleasant (dukkha), Lord (bhante)”.

“That impermanent (anicca), unpleasant (dukkha), changing thing, is it suitable to be considered, that is mine (mama), that I am (asmi), that is my self (attā)?” “No, Lord (bhante)”.

“What do you think, bhikkhus, is feeling (vedanā)… perception (saññā)… volitional formations (saṅkhārā)… consciousness (viññāṇa) permanent (nicca) or impermanent (anicca)?” “Impermanent (anicca), Lord (bhante)”.

“That which is impermanent (anicca), is that unpleasant (dukkha) or pleasant (sukha)?” “Unpleasant (dukkha), Lord (bhante)”.

“That which is impermanent (anicca), unpleasant (dukkha), subject to change, is it fitting to regard thus: ‘This is mine (mama), this I am (asmi), this is my self (attā)’?” “No, Lord (bhante)”.

“Therefore, bhikkhus, any kind of material form (rūpa) whatever, whether past (atīta), future (anāgata), or present (paccuppanna), internal (ajjhatta) or external (bahiddhā), gross (oḷārika) or subtle (sukhuma), inferior (hīna) or superior (paṇīta), far (dūre) or near (santike), all material form (rūpa) should be seen as it actually is with proper wisdom (sammappaññā) thus: “This is not mine (mama), this I am not (asmi), this is not my self (attā)’.”

Any kind of feeling (vedanā) whatsoever… Any kind of perception (saññā) whatsoever…Any kind of volitional formations (saṅkhārā) whatsoever…Any kind of consciousness (viññāṇa) whatsoever, whether past (atīta), future (anāgata), or present (paccuppanna), internal (ajjhatta) or external (bahiddhā), gross (oḷārika) or subtle (sukhuma), inferior (hīna) or superior (paṇīta), far (dūre) or near (santike) - one sees all consciousness (viññāṇa) as it really is with correct wisdom (sammappaññā) thus: ‘This is not mine (mama), this I am not (asmi), this is not my self (attā)’."

Seeing thus, bhikkhus, the instructed noble disciple (ariyasāvako) experiences revulsion (nibbindati) towards form (rūpa), revulsion (nibbindati) towards feeling (vedanā), revulsion (nibbindati) towards perception (saññā), revulsion (nibbindati) towards volitional formations (saṅkhārā), revulsion (nibbindati) towards consciousness (viññāṇa). Experiencing revulsion (nibbindaṃ), he becomes dispassionate (virajjati). Through dispassion (virāgā) his mind is liberated (vimuccati).

When it is liberated (vimuttasmiṃ) there comes the knowledge (ñāṇaṃ): ‘It is liberated (vimuttamiti)’ He understands: ‘Destroyed is birth (jāti), the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ) has been lived, what had to be done (karaṇīyaṃ) has been done, there is no more coming back to any state of being (itthattāyā)’.”

Thus spoke the Blessed One (bhagavā). Those bhikkhus were satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) words (bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti).

And while this explanation (veyyākaraṇasmiṃ) was being given, the hearts (cittāni) of 60 bhikkhus, through no clinging (anupādāya), were fully released (vimucciṃsūti) from fermentation/effluents (āsavehi).

The Great Full Moon Discourse (mahāpuṇṇamasuttaṃ) is finished, the ninth.
90.Anattakatānīti anattani ṭhatvā katāni. Kamattānaṃ phusissantīti katarasmiṃ attani ṭhatvā vipākaṃ dassentīti sassatadassanaṃ okkamanto evamāha. Taṇhādhipateyyenāti taṇhājeṭṭhakena. Tatra tatrāti tesu tesu dhammesu. Saṭṭhimattānanti ime bhikkhū pakatikammaṭṭhānaṃ jahitvā aññaṃ navakammaṭṭhānaṃ sammasantā pallaṅkaṃ abhinditvā tasmiṃyeva āsane arahattaṃ pāpuṇiṃsu. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Mahāpuṇṇamasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
