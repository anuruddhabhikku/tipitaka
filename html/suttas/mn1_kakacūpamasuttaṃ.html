Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Now at that time the venerable Moliya Phagguna was associating excessively with nuns (bhikkhunīs).

The venerable Moliya Phagguna was associating with nuns in such a way that if any monk spoke disparagingly of those nuns in the presence of the venerable Moliya Phagguna, the venerable Moliya Phagguna would become angry and displeased and would even make an issue of it (adhikaraṇaṃ karoti).

But if any monk spoke disparagingly of the venerable Moliya Phagguna in the presence of those nuns, then those nuns would become angry and displeased and would even make an issue of it.

The venerable Moliya Phagguna was associating with nuns in this way.

Then a certain monk approached the Blessed One, and after paying homage to the Blessed One, sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, that monk said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, Moliya Phagguna is associating excessively with nuns.

Venerable sir, the venerable Moliya Phagguna is associating with nuns in such a way that if any monk speaks disparagingly of those nuns in the presence of the venerable Moliya Phagguna, the venerable Moliya Phagguna becomes angry and displeased and even makes an issue of it (adhikaraṇaṃ karoti).

But if any monk speaks disparagingly of the venerable Moliya Phagguna in the presence of those nuns, then those nuns become angry and displeased and even make an issue of it.

Venerable sir, the venerable Moliya Phagguna is associating with nuns in this way."

Thus have I heard: the Kakacūpamasutta.

There, moḷiyaphagguno means moḷī is called a crest. As it was said:

"Having cut the crest, scented with excellent perfumes,
The Sakyan bull threw it into the sky;
Vāsava, with a jewel diadem,
With a thousand eyes, received it on his head."

That was very great in his householder days, therefore the designation moḷiyaphagguno arose for him. Even when he had gone forth, they recognized him by that same name. Ativela means having transgressed the time (vela). There, there are three kinds of time: time of day (kālavelā), boundary time (sīmavelā), and morality time (sīlavelā). “At that time, he uttered this utterance” (Dhammapada, summary of the chapters, summary of the verses; Mahāva. 1-3) this is called time of day. “One established in the Dhamma does not transgress the time” (Cūḷava. 384; Udāna 45; A. Ni. 8.19) this is called boundary time. And “non-transgression of time is a dam” (Dha. Sa. 299-301), and “this is time by way of non-transgression” this is called morality time. He transgressed all three of those. For there is a time for admonishing nuns, even when the sun had set, he was admonishing, thus he transgressed that time of day too. For the admonishment of nuns, there is a measure, a boundary, a limit. Admonishing with more than five or six utterances, he transgressed that boundary time too. But when speaking, having made it accompanied by burning, he speaks what is suitable for an offense of wrong speech; thus he transgressed the morality time too.

Ativela saṃsaṭṭho means having become mixed, having become one who shares the same happiness and suffering. Sammukhā means in front of. Avaṇṇaṃ bhāsatī means seeing those nuns doing cooking, pounding, and so on, he says, "There is no non-offense for these, these nuns are of bad conduct, difficult to speak to, impudent," he speaks what is not good. Adhikaraṇampi karotī means, "From the time they are seen, these nuns burn the eyes of these monks; in this monastery, flower offerings or washing seats or preparing paraphernalia or whatever, proceed according to these nuns. These daughters of good families are ashamed, you say this and that to these nuns; this is an offense for you," having come to the presence of the Vinaya-holders, he drags up a case, saying, "Give me a judgment."

Moḷiyaphaggunassa avaṇṇaṃ bhāsatī means, "There is no non-offense for this monk." He speaks what is not good, saying, "The door of this monk's enclosure is never empty of nuns."
Because they also create disputes (adhikaraṇampi karontī), these monks' eyes burn from the time they saw the Elder Moḷiyaphagguna. In this monastery, it is not possible to even look at other dwelling places. Nuns who come to the monastery receive instruction, friendly conversation, or a recitation-section only by relying on the Elder. The clansmen are ashamed and scrupulous, saying, "You say such and such things; come, let's give a judgement in the presence of the Vinaya-holders," and they stir up disputes.

They speak disparagingly of Moḷiyaphagguna, and they also create disputes (adhikaraṇampi karontī).

That monk (so bhikkhu bhagavantaṃ etadavocā) spoke to the Blessed One not out of a desire to be liked, nor with the intention to cause dissension, but out of a desire for what was beneficial. For he had this one thought: "If this monk lives in such close association, dishonor will arise for him. That is also a cause for disparagement of the teaching itself. But if someone else says this, he will not desist; if the Blessed One teaches the Dharma and gives instruction, he will desist." Because he desired what was beneficial for him, he spoke these words to the Blessed One, beginning with, "Venerable sir."

That monk (so bhikkhu bhagavantaṃ etadavocā)

Then the Blessed One addressed a certain bhikkhu (monk) thus: “Come, bhikkhu, tell the bhikkhu Moliya Phagguna in my name that the Teacher calls you, friend Phagguna.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” that bhikkhu replied to the Blessed One, and he went to the venerable Moliya Phagguna, and having gone there, he said to the venerable Moliya Phagguna: “The Teacher calls you, friend Phagguna.”

“Very good, friend,” the venerable Moliya Phagguna replied to that bhikkhu, and he went to the Blessed One, and having gone there, paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side.

As the venerable Moliya Phagguna was sitting at one side, the Blessed One said to him:

“Phagguna, is it true that you are living in overly close association with bhikkhunīs (nuns)? Is it true that you are living in such close association with bhikkhunīs that if any bhikkhu speaks ill of those bhikkhunīs in your presence, you become angry and displeased and even bring a case? And that if any bhikkhu speaks ill of you in the presence of those bhikkhunīs, then they become angry and displeased and even bring a case? Is it true that you are living in such close association with bhikkhunīs?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Phagguna, aren't you a son of good family who went forth from the house life into homelessness out of faith (śraddhā)?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

Since it is not possible to draw a form or show the manifestation of form in it, therefore it is said to be "formless" (arūpī).

"Without appearance" (anidassano) means not being in the range of vision, of visual consciousness (cakṣurvijñāna).

Here, in the connection with the simile, the loving-kindness (maitrī) mind is like space (ākāśa).

Just as four kinds of colors are like five ways of speech with the fifth [color] as brush, so a person who has come taking the five ways of speaking is like a person who has come taking the color with the fifth brush.

Just as he cannot bring about the manifestation of form in space (ākāśa) with those five colors [using] a brush, so also a person who has come grasping the five paths of speech, by changing the loving-kindness (maitrī) mind, will not be able to show the arising of faults.

230. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, puriso āgaccheyya ādittaṃ tiṇukkaṃ ādāya. So evaṃ vadeyya – ‘ahaṃ imāya ādittāya tiṇukkāya gaṅgaṃ nadiṃ santāpessāmi saṃparitāpessāmī’ti. Taṃ kiṃ maññatha, bhikkhave, api nu so puriso ādittāya tiṇukkāya gaṅgaṃ nadiṃ santāpeyya saṃparitāpeyyā’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Taṃ kissa hetu’’? ‘‘Gaṅgā hi, bhante, nadī gambhīrā appameyyā. Sā na sukarā ādittāya tiṇukkāya santāpetuṃ saṃparitāpetuṃ; yāvadeva ca pana so puriso kilamathassa vighātassa bhāgī assā’’ti. ‘‘Evameva kho, bhikkhave, pañcime vacanapathā yehi vo pare vadamānā vadeyyuṃ kālena vā akālena vā…pe… ‘na ceva… tadārammaṇañca sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ gaṅgāsamena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharissāmā’’ti. Evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṃ.
“Bhikkhus, suppose a man came with a blazing grass torch and said: ‘I shall heat up and burn away the river Ganges (gaṅgā nadī) with this blazing grass torch.’ What do you think, bhikkhus? Could that man heat up and burn away the river Ganges (gaṅgā nadī) with that blazing grass torch?” “No, venerable sir.” “What is the reason for that?” “Because the river Ganges (gaṅgā), venerable sir, is deep and immense; it cannot possibly be heated up and burned away with a blazing grass torch; that man would only get his fill of weariness and vexation.” “So too, bhikkhus, there are these five courses of speech that others may use when they address you: their speech may be timely or untimely… ‘Our minds will remain unaffected…and starting with him, we shall abide pervading the all-encompassing world (lokaṃ) with a mind similar to the river Ganges (gaṅgāsamena cetasā), abundant (vipulena), exalted (mahaggatena), immeasurable (appamāṇena), without hostility (averena) and without ill will (abyābajjhena).’ That is how you should train, bhikkhus (evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṃ).
230. Tatiyaupamāyaṃ ādittanti pajjalitaṃ. Gambhīrā appameyyāti imissā gaṅgāya gambhīraṭṭhānaṃ gāvutampi atthi, aḍḍhayojanampi, yojanampi. Puthulaṃ panassā evarūpaṃyeva, dīghato pana pañcayojanasatāni. Sā kathaṃ gambhīrā appameyyāti? Etena payogena parivattetvā uddhane udakaṃ viya tāpetuṃ asakkuṇeyyato. Ṭhitodakaṃ pana kenaci upāyena aṅgulamattaṃ vā aḍḍhaṅgulamattaṃ vā evaṃ tāpetuṃ sakkā bhaveyya, ayaṃ pana na sakkā, tasmā evaṃ vuttaṃ. Upamāsaṃsandane panettha gaṅgā viya mettacittaṃ, tiṇukkaṃ ādāya āgatapuriso viya pañca vacanapathe gahetvā āgatapuggalo. Yathā so ādittāya tiṇukkāya gaṅgaṃ tāpetuṃ na sakkoti, evaṃ vo pañca vacanapathe gahetvā āgatapuggalo mettacittassa aññathattaṃ kātuṃ na sakkhissatīti.
230.
"Āditta" in the third simile means blazing.

"Gambhīrā appameyyā": This Gaṅgā has deep places a gāvuta in depth, or half a yojana, or a yojana.

Its width is just like that, but in length it is five hundred yojanas.

How is it deep and immeasurable? Because it cannot be heated by turning it around with this effort, like water in a pot.

Still water, however, could be heated to the extent of a finger's breadth or half a finger's breadth by some means; but this is not possible, therefore it was said thus.

Here in the connection of similes, mettacitta is like the Gaṅgā; a person who has come taking a grass torch is like a person who has come taking hold of the five paths of speech.

Just as he cannot heat the Gaṅgā with a blazing grass torch, so a person who has come taking hold of the five paths of speech will not be able to change the mettacitta.

231. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, biḷārabhastā madditā sumadditā suparimadditā, mudukā tūlinī chinnasassarā chinnabhabbharā. Atha puriso āgaccheyya kaṭṭhaṃ vā kathalaṃ kaṭhalaṃ (sī. syā. pī.) vā ādāya. So evaṃ vadeyya – ‘ahaṃ imaṃ biḷārabhastaṃ madditaṃ sumadditaṃ suparimadditaṃ, mudukaṃ tūliniṃ, chinnasassaraṃ chinnabhabbharaṃ kaṭṭhena vā kathalena vā sarasaraṃ karissāmi bharabharaṃ karissāmī’ti . Taṃ kiṃ maññatha, bhikkhave, api nu so puriso amuṃ biḷārabhastaṃ madditaṃ sumadditaṃ suparimadditaṃ, mudukaṃ tūliniṃ, chinnasassaraṃ chinnabhabbharaṃ kaṭṭhena vā kathalena vā sarasaraṃ kareyya, bharabharaṃ kareyyā’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Taṃ kissa hetu’’? ‘‘Amu hi, bhante, biḷārabhastā madditā sumadditā suparimadditā, mudukā tūlinī, chinnasassarā chinnabhabbharā. Sā na sukarā kaṭṭhena vā kathalena vā sarasaraṃ kātuṃ bharabharaṃ kātuṃ; yāvadeva ca pana so puriso kilamathassa vighātassa bhāgī assā’’ti. ‘‘Evameva kho, bhikkhave, pañcime vacanapathā yehi vo pare vadamānā vadeyyuṃ kālena vā akālena vā; bhūtena vā abhūtena vā; saṇhena vā pharusena vā; atthasaṃhitena vā anatthasaṃhitena vā; mettacittā vā dosantarā vā. Kālena vā bhikkhave pare vadamānā vadeyyuṃ akālena vā; bhūtena vā, bhikkhave, pare vadamānā vadeyyuṃ abhūtena vā; saṇhena vā, bhikkhave, pare vadamānā vadeyyuṃ pharusena vā; atthasaṃhitena vā, bhikkhave, pare vadamānā vadeyyuṃ anatthasaṃhitena vā; mettacittā vā, bhikkhave, pare vadamānā vadeyyuṃ dosantarā vā. Tatrāpi vo, bhikkhave, evaṃ sikkhitabbaṃ – ‘na ceva no cittaṃ vipariṇataṃ bhavissati, na ca pāpikaṃ vācaṃ nicchāressāma hitānukampī ca viharissāma mettacittā na dosantarā. Tañca puggalaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā pharitvā viharissāma, tadārammaṇañca sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ biḷārabhastāsamena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharissāmā’ti. Evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṃ.
231. Bhikkhus, suppose there were a catskin bag ( biḷārabhastā) that was rubbed, well-rubbed, thoroughly well-rubbed, soft, silky (tūlinī), rid of rustling, rid of crackling.

Then a man came with a stick (kaṭṭhaṃ) or a potsherd (kathalaṃ)— or a kaṭhalaṃ as some say— and took it up.

He would say thus: ‘I shall make this catskin bag ( biḷārabhastaṃ) that is rubbed, well-rubbed, thoroughly well-rubbed, soft, silky, rid of rustling, rid of crackling, rustle (sarasaraṃ) and crackle (bharabharaṃ) with the stick or the potsherd.’

What do you think, bhikkhus? Could that man make that catskin bag ( biḷārabhastaṃ) that is rubbed, well-rubbed, thoroughly well-rubbed, soft, silky, rid of rustling, rid of crackling, rustle (sarasaraṃ) and crackle (bharabharaṃ) with the stick or the potsherd?” “No, venerable sir.”

“What is the reason for that?” “Because that catskin bag ( biḷārabhastā) being rubbed, well-rubbed, thoroughly well-rubbed, soft, silky (tūlinī), rid of rustling, rid of crackling, cannot possibly be made to rustle (sarasaraṃ) or crackle (bharabharaṃ) with the stick or the potsherd. Eventually the man would reap only weariness and disappointment.”

“So too, bhikkhus, there are these five courses of speech that others may use when they address you: their speech may be timely or untimely; true or untrue; gentle or harsh; connected with good or with harm; spoken with a mind of loving-kindness ( mettacittā) or with inner hate (dosantarā).

When others address you, their speech may be timely or untimely; when others address you, their speech may be true or untrue; when others address you, their speech may be gentle or harsh; when others address you, their speech may be connected with good or with harm; when others address you, their speech may be spoken with a mind of loving-kindness ( mettacittā) or with inner hate (dosantarā).

Herein, bhikkhus, you should train thus: ‘Our minds will remain unaffected, and we shall utter no evil words; we shall abide compassionate for their welfare, with a mind of loving-kindness ( mettacittā), without inner hate (dosantarā).

We shall abide pervading that person with a mind imbued with loving-kindness ( mettāsahagatena cetasā); and starting with him, we shall abide pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind similar to a catskin bag ( biḷārabhastāsamena cetasā), abundant, exalted, immeasurable (appamāṇena), without hostility (averena) and without ill will (abyābajjhena).’

That is how you should train, bhikkhus.
231. Catutthaupamāyaṃ biḷārabhastāti biḷāracammapasibbakā. Sumadditāti suṭṭhu madditā. Suparimadditāti anto ca bahi ca samantato suparimadditā. Tūlinīti simbalitūlalatātūlasamānā. Chinnasassarāti chinnasassarasaddā. Chinnabhabbharāti chinnabhabbharasaddā. Upamāsaṃsandane panettha biḷārabhastā viya mettacittaṃ, kaṭṭhakaṭhalaṃ ādāya āgatapuriso viya pañca vacanapathe gahetvā āgatapuggalo. Yathā so kaṭṭhena vā kaṭhalena vā biḷārabhastaṃ sarasaraṃ bharabharaṃ saddaṃ kātuṃ na sakkoti, evaṃ vo pañca vacanapathe gahetvā āgatapuggalo mettacittassa aññathattaṃ katvā dosānugatabhāvaṃ kātuṃ na sakkhissatīti.
231. In the fourth simile, "biḷārabhastā" means a cat-skin bag.

"sumadditā" means well-rubbed.

"suparimadditā" means well-rubbed inside and outside, everywhere.

"tūlinī" means similar to simbali cotton, latā cotton, and tūla cotton.

"chinnasassarā" means a cut, hissing sound.

"chinnabhabbharā" means a cut bhabbhara sound.

Here, in the connection of similes, biḷārabhastā viya mettacittaṃ (the mind of loving-kindness is like a cat-skin bag), kaṭṭhakaṭhalaṃ ādāya āgatapuriso viya pañca vacanapathe gahetvā āgatapuggalo (a person who has come taking a wooden dish is like a person who has come grasping the five paths of speech).

Just as he is not able to make the sound sarassaraṃ bharabharaṃ with a wooden or clay biḷārabhastaṃ, so too, that person who has come grasping the five paths of speech, having made the otherness of the mind of loving-kindness, will not be able to make a state following aversion, thus (iti).

232. ‘‘Ubhatodaṇḍakena cepi, bhikkhave, kakacena corā ocarakā aṅgamaṅgāni okanteyyuṃ, tatrāpi yo mano padūseyya, na me so tena sāsanakaro. Tatrāpi vo, bhikkhave , evaṃ sikkhitabbaṃ – ‘na ceva no cittaṃ vipariṇataṃ bhavissati, na ca pāpikaṃ vācaṃ nicchāressāma, hitānukampī ca viharissāma mettacittā na dosantarā. Tañca puggalaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā pharitvā viharissāma tadārammaṇañca sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyābajjhena pharitvā viharissāmā’ti. Evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṃ.
232. Bhikkhus, even if bandits were to sever you savagely limb by limb with a two-handled saw (ubhatodaṇḍakena kakacena), he who gave rise to a mind of hate (mano padūseyya) towards them would not be carrying out my teaching (sāsanakaro).

Herein, bhikkhus, you should train thus (evaṃ sikkhitabbaṃ): ‘Our minds will remain unaffected (cittaṃ vipariṇataṃ na bhavissati), and we shall utter no evil words (pāpikaṃ vācaṃ na nicchāressāma); we shall abide compassionate for their welfare (hitānukampī viharissāma), with a mind of loving-kindness (mettacittā), without inner hate (na dosantarā).

We shall abide pervading them with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettāsahagatena cetasā pharitvā viharissāma); and starting with them, we shall abide pervading the all-encompassing world (sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ) with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettāsahagatena cetasā), abundant (vipulena), exalted (mahaggatena), immeasurable (appamāṇena), without hostility (averena) and without ill will (abyābajjhena).’

That is how you should train, bhikkhus (evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbaṃ).
232.Ocarakāti avacarakā heṭṭhācarakā, nīcakammakārakāti attho. Yo mano padūseyyāti yo bhikkhu vā bhikkhunī vā mano padūseyya, taṃ kakacena okantanaṃ nādhivāseyya. Na me so tena sāsanakaroti so tena anadhivāsanena mayhaṃ ovādakaro na hoti. Āpatti panettha natthi.
Ocarakā: avacarakā means heṭṭhācarakā, meaning nīcakammakārakā.

Yo mano padūseyyā: Yo, whether a bhikkhu or bhikkhunī, if one corrupts the mind ( mano padūseyya), one should not tolerate being cut with a saw ( kakacena okantanaṃ nādhivāseyya).

Na me so tena sāsanakaro: so tena anadhivāsanena means he is not my advisor ( ovādakaro).

There is no offense (āpatti) here (ettha natthi).

233. ‘‘Imañca imañce (?) tumhe, bhikkhave, kakacūpamaṃ ovādaṃ abhikkhaṇaṃ manasi kareyyātha. Passatha no tumhe, bhikkhave, taṃ vacanapathaṃ, aṇuṃ vā thūlaṃ vā, yaṃ tumhe nādhivāseyyāthā’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Tasmātiha, bhikkhave, imaṃ kakacūpamaṃ ovādaṃ abhikkhaṇaṃ manasikarotha. Taṃ vo bhavissati dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāyā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Kakacūpamasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.
233.

“And therefore, bhikkhus, you should keep this advice on the simile of the saw (kakacūpamaṃ ovādaṃ) constantly in mind (abhikkhaṇaṃ manasi kareyyātha).

Do you see any course of speech, trivial (aṇuṃ) or gross (thūlaṃ), that you could not endure?” “No, venerable sir.”

“Therefore, bhikkhus, you should keep this advice on the simile of the saw (kakacūpamaṃ ovādaṃ) constantly in mind (abhikkhaṇaṃ manasikarotha).

That will lead to your welfare (hitāya) and happiness (sukhāya) for a long time (dīgharattaṃ).”

Thus spoke the Blessed One (bhagavā).

Elated (attamanā), those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement (bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti).

The Kakacūpamasuttaṃ, the first, is finished.
233.Aṇuṃ vā thūlaṃ vāti appasāvajjaṃ vā mahāsāvajjaṃ vā. Yaṃ tumhe nādhivāseyyāthāti yo tumhehi adhivāsetabbo na bhaveyyāti attho. No hetaṃ, bhanteti, bhante, anadhivāsetabbaṃ nāma vacanapathaṃ na passāmāti adhippāyo. Dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāyāti iti bhagavā arahattena kūṭaṃ gaṇhanto yathānusandhinā desanaṃ niṭṭhapesīti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Kakacūpamasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
233. "Aṇuṃ vā thūlaṃ vā" means whether it is a small offense (appasāvajjaṃ) or a great offense (mahāsāvajjaṃ).

"Yaṃ tumhe nādhivāseyyāthā" means the meaning (attho) is that which should not be tolerated (adhivāsetabbo) by you (tumhehi), should not happen (na bhaveyya).

"No hetaṃ, bhante" means, O venerable one (bhante), the meaning (adhippāyo) is that we do not see (na passāmāti) a way of speaking (vacanapathaṃ) called (nāma) intolerable (anadhivāsetabbaṃ).

"Dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāyā" thus, the Blessed One (bhagavā), grasping (gaṇhanto) the peak (kūṭaṃ) through Arahantship (arahattena), completed (niṭṭhapesīti) the teaching (desanaṃ) in accordance with the connection (yathānusandhinā).
