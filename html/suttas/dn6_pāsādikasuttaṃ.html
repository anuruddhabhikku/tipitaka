Thus I have heard: On one occasion the Blessed One (Bhagavān) was staying among the Sakyans (Śākyas) in the mansion in the mango grove of the Sakyan family called Vedhañña.

Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta's Death

And on that occasion, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta had just died in Pāvā.

On his death, the Nigaṇṭhas had split into two factions. They kept arguing, quarreling, & disputing, stabbing one another with weapons of the mouth: “You don’t understand this Dhamma-Vinaya. I understand this Dhamma-Vinaya. How could you understand this Dhamma-Vinaya? Your way is wrong. My way is right. I am consistent. You are inconsistent. What should have been said first you said last. What should have been said last you said first. What you cogitated so long has been turned inside out. Your position has been overthrown. You’re trapped, so go try to release your position or disentangle yourself if you can!”

It seems to me that only slaughter (vadho) goes on among the Nigaṇṭhas, the followers of Nāṭaputta.

And his white-clothed lay disciples were disgusted, dismayed, and disappointed with the Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta’s pupils, as they were with his badly proclaimed and badly expounded Dhamma-Vinaya, which was unemancipating, unconducive to peace, expounded by one not fully enlightened, and was now with its shrine broken, left without a refuge.

Herein, the explanation of the terms that are not obvious: vedhaññā nāma sakyā means the Śākyas called Vedhañña who have practiced archery.

tesaṃ ambavane pāsāde means that in their mango grove there is a long palace built for learning crafts, there [they] live.

adhunā kālaṅkato means now he has died.

dvedhikajātā means those born of two branches, those born of two parts.

bhaṇḍanādīsu quarreling is the preliminary stage of strife; when that is increased by means of punishment, seizure, transgression of the precepts and so forth, [it is called] kalaho, strife.

"You do not understand this Dharma-vinaya" and so forth; speech that is contradictory in such a way is vivādo, dispute.

vitudantā means piercing.

sahitaṃ me means my speech is connected with meaning (attha).

adhiciṇṇaṃ te viparāvatta means that which is habitually done by you, [that which] is familiar through continuous practice over a long time, that, having come to my vāda, doctrine, has ceased.

āropito te vādo means a fault has been imputed by me onto you.

cara vādappamokkhāyā means having taken a bowl of food, wandering about seeking further means for release from the vāda, doctrine, approach them.

nibbeṭhehi vā means or else free yourself from the fault imputed by me.

sace pahosī means if you are able.

vadhoyevā means it is just death.

nāṭaputtiyesū means among the disciples of Nāṭaputta.

nibbinnarūpā means because of [their] disgusted nature, they do not do even salutations and so forth.

virattarūpā means devoid of affection.

paṭivānarūpā means because of the nature of turning away from their respectful actions.

yathā ta means just as, in a Dharma-vinaya that is badly proclaimed and so forth, one must be of a disgusted, detached, and averse nature; the meaning is that they are just like that.

durakkhāte means badly spoken.

duppavedite means badly explained.

anupasamasaṃvattanike means unable to calm the passions and so forth.

bhinnathūpe means with a broken stupa.

Here, Nāṭaputta himself is their stupa in the sense of being a foundation.

But he is thought of as broken.

Therefore it was said, "with a broken stupa."

appaṭisaraṇe means without a refuge because of the absence of that very thing.

Now, why did this Nāṭaputta, a resident of Nāḷanda, die in Pāvā? It is said that Upāli, having heard the Buddha's virtues spoken in ten verses expressing the truth discerned by the householder, vomited hot blood. Then, having taken him away as he was unwell, they went to Pāvā. There he died. While dying, he thought: "My doctrine is not one that leads out [of suffering], it is devoid of essence. We, indeed, are destroyed. May others not be filled with misfortune. But if I say, 'My teaching does not lead out [of suffering],' they will not believe it. I will have two people learn [it] in a way that is not unified. They will dispute with each other at my passing away. The Teacher, having relied upon that dispute, will speak a discourse on the Dharma. Then they will know the greatness of the teaching."

"Yes, venerable sir," the novice Cunda replied to the venerable Ānanda.

Then, the venerable Ānanda and the novice Cunda approached the Blessed One; having approached, they paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side.

188. Therefore, he is called the tathāgata.
Just as it should be spoken, so it is said; from speaking, having made the takāra from the dakāra, therefore, it is said that he is the tathāgata.
Diṭṭha means the rūpāyatana (sense-sphere of forms).
Suta means the saddāyatana (sense-sphere of sounds).
Muta means, having sensed, having attained, because it is apprehended, the gandhāyatana (sense-sphere of smells), the rasāyatana (sense-sphere of tastes), the phoṭṭhabbāyatana (sense-sphere of tangibles).
Viññāta means the sukhadukkhādidhammāyatanaṃ (sense-sphere of phenomena relating to pleasure, pain, etc.).
Patta means either by seeking, or without seeking, it is attained (pattaṃ).
Pariyesita means either attained (pattaṃ) or unattained (apattaṃ) by seeking (pariyesitaṃ).
Anuvicaritaṃ manasā means it is mentally traversed by the mind (cittena anusañcaritaṃ).

“The tathāgata is completely awakened (abhisambuddha)” is what this shows. Namely, in the immeasurable world-systems (lokadhātūsu), to this world together with the devas (sadevakassa lokassa), blue, yellow, and so forth—the rūpārammaṇa (object of visual form) comes into the range (āpāthamāgacchati) of the eye-door (cakkhudvāre); “This being (satto), in this moment (khaṇe), having seen this such-and-such rūpārammaṇa (object of visual form), has been born pleasant (sumano), or unpleasant (dummano), or neutral (majjhatto)”—all that is thus completely awakened to the tathāgata (tathāgatassa evaṃ abhisambuddhaṃ).

Similarly, that which, in the immeasurable world-systems (lokadhātūsu), to this world together with the devas (sadevakassa lokassa), the sound of the kettledrum, the sound of the mudiṅga, and so forth—the saddārammaṇa (object of sound) comes into the range (āpāthamāgacchati) of the ear-door (sotadvāre).

The root-smell, the bark-smell, and so forth—the gandhārammaṇa (object of smell) comes into the range (āpāthamāgacchati) of the nose-door (ghānadvāre).

The root-taste, the trunk-taste, and so forth—the rasārammaṇa (object of taste) comes into the range (āpāthamāgacchati) of the tongue-door (jivhādvāre).

Roughness, softness, and so forth—the earth-element, fire-element, air-element division—the phoṭṭhabbārammaṇa (object of tactile sensation) comes into the range (āpāthamāgacchati) of the body-door (kāyadvāre).

“This being (satto), in this moment (khaṇe), having touched this such-and-such phoṭṭhabbārammaṇa (object of tactile sensation), has been born pleasant (sumano), or unpleasant (dummano), or neutral (majjhatto)”—all that is thus completely awakened to the tathāgata (tathāgatassa evaṃ abhisambuddhaṃ).

Similarly, that which, in the immeasurable world-systems (lokadhātūsu), to this world together with the devas (sadevakassa lokassa), pleasure, pain, and so forth division—the dhammārammaṇa (object of phenomena) comes into the range (āpāthamāgacchati) of the mind-door (manodvārassa), “This being (satto), in this moment (khaṇe), having cognized this such-and-such dhammārammaṇa (object of phenomena), has been born pleasant (sumano), or unpleasant (dummano), or neutral (majjhatto)”—all that is thus completely awakened to the tathāgata (tathāgatassa evaṃ abhisambuddhaṃ).

Namely, Cunda, to these beings (imesaṃ sattānaṃ), what is seen (diṭṭhaṃ), heard (sutaṃ), sensed (mutaṃ), cognized (viññātaṃ) there, nothing is unseen (adiṭṭhaṃ), or unheard (asutaṃ), or unsensed (amutaṃ), or uncognized (aviññātaṃ) by the tathāgata.
To this great multitude (imassa mahājanassa), having sought, there is attainment (pattampi atthi); having sought, there is non-attainment (appattampi atthi).
Without seeking, there is attainment (pattampi atthi); without seeking, there is non-attainment (appattampi atthi).
All of that, there is nothing un-attained by the tathāgata (tathāgatassa appattaṃ nāma natthi), namely, un-experienced by knowledge (ñāṇena asacchikataṃ nāma).
‘‘Therefore, he is called the tathāgata (tasmā tathāgatoti vuccatī)’’.
That which is gone to by the world (yaṃ yathā lokena gataṃ), because of his having gone to that in just that way (tassa tatheva gatattā), ‘‘he is called the tathāgata (tathāgato’’ti vuccati)’’.
In the pāḷi, however, it is said completely awakened (abhisambuddhanti vuttaṃ); that is one in meaning with gone to (gatasaddena ekatthaṃ).
By this method (iminā nayena), in all instances (sabbavāresu), the meaning of the conclusion ‘‘tathāgato (tathāgato’’ti nigamanassa attho),’ is to be understood (veditabbo); its reasoning (tassa yutti) is stated in detail about the word tathāgata (tathāgatasaddavitthāre vuttāyeva) in the brahmajāla.

And I do not see that this doctrine is even equal to mine, so how better?

In fact, my doctrine is the better one, that is, the super-wisdom (adhiprajñāpti)."

<p n="195" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">195</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Tatra, cunda, ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘arūpī attā hoti…pe… rūpī ca arūpī ca attā hoti… nevarūpīnārūpī attā hoti… saññī attā hoti… asaññī attā hoti… nevasaññīnāsaññī <pb ed="T" n="3.0155"/> attā hoti… attā ucchijjati vinassati na hoti paraṃ maraṇā, idameva saccaṃ moghamañña’nti. Tyāhaṃ upasaṅkamitvā evaṃ vadāmi – ‘atthi nu kho idaṃ, āvuso, vuccati – ‘‘attā ucchijjati vinassati na hoti paraṃ maraṇā’’’ti? Yañca kho te, cunda, evamāhaṃsu – ‘idameva saccaṃ moghamañña’nti. Taṃ tesaṃ nānujānāmi. Taṃ kissa hetu? Aññathāsaññinopi hettha, cunda, santeke sattā. Imāyapi kho ahaṃ, cunda, paññattiyā neva attanā <pb ed="M" n="3.0116"/> samasamaṃ samanupassāmi, kuto bhiyyo. Atha kho ahameva tattha bhiyyo yadidaṃ adhipaññatti. Ime kho te, cunda, aparantasahagatā diṭṭhinissayā, ye vo mayā byākatā <pb ed="P" n="3.0141"/>, yathā te byākātabbā. Yathā ca te na byākātabbā, kiṃ vo ahaṃ te tathā byākarissāmīti <note>byākarissāmīti (sī. ka.)</note>?

195. There, Cunda, those ascetics and Brahmins who hold such views and opinions: ‘The self is formless ( arūpī attā hoti )…pe… The self is both with form and formless ( rūpī ca arūpī ca attā hoti )… The self is neither with form nor formless ( nevarūpīnārūpī attā hoti )… The self is percipient ( saññī attā hoti )… The self is non-percipient ( asaññī attā hoti )… The self is neither percipient nor non-percipient ( nevasaññīnāsaññī attā hoti )… The self is annihilated and destroyed at death, and does not exist beyond death ( attā ucchijjati vinassati na hoti paraṃ maraṇā ), only this is true, anything else is worthless.’

Approaching them, I say thus: ‘Is it the case, friend, that it is said: “The self is annihilated and destroyed at death, and does not exist beyond death ( attā ucchijjati vinassati na hoti paraṃ maraṇā )”’? And what they say thus, Cunda: ‘Only this is true, anything else is worthless.’

I don’t grant their point ( taṃ tesaṃ nānujānāmi ).

Why is that ( taṃ kissa hetu )? Because there are sentient beings here, Cunda, who are otherwise percipient.

And I do not consider this doctrine to be even equal to mine, let alone better.

In fact, my doctrine is the better one, which is super-wisdom ( adhipaññatti ).

These, Cunda, are the view-dependencies concerning endpoints, which have been declared by me, as they are to be declared ( yathā te byākātabbā ).

And as they are not to be declared, why would I declare them to you?

<p n="196" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">196</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Imesañca, cunda, pubbantasahagatānaṃ diṭṭhinissayānaṃ imesañca aparantasahagatānaṃ diṭṭhinissayānaṃ <pb ed="V" n="3.0105"/> pahānāya samatikkamāya evaṃ mayā cattāro satipaṭṭhānā desitā paññattā. Katame cattāro? Idha, cunda, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ. Vedanāsu vedanānupassī…pe… citte cittānupassī… dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā, vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ. Imesañca cunda, pubbantasahagatānaṃ diṭṭhinissayānaṃ imesañca aparantasahagatānaṃ diṭṭhinissayānaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0156"/> pahānāya samatikkamāya. Evaṃ mayā ime cattāro satipaṭṭhānā desitā paññattā’’ti.

“It’s for the abandoning & transcending of these view-dependencies concerning beginning-points and view-dependencies concerning endpoints that I have thus taught & formulated the four satipaṭṭhānas. Which four? Here, Cunda, a bhikkhu remains focused on the body in & of itself—ardent (ātāpī), alert (sampajāno), & mindful (satimā)—subduing greed (abhijjhā) & distress (domanassa) with reference to the world. He remains focused on feelings… mind… mental qualities in & of themselves—ardent, alert, & mindful—subduing greed & distress with reference to the world. It’s for the abandoning & transcending of these view-dependencies concerning beginning-points and view-dependencies concerning endpoints that I have thus taught & formulated these four satipaṭṭhānas.”
<p n="196" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">196</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi><hi rend="bold">Pahānāyā</hi>ti pajahanatthaṃ. <hi rend="bold">Samatikkamāyā</hi>ti tasseva vevacanaṃ. <hi rend="bold">Desitā</hi>ti kathitā. <hi rend="bold">Paññattā</hi>ti ṭhapitā. Satipaṭṭhānabhāvanāya hi ghanavinibbhogaṃ katvā sabbadhammesu yāthāvato diṭṭhesu ‘‘suddhasaṅkhārapuñjoyaṃ nayidha sattūpalabbhatī’’ti sanniṭṭhānato sabbadiṭṭhinissayānaṃ pahānaṃ hotīti. Tena vuttaṃ. Diṭṭhinissayānaṃ pahānāya samatikkamāya evaṃ mayā ime cattāro satipaṭṭhānā desitā paññattā’’ti. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti.

</hi><hi rend="bold">Pahānāyā</hi>ti: with the meaning of abandoning (pajahanatthaṃ).
<hi rend="bold">Samatikkamāyā</hi>ti: a synonym for that itself (tasseva vevacanaṃ).
<hi rend="bold">Desitā</hi>ti: spoken (kathitā).
<hi rend="bold">Paññattā</hi>ti: established (ṭhapitā).
Indeed, through the cultivation of the establishments of mindfulness (satipaṭṭhānabhāvanāya), having made a solid discernment (ghanavinibbhogaṃ katvā), when all phenomena are seen as they actually are (sabbadhammesu yāthāvato diṭṭhesu), from the conviction that "this is merely a mass of pure formations (suddhasaṅkhārapuñjoyaṃ), no being is found here (nayidha sattūpalabbhatī)", the abandoning of all view-dependencies (sabbadiṭṭhinissayānaṃ pahānaṃ) occurs (hotīti).
Therefore it has been said (tena vuttaṃ):
"For the abandoning and transcending of view-dependencies (diṭṭhinissayānaṃ pahānāya samatikkamāya), thus, by me, these four establishments of mindfulness (ime cattāro satipaṭṭhānā) have been taught and established (desitā paññattā)."
The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti).

Pāsādiko vatāyaṃ, bhante, dhammapariyāyo; supāsādiko vatāyaṃ bhante, dhammapariyāyo, ko nāmāyaṃ bhante dhammapariyāyo’’ti? ‘‘Tasmātiha tvaṃ, upavāṇa, imaṃ dhammapariyāyaṃ ‘pāsādiko’ tveva naṃ dhārehī’’ti. <p rend="centre">Pāsādikasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ.

At that time, the venerable Upavāṇa was standing behind the Blessed One, fanning him.

Then the venerable Upavāṇa said this to the Blessed One: “Amazing, venerable sir, astonishing, venerable sir! How pleasing is this exposition of the Dharma, venerable sir; how very pleasing is this exposition of the Dharma, venerable sir. What is the name of this exposition of the Dharma, venerable sir?”

“Therefore, Upavāṇa, you should remember this exposition of the Dharma as ‘Pleasing (pāsādiko).’”

This is what the Buddha said.

The venerable Upavāṇa, pleased, rejoiced at the Blessed One’s words.

The Pāsādika Sutta, the sixth, is finished.
