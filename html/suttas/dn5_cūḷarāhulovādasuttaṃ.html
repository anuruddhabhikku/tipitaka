<p n="416" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">416</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0504"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0334"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho bhagavato rahogatassa paṭisallīnassa evaṃ cetaso parivitakko udapādi – ‘‘paripakkā kho <pb ed="M" n="3.0325"/> rāhulassa vimuttiparipācanīyā dhammā. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ rāhulaṃ uttariṃ āsavānaṃ khaye vineyya’’nti. Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi. Sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya caritvā pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto āyasmantaṃ rāhulaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘gaṇhāhi, rāhula, nisīdanaṃ; yena andhavanaṃ tenupasaṅkamissāma divāvihārāyā’’ti <pb ed="P" n="3.0278"/>. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā rāhulo bhagavato paṭissutvā nisīdanaṃ ādāya bhagavantaṃ piṭṭhito piṭṭhito anubandhi.

<p rend="bodytext">Tena kho pana samayena anekāni devatāsahassāni bhagavantaṃ anubandhāni honti – ‘‘ajja bhagavā āyasmantaṃ rāhulaṃ uttariṃ āsavānaṃ khaye vinessatī’’ti. Atha kho bhagavā andhavanaṃ ajjhogāhetvā aññatarasmiṃ rukkhamūle paññatte āsane nisīdi. Āyasmāpi kho rāhulo bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho āyasmantaṃ rāhulaṃ bhagavā etadavoca –

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Savatthi in the Jeta Grove, Anathapindika’s Park.

Then, when the Blessed One was in seclusion, withdrawn, this thought arose in his mind: "The qualities conducive to the deliverance of Rāhula are mature. Suppose I were to further train Rāhula in the destruction of the āsavas (influxes, taints)."

Then, early in the morning, the Blessed One, having dressed and taken his bowl and robe, went into Savatthi for alms. After wandering for alms in Savatthi, after the meal, returning from his alms round, he addressed the venerable Rāhula, "Take the sitting cloth, Rāhula; we will go to the Andhavana (Blind Men's Grove) for the day's retreat."

"So be it, Lord," replied the venerable Rāhula, and taking his sitting cloth, he followed the Blessed One closely behind.

Then, on that occasion, many thousands of deities followed the Blessed One, thinking, "Today the Blessed One will further train the venerable Rāhula in the destruction of the āsavas."

Then the Blessed One entered the Andhavana (Blind Men's Grove) and sat down at the foot of a certain tree, on a seat made ready.

The venerable Rāhula also paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side.

Having sat down to one side, the Blessed One said to the venerable Rāhula:

417. “Rahula, what do you think? Is the eye permanent (nityaṃ) or impermanent (anityaṃ)?” “Impermanent (anityaṃ), venerable sir (bhante).”
“But that which is impermanent (anityaṃ), is that suffering (duḥkhaṃ) or happiness (sukhaṃ)?” “Suffering (duḥkhaṃ), venerable sir (bhante).”
“And is it fitting to regard what is impermanent (anityaṃ), suffering (duḥkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadharmaṃ) as: ‘This is mine (etaṃ mama). This is my self (esohamasmi). This is what I am (eso me attā)’?” “No, venerable sir (no hetaṃ, bhante).”
“Rahula, what do you think? Are forms (rūpā) permanent (niccā) or impermanent (aniccā)?” “Impermanent (aniccā), venerable sir (bhante).”
“But that which is impermanent (anityaṃ), is that suffering (duḥkhaṃ) or happiness (sukhaṃ)?” “Suffering (duḥkhaṃ), venerable sir (bhante).”
“And is it fitting to regard what is impermanent (anityaṃ), suffering (duḥkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadharmaṃ) as: ‘This is mine (etaṃ mama). This is my self (esohamasmi). This is what I am (eso me attā)’?” “No, venerable sir (no hetaṃ, bhante).”
“Rahula, what do you think? Is eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇaṃ) permanent (niccaṃ) or impermanent (anityaṃ)?” “Impermanent (anityaṃ), venerable sir (bhante).”
“But that which is impermanent (anityaṃ), is that suffering (duḥkhaṃ) or happiness (sukhaṃ)?” “Suffering (duḥkhaṃ), venerable sir (bhante).”

“But that which is impermanent (anityaṃ), is that suffering (duḥkhaṃ) or happiness (sukhaṃ)?” “Suffering (duḥkhaṃ), venerable sir (bhante).”
“And is it fitting to regard what is impermanent (anityaṃ), suffering (duḥkhaṃ), subject to change (vipariṇāmadharmaṃ) as: ‘This is mine (etaṃ mama). This is my self (esohamasmi). This is what I am (eso me attā)’?” “No, venerable sir (no hetaṃ, bhante).”

Are any feeling (vedanāgataṃ), any perception (saññāgataṃ), any formations (saṅkhāragataṃ), any consciousness (viññāṇagataṃ) that arise with eye-contact (cakkhusamphassa) as condition per-manent (niccaṃ) or impermanent (aniccaṃ)?” “Impermanent (anityaṃ), venerable sir (bhante).”

418. “Rahula, what do you think? Is the ear permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir…pe… Is the nose permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir…pe… Is the tongue permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir…pe… Is the body permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir…pe… Is the mind permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But that which is impermanent, is that suffering or happiness?”

“Suffering, venerable sir.”

“And is it fitting to regard what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Rahula, what do you think? Are phenomena ( dharmas) permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But that which is impermanent, is that suffering or happiness?”

“Suffering, venerable sir.”

“And is it fitting to regard what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Rahula, what do you think? Is mind-consciousness ( manoviññāṇa) permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But that which is impermanent, is that suffering or happiness?”

“Suffering, venerable sir.”

“And is it fitting to regard what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Rahula, what do you think? Is mind-contact ( manosamphassa) permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But that which is impermanent, is that suffering or happiness?”

“Suffering, venerable sir.”

“And is it fitting to regard what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

“Rahula, what do you think? Any feeling ( vedanā), any perception ( saññā), any formations ( saṅkhāra), any consciousness ( viññāṇa) that arise with mind-contact ( manosamphassa) as condition, are these permanent or impermanent?”

“Impermanent, venerable sir.”

“But that which is impermanent, is that suffering or happiness?”

“Suffering, venerable sir.”

“And is it fitting to regard what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change as: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?”

“No, venerable sir.”

Nibbindaṃ virajjati, virāgā vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānātī’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā rāhulo bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Imasmiñca pana veyyākaraṇasmiṃ bhaññamāne āyasmato rāhulassa anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimucci. Tāsañca anekānaṃ devatāsahassānaṃ virajaṃ vītamalaṃ dhammacakkhuṃ udapādi – ‘yaṃ kiñci samudayadhammaṃ sabbaṃ taṃ nirodhadhamma’’nti.

419. Seeing thus, Rāhula, a well-taught noble disciple becomes disenchanted with the eye (cakṣusmiṃ), disenchanted with forms (rūpesu), disenchanted with eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇe), disenchanted with eye-contact (cakkhusamphasse), and disenchanted with any feeling (vedanāgataṃ), any perception (saññāgataṃ), any formations (saṅkhāragataṃ), any consciousness (viññāṇagataṃ) that arise with eye-contact as condition.

He becomes disenchanted with the ear (sotasmiṃ), disenchanted with sounds (saddesu)…pe… , He becomes disenchanted with the nose (ghānasmiṃ), disenchanted with odours (gandhesu)… He becomes disenchanted with the tongue (jivhāya), disenchanted with tastes (rasesu)… He becomes disenchanted with the body (kāyasmiṃ), disenchanted with tangibles (phoṭṭhabbesu)… He becomes disenchanted with the mind (manasmiṃ), disenchanted with mind-objects (dhammesu), disenchanted with mind-consciousness (manoviññāṇe), disenchanted with mind-contact (manosamphasse), and disenchanted with any feeling (vedanāgataṃ), any perception (saññāgataṃ), any formations (saṅkhāragataṃ), any consciousness (viññāṇagataṃ) that arise with mind-contact as condition.

Being disenchanted (nibbindaṃ), he becomes dispassionate (virajjati). Through dispassion (virāgā), he is liberated (vimuccati). When it is liberated (vimuttasmiṃ) there comes the knowledge (ñāṇaṃ): ‘It is liberated (vimuttamiti)’. He understands (pajānāti): ‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ) has been lived (vusitaṃ), what had to be done (karaṇīyaṃ) has been done (kataṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti)’”.

The Blessed One (bhagavā) spoke this (idamavoca). The venerable (āyasmā) Rāhula was satisfied and delighted (attamano abhinandīti) in the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) words (bhāsitaṃ). Now while this discourse (veyyākaraṇasmiṃ bhaññamāne) was being spoken, through not clinging (anupādāya) the venerable (āyasmato) Rāhula’s mind (cittaṃ) was liberated (vimucci) from the taints (āsavehi). And in those many thousands of deities (tāsañca anekānaṃ devatāsahassānaṃ) there arose the spotless, immaculate vision of the Dhamma (virajaṃ vītamalaṃ dhammacakkhuṃ udapādi): “All that is subject to arising (yaṃ kiñci samudayadhammaṃ) is subject to cessation (sabbaṃ taṃ nirodhadhammaṃ)”.

The Cūḷarāhulovādasuttaṃ, the fifth, is finished (niṭṭhitaṃ pañcamaṃ).
