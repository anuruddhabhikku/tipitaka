364. Thus I have heard: At one time the Blessed One was dwelling among the Sakyas at Medāḷupa, a Sakyan town.

Now on that occasion King Pasenadi of Kosala had arrived at Nagaraka for some business or other.

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala addressed Dīgha Kārāyana: “Dear Kārāyana, have the state carriages prepared. Let us go to the pleasure garden (uyyānabhūmi) to see a pleasing spot.”

“Yes, sire,” replied Dīgha Kārāyana to King Pasenadi of Kosala. When the state carriages (bhadrāni bhadrāni yānāni) were prepared, he informed King Pasenadi of Kosala: “Sire, the state carriages (bhadrāni bhadrāni yānāni) are ready for you. Now is the time, sir, to do as you think fit.”

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala mounted a state carriage (bhadraṃ yānaṃ), and accompanied by the other carriages (bhadrehi bhadrehi yānehi), he drove out from Nagaraka with the full pomp of royalty.

He proceeded towards the park (ārāma).

He went by chariot as far as possible, then dismounted from his carriage (yānā), and entered the park (ārāmaṃ) on foot.

King Pasenadi of Kosala saw in the park (ārāme) the roots of trees (rukkhamūlāni) that were lovely, inspiring, quiet and undisturbed by voices, with an atmosphere of seclusion, remote from people, favourable for retreat (paṭisallānasāruppāni).

The sight of these reminded him of the Blessed One thus: “These roots of trees (rukkhamūlāni) are lovely and inspiring, quiet and undisturbed by voices, with an atmosphere of seclusion, remote from people, favourable for retreat (paṭisallānasāruppāni), like the places where we used to pay respect to the Blessed One, the Arhat, the Fully Enlightened One (sammāsambuddha).”

kenacideva karaṇīyenā means not by any other doing. But he ordered Bandhula, the general, "Go with thirty-two sons and seize [them] in one day!" On that very day, the Blessed One was invited by Mallikā, the wife of him, together with five hundred monks. Having come to the house in the Sangha headed by the Buddha, as soon as they were seated, they brought the message "The general has died" and gave it to Mallikā.

She took the letter and asked for the verbal message.

"General, together with thirty-two sons, has been seized by the king in a single strike, O lady!" they announced.

"Lest it become associated with a great crowd," having made a letter with an ovaṭṭika, she served the Sangha of monks.

At that time, a pot of ghee was brought forth. It broke, having struck the threshold. Having removed that and having had another brought, she served the Sangha of monks.

The Teacher, having finished the meal, for the purpose of starting a conversation, said, "One should not be concerned about the broken condition of the ghee pot."

At that time, Mallikā, having brought forth a letter, having placed it before the Blessed One, said, "Blessed One, this is the message of the general's death, together with thirty-two sons. I do not even consider this. Why should I consider [the breaking of] the ghee pot?"

The Blessed One said, "Mallikā, do not be concerned, this happens to those who are wandering in the round of rebirths which has no known beginning." Having made a Dhamma talk connected with impermanence and so on, he departed.

Mallikā, having had thirty-two daughters-in-law summoned, gave an exhortation.

The king, having had Mallikā summoned, asked, "Is there any offense different among us of the general?"

"There is no lord," [she said].

He, knowing the state of being without offense of him by her word, produced remorse, a strong grief.

He, "Having considered me a friend who does no wrong, he has destroyed a helper who had come," from then on, not obtaining enjoyment of the mind in palaces or in dramas or in royal pleasures, began to wander here and there.

This very thing became the duty.

This was said with reference to that: "By some doing."

This was said with reference to him.

Then King Pasenadi of Kosala addressed Dīgha Kārāyana: "Indeed, friend Kārāyana, these are tree roots, inspiring, delightful, quiet, without noise, with breezes blowing through deserted spots, hidden from people, suitable for retreat, where we might attend to the Blessed One, the arhat, the Fully Awakened One (sammāsambuddha)."

Te bhagavatā dhammiyā kathāya sandassitā samādapitā samuttejitā sampahaṃsitā na ceva bhagavantaṃ pañhaṃ pucchanti, kuto vādaṃ āropessanti? Aññadatthu bhagavantaṃyeva okāsaṃ yācanti agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajjāya. Te bhagavā pabbājeti. Te tathāpabbajitā samānā ekā vūpakaṭṭhā appamattā ātāpino pahitattā viharantā nacirasseva – yassatthāya kulaputtā sammadeva agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajanti tadanuttaraṃ – brahmacariyapariyosānaṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharanti. Te evamāhaṃsu – ‘manaṃ vata, bho, anassāma; manaṃ vata, bho, panassāma’. Mayañhi pubbe assamaṇāva samānā samaṇāmhāti paṭijānimhā, abrāhmaṇāva samānā brāhmaṇāmhāti paṭijānimhā, anarahantova samānā arahantāmhāti paṭijānimhā. ‘Idāni khomha samaṇā, idāni khomha brāhmaṇā, idāni khomha arahanto’ti. Ayampi kho me, bhante, bhagavati dhammanvayo hoti – ‘sammāsambuddho bhagavā, svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo, suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho’ti.
“Furthermore, venerable sir, I see here certain learned brāhmaṇas…pe… learned householders…pe… learned ascetics, who are clever, skilled in refuting the doctrines of others, and like hairs split by an arrow.

They wander about, as it were, demolishing the views of others with their sharp wits (prajñā).

They hear: ‘The śramaṇa Gotama will visit such and such a village or town.’

They formulate a question thus: ‘We will go to the śramaṇa Gotama and ask him this question.

If he is asked like this, he will answer like this, and so we will refute his doctrine (vāda) in this way; and if he is asked like that, he will answer like that, and so we will refute his doctrine in that way.’

They hear: ‘The śramaṇa Gotama has come to visit such and such a village or town.’

They go to the Blessed One (bhagavā).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) instructs, urges, rouses, and encourages them with a talk on the Dharma (dhammiyā kathāya).

After they have been instructed, urged, roused, and encouraged by the Blessed One (bhagavatā) with a talk on the Dharma (dhammiyā kathāya), they do not so much as ask him the question, so how should they refute his doctrine (vāda)? Instead, they only request the opportunity from the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃyeva) to go forth from the house life (agārasmā) into homelessness (anagāriyaṃ) in the pravrajya.

The Blessed One (bhagavā) gives them the going forth (pabbājeti).

Not long after they have thus gone forth (tathāpabbajitā), dwelling alone, withdrawn, diligent, ardent, and resolute, by realizing for themselves with direct knowledge they here and now enter upon and abide in that supreme goal of the holy life (brahmacariyapariyosānaṃ) for the sake of which clansmen rightly go forth from the home life (agārasmā) into homelessness (anagāriyaṃ) (pabbajanti).

They say thus: ‘We were very nearly lost, we very nearly perished.’

For formerly we claimed that we were śramanas though we were not really śramanas; we claimed that we were brāhmaṇas though we were not really brāhmaṇas; we claimed that we were arhats though we were not really arhats.

But now we are śramanas, now we are brāhmaṇas, now we are arhats.’

This too, venerable sir (bhante), is why I infer according to Dharma (dhammanvayo) about the Blessed One (bhagavati): ‘The Blessed One (bhagavā) is a fully enlightened one (sammāsambuddho), the Dharma (dhammo) is well proclaimed by the Blessed One (bhagavatā), the Sangha (saṅgho) of the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) disciples (sāvaka) is practising the good way.’"

373. ‘‘Puna caparāhaṃ, bhante, ime isidattapurāṇā thapatayo mamabhattā mamayānā, ahaṃ nesaṃ jīvikāya jīvitassa (sī.), jīvikaṃ (sī. aṭṭha.), jīvitaṃ (syā. kaṃ. pī. ka.) dātā, yasassa āhattā; atha ca pana no tathā mayi nipaccakāraṃ karonti yathā bhagavati. Bhūtapubbāhaṃ, bhante, senaṃ abbhuyyāto samāno ime ca isidattapurāṇā thapatayo vīmaṃsamāno aññatarasmiṃ sambādhe āvasathe vāsaṃ upagacchiṃ. Atha kho, bhante, ime isidattapurāṇā thapatayo bahudeva rattiṃ dhammiyā kathāya vītināmetvā, yato ahosi bhagavā assosuṃ kho bhagavantaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) tato sīsaṃ katvā maṃ pādato karitvā nipajjiṃsu. Tassa mayhaṃ, bhante, etadahosi – ‘acchariyaṃ vata, bho, abbhutaṃ vata, bho! Ime isidattapurāṇā thapatayo mamabhattā mamayānā, ahaṃ nesaṃ jīvikāya dātā, yasassa āhattā; atha ca pana no tathā mayi nipaccakāraṃ karonti yathā bhagavati. Addhā ime āyasmanto tassa bhagavato sāsane uḷāraṃ pubbenāparaṃ visesaṃ jānantī’ti. Ayampi kho me, bhante, bhagavati dhammanvayo hoti – ‘sammāsambuddho bhagavā, svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo, suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho’ti.
Again, venerable sir, Isidatta and Purāṇa, my two inspectors, eat my food and use my carriages; I provide them with a livelihood (jīvikā) and bring them fame. Yet in spite of this, they are less respectful towards me than they are towards the Blessed One (bhagavat).

Once, venerable sir, when I was on a military campaign, while considering these two inspectors, Isidatta and Purāṇa, I approached lodging in a crowded rest house. Then these two inspectors, Isidatta and Purāṇa, after spending much of the night in talk on the dharma, lay down with their heads in the direction where they had heard the Blessed One (bhagavat) was staying and with their feet towards me.

Then, venerable sir, it occurred to me: ‘It is wonderful, truly amazing! These two inspectors, Isidatta and Purāṇa, eat my food and use my carriages; I provide them with a livelihood (jīvikā) and bring them fame. Yet in spite of this, they are less respectful towards me than they are towards the Blessed One (bhagavat). Surely these good people perceive successive states of lofty distinction in the Blessed One’s (bhagavat) Dispensation.’

This too, venerable sir, is why I infer according to dharma about the Blessed One (bhagavat): ‘The Blessed One (bhagavat) is fully enlightened (sammāsambuddha), the dharma is well proclaimed by the Blessed One (bhagavat), the saṅgha of the Blessed One’s (bhagavat) disciples is practising the good way.’

Yena rājā, tena sīse kate satthā pādato hoti, kiṃ karissāmā’’ti? Tato nesaṃ etadahosi – ‘‘rājā kuppamāno yaṃ amhākaṃ deti, taṃ acchindeyya. Na kho pana mayaṃ sakkoma jānamānā satthāraṃ pādato kātu’’nti rājānaṃ pādato katvā nipajjiṃsu. Taṃ sandhāya ayaṃ rājā evamāha.
373. Isidattapurāṇā means Isidatta and Purāṇa.

Among them, one was a celibate (brahmacārī), the other content with his own wife (sadārasantuṭṭho).

Mamabhattā means my own food (mama santakaṃ bhattaṃ). These are mamabhattā.

Mamayānā means my own vehicle (mama santakaṃ yānaṃ). These are mamayānā.

Jīvikāya dātā means the giver of livelihood (jīvitavuttiṃ dātā).

Vīmaṃsamāno means examining (upaparikkhamāno).

Then, it seems the king, as if sleep had not come upon him, lay down having the appearance of one who was asleep.

Then those stewards, having asked, "In which direction is the Blessed One (bhagavā)?" and having heard, "In such and such a place," conferred, "When the Perfectly Awakened One (sammāsambuddho) is at the head, the king is at the feet.

When the king is at the head, the teacher (satthā) is at the feet, what shall we do?" Then this occurred to them: "The king, being angry, might take back what he gives to us.

Surely, we are not able, knowingly, to place the teacher (satthāraṃ) at the feet," and they lay down having placed the king at the head.

374. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhante, bhagavāpi khattiyo, ahampi khattiyo; bhagavāpi kosalo, ahampi kosalo; bhagavāpi āsītiko, ahampi āsītiko. Yampi, bhante, bhagavāpi khattiyo ahampi khattiyo, bhagavāpi kosalo ahampi kosalo, bhagavāpi āsītiko ahampi āsītiko; imināvārahāmevāhaṃ imināpāhaṃ (ka.), bhante, bhagavati paramanipaccakāraṃ kātuṃ, mittūpahāraṃ upadaṃsetuṃ. Handa, ca dāni mayaṃ, bhante, gacchāma; bahukiccā mayaṃ bahukaraṇīyā’’ti. ‘‘Yassadāni tvaṃ, mahārāja, kālaṃ maññasī’’ti. Uggaṇhatha, bhikkhave, dhammacetiyāni; pariyāpuṇātha, bhikkhave , dhammacetiyāni; dhāretha, bhikkhave, dhammacetiyāni. Atthasaṃhitāni, bhikkhave, dhammacetiyāni ādibrahmacariyakānī’’ti. “Furthermore, venerable sir, the Blessed One is also a noble (kṣatriya), and I am also a noble; the Blessed One is also a Kosalan, and I am also a Kosalan; the Blessed One is eighty years old, and I am eighty years old.

Since that is so, venerable sir, I think it is proper to do such supreme honor (paramanipaccakāra) to the Blessed One and to show such friendship (mittūpahāra).

And now, venerable sir, we depart. We are busy and have much to do.”

“Do whatever seems fit, great king (mahārāja).”

Then King Pasenadi Kosala got up from his seat, bowed down to the Blessed One, and keeping him to his right, departed.

Then, soon after King Pasenadi of Kosala had left, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus thus: “Bhikkhus, this King Pasenadi of Kosala, having spoken of the monuments to the Dharma (dharmacetiyāni), rose from his seat and departed.

Learn the monuments to the Dharma (dharmacetiyāni), bhikkhus; master the monuments to the Dharma (dharmacetiyāni); remember the monuments to the Dharma (dharmacetiyāni).

The monuments to the Dharma (dharmacetiyāni) are beneficial (atthasaṃhitāni), bhikkhus, and they belong to the fundamentals of the holy life (ādibrahmacariyakāni).”

374. "Having departed ( pakkāmī)," [means that] having left the Perfumed Chamber, he went to Kārāyana's dwelling place. Not seeing him there, he went to the encampment; even there, not seeing another person, he asked that woman.
She told him everything, the whole story (pavattiṃ ācikkhi).
The king [thought]: "Now I must not go there alone. Having gone to Rājagaha, having come back together with my nephew, I will take my kingdom (rajjaṃ gaṇhissāmī)." As he was going to Rājagaha, on the road, he ate some kaṇājaka food and also drank a lot of water.
The food did not digest well in him, accustomed as he was to delicate nourishment.
Though he reached Rājagaha, he arrived at an improper time, when the gates were shut.
"Today, having slept in the hall, tomorrow I will see my nephew (bhāgineyyaṃ passissāmī)," he thought and lay down in the hall outside the city.
In the night, he had to get up many times; a few times he went outside.
From then on, unable to go on foot, having lain down in that woman's lap, at the break of dawn, he died (kālamakāsi).
She, having realized that he was dead, lamenting, said: "Having had kingship done in two kingdoms, now in another's hall outside the city, having done a death without protection, lies my lord, the Kosala king (kosalarājā)," and so forth, and began to lament loudly (uccāsaddena paridevituṃ ārabhi).
The people heard and announced it to the king (rañño ārocesuṃ).
The king, having come, having seen, having recognized, having understood the reason for his coming, having performed the last rites with great ceremony, proclaimed (bheriṃ carāpetvā): "I will seize Viṭaṭūbha (viṭaṭūbhaṃ gaṇhissāmī)," and assembled his army.
The ministers, having fallen at his feet, said: "If, your majesty, your maternal uncle were healthy, it would be fitting for you to go. But now, Viṭaṭūbha too, relying on you, is able to raise the umbrella," and having made him understand, they prevented him.

"Dhammacetiyānī " means words honoring the Dhamma.
Since, when honor is done to any one of the three jewels, honor is done to all, therefore, when honor is done to the Blessed One, honor is also done to the Dhamma, so the Blessed One said "dhammacetiyānī."
"Ādibrahmacariyakānī" means the first elements of the path of holy life, the preliminary practices.
The rest is clear throughout.

Dhammacetiyānī, ādibrahmacariyakānī.
