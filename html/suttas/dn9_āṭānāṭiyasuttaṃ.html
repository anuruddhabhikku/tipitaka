275. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Rājagṛha on the mountain Vulture Peak (Gṛdhrakūṭa).

Then, the Four Great Kings (cattāro mahārājā), with a large army of yakṣas (yakṣasenā), a large army of gandharvas (gandhabbasenā), a large army of kumbhāṇḍas (kumbhaṇḍasenā), and a large army of nāgas (nāgasenā), having placed a guard over the four quarters, having placed troops in the four directions, having placed a barricade on four sides, when the night was far advanced, illuminating the entire Vulture's Peak with their surpassing radiance, approached the Blessed One; having approached, they respectfully greeted the Blessed One and sat down on one side.

Then some of those yakṣas, after worshipping the Blessed One, sat down on one side; some exchanged greetings with the Blessed One, and after exchanging polite and courteous greetings, sat down on one side; some, after raising their hands in respectful salutation (añjali) to the Blessed One, sat down on one side; some, after announcing their name and clan, sat down on one side; some, remaining silent, sat down on one side.

275. Thus have I heard: the Āṭānāṭiya Sutta.

Here is the word-by-word commentary: "Having established a defense in the four directions" means for the purpose of preventing the army of asuras, Sakka, the lord of the gods, having established protection in the four directions.

"Having established a troop" means having established a troop of strength.

"Having established a barrier" means having established guards in the four directions.

Having thus made the defense of Sakka, the lord of the gods, very well arranged, the seven Buddhas residing in Āṭānāṭa city, having composed this paritta, "Those who do not listen to the command of the Teacher, the dhamma, and our royal command, we will do this and that to them," having made this proclamation, and having arranged a defense for themselves in the four directions with four armies, beginning with the great army of yakkhas, [they] sat down to one side as the night was passing away…

Here, abhikkanta appears in the sense of exhaustion, beauty, handsomeness, approval, etc.

Here, in such passages as "The night is far spent, venerable sir, the first watch has passed, the community of monks has been sitting for a long time, may the Blessed One recite the Pātimokkha to the monks," it appears in the sense of exhaustion.

In such passages as "This is the most outstanding and excellent of these four persons," it is good.

abhikkantāya rattiyā

"Who bows to my feet, blazing with power and glory;
With surpassing beauty, illuminating all directions."

In such passages, [it means] handsome.

In such passages as "It is superb, Gotama," [it means] approval.

Here, however, it is in the sense of exhaustion. Therefore, abhikkantāya rattiyā means parikkhīṇāya rattiyāti, "when the night was becoming exhausted."

Here, abhikkanta appears in the sense of handsomeness. But vaṇṇa appears in the sense of skin, praise, clan group, reason, shape, measure, the form base, and so on.

Here, in such passages as "The Blessed One is golden-skinned," it refers to skin.

In such passages as "When did you concoct that hymn of praise to the monk Gotama, householder?", it is eulogy.
“‘These four, oh Gotama, are colors’ etc. (Dīgha Nikāya 3.1.0143, in the section on families).

“‘Then by what token can one tell a man to be a thief of scents?’ etc. (Saṃyutta Nikāya 1.234), it is a reason.

“‘Having created a great elephant-king-color’ etc. (Saṃyutta Nikāya 1.138), it is in configuration.

Consequently ‘the extreme of whose brilliance’ means ‘the beauty of whose skin’.

The word kevalakappa here has various meanings such as remainderlessness (anavasesa), the most part (yebhuyya), unmixedness (abyāmissa), nothing extra (anatireka), firmness (daḷhattha), dissociation (visaṃyoga), and so on.

For in such passages as ‘A Divine Life as utterly perfect (kevalaparipuṇṇa) and pure (parisuddha), the meaning is remainderlessness (anavasesatā).

In such passages as ‘The whole (kevalakappa) of Aṅga and Magadha will come bringing quantities of food’ it is the most part (yebhuyyatā).

In such passages as “Of the whole (kevalassa) mass of suffering (dukkakkhandha) there is origination (samudayo)", it means unmixedness (abyāmissatā).

In such passages as ‘Surely it is only (kevalaṃ) by mere faith that this venerable one…’ it is with nothing extra (anatirekatā).

In such passages as ‘This co-resident of the venerable Anuruddha’s named Bāhika has taken his stand entirely (kevalakappaṃ) for the schism in the Community’ it is firmness (daḷhatthatā).

In such passages as ‘One who has reached the absolute (kevalī) lived out the life, is called the Supreme Man’ it is dissociation (visaṃyoga).

Here, however, the meaning intended is remainderlessness (anavasesattha).

The word kappa has also many meanings such as exercise of faith (abhisaddahana), lawfulness (vohāra), time (kāla), description (paññatti), cutting (chedana), interpretation (vikappa), fraction (lesa), surroundings (samantabhāva), and so on.
And thus, in such passages as, “That must be believed as a statement of Master Gotama’s, since he is an arhat, a perfectly enlightened one" (Ma. Ni. 1.387), the meaning is the exercise of faith.

In such passages as, “I allow you, monks, to partake of the fruits with five types of samaṇa-kappa” (Cūḷava. 250), it is usage.

In such passages as, "In which I constantly abide" (Ma. Ni. 1.387), it is time.

In such passages as, "Thus spoke venerable Kappa" (Su. Ni. 1098), it is designation.

In such passages as, "Adorned, with trimmed hair and beard" (Vi. Va. 1094), it is cutting.

In such passages as, "A two-finger length is allowable" (Cūḷava. 446), it is alternative; and in such passages as, "Is there a possibility to lie down?" (A. Ni. 8.80), it is a hint.

In such passages as, "Having illuminated the entire veḷuvana" (Saṃ. Ni. 1.94), it is all-aroundness (samantabhāva).

Here, however, the meaning of all-aroundness is intended.

Therefore, "the entire gijjhakūṭa" should be regarded as meaning "Gijjhakūṭa together with its surroundings, without remainder."

Obhāsetvā: Having pervaded with the radiance arising from robes, garlands, ornaments, and the body, making it a single illumination, a single blaze, like the moon or the sun.

Ekantaṃ nisīdiṃsū: The place where the deities sat near the Ten-Powered One was not large, but in this sutta, they sat down out of slight reverence.

276. "Vessavaṇo": Although the four great kings ( catvāro mahārājāno) came, Vessavaṇa was trustworthy to the one with the ten powers (dasabalassa), skilled in initiating conversation, and well-trained. Therefore, Vessavaṇa, the great king ( mahārājā), said this to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ).

"Uḷārā": possessing great power and influence (mahesakkhānubhāvasampannā).

"Pāṇātipātā veramaṇiyā": Having shown the immediate and future dangers (diṭṭhadhammikasamparāyikaṃ ādīnavaṃ) in taking life (pāṇātipāte), he then teaches the Dharma of abstaining from taking life (pāṇātipātā veramaṇiyā).

"Tattha santi uḷārā yakkhā nivāsino": In those lodgings, there are magnificent yakṣas who dwell there permanently (tesu senāsanesu santi uḷārā yakkhā nibaddhavāsino).

"Āṭānāṭiya": because it is connected with the city of Āṭānāṭa, hence the name (āṭānāṭanagare baddhattā evaṃnāmaṃ).

But is there any teaching (apaccakkhadhammo) of the Blessed One (bhagavato) that is not directly known? No (natthi).

Then why did Vessavaṇa say, "May the Blessed One learn it, venerable sir (bhante)?" It was to make an opportunity (okāsakaraṇatthaṃ).

Having made a woman a vehicle, they go in every direction;

The attendants of that king.

Divine vehicles are present;

Palaces and litters,

Āṭānāṭā, Kusināṭā, Parakusināṭā,

To the north are farmers,

And a multitude of people to the west;

Navanavatiya, Ambara-ambaravatiya,

Ālakamandā by name, the king's capital.

For Kuvera, good sir, the great king, Visāṇā by name is the capital;

Therefore Kuvera, the great king, is called "Vessavaṇa."

Ojasi, Tejasi, Tatojasi, Sūra, Rājā, Ariṭṭha, Nemi.

From which the rains descend, and there is also a hall, Sālavatī by name.

Where the yakṣas attend, there are trees with constant fruit;

Various flocks of birds are joined, resounding with peacocks and herons;

With the lovely calls of cuckoos and others.

There, the sound of the jīvañjīvaka bird, and also oṭṭhava and cittaka;

Kukkuṭaka and kuḷīraka, in the forest, pokkharasātaka.

There, the sound of śuka and sāḷika, and daṇḍamāṇavaka;

That Kuvera's lotus pool always shines at all times.

"From here is the northern direction," thus people declare;

The direction that the glorious great king protects.

And the lord of the yakṣas, "Kuvera" by name;

292. ‘Ayaṃ kho sā, mārisa , āṭānāṭiyā rakkhā bhikkhūnaṃ bhikkhunīnaṃ upāsakānaṃ upāsikānaṃ guttiyā rakkhāya avihiṃsāya phāsuvihārāya. 292. ‘This, dear Sir, is the Āṭānāṭiya protection, for the safety, defense, non-violence, and comfortable dwelling of the monks (bhikkhu), nuns (bhikkhunī), male lay practitioners (upāsaka), and female lay practitioners (upāsikā).

Whoever, dear Sir, be they monk (bhikkhu) or nun (bhikkhunī) or male lay practitioner (upāsaka) or female lay practitioner (upāsikā), if this Āṭānāṭiya protection is well-grasped, complete, and learned, then if any non-human being, be they male yakṣa or female yakṣa…pe… male gandharva or female gandharva…pe… male kumbhāṇḍa or female kumbhāṇḍa…pe… male nāga or female nāga or young nāga or young female nāga or great nāga minister or nāga councilor or nāga messenger, with corrupted mind, should follow or approach a monk (bhikkhu) or nun (bhikkhunī) or male lay practitioner (upāsaka) or female lay practitioner (upāsikā) who is walking, standing, sitting, or lying down,

that non-human being, dear Sir, would not receive honor or respect in my villages or towns.

That non-human being, dear Sir, would not receive ground or dwelling in my royal city, Āḷakamandā by name.

That non-human being, dear Sir, would not be able to go to the gathering of the yakṣas.

Further, dear Sir, non-human beings would not give or take him in marriage.

Further, dear Sir, non-human beings would abuse him with very personal abuse.

Further, dear Sir, non-human beings would drop an empty bowl over his head.

Further, dear Sir, non-human beings would split his head into seven pieces.

For there are non-human beings, dear Sir, who are fierce, cruel, and violent, they do not take notice of the Great Kings (mahārāja), they do not take notice of the Great Kings’ men, they do not take notice of the Great Kings’ men’s men.

And those non-human beings, dear Sir, are said to be in revolt against the Great Kings (mahārāja).

Just as, dear Sir, there are great thieves in the realm of the king of Magadha,

who do not take notice of the king of Magadha, who do not take notice of the king of Magadha’s men, who do not take notice of the king of Magadha’s men’s men,

and those great thieves, dear Sir, are said to be in revolt against the king of Magadha.

Even so, dear Sir, there are non-human beings who are fierce, cruel, and violent, they do not take notice of the Great Kings (mahārāja), they do not take notice of the Great Kings’ men, they do not take notice of the Great Kings’ men’s men.

And those non-human beings, dear Sir, are said to be in revolt against the Great Kings (mahārāja).

Now, dear Sir, whatever non-human being, be they male yakṣa or female yakṣa…pe… male gandharva or female gandharva…pe… male kumbhāṇḍa or female kumbhāṇḍa…pe… male nāga or female nāga…pe… with corrupted mind, should approach a monk (bhikkhu) or nun (bhikkhunī) or male lay practitioner (upāsaka) or female lay practitioner (upāsikā) who is walking, standing, sitting, or lying down,

then to these yakṣas, great yakṣas, generals, and great generals, one should call out, one should shout out, one should cry out: ‘This yakṣa has seized me, this yakṣa has grabbed me, this yakṣa annoys me, this yakṣa harasses me, this yakṣa hurts me, this yakṣa injures me, this yakṣa will not release me.’
