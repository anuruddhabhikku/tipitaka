201. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati gijjhakūṭe pabbate sūkarakhatāyaṃ. Atha kho dīghanakho paribbājako yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho dīghanakho paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ahañhi, bho gotama, evaṃvādī evaṃdiṭṭhi – ‘sabbaṃ me nakkhamatī’’’ti. ‘‘Yāpi kho te esā, aggivessana, diṭṭhi – ‘sabbaṃ me nakkhamatī’ti, esāpi te diṭṭhi nakkhamatī’’ti? ‘‘Esā ce esāpi (ka.) me, bho gotama, diṭṭhi khameyya, taṃpassa tādisameva, taṃpassa tādisamevā’’ti. ‘‘Ato kho te, aggivessana, bahū hi bahutarā lokasmiṃ ye evamāhaṃsu – ‘taṃpassa tādisameva, taṃpassa tādisamevā’ti. Te tañceva diṭṭhiṃ nappajahanti aññañca diṭṭhiṃ upādiyanti. Ato kho te, aggivessana, tanū hi tanutarā lokasmiṃ ye evamāhaṃsu – ‘taṃpassa tādisameva, taṃpassa tādisamevā’ti. Te tañceva diṭṭhiṃ pajahanti aññañca diṭṭhiṃ na upādiyanti. Santaggivessana, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘sabbaṃ me khamatī’ti; santaggivessana, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘sabbaṃ me nakkhamatī’ti; santaggivessana , eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘ekaccaṃ me khamati, ekaccaṃ me nakkhamatī’ti. Tatraggivessana, ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘sabbaṃ me khamatī’ti tesamayaṃ diṭṭhi sārāgāya santike, saññogāya santike, abhinandanāya santike ajjhosānāya santike upādānāya santike; tatraggivessana ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘sabbaṃ me nakkhamatī’ti tesamayaṃ diṭṭhi asārāgāya santike, asaññogāya santike, anabhinandanāya santike, anajjhosānāya santike, anupādānāya santike’’ti.
201. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Boar’s Cave on the mountain Vulture Peak.

Then the wanderer Dīghanakha went to the Blessed One and exchanged greetings with him.

After an exchange of friendly greetings & courtesies, he stood to one side.

Standing to one side, the wanderer Dīghanakha said to the Blessed One: “Master Gotama, my doctrine and view is this: ‘Nothing is acceptable to me.’”

“This view of yours, Aggivessana, ‘Nothing is acceptable to me’ - is not at least that view acceptable to you?” “If that view were acceptable to me, Master Gotama, then look at that, it is just the same, look at that, it is just the same."

“Well, Aggivessana, there are plenty in the world who say: ‘It too would be the same, it too would be the same,’

yet they do not abandon that view and they take up still some other view.

Those are few in the world who say: ‘It too would be the same, it too would be the same,’

and who abandon that view and do not take up some other view.

Aggivessana, there are some śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas whose doctrine and view is this: ‘Everything is acceptable to me.’ Aggivessana, there are some śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas whose doctrine and view is this: ‘Nothing is acceptable to me.’ Aggivessana, there are some śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas whose doctrine and view is this: ‘Something is acceptable to me, something is not acceptable to me.’

Among these, the view of those śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas who hold the doctrine and view ‘Everything is acceptable to me’ is close to lust (sārāga), close to bondage (saṃyoga), close to delighting (abhinandana), close to holding (ajjhosāna), close to clinging (upādāna). The view of those śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas who hold the doctrine and view ‘Nothing is acceptable to me’ is close to non-lust (asārāga), close to non-bondage (asaṃyoga), close to non-delighting (anabhinandana), close to non-holding (anajjhosāna), close to non-clinging (anupādāna).”
201.Evaṃme sutanti dīghanakhasuttaṃ. Tattha sūkarakhatāyanti sūkarakhatāti evaṃnāmake leṇe. Kassapabuddhakāle kira taṃ leṇaṃ ekasmiṃ buddhantare pathaviyā vaḍḍhamānāya antobhūmigataṃ jātaṃ. Athekadivasaṃ eko sūkaro tassa chadanapariyantasamīpe paṃsuṃ khaṇi. Deve vuṭṭe paṃsudhoto chadanapariyanto pākaṭo ahosi. Eko vanacarako disvā – ‘‘pubbe sīlavantehi paribhuttaleṇena bhavitabbaṃ, paṭijaggissāmi na’’nti samantato paṃsuṃ apanetvā leṇaṃ sodhetvā kuṭṭaparikkhepaṃ katvā dvāravātapānaṃ yojetvā supariniṭṭhita-sudhākammacittakammarajatapaṭṭasadisāya vālukāya santhatapariveṇaṃ leṇaṃ katvā mañcapīṭhaṃ paññāpetvā bhagavato vasanatthāya adāsi. Leṇaṃ gambhīraṃ ahosi otaritvā abhiruhitabbaṃ. Taṃ sandhāyetaṃ vuttaṃ. Dīghanakhoti tassa paribbājakassa nāmaṃ. Upasaṅkamīti kasmā upasaṅkami? So kira there aḍḍhamāsapabbajite cintesi – ‘‘mayhaṃ mātulo aññaṃ pāsaṇḍaṃ gantvā na ciraṃ tiṭṭhati, idāni panassa samaṇassa gotamassa santikaṃ gatassa aḍḍhamāso jāto. Bhagavā panassa adhippāyaṃ muñcitvā akkhare tāva dosaṃ dassento yāpi kho tetiādimāha. Tattha esāpi te diṭṭhi nakkhamatīti esāpi te paṭhamaṃ ruccitvā khamāpetvā gahitadiṭṭhi nakkhamatīti. Esā ce me, bho gotama, diṭṭhi khameyyāti mayhañhi sabbaṃ nakkhamatīti diṭṭhi, tassa mayhaṃ yā esā sabbaṃ me nakkhamatīti diṭṭhi, esā me khameyya. Yaṃ taṃ ‘‘sabbaṃ me nakkhamatī’’ti vuttaṃ, tampissa tādisameva. Yathā sabbagahaṇena gahitāpi ayaṃ diṭṭhi khamati, evamevaṃ tampi khameyya . Evaṃ attano vāde āropitaṃ dosaṃ ñatvā taṃ pariharāmīti saññāya vadati, atthato panassa ‘‘esā diṭṭhi na me khamatī’’ti āpajjati. Yassa panesā na khamati na ruccati, tassāyaṃ tāya diṭṭhiyā sabbaṃ me na khamatīti diṭṭhi rucitaṃ. Tena hi diṭṭhiakkhamena arucitena bhavitabbanti sabbaṃ khamatīti ruccatīti āpajjati. Na panesa taṃ sampaṭicchati, kevalaṃ tassāpi ucchedadiṭṭhiyā ucchedameva gaṇhāti. Tenāha bhagavā ato kho te, aggivessana,…pe… aññañca diṭṭhiṃ upādiyantīti. Tattha atoti pajahanakesu nissakkaṃ, ye pajahanti, tehi ye nappajahantīti vucciyanti, teva bahutarāti attho. Bahū hi bahutarāti ettha hikāro nipātamattaṃ, bahū bahutarāti attho. Mūlasassataṃ pana appahāya aññaṃ sassatameva sakkā gahetuṃ. Kathaṃ? Yathā ca khandhesu, evaṃ āyatanesupi yojetabbaṃ. Idaṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ – ‘‘tañceva diṭṭhiṃ nappajahanti, aññañca diṭṭhiṃ upādiyantī’’ti. Dutiyavāre atoti appajahanakesu nissakkaṃ, ye nappajahanti, tehi, ye pajahantīti vucciyanti, teva tanutarā appatarāti attho. Ekasmiñhi samaye ‘‘rūpaṃ sassata’’nti gahetvā aparasmiṃ samaye tattha ādīnavaṃ disvā ‘‘oḷārikametaṃ mayhaṃ dassana’’nti pajahati ‘‘na kevalañca rūpaṃ sassatanti dassanameva oḷārikaṃ, vedanāpi sassatā…pe… viññāṇampi sassatanti dassanaṃ oḷārikamevā’’ti vissajjeti . Ucchedepi ekaccasassatepi eseva nayo. Yathā ca khandhesu, evaṃ āyatanesupi yojetabbaṃ. Evaṃ tañca mūladassanaṃ pajahanti, aññañca dassanaṃ na gaṇhanti. ato Tañceva diṭṭhiṃ pajahanti, aññañca diṭṭhiṃ na upādiyantī Santaggivessanāti kasmā ārabhi? Ayaṃ ucchedaladdhiko attano laddhiṃ nigūhati, tassā pana laddhiyā vaṇṇe vuccamāne attano laddhiṃ pātukarissatīti tisso laddhiyo ekato dassetvā vibhajituṃ imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi. Santaggivessanā Sārāgāya santiketiādīsu rāgavasena vaṭṭe rajjanassa āsannā taṇhādiṭṭhisaṃyojanena vaṭṭasaṃyojanassa santike. Abhinandanāyāti taṇhādiṭṭhivaseneva gilitvā pariyādiyanassa gahaṇassa ca āsannāti attho. Asārāgāya santiketiādīsu vaṭṭe arajjanassa āsannātiādinā nayena attho veditabbo. Sārāgāya santike Abhinandanāyā Asārāgāya santike Ettha ca sassatadassanaṃ appasāvajjaṃ dandhavirāgaṃ, ucchedadassanaṃ mahāsāvajjaṃ khippavirāgaṃ. Kathaṃ? Sassatavādī hi idhalokaṃ paralokañca atthīti jānāti, sukatadukkaṭānaṃ phalaṃ atthīti jānāti, kusalaṃ karoti, akusalaṃ karonto bhāyati, vaṭṭaṃ assādeti, abhinandati. Buddhānaṃ vā buddhasāvakānaṃ vā sammukhībhūto sīghaṃ laddhiṃ jahituṃ na sakkoti. Tasmā taṃ sassatadassanaṃ appasāvajjaṃ dandhavirāganti vuccati. 201. Thus I have heard: the Dīghanakha Sūtra.

There, sūkarakhatāya means in a cave named Sūkarakhatā.

It is said that in the time of the Buddha Kassapa, that cave became located inside the earth as the earth grew during one Buddha-interval.

Then, one day, a certain pig dug the earth near the edge of its roof.

When it rained, the edge of the roof, cleansed by the rain, became visible.

A certain forester saw it and thought, “It must have been a cave frequented by virtuous people in the past. I will take care of it.” Having removed the earth from all around, cleaned the cave, made a wall enclosure, connected the door and window, made the cave a seat-enclosure with sand like a very well-finished stucco work, a painting, a silver plate, prepared a bed and seat, and gave it to the Blessed One for residence.

The cave was deep; one had to descend and ascend.

This was said with reference to that.

Dīghanakha means the name of that wanderer.

Why did [he] approach? A month after he had renounced [the world], he thought: “My maternal uncle, having gone to another sect, does not stay long. Now, a half-month has passed since he went to the presence of the ascetic Gotama.

I do not hear of his conduct. Is the teaching substantial? I will know," he became desirous of going.

Therefore, he approached.

Ekamantaṃ ṭhito means that at that time, the elder, fanning the Blessed One, was standing, [and] the wanderer, being respectfully and humbly [approached] the maternal uncle, asked a question.

Therefore, it was said, “Standing to one side.”

Sabbaṃ me nakkhamatī means all my upapattis do not please me, all my rebirths do not please me, [thus] he speaks with that intention.

By this much, it is made known “I am an annihilationist."

The Blessed One, having released his intention, while showing the fault in the letters themselves, said, “Whatever, Aggivessana,…”

There, esāpi te diṭṭhi nakkhamatī means this view also does not please you; this view which was first liked by you, having been caused to be forgiven, was taken; that view does not please you.

Esā ce me, bho Gotama, diṭṭhi khameyya means all does not please me; that view, whatever view of mine [says] all does not please me, may that please me.

That which was said, “All does not please me,” that too is just like that.

Just as this view, though taken with all grasping, is pleasing, even so may that please.

Thus, knowing the fault attributed to his own statement, he speaks with the perception, “I will avoid that.” In reality, however, he incurs, “This view does not please me.”

For whomever this is not pleasing, nor delights [him], for him, by that view, the view that all is not pleasing to me is pleasing.

Therefore, it should be that by the unpleasant, disagreeable view, all is pleasing; thus, he incurs delight.

But he does not accept that, but only grasps only annihilation by that annihilationist view.

Therefore, the Blessed One said: “Therefore, Aggivessana,…pe… they undertake another view.”

There, ato means without firmness in those to be abandoned. Those who abandon, and by those who are said not to abandon, they are more numerous, is the meaning.

Bahū hi bahutarāti here the hi sound is merely a particle; many are more numerous, is the meaning.

After that, tanū hi tanutarāti this is also the same method in that phrase.

Ye evamāhaṃsūti means those who say thus.

Tañceva diṭṭhiṃ nappajahanti, aññañca diṭṭhiṃ upādiyantīti they do not abandon the root view, they undertake another view.
Everything is not pleasing to me. Indeed, that view is not pleasing to you either. If that view were pleasing to me, O Gotama...

Therefore, O Aggivessana,… and who take up some other view. Therefore, many indeed are far more numerous.

Thin indeed, thinner still are those who have said thus, and who do not abandon that very view, and who take up some other view.

Herein, having taken permanence and without abandoning that, it is not possible to take annihilation or partial permanence; and having taken annihilation, without abandoning that, it is not possible to take permanence or partial permanence; and having taken partial permanence, without abandoning that, it is not possible to take permanence or annihilation.

But without abandoning fundamental permanence, it is only possible to take another permanence.

How? At one time, having taken, “Form is permanent,” at another time he takes, “Not only is form permanent, feeling too is permanent, consciousness too is permanent.”

In the case of annihilation and partial permanence too, the same method applies.

Just as with the aggregates, so too it should be connected with the sense-bases.

This was said with reference to, "and who do not abandon that very view, and who take up some other view."

In the second utterance, therefore, "because" means certainty with regard to those who do not abandon. Those who do not abandon, by them, those who are said to abandon, their meaning is that they are thinner, fewer.

"And who abandon that very view, and who do not take up some other view" means they abandon that fundamental view, and do not take up some other view.

How? At one time, having taken, "Form is permanent," at another time, having seen the fault therein, he abandons, "This view of mine is coarse." "Not only is form permanent, but the view itself is coarse, feeling too is permanent… consciousness too is permanent, so the view is coarse," he discards.

In the case of annihilation and partial permanence too, the same method applies.

Just as with the aggregates, so too it should be connected with the sense-bases.

Thus, they abandon that fundamental view, and do not take up some other view.

Therefore.

"Contentious Aggivessana"

Why was it begun? This annihilationist conceals his own view, but when the good qualities of that view are being spoken of, he will reveal his own view. Therefore, having shown the three views together, he began this teaching in order to distinguish them.

"Contentious Aggivessana"

"Near to passion," and so forth, means near to the attachment of passion, near to the attachment of the cycle, through the fetter of craving and views.

"For rejoicing" means near to the seizing and devouring and consuming, precisely through the power of craving and views, is the meaning.

"Near to non-passion," and so forth, the meaning should be understood by the method beginning with near to the non-attachment of the cycle.

Near to passion, for rejoicing, near to non-passion.

Herein, the view of permanence is of little fault, of slow fading away, the view of annihilation is of great fault, of rapid fading away.

How? The proponent of permanence knows, "There is this world and the next world," he knows, "There is fruit of good and bad actions," he does good, fearing when doing bad, he savors the cycle, he rejoices.

Being face to face with the Buddhas or the Buddha's disciples, he is not able to quickly abandon his view.

Therefore, that view of permanence is said to be of little fault, of slow fading away.

But the proponent of annihilation knows, "There is this world and the next world," he knows, "There is fruit of good and bad actions," he does not do good, not fearing when doing bad, he does not savor the cycle, he does not rejoice, in the presence of the Buddhas or the Buddha's disciples, he quickly abandons his view.

Being able to fulfill the perfections, he becomes a Buddha, not being able, having made an aspiration, he becomes a disciple and attains final nirvāṇa.

Therefore, the view of annihilation is said to be of great fault, of rapid fading away.

Evaṃ vutte, dīghanakho paribbājako bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ukkaṃseti ukkaṃsati (sī. pī. ka.) me bhavaṃ gotamo diṭṭhigataṃ, samukkaṃseti sampahaṃsati (ka.) me bhavaṃ gotamo diṭṭhigata’’nti. ‘‘Tatraggivessana, ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘ekaccaṃ me khamati, ekaccaṃ me nakkhamatī’ti. Yā hi tesaṃ khamati sāyaṃ diṭṭhi sārāgāya santike, saññogāya santike, abhinandanāya santike, ajjhosānāya santike, upādānāya santike; yā hi tesaṃ nakkhamati sāyaṃ diṭṭhi asārāgāya santike, asaññogāya santike, anabhinandanāya santike, anajjhosānāya santike, anupādānāya santike. Tatraggivessana, ye te samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘sabbaṃ me khamatī’ti tattha viññū puriso iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘yā kho me ayaṃ diṭṭhi – sabbaṃ me khamatīti, imañce ahaṃ diṭṭhiṃ thāmasā parāmāsā abhinivissa vohareyyaṃ – idameva saccaṃ moghamaññanti; dvīhi me assa viggaho – yo cāyaṃ samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā evaṃvādī evaṃdiṭṭhi – sabbaṃ me nakkhamatīti, yo cāyaṃ samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā evaṃvādī evaṃdiṭṭhi – ekaccaṃ me khamati, ekaccaṃ me nakkhamatīti – imehi assa dvīhi viggaho. Iti viggahe sati vivādo, vivāde sati vighāto, vighāte sati vihesā’. Iti so viggahañca vivādañca vighātañca vihesañca attani sampassamāno tañceva diṭṭhiṃ pajahati aññañca diṭṭhiṃ na upādiyati. Evametāsaṃ diṭṭhīnaṃ pahānaṃ hoti, evametāsaṃ diṭṭhīnaṃ paṭinissaggo hoti.

When this was said, the wanderer Dīghanakha addressed the Blessed One, saying: “Reverend Gotama, you commend my point of view (dṛṣṭigata), Reverend Gotama, you highly commend my point of view.”

“Aggivessana, as to those śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas who hold this doctrine and this view: ‘Something is acceptable to me, something is not acceptable to me,’

- the view of theirs as to what is acceptable is close to lust (sārāga), close to bondage (saṃyoga), close to delighting (abhinandana), close to holding (ajjhosāna), close to clinging (upādāna), while the view of theirs as to what is not acceptable is close to non-lust (asārāga), close to non-bondage (asaṃyoga), close to non-delighting (anabhinandana), close to non-holding (anajjhosāna), close to non-clinging (anupādāna).

“Now, Aggivessana, a wise man among those śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas who hold the doctrine and view ‘Everything is acceptable to me’ considers thus: ‘If I obstinately adhere to my view “Everything is acceptable to me” and declare: “Only this is true, anything else is wrong,” then I may clash with the two others: with a śramaṇa or brāhmaṇa who holds the doctrine and view “Nothing is acceptable to me” and with a śramaṇa or brāhmaṇa who holds the doctrine and view “Something is acceptable to me, something is not acceptable to me.”

When there is a clash (vigraha), there are disputes (vivāda); when there are disputes, there are quarrels (vighāta); when there are quarrels, there is vexation (vihesā)’.

Foreseeing for himself clashes, disputes, quarrels, and vexation, he abandons that view (dṛṣṭi) and does not take up some other view.

This is how there comes to be the abandoning (pahāna) of these views; this is how there comes to be the relinquishing (pratinissagga) of these views.

Now, Aggivessana, this body (kāya), made of material form (rūpī), consisting of the four great elements (cātumahābhūtika), procreated by a mother and father (mātāpettikasambhava), and built up out of boiled rice and porridge (odanakummāsupacaya), is subject to impermanence, to being worn and rubbed away, to dissolution and disintegration (aniccucchādanaparimaddanabhedanaviddhaṃsanadhamma). It should be regarded as impermanent, as suffering (dukkha), as a disease (roga), as a tumor (gaṇḍa), as a dart (salla), as a calamity (agha), as an affliction (ābādha), as alien, as disintegrating, as void (śūnyatā), as not self (anatta).

When one regards this body (kāya) as impermanent, as suffering (dukkha), as a disease (roga), as a tumor (gaṇḍa), as a dart (salla), as a calamity (agha), as an affliction (ābādha), as alien, as disintegrating, as void (śūnyatā), as not self (anatta), one abandons desire for the body (kāyachanda), affection for the body (kāyasneha), subservience to the body (kāyanvayatā).

There are, Aggivessana, three kinds of feeling (vedanā): pleasant feeling (sukhā vedanā), painful feeling (dukkha vedanā), and neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhā vedanā).

On the occasion when one feels pleasant feeling (sukhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti), Aggivessana, one does not feel painful feeling (dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti); on that occasion one feels only pleasant feeling (sukhaṃyeva tasmiṃ samaye vedanaṃ vedeti).

On the occasion when one feels painful feeling (dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti), Aggivessana, one does not feel pleasant feeling (sukhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti); on that occasion one feels only painful feeling (dukkhaṃyeva tasmiṃ samaye vedanaṃ vedeti).

On the occasion when one feels neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti), Aggivessana, one does not feel pleasant feeling (sukhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti) or painful feeling (dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ vedeti); on that occasion one feels only neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhaṃyeva tasmiṃ samaye vedanaṃ vedeti).

Pleasant feeling (sukhā vedanā), Aggivessana, is impermanent (aniccā), conditioned (saṅkhatā), dependently arisen (paṭiccasamuppannā), subject to destruction (khayadhammā), vanishing (vayadhammā), fading away (virāgadhammā), and ceasing (nirodhadhammā). Painful feeling (dukhā vedanā) too, Aggivessana, is impermanent (aniccā), conditioned (saṅkhatā), dependently arisen (paṭiccasamuppannā), subject to destruction (khayadhammā), vanishing (vayadhammā), fading away (virāgadhammā), and ceasing (nirodhadhammā). Neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhā vedanā) too, Aggivessana, is impermanent (aniccā), conditioned (saṅkhatā), dependently arisen (paṭiccasamuppannā), subject to destruction (khayadhammā), vanishing (vayadhammā), fading away (virāgadhammā), and ceasing (nirodhadhammā).

Seeing thus, Aggivessana, a well-taught noble disciple (ariyasāvako) becomes disenchanted (nibbindati) with pleasant feeling (sukhāya vedanāya), disenchanted (nibbindati) with painful feeling (dukhāyapi vedanāya), disenchanted (nibbindati) with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhāyapi vedanāya); being disenchanted (nibbindaṃ), he becomes dispassionate (virajjati); through dispassion (virāgā) his mind is liberated (vimuccati).

When it is liberated (vimuttasmiṃ), there comes the knowledge (ñāṇaṃ hoti): ‘It is liberated (vimuttamiti)’. He understands (pajānāti): ‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the spiritual life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming back to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā)’.

A bhikkhu whose mind is liberated (vimuttacitto) thus, Aggivessana, sides with none and disputes with none; he employs the speech currently used in the world without adhering to it."

Having known form (rūpaṃ abhiññāya), it speaks of abandoning form (rūpassa pahānaṃ vadatīti)—in this way, the meaning here should be understood (atthena veditabbo).

"Paṭisañcikkhato": of one who reflects. (paccavekkhantassa.)

"Anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimuccī": The mind is liberated from the āsavas without clinging due to the cessation of arising; the mind is liberated since the āsavas are blocked without grasping (anuppādanirodhena niruddhehi āsavehi aggahetvāva cittaṃ vimucci).

Thus, having enjoyed food increased by another (parassa vaḍḍhitaṃ bhattaṃ bhuñjitvā), as if dispelling hunger, having sent forth wisdom toward the teaching of the Dharma that was begun by another (āraddhāya dhammadesanāya ñāṇaṃ pesetvā), having increased insight (vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhetvā), he attained arahantship (arahattañceva patto), the summit of the knowledge of a disciple's perfections (sāvakapāramīñāṇassa ca matthakaṃ), and stood having pierced through the sixteen knowledges (soḷasa ca paññā paṭivijjhitvā ṭhito).

But Dīghanakha, having attained the fruit of stream-entry (sotāpattiphalaṃ patvā), was established in the refuges (saraṇesu patiṭṭhito).

"Paṭisañcikkhato"

"Anupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimuccī"

The Blessed One, indeed, having completed this teaching right when the sun was holding [its place], having descended from Vulture Peak (gijjhakūṭā oruyha), having gone to the Bamboo Grove (veḷuvanaṃ gantvā), made an assembly of disciples (sāvakasannipātamakāsi); the assembly was endowed with four factors (caturaṅgasamannāgato sannipāto ahosi).

These are the factors (aṅgāni): it was a full-moon uposatha day joined with the Māgha constellation (māghanakkhattena yutto puṇṇamauposathadivaso), one thousand two hundred fifty monks assembled on their own by nature, without being invited by anyone (kenaci anāmantitāni hutvā attanoyeva dhammatāya sannipatitāni aḍḍhatelasāni bhikkhusatāni), among them there was not even one ordinary person, or one of the stream-enterers, once-returners, non-returners, dry-insight practitioners or arahants, all had the six abhiññās (tesu ekopi puthujjano vā sotāpanna-sakadāgāmi-anāgāmi-sukkhavipassaka-arahantesu vā aññataro natthi, sabbe chaḷabhiññāva), there was not even one here who had gone forth having cut off his hair with a razor (ekopi cettha satthakena kese chinditvā pabbajito nāma natthi), all were "come, monks!" (ehibhikkhunoyevāti).

The Dīghanakhasutta Commentary in the Papañcasūdanī of the Majjhimanikāya is finished (dīghanakhasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā).
