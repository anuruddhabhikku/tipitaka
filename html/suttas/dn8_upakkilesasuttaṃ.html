236. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Kosambi in Ghosita’s Park.

Now on that occasion the bhikkhus at Kosambi had taken to quarrelling and brawling and were deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers.

Then another bhikkhu approached the place where the Blessed One was; having approached, he bowed down to the Blessed One and stood to one side.

As he was standing to one side that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, the bhikkhus here at Kosambi have taken to quarrelling and brawling and are deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers.

It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would go to those bhikkhus out of compassion.”

The Blessed One consented by maintaining silence.

Then the Blessed One went to those bhikkhus and said to them: “Enough, bhikkhus, let there be no quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, or dispute.”

When this was said, another bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Wait, venerable sir! The Blessed One is the master of the Dhamma; let the Blessed One, without concern, abide engaged in dwelling in the bliss of the Dhamma here and now; we are the ones who will be responsible for this quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, and dispute.”

For a second time the Blessed One said to the bhikkhus: “Enough, bhikkhus, let there be no quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, or dispute.”

For a second time that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Wait, venerable sir! The Blessed One is the master of the Dhamma; let the Blessed One, without concern, abide engaged in dwelling in the bliss of the Dhamma here and now; we are the ones who will be responsible for this quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, and dispute.”

For a third time the Blessed One said to the bhikkhus: “Enough, bhikkhus, let there be no quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, or dispute.”

For a third time that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Wait, venerable sir! The Blessed One is the master of the Dhamma; let the Blessed One, without concern, abide engaged in dwelling in the bliss of the Dhamma here and now; we are the ones who will be responsible for this quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, and dispute.”

Then, in the morning, the Blessed One dressed and, taking bowl and robe, entered Kosambi for alms.

Having walked for alms in Kosambi and had returned from the alms round, after his meal he set his lodging in order, took his bowl and robe, and standing right there, he uttered these verses:

237. “The crowd makes a confused noise, no fool thinks himself such;

when the Saṅgha is being split, they think no one else knows more.

“Their wisdom is muddled, they’re only clever in speech,

they want free play for their mouths, but don’t know where they’re led.

‘He abused me, he struck me, he defeated me, he robbed me’—

for those who harbor such thoughts, hatred never ends.

‘He abused me, he struck me, he defeated me, he robbed me’—

for those who do not harbor such thoughts, hatred is stilled.

“For hatreds are never stilled by hatred in this world;

they are stilled by non-hatred — this is the eternal dharma.

“Others do not realize that in this quarrel we are perishing;

those who do realize it, their quarrels are calmed.

“Those who cut bones, take life, and steal cattle, horses, and wealth,

and ravage the realm: even they can have concord.

Why should not you?

“If one can find a prudent friend,

a companion who lives righteously and is wise,

one should live with him joyfully and mindfully,

overcoming all dangers.

“But if one cannot find a prudent friend,

a companion who lives righteously and is wise,

one should wander alone, like a king renouncing his conquered realm,

or like the elephant Matanga in the wilderness.
It is better to wander alone; there is no companionship with fools. One should wander alone and not do evil, unconcerned, like an elephant in the elephant forest (mātaṅgaraññeva nāgo).

238. Then the Blessed One, remaining there, after uttering these verses, went to Bālakaloṇakāragāma.

At that time, the venerable Bhagu was staying at Bālakaloṇakāragāma. The venerable Bhagu saw the Blessed One coming from afar. Seeing him, he arranged a seat and water for washing the feet. Then the Blessed One, having instructed, urged, roused, and encouraged the venerable Bhagu with a Dhamma talk, got up from his seat and went to the Eastern Bamboo Park (Pācīnavaṃsadāya).

At that time, the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Nandiya, and the venerable Kimila were staying at the Eastern Bamboo Park. The park keeper saw the Blessed One coming from afar. Seeing the Blessed One, he said: "Do not enter this park, great contemplative. There are three young men of good family living here, desiring their own well-being. Do not disturb them." The venerable Anuruddha heard the park keeper speaking to the Blessed One. Hearing him, he said to the park keeper: "Do not keep the Blessed One out, friend park keeper. Our teacher, the Blessed One, has arrived."

239. Then the venerable Anuruddha went to the venerable Nandiya and the venerable Kimbila and said: “Come out, venerable sirs, come out! Our Teacher, the Blessed One, has come.”

Then the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Nandiya, and the venerable Kimbila went to meet the Blessed One. One took his bowl and outer robe, one prepared a seat, and one set out water for washing the feet (pādodakaṃ upaṭṭhapesi).

The Blessed One sat down on the seat that was prepared (nisīdi bhagavā paññatte āsane).

Having sat down, he washed his feet (nisajja pāde pakkhālesi).

Then those venerable ones paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down at one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu).

As they were sitting to one side, the Blessed One said to the venerable Anuruddha: “I hope you are well, Anuruddha, I hope you are comfortable, I hope you are not having any trouble getting almsfood?”

“We are keeping well, Blessed One, we are comfortable, and we are not having any trouble getting almsfood.”

“But, Anuruddha, are you living harmoniously, cordially, and without dispute, blending like milk and water, looking at one another with eyes of affection?”

“Indeed, venerable sir, we are living harmoniously, cordially, and without dispute, blending like milk and water, looking at one another with eyes of affection.”

“But how is it that you, Anuruddha, are living harmoniously, cordially, and without dispute, blending like milk and water, looking at one another with eyes of affection?”

“Here, venerable sir, I think thus: ‘It is a gain for me (lābhā vata me), it is a great gain for me (suladdhaṃ vata me) that I am living with such companions in the holy life.’

I maintain bodily acts of loving-kindness (mettaṃ kāyakammaṃ) towards those venerable ones both openly (āvi) and privately (raho ca), I maintain verbal acts of loving-kindness (mettaṃ vacīkammaṃ) towards them both openly (āvi) and privately (raho ca), I maintain mental acts of loving-kindness (mettaṃ manokammaṃ) towards them both openly (āvi) and privately (raho ca).

I consider: ‘Why should I not set aside what I wish to do and do what these venerable ones wish to do (sakaṃ cittaṃ nikkhipitvā imesaṃyeva āyasmantānaṃ cittassa vasena vatteyya)?’

And having put aside my own thoughts, I do conduct myself in line with the thoughts of these venerable ones.

Our bodies are separate, Lord, but we are one in mind, as it were (nānā hi kho no, bhante, kāyā, ekañca pana maññe citta).’”

The venerable Nandiya…pe… The venerable Kimila said this to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, I too think thus: ‘It is a gain for me, it is a great gain for me that I am living with such companions in the holy life.’

I maintain bodily acts of loving-kindness towards those venerable ones both openly and privately, I maintain verbal acts of loving-kindness towards them both openly and privately, I maintain mental acts of loving-kindness towards them both openly and privately.

I consider: ‘Why should I not set aside what I wish to do and do what these venerable ones wish to do?’

Thus, venerable sir, we are living harmoniously, cordially, and without dispute, blending like milk and water, looking at one another with eyes of affection.”

240. “Excellent, excellent, Anuruddhas! But do you, Anuruddhas, abide diligent, ardent, and resolute?” “Surely, venerable sir, we abide diligent, ardent, and resolute.”

“But, Anuruddhas, how do you abide diligent, ardent, and resolute?” “Here, venerable sir, whichever of us returns first from the village for alms lays out the seats, sets out water for drinking (pānīya) and water for washing (paribhojanīya), and sets out the refuse bucket.

Whichever of us returns last from the village for alms, if there are remains of a meal, eats them if he wishes; if he does not wish, he throws them away where there is little greenery or drops them into water where there are no living beings. He puts away the seats, puts away the water for drinking (pānīya) and water for washing (paribhojanīya), washes and puts away the refuse bucket, and sweeps the refectory.

Whoever sees that the pot for drinking water (pānīyaghaṭaṃ), the pot for washing water (paribhojanīyaghaṭaṃ), or the water pot in the restroom (vaccaghaṭaṃ) is empty (rittaṃ) refills it.

If it occurs to him, ‘It’s too much for me,’ calling another by waving—using hand signals—we refill it, we do not, for that reason, break out into speech.

And every five days we sit together the whole night discussing the dharma (dhammiyā kathāya).

That is how we abide diligent, ardent, and resolute.”

243. “As, Anuruddha, I was abiding diligent, ardent, and resolute, I perceived light but I did not see forms; I saw forms but I did not perceive light, even for a whole night or a whole day, or a whole day and night.”

I thought: ‘What is the cause and condition for this? ‘On the occasion when I do not attend to the sign of forms but attend to the sign of light, I then perceive light but do not see forms. On the occasion when I do not attend to the sign of light but attend to the sign of forms, I then see forms but do not perceive light, even for a whole night or a whole day or a whole day and night.’

“As, Anuruddha, I was abiding diligent, ardent, and resolute, I perceived limited light and saw limited forms; I perceived immeasurable light and saw immeasurable forms, even for a whole night or a whole day or a whole day and night.

I thought: ‘What is the cause and condition for this? On the occasion when concentration is limited, my vision is limited, and with limited vision I perceive limited light and limited forms. But on the occasion when concentration is immeasurable, my vision is immeasurable, and with immeasurable vision I perceive immeasurable light and see immeasurable forms, even for a whole night or a whole day or a whole day and night.’

When, Anuruddha, I understood that doubt (vicikicchā) is an imperfection (upakkileso) of the mind (cittassa)—thus knowing (viditvā), doubt (vicikicchā) , an imperfection (upakkileso) of the mind (cittassa), was abandoned (pahīno ahosi); when I understood that inattention (amanasikāro) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, inattention (amanasikāro), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that sloth and torpor (thinamiddhaṃ) are an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, sloth and torpor (thinamiddhaṃ), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that fear (chambhitattaṃ) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, fear (chambhitattaṃ), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that elation (uppilaṃ) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, elation (uppilaṃ), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that inertia (duṭṭhullaṃ) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, inertia (duṭṭhullaṃ), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that excess of energy (accāraddhavīriyaṃ) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, excess of energy (accāraddhavīriyaṃ), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that deficiency of energy (atilīnavīriyaṃ) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, deficiency of energy (atilīnavīriyaṃ), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that longing (abhijappā) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, longing (abhijappā), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that perception of diversity (nānattasaññā) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, perception of diversity (nānattasaññā), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned; when I understood that excessive meditation (atinijjhāyitattaṃ) upon forms (rūpānaṃ) is an imperfection of the mind—thus knowing, excessive meditation (atinijjhāyitattaṃ) upon forms (rūpānaṃ), an imperfection of the mind, was abandoned.

Then I thought: ‘I have abandoned those imperfections (upakkilesa) of the mind.
Come now, I shall develop concentration (samādhi) in three ways.’
Thereupon, Anuruddha, I developed concentration with applied thought (vitakka) and sustained thought (vicāra); I developed concentration without applied thought but with sustained thought only; I developed concentration without applied thought and without sustained thought; I developed concentration with rapture (prīti); I developed concentration without rapture; I developed concentration accompanied by enjoyment (sāta); I developed concentration accompanied by equanimity (upekkhā).
“When, Anuruddha, I had developed concentration with applied thought and sustained thought…when I had developed concentration accompanied by equanimity,
the knowledge (jñāna) and vision (darśana) arose in me: ‘My deliverance is unshakeable; this is my last birth; now there is no renewal of being.’”

The Blessed One spoke thus.
The venerable Anuruddha was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.
