Paragraph 359
Mūla (Source Text)
359. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā vesāliyaṃ viharati mahāvane kūṭāgārasālāyaṃ. Tena kho pana samayena sambahulā kosalakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā māgadhakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā vesāliyaṃ paṭivasanti kenacideva karaṇīyena. Assosuṃ kho te kosalakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā māgadhakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā – ‘‘samaṇo khalu, bho, gotamo sakyaputto sakyakulā pabbajito vesāliyaṃ viharati mahāvane kūṭāgārasālāyaṃ. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’. So imaṃ lokaṃ sadevakaṃ samārakaṃ sabrahmakaṃ sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ pajaṃ sadevamanussaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti. So dhammaṃ deseti ādikalyāṇaṃ majjhekalyāṇaṃ pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’ti.
English Translation
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Vesali in the Great Wood in the Hall with the Peaked Roof. And at that time a large number of Brahmin emissaries from Kosala and Magadha were staying at Vesali on some business. And they heard say: ‘The ascetic Gotama, son of the Sakyans, who has gone forth from the Sakya clan, is staying at Vesali, at the Gabled Hall in the Great Forest. And concerning that Blessed Lord Gotama a good report had been spread about: ‘Indeed, the Blessed One is an *arhat* (worthy one), a fully enlightened Buddha (*sammāsambuddho*), perfected in knowledge and conduct (*vijjācaraṇasampanno*), a Well-Farer (*sugato*), Knower of the worlds (*lokavidū*), unequalled Trainer of men to be tamed (*anuttaro purisadammasārathi*), Teacher of gods and humans (*satthā devamanussānaṃ*), a Buddha, a Blessed Lord (*bhagavā*).’ He, having realised it by his own super-knowledge (*abhiññā*), proclaims this world with its gods (*sadevakaṃ*), its Māras (*samārakaṃ*), and its Brahmās (*sabrahmakaṃ*), this generation with its ascetics and Brahmins (*sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ*), its rulers and people (*sadevamanussaṃ*). He teaches the Dharma (*dhammaṃ*) that is good at the beginning (*ādikalyāṇaṃ*), good in the middle (*majjhekalyāṇaṃ*), and good at the end (*pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ*); he reveals the spiritual life (*brahmacariyaṃ*) that is utterly perfect (*kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ*) and pure (*parisuddhaṃ*), with its true meaning (*sātthaṃ*) and proper expression (*sabyañjanaṃ*). It is good to see such *arahants* (worthy ones)."
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
359.Evaṃme sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā vesāliyanti mahālisuttaṃ. Tatrāyaṃ apubbapadavaṇṇanā. Vesāliyanti punappunaṃ visālabhāvūpagamanato vesālīti laddhanāmake nagare. Mahāvaneti bahinagare himavantena saddhiṃ ekābaddhaṃ hutvā ṭhitaṃ sayaṃ jātavanaṃ atthi, yaṃ mahantabhāveneva mahāvananti vuccati, tasmiṃ mahāvane. Kūṭāgārasālāyanti tasmiṃ vanasaṇḍe saṅghārāmaṃ patiṭṭhapesuṃ. Tattha kaṇṇikaṃ yojetvā thambhānaṃ upari kūṭāgārasālāsaṅkhepena devavimānasadisaṃ pāsādaṃ akaṃsu, taṃ upādāya sakalopi saṅghārāmo ‘‘kūṭāgārasālā’’ti paññāyittha. Bhagavā taṃ vesāliṃ upanissāya tasmiṃ saṅghārāme viharati. Tena vuttaṃ – ‘‘vesāliyaṃ viharati mahāvane kūṭāgārasālāya’’nti. Kosalakāti kosalaraṭṭhavāsino. Māgadhakāti magadharaṭṭhavāsino. Karaṇīyenāti avassaṃ kattabbakammena. Yañhi akātumpi vaṭṭati, taṃ kiccanti vuccati, yaṃ avassaṃ kātabbameva, taṃ karaṇīyaṃ nāma.
English Commentary
Thus have I heard: At one time the Blessed One was staying near Vesālī in the Mahālisutta. Herein is an explanation of the unfamiliar terms. "Vesālī": in the city called Vesālī, which repeatedly approached a state of expansiveness. "Mahāvana": Outside the city, connected to the Himalayas, there exists a self-grown forest that remains. Because of its great size, it is called the Great Wood (mahāvana). In that Great Wood. "Kūṭāgārasālā": In that forest grove, they established a monastery (saṃghārāma). There, having joined a pinnacle (kaṇṇika) and above the pillars, in brief, like a palace of the gods (devavimāna), they made a mansion (pāsāda). Relying on that, the entire monastery (saṃghārāma) was known as "the Hall with the Peaked Roof (kūṭāgārasālā)." The Blessed One, depending on that Vesālī, was dwelling in that monastery (saṃghārāma). Therefore, it was said: "He dwells in Vesālī, in the Great Wood, in the Hall with the Peaked Roof." "Kosalakā": inhabitants of the Kosala country. "Māgadhakā": inhabitants of the Magadha country. "Karaṇīyena": with actions that must necessarily be done. Indeed, that which is proper even if not done, that is called "kicca"; that which must necessarily be done, that is called "karaṇīya."
Paragraph 360
Mūla (Source Text)
360. Atha kho te kosalakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā māgadhakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā yena mahāvanaṃ kūṭāgārasālā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu. Tena kho pana samayena āyasmā nāgito bhagavato upaṭṭhāko hoti. Atha kho te kosalakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā māgadhakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā yenāyasmā nāgito tenupasaṅkamiṃsu. Upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ nāgitaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘kahaṃ nu kho, bho nāgita, etarahi so bhavaṃ gotamo viharati? Dassanakāmā hi mayaṃ taṃ bhavantaṃ gotama’’nti. ‘‘Akālo kho, āvuso, bhagavantaṃ dassanāya, paṭisallīno bhagavā’’ti. Atha kho te kosalakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā māgadhakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā tattheva ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu – ‘‘disvāva mayaṃ taṃ bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ gamissāmā’’ti.
English Translation
And then those Brahmin emissaries from Kosala and Magadha went to the Great Forest, to the Gabled Hall (mahāvanaṃ kūṭāgārasālā). Now at that time, the venerable Nāgita was the Blessed One's (bhagavato) attendant (upaṭṭhāko). And then those Brahmin emissaries from Kosala and Magadha went to the venerable Nāgita. Approaching the venerable Nāgita, they said this: "Where now, reverend Nāgita, is the esteemed Gotama staying? For we desire to see the esteemed Gotama." "It is the wrong time, friend, to see the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ dassanāya); the Blessed One (bhagavā) is in solitary retreat." And then those Brahmin emissaries from Kosala and Magadha sat down to one side, saying: "Only when we have seen the esteemed Gotama will we go."
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
360.Paṭisallīno bhagavāti nānārammaṇacārato paṭikkamma sallīno nilīno, ekībhāvaṃ upagamma ekattārammaṇe jhānaratiṃ anubhavatīti attho. Tatthevāti tasmiññeva vihāre. Ekamantanti tasmā ṭhānā apakkamma tāsu tāsu rukkhacchāyāsu nisīdiṃsu.
English Commentary
The Blessed One, withdrawing from wandering with various objects, secluded and withdrawn, having approached oneness, experiences the delight of absorption with a single object; this is the meaning. "There itself" means in that very dwelling (*vihāra*). "To one side" means withdrawing from that place, they sat down in the shade of various trees.
Paragraph 361
Mūla (Source Text)
361. Oṭṭhaddhopi licchavī mahatiyā licchavīparisāya saddhiṃ yena mahāvanaṃ kūṭāgārasālā yenāyasmā nāgito tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ nāgitaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho oṭṭhaddhopi licchavī āyasmantaṃ nāgitaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kahaṃ nu kho, bhante nāgita, etarahi so bhagavā viharati arahaṃ sammāsambuddho, dassanakāmā hi mayaṃ taṃ bhagavantaṃ arahantaṃ sammāsambuddha’’nti. ‘‘Akālo kho, mahāli, bhagavantaṃ dassanāya, paṭisallīno bhagavā’’ti. Oṭṭhaddhopi licchavī tattheva ekamantaṃ nisīdi – ‘‘disvāva ahaṃ taṃ bhagavantaṃ gamissāmi arahantaṃ sammāsambuddha’’nti.
English Translation
361. Then Oṭṭhaddha, the Licchavi, together with a large company of Licchavis, went to the Gabled Hall in the Great Wood, where the venerable Nāgita was. Having approached, he greeted the venerable Nāgita and stood to one side. Standing to one side, Oṭṭhaddha, the Licchavi, said this to the venerable Nāgita: "Where, venerable Nāgita, is the Blessed One (bhagavā) now staying, the worthy one (arahaṃ), the fully enlightened Buddha (sammāsambuddho)? For we desire to see that Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ), the worthy one (arahantaṃ), the fully enlightened Buddha (sammāsambuddha)." "It is the wrong time, Mahāli, to see the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ), the Blessed One (bhagavā) is in seclusion." Oṭṭhaddha, the Licchavi, then sat down to one side, saying: "Only when I have seen that Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ), the worthy one (arahantaṃ), the fully enlightened Buddha (sammāsambuddha), will I go."
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
361.Oṭṭhaddhoti addhoṭṭhatāya evaṃladdhanāmo. Mahatiyā licchavīparisāyāti purebhattaṃ buddhappamukhassa bhikkhusaṅghassa dānaṃ datvā bhagavato santike uposathaṅgāni adhiṭṭhahitvā gandhamālādīni gāhāpetvā ugghosanāya mahatiṃ licchavirājaparisaṃ sannipātāpetvā tāya nīlapītādivaṇṇavatthābharaṇavilepanapaṭimaṇḍitāya tāvatiṃsaparisasappaṭibhāgāya mahatiyā licchaviparisāya saddhiṃ upasaṅkami. Akālo kho mahālīti tassa oṭṭhaddhassa mahālīti mūlanāmaṃ, tena mūlanāmamattena naṃ thero mahālīti ālapati. Ekamantaṃ nisīdīti patirūpāsu rukkhacchāyāsu tāya licchaviparisāya saddhiṃ ratanattayassa vaṇṇaṃ kathayanto nisīdi.
English Commentary
361. Oṭṭhaddha, because of his being "half-lipped," thus he obtained his name. "To the great assembly of the Licchavis": Having given alms consisting of the morning meal to the Saṃgha of monks with the Buddha at its head, and having undertaken the *uposatha* vows in the presence of the Blessed One, and having caused perfumes, garlands, and so on to be taken, having convened a great assembly of Licchavi kings for proclamation, adorned with garments, ornaments, and unguents of various colors such as blue and yellow, resembling the assembly of the Tāvatiṃsa gods, he approached the great assembly of the Licchavis. "It is the wrong time, Mahāli": Mahāli was the original name of Oṭṭhaddha; therefore, the elder addressed him by his original name, Mahāli. "Sit down on one side": He sat down in a suitable tree shade, and together with that assembly of Licchavis, he sat recounting the virtues of the Triple Gem (*ratnatraya*).
Paragraph 362
Mūla (Source Text)
362. Atha kho sīho samaṇuddeso yenāyasmā nāgito tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ nāgitaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho sīho samaṇuddeso āyasmantaṃ nāgitaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ete, bhante kassapa, sambahulā kosalakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā māgadhakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā idhūpasaṅkantā bhagavantaṃ dassanāya; oṭṭhaddhopi licchavī mahatiyā licchavīparisāya saddhiṃ idhūpasaṅkanto bhagavantaṃ dassanāya, sādhu, bhante kassapa, labhataṃ esā janatā bhagavantaṃ dassanāyā’’ti. ‘‘Tena hi, sīha, tvaññeva bhagavato ārocehī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho sīho samaṇuddeso āyasmato nāgitassa paṭissutvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho sīho samaṇuddeso bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ete, bhante, sambahulā kosalakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā māgadhakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā idhūpasaṅkantā bhagavantaṃ dassanāya, oṭṭhaddhopi licchavī mahatiyā licchavīparisāya saddhiṃ idhūpasaṅkanto bhagavantaṃ dassanāya. Sādhu, bhante, labhataṃ esā janatā bhagavantaṃ dassanāyā’’ti. ‘‘Tena hi, sīha, vihārapacchāyāyaṃ āsanaṃ paññapehī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho sīho samaṇuddeso bhagavato paṭissutvā vihārapacchāyāyaṃ āsanaṃ paññapesi.
English Translation
362. Then the novice Sīha approached the venerable Nāgita; having approached, having greeted the venerable Nāgita, he stood to one side. Standing to one side, the novice Sīha said this to the venerable Nāgita: "Venerable sir, these many *brāhmaṇa* emissaries from Kosala and *brāhmaṇa* emissaries from Magadha have come here to see the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ darśanāya); Otthaddha the Licchavi, too, has come with a large company of Licchavis to see the Blessed One. It would be well, venerable Kassapa, if this populace might be allowed to see the Blessed One." "Well then, Sīha, you yourself announce them to the Blessed One." "Yes, venerable sir," said the novice Sīha in reply to the venerable Nāgita; then he approached the Blessed One; having approached, having greeted the Blessed One, he stood to one side. Standing to one side, the novice Sīha said this to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, these many *brāhmaṇa* emissaries from Kosala and *brāhmaṇa* emissaries from Magadha have come here to see the Blessed One; Otthaddha the Licchavi, too, has come with a large company of Licchavis to see the Blessed One. It would be well, venerable sir, if this populace might be allowed to see the Blessed One." "Well then, Sīha, prepare a seat in the shade of the dwelling (vihārapacchāyāyaṃ āsanaṃ paññapehi)." "Yes, venerable sir," said the novice Sīha in reply to the Blessed One; then he prepared a seat in the shade of the dwelling.
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
362.Sīho samaṇuddesoti āyasmato nāgitassa bhāgineyyo sattavassakāle pabbajitvā sāsane yuttapayutto ‘‘sīho’’ti evaṃnāmako sāmaṇero, so kira taṃ mahāparisaṃ disvā – ‘‘ayaṃ parisā mahatī, sakalaṃ vihāraṃ pūretvā nisinnā, addhā bhagavā ajja imissā parisāya mahantena ussāhena dhammaṃ desessati, yaṃnūnāhaṃ upajjhāyassācikkhitvā bhagavato mahāparisāya sannipatitabhāvaṃ ārocāpeyya’’nti cintetvā yenāyasmā nāgito tenupasaṅkami. Bhante kassapāti theraṃ gottena ālapati. Esā janatāti eso janasamūho. Tvaññeva bhagavato ārocehīti sīho kira bhagavato vissāsiko, ayañhi thero thūlasarīro, tenassa sarīragarutāya uṭṭhānanisajjādīsu ālasiyabhāvo īsakaṃ appahīno viya hoti. Athāyaṃ sāmaṇero bhagavato kālena kālaṃ vattaṃ karoti. Tena naṃ thero ‘‘tvampi dasabalassa vissāsiko’’ti vatvā gaccha tvaññevārocehīti āha. Vihārapacchāyāyanti vihārachāyāyaṃ, kūṭāgāramahāgehacchāyāya pharitokāseti attho. Sā kira kūṭāgārasālā dakkhiṇuttarato dīghā pācīnamukhā, tenassā purato mahatī chāyā patthaṭā hoti, sīho tattha bhagavato āsanaṃ paññapesi.
English Commentary
362. Sīha was a novice ( *sāmaṇuddesa*) named Sīha, the nephew (*bhāgineyya*) of the venerable Nāgita. He had gone forth at the age of seven, diligently applying himself to the teachings (*sāsana*). Seeing that large assembly (*mahāparisa*) he thought, "This assembly is large, filling the entire monastery (*vihāra*) as they are seated. Surely today, the Blessed One (*bhagavā*) will teach the Dharma (*dhamma*) to this assembly with great enthusiasm (*ussāha*). Why don't I inform my preceptor (*upajjhāya*) and announce the gathering of the great assembly of the Blessed One?" Thinking this, he approached the venerable Nāgita. "Venerable Kassapa," he addressed the elder (*thera*) by his clan (*gotta*). "This is a crowd (*janatā*)," meaning, "This is a multitude of people (*janasamūho*)." "You yourself should announce it to the Blessed One." Sīha was, it seems, trusted (*vissāsika*) by the Blessed One. Furthermore, this elder was stout (*thūlasarīra*), so due to the heaviness (*garutā*) of his body, his laziness (*ālasiyabhāvo*) in rising, sitting, and so on, was not easily overcome, it seemed. Then this novice (*sāmaṇera*) performed his duties (*vattaṃ karoti*) for the Blessed One at the proper times (*kālena kālaṃ*). Therefore, the elder said to him, "You too are trusted by the Ten-Powered One (*dasabala*)." Saying this, he said, "Go, you yourself should announce it." "Vihārapacchāyāyanti" means "in the shadow (*chāyā*) of the monastery (*vihāra*)", meaning "in the open space shaded by the shadow of the upper room (*kūṭāgāra*) or great house (*mahāgeha*)". It seems that hall (*sālā*) with an upper room (*kūṭāgāra*) was long from south to north and faced east (*pācīnamukhā*), therefore a great shadow (*mahatī chāyā*) stretched out in front of it. There, Sīha prepared a seat (*āsanaṃ paññapesi*) for the Blessed One.
Paragraph 363
Mūla (Source Text)
363. Atha kho bhagavā vihārā nikkhamma vihārapacchāyāyaṃ paññatte āsane nisīdi. Atha kho te kosalakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā māgadhakā ca brāhmaṇadūtā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodiṃsu. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Oṭṭhaddhopi licchavī mahatiyā licchavīparisāya saddhiṃ yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi.
English Translation
Then, the Blessed One, emerging from his dwelling, sat down on a prepared seat in the shade of the dwelling. Then, the Kosalan and Magadhan Brahmin messengers approached the Blessed One; having approached, they exchanged greetings with the Blessed One. Having concluded their conversation of courteous greetings, they sat down to one side. Then Oṭṭhaddha, the Licchavi, together with a large assembly of Licchavis, approached the Blessed One; having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side.
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
363. Atha kho bhagavā dvārantarehi ceva vātapānantarehi ca nikkhamitvā vidhāvantāhi vippharantīhi chabbaṇṇāhi buddharasmīhi saṃsūcitanikkhamano valāhakantarato puṇṇacando viya kūṭāgārasālato nikkhamitvā paññattavarabuddhāsane nisīdi. Tena vuttaṃ – ‘‘atha kho bhagavā vihārā nikkhamma vihārapacchāyāya paññatte āsane nisīdī’’ti.
English Commentary
363. Then, the Blessed One, having exited through the openings between the doors and windows, his exit heralded by the six-colored rays of the Buddha ( *buddharasmi* ) darting and flashing about, like the full moon (*pūrṇacandra*) from behind the clouds, having emerged from the hall of the peaked house, sat down on the excellent Buddha-seat (*paññattavarabuddhāsana*) prepared for him. Therefore, it was said: "Then, the Blessed One, having left the monastery (*vihāra*), sat down on the seat (*āsana*) prepared in the shadow of the monastery (*vihārapacchāyā*)."
Paragraph 364
Mūla (Source Text)
364. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho oṭṭhaddho licchavī bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘purimāni, bhante, divasāni purimatarāni sunakkhatto licchaviputto yenāhaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā maṃ etadavoca – ‘yadagge ahaṃ, mahāli, bhagavantaṃ upanissāya viharāmi, na ciraṃ tīṇi vassāni, dibbāni hi kho rūpāni passāmi piyarūpāni kāmūpasaṃhitāni rajanīyāni, no ca kho dibbāni saddāni suṇāmi piyarūpāni kāmūpasaṃhitāni rajanīyānī’ti. Santāneva nu kho, bhante, sunakkhatto licchaviputto dibbāni saddāni nāssosi piyarūpāni kāmūpasaṃhitāni rajanīyāni, udāhu asantānī’’ti?
English Translation
364. Then Oṭṭhaddha the Licchavi, having sat down to one side, said this to the Blessed One: "Some days ago, venerable sir, Sunakkhatta the Licchavi's son came to me, and having come, said this to me: 'Since the time I have been living in dependence on the Blessed One, not long—three years—I have seen heavenly forms (divyāni rūpāṇi), which are pleasing forms (piyarūpāṇi), connected with sensual pleasure (kāmūpasaṃhitāni), and delightful (rajanīyāni), but I have not heard heavenly sounds (dibbāni saddāni) which are pleasing forms (piyarūpāṇi), connected with sensual pleasure (kāmūpasaṃhitāni), and delightful (rajanīyāni).' "Now, venerable sir, are there in reality such heavenly sounds (dibbāni saddāni) which Sunakkhatta the Licchavi's son has not heard, which are pleasing forms (piyarūpāṇi), connected with sensual pleasure (kāmūpasaṃhitāni), and delightful (rajanīyāni), or are there not?"
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
364-365.Purimāni, bhante, divasāni purimatarānīti ettha hiyyo divasaṃ purimaṃ nāma, tato paraṃ purimataraṃ. Tato paṭṭhāya pana sabbāni purimāni ceva purimatarāni ca honti. Yadaggeti mūladivasato paṭṭhāya yaṃ divasaṃ aggaṃ parakoṭiṃ katvā viharāmīti attho, yāva vihāsinti vuttaṃ hoti. Idāni tassa parimāṇaṃ dassento ‘‘naciraṃ tīṇi vassānī’’ti āha. Then, inquiring of the Blessed One, he said, "Is there, Venerable Sir…" etc. *Aṭṭhaṅgiko* means that just as a musical instrument with five parts is *pañcaṅgika*, or just as a village with eight parts is *aṭṭhaṅgiko*, so too, being merely eight parts, it is *aṭṭhaṅgiko*; there is no other path called *maggo* apart from the parts. Right intention (sammāsaṅkappo) is characterized by right application [of the mind]. Right speech (sammāvācā) is characterized by right acceptance. Right action (sammākammanto) is characterized by right origination. Right livelihood (sammāājīvo) is characterized by right cleansing. Right effort (sammāvāyāmo) is characterized by right exertion. Right mindfulness (sammāsati) is characterized by right establishment. Right concentration (sammāsamādhi) is characterized by right steadiness. Among these, each has three functions. That is to say, right view, indeed, abandons wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi) together with the other defilements that are its adversaries; it makes cessation (nirodha) its object; and it sees the associated phenomena without confusion (asammohato) by dispelling the delusion (moha) that obscures them. Likewise, right intention (sammāsaṅkappo) and the others abandon wrong intention and so on; they make cessation their object; and in particular, right intention applies itself to the co-arisen phenomena. Right speech rightly accepts. Right action rightly originates. Right livelihood rightly cleanses. Right effort rightly exerts. Right mindfulness rightly establishes. Right concentration rightly steadies. Moreover, this right view, in the prior part, has diverse moments and diverse objects; in the moment of the path (maggakkhaṇe), it has a single moment and a single object. However, in terms of function (kiccato), it obtains four names, beginning with "knowledge of suffering" (dukkhe ñāṇa). Right intention and the others also have diverse moments and diverse objects in the prior part. In the moment of the path, it has a single moment and a single object. Among these, right intention, in terms of function, obtains three names, beginning with "intention of renunciation" (nekkhammasaṅkappo). Right speech and the other three are also abstinences (viratiyo), and they are also volitions (cetanādayo); however, in the moment of the path, they are only abstinences. This pair, right effort and right mindfulness, also obtain four names in terms of function, as right exertion and mindfulness. Right concentration, however, is right concentration both in the prior part and in the moment of the path. Thus, among these eight factors (dhammesu), because of the great benefit (bahukārattā) to the yogi who has embarked upon the attainment of *nirvāṇa*, right view (sammādiṭṭhi) was taught first by the Blessed One (bhagavatā). For it is said to be "the lamp of wisdom, the weapon of wisdom." Therefore, by means of this right view, reckoned as the knowledge of insight (vipassanāñāṇa) in the prior part, the yogāvacara, dispelling the darkness of ignorance (avijjandhakāraṃ), slaying the thieves of defilements (kilesacore ghātento), attains *nirvāṇa* safely. Therefore, it was said, "Because of the great benefit to the yogi who has embarked upon the attainment of *nirvāṇa*, right view was taught first." Right intention, however, is of great benefit to that, therefore it was mentioned immediately after. Just as a goldsmith, turning a *kahāpaṇa* over and over in his hand, examines it with his eye, knowing, "This is genuine, this is counterfeit," so too, the yogāvacara, pondering with thought (vitakkena vitakketvā) in the prior part, examining with the wisdom of insight (vipassanāpaññāya), understands, "These phenomena are of the sense-sphere, these phenomena are of the form-sphere, and so on." Or just as a carpenter, having received a large tree given to him after a man has turned it over and over, taking it by the end, shapes it with an axe and applies it to his work, so too, the yogāvacara, having pondered with thought (vitakkena vitakketvā) the phenomena given to him, distinguishing with wisdom, "These are of the sense-sphere, these are of the form-sphere," and so on, applies them to his work. Therefore, it was said, "Right intention, however, is of great benefit to that, therefore it was mentioned immediately after." Just as right view is helpful, so too is right speech. As it is said: "First, having thought and reflected, afterwards one utters speech," (M. N. 1. 463) Therefore, right speech is stated immediately after that. Since people first make an agreement in speech, saying "We will do this and that," and then apply themselves to actions in the world; therefore, because speech is helpful to bodily action, right action is stated immediately after right speech. Since, having abandoned the four kinds of verbal misconduct and the three kinds of bodily misconduct, one fulfills both kinds of good conduct, one fulfills the eightfold path of livelihood, not another; therefore, right livelihood is stated immediately after those two. Thus, with purified livelihood, it is not fitting to be content with merely thinking, "My livelihood is purified," and to live with scattered mindfulness, but to show that "this effort should be undertaken in all activities," right effort is stated immediately after that. Then, in order to show that "even with aroused effort, mindfulness should be well established in the four foundations of mindfulness, beginning with the body," right mindfulness is taught immediately after that. Since, when mindfulness is thus well established, it is possible to investigate the tendencies of dharmas that are helpful and unhelpful to *samādhi*, and to concentrate the mind on a single object, therefore it should be understood that right *samādhi* is taught immediately after right mindfulness. "For the realization of these *dharmas*": for the purpose of directly experiencing these fruits, beginning with stream-entry.

Right livelihood (sammāājīva) has the characteristic of right cleansing. Right effort (sammāvāyāma) has the characteristic of right exertion. Right mindfulness (sammāsati) has the characteristic of right establishing. Right concentration (sammāsamādhi) has the characteristic of right composing. Each of these has three functions. That is to say, right view, first of all, abandons wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi) along with other defilements (kilesa) that are its adversaries, makes cessation (nirodha) its object (ārammaṇa), and sees the associated phenomena without confusion (asammohato) by eliminating the delusion (moha) that conceals it. Likewise, right intention and the others abandon wrong intention (micchāsaṅkappa) and the others, and make cessation their object. However, in this case, right intention especially directs towards co-arisen phenomena. Right speech rightly grasps. Right action rightly establishes. Right livelihood rightly cleanses. Right effort rightly exerts. Right mindfulness rightly establishes. Right concentration rightly composes. Moreover, this right view, in the prior stage, has diverse moments and diverse objects, but in the moment of the path (maggakkhaṇe), it has a single moment and a single object. In terms of function, it obtains four names, such as "knowledge of suffering (dukkhe ñāṇa)." Right intention and the others also have diverse moments and diverse objects in the prior stage. In the moment of the path, it has a single moment and a single object. Among these, right intention obtains three names in terms of function, such as "intention of renunciation (nekkhammasaṅkappo)." Right speech and the other three are also abstinences (virati), and they are also volitions (cetanā), but in the moment of the path, they are only abstinences. This pair, right effort and right mindfulness, also obtain four names in terms of function, as right effort and mindfulness, respectively. Right concentration, however, is right concentration in both the prior stage and the moment of the path. Thus, among these eight qualities, because the Blessed One greatly benefits the yogi who has embarked on attaining *nirvāṇa*, right view was taught first. For this is also said to be "the lamp of wisdom (paññāpajjoto), the sword of wisdom (paññāsattha)." (Dha. Sa. 20) Therefore, by dispelling the darkness of ignorance (avijjandhakāra) with this right view, which in the prior stage is reckoned as the knowledge of insight (vipassanāñāṇa), and by slaying the thieves of defilements, the yogāvacara attains *nirvāṇa* safely. Therefore, it was said: "Because the Blessed One greatly benefits the yogi who has embarked on attaining *nirvāṇa*, right view was taught first." Right intention, however, is of great benefit to that, therefore it was mentioned immediately after. Just as a goldsmith, turning and turning a coin in his hand, examining it with his eye, knows, "This is genuine, this is counterfeit." So also, the yogāvacara, reflecting and reflecting with thought in the prior stage, examining with insight-wisdom, understands, "These phenomena belong to the realm of desire (kāmāvacarā), these phenomena belong to the realm of form (rūpāvacarā)," and so on. Or, just as a carpenter, having been given a large tree that a man has turned and turned by grasping it at the end, planes it with an axe and puts it to work, so also the yogāvacara, having been given phenomena that have been thought about and thought about with thought, distinguishing with wisdom in the manner, "These belong to the realm of desire, these belong to the realm of form," puts it to work. Therefore, it was said: "Right intention, however, is of great benefit to that, therefore it was mentioned immediately after." Just as right view is helpful, so too is right speech. As it was said: "First, having thought and reflected, O Visākha, one then utters speech," (M. N. 1. 463) Therefore, right speech was stated after that. Since people first make an agreement in speech, saying "We will do this and that," and then apply themselves to actions in the world, speech is helpful to bodily action; therefore, right action was stated after right speech. Since one who abandons the four kinds of verbal misconduct and the three kinds of bodily misconduct and fulfills both kinds of good conduct fulfills the eightfold means of livelihood, and not another, therefore, right livelihood was stated after those two. Thus, with purified livelihood, it is not fitting to make contentment with "My livelihood is purified" and live heedlessly, but to show that "This effort should be undertaken in all activities," right effort was stated after that. Then, "Even with aroused effort, mindfulness should be well-established in the four foundations of mindfulness, beginning with the body," therefore, right mindfulness was taught after that, for the purpose of showing that. Since, with mindfulness thus well-established, one is able to investigate the courses of dharmas that are helpful and unhelpful to *samādhi* and to concentrate the mind on a single object, therefore, it should be understood that right *samādhi* was taught after right mindfulness. "For the direct realization of these *dharmas*" means for the purpose of the direct realization of the fruits of stream-entry and so forth.
