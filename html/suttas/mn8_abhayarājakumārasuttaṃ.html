83. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe. Atha kho abhayo rājakumāro yena nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā nigaṇṭhaṃ nāṭaputtaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho abhayaṃ rājakumāraṃ nigaṇṭho nāṭaputto etadavoca – ‘‘ehi tvaṃ, rājakumāra, samaṇassa gotamassa vādaṃ āropehi. Evaṃ te kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggacchissati – ‘abhayena rājakumārena samaṇassa gotamassa evaṃ mahiddhikassa evaṃ mahānubhāvassa vādo āropito’’’ti. ‘‘Yathā kathaṃ panāhaṃ, bhante, samaṇassa gotamassa evaṃ mahiddhikassa evaṃ mahānubhāvassa vādaṃ āropessāmī’’ti? ‘‘Ehi tvaṃ, rājakumāra, yena samaṇo gotamo tenupasaṅkama; upasaṅkamitvā samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ vadehi – ‘bhāseyya nu kho, bhante, tathāgato taṃ vācaṃ yā sā vācā paresaṃ appiyā amanāpā’ti? Sace te samaṇo gotamo evaṃ puṭṭho evaṃ byākaroti – ‘bhāseyya, rājakumāra, tathāgato taṃ vācaṃ yā sā vācā paresaṃ appiyā amanāpā’ti, tamenaṃ tvaṃ evaṃ vadeyyāsi – ‘atha kiñcarahi te, bhante, puthujjanena nānākaraṇaṃ? Sace pana te samaṇo gotamo evaṃ puṭṭho evaṃ byākaroti – ‘na, rājakumāra, tathāgato taṃ vācaṃ bhāseyya yā sā vācā paresaṃ appiyā amanāpā’ti, tamenaṃ tvaṃ evaṃ vadeyyāsi – ‘atha kiñcarahi te, bhante, devadatto byākato – ‘‘āpāyiko devadatto, nerayiko devadatto, kappaṭṭho devadatto, atekiccho devadatto’’ti? Tāya ca pana te vācāya devadatto kupito ahosi anattamano’ti. Imaṃ kho te, rājakumāra, samaṇo gotamo ubhatokoṭikaṃ pañhaṃ puṭṭho samāno neva sakkhiti uggilituṃ na sakkhiti ogilituṃ. Seyyathāpi nāma purisassa ayosiṅghāṭakaṃ kaṇṭhe vilaggaṃ, so neva sakkuṇeyya uggilituṃ na sakkuṇeyya ogilituṃ; evameva kho te, rājakumāra, samaṇo gotamo imaṃ ubhatokoṭikaṃ pañhaṃ puṭṭho samāno neva sakkhiti uggilituṃ na sakkhiti ogilitu’’nti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho abhayo rājakumāro nigaṇṭhassa nāṭaputtassa paṭissutvā uṭṭhāyāsanā nigaṇṭhaṃ nāṭaputtaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi.
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rajagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary.

Then Prince Abhaya went to the Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta, and after paying homage to him, sat down at one side.

Seated to one side, Prince Abhaya was addressed by the Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta thus: “Come, prince, refute the doctrine of the śramaṇa Gotama (Śramaṇassa Gotamassa vādaṃ āropehi).

Thus a good report of you will be spread to this effect: ‘Prince Abhaya has refuted the doctrine of the śramaṇa Gotama, who is so powerful and mighty.’”

“How, venerable sir (bhante), can I refute the doctrine of the śramaṇa Gotama, who is so powerful and mighty?” “Go, prince, to where the śramaṇa Gotama is, and on arrival ask the śramaṇa Gotama: ‘Venerable sir (bhante), would the Tathāgata utter speech that would be unwelcome and disagreeable to others?’ If the śramaṇa Gotama, on being asked thus, answers: ‘The Tathāgata, prince, would utter speech that would be unwelcome and disagreeable to others,’ then say to him: ‘Then why, venerable sir (bhante), is there any distinction from an ordinary person (puthujjanena nānākaraṇaṃ)? For even an ordinary person (puthujjanopi) would utter speech that would be unwelcome and disagreeable to others.’

But if the śramaṇa Gotama, on being asked thus, answers: ‘No, prince, the Tathāgata would not utter speech that would be unwelcome and disagreeable to others,’ then say to him: ‘Then why, venerable sir (bhante), has Devadatta been declared: “Devadatta is destined for the states of deprivation (āpāyiko devadatto), Devadatta is destined for hell (nerayiko devadatto), Devadatta will remain in hell for the aeon (kappaṭṭho devadatto), Devadatta is incorrigible (atekiccho devadatto)”? And by that speech Devadatta was angered and displeased.’

When the śramaṇa Gotama is posed this two-horned question by you, he will not be able either to gulp it down or to throw it up.

If an iron spike were stuck in a man’s throat, he would not be able either to gulp it down or to throw it up; so too, prince, when the śramaṇa Gotama is posed this two-horned question by you, he will not be able either to gulp it down or to throw it up.”

“Yes, venerable sir (evaṃ bhante),” Prince Abhaya replied, and he rose from his seat, and after paying homage to the Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta, keeping him on his right, he left and went to the Blessed One.

Atekicchoti buddhasahassenāpi tikicchituṃ na sakkā. Uggilitunti dve ante mocetvā kathetuṃ asakkonto uggilituṃ bahi nīharituṃ na sakkhiti. Ogilitunti pucchāya dosaṃ datvā hāretuṃ asakkonto ogilituṃ anto pavesetuṃ na sakkhiti. Evaṃ, bhanteti nigaṇṭho kira cintesi – ‘‘samaṇo gotamo mayhaṃ sāvake bhinditvā gaṇhāti, handāhaṃ ekaṃ pañhaṃ abhisaṅkharomi, yaṃ puṭṭho samaṇo gotamo ukkuṭiko hutvā nisinno uṭṭhātuṃ na sakkhissatī’’ti. Athassa etadahosi – ‘‘imassa pañhassa pucchāya vā vissajjane vā na sakkā doso dātuṃ, ovaṭṭikasāro ayaṃ, ko nu kho imaṃ gahetvā samaṇassa gotamassa vādaṃ āropessatī’’ti. Thus I have heard: the Abhayasuttaṃ.

There, abhayo means his name.

rājakumāro means Bimbisāra's legitimate son.

vādaṃ āropehī means attribute fault.

nerayiko means one who is reborn in hell.

kappaṭṭho means kappaṭṭhitiko.

atekiccho means it is not possible to cure even with a thousand Buddhas.

uggilitu means being unable to loosen the two ends and speak, being unable to expel it, to bring it out.

ogilitu means being unable, when asked a question, to give a fault and defeat [the opponent], being unable to swallow it, to cause it to enter within.

evaṃ, bhante

nigaṇṭho reflected: "The renunciate Gotama breaks up my disciples and takes them. Come, let me concoct a question such that, when asked, the renunciate Gotama, squatting, sitting, will not be able to arise."

He, having had the rice removed from Abhaya's house, while enjoying smooth food, having concocted many questions - "Here the renunciate Gotama will show this fault by name, here this by name" - having abandoned all, at the end of four months, saw this question.

Then it occurred to him: "It is not possible to give a fault either by asking or by answering this question. This is a rolling essence. Who now, having taken this, will attribute fault to the renunciate Gotama?"

Then, "Abhayo is a wise prince; he will be able," having gone to the conclusion, he taught [it].

He, agreeing with his words due to his inclination towards debate, said, "evaṃ, bhante."

Sve dānāhaṃ sake nivesane bhagavato vādaṃ āropessāmī’’ti bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘adhivāsetu me, bhante, bhagavā svātanāya attacatuttho bhatta’’nti. Adhivāsesi bhagavā tuṇhībhāvena. Atha kho abhayo rājakumāro bhagavato adhivāsanaṃ viditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā pakkāmi. Atha kho bhagavā tassā rattiyā accayena pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena abhayassa rājakumārassa nivesanaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā paññatte āsane nisīdi. Atha kho abhayo rājakumāro bhagavantaṃ paṇītena khādanīyena bhojanīyena sahatthā santappesi sampavāresi. Atha kho abhayo rājakumāro bhagavantaṃ bhuttāviṃ onītapattapāṇiṃ aññataraṃ nīcaṃ āsanaṃ gahetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi.
84. Then, Prince Abhaya, having sat down to one side, looked up at the sun (note: sūriyaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)) and thought: "This is not the right time to refute the Blessed One's doctrine. Tomorrow, I shall refute the Blessed One’s doctrine in my own house." Then he said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, let the Blessed One with three others consent to accept tomorrow's meal from me." The Blessed One assented by silence ( tuṇhībhāvena).

Then, Prince Abhaya, knowing that the Blessed One had consented ( adhivāsanaṃ viditvā), rose from his seat, and after paying homage to him ( abhivādetvā), circumambulating him ( padakkhiṇaṃ katvā), he departed.

Then, when the night had ended, it being morning, the Blessed One dressed, and taking his bowl and outer robe (pattacīvaramādāya), he went to Prince Abhaya’s house and sat down on the seat made ready ( paññatte āsane nisīdi).

Then, Prince Abhaya served and satisfied the Blessed One with his own hands ( sahatthā santappesi sampavāresi) with choice hard and soft food (paṇītena khādanīyena bhojanīyena).

Then, Prince Abhaya, when the Blessed One had finished eating (bhuttāviṃ) and had withdrawn his hand from the bowl (onītapattapāṇiṃ), took a low seat (nīcaṃ āsanaṃ gahetvā) and sat down to one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdi).

85. Having sat down to one side, Prince Abhaya said this to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, would the Tathāgata utter speech that is unwelcome and disagreeable to others?”

“Prince, there is no categorical answer to that.”

“Then, venerable sir, the Nigaṇṭhas have lost in this.”

“Why do you say this, prince: ‘Then, venerable sir, the Nigaṇṭhas have lost in this’?”

“Venerable sir, I went to where the Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta was; having gone there, I greeted the Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta, and sat down to one side.

Having sat down to one side, the Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta said this to me: ‘Come, prince, refute the doctrine of the Śramaṇa Gotama.

In this way, a good report of you will be spread: ‘Prince Abhaya has refuted the doctrine of the Śramaṇa Gotama, who is so powerful and mighty.’

When this was said, I said this to the Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta: ‘But how, venerable sir, could I refute the doctrine of the Śramaṇa Gotama, who is so powerful and mighty?’ ‘Come, prince, go to where the Śramaṇa Gotama is; having gone there, say this to the Śramaṇa Gotama: ‘Venerable sir, would the Tathāgata utter speech that is unwelcome and disagreeable to others?’ If the Śramaṇa Gotama, being asked thus, answers thus: ‘The Tathāgata, prince, would utter speech that is unwelcome and disagreeable to others,’ then you should say this to him: ‘Then in what way, venerable sir, is there any distinction for you from an ordinary person ( pṛthagjana)? For an ordinary person ( pṛthagjana) also would utter speech that is unwelcome and disagreeable to others.’

But if the Śramaṇa Gotama, being asked thus, answers thus: ‘No, prince, the Tathāgata would not utter speech that is unwelcome and disagreeable to others,’ then you should say this to him: ‘Then why, venerable sir, was Devadatta declared by you to be destined for the states of deprivation ( āpāyika), destined for hell ( nerayika), destined to remain in hell for the aeon ( kappaṭṭha), incorrigible ( atekiccha)?’ And Devadatta was angered and displeased by that speech of yours.

This two-horned question, prince, the Śramaṇa Gotama, being asked it, will not be able either to gulp it down or to throw it up.

Just as if an iron spike were stuck in a man’s throat, he would not be able either to gulp it down or to throw it up; so too, prince, the Śramaṇa Gotama, being asked this two-horned question, will not be able either to gulp it down or to throw it up.”
85.Na khvettha, rājakumāra, ekaṃsenāti na kho, rājakumāra, ettha pañhe ekaṃsena vissajjanaṃ hoti. Evarūpañhi vācaṃ tathāgato bhāseyyāpi na bhāseyyāpi. Bhāsitapaccayena atthaṃ passanto bhāseyya, apassanto na bhāseyyāti attho. Iti bhagavā mahānigaṇṭhena catūhi māsehi abhisaṅkhataṃ pañhaṃ asanipātena pabbatakūṭaṃ viya ekavacaneneva saṃcuṇṇesi. Anassuṃ nigaṇṭhāti naṭṭhā nigaṇṭhā.
“There is no one-sided answer to that, prince.” ( na khvettha, rājakumāra, ekaṃsenā ti ) That is, prince, there is no one-sided answer to this question ( pañha ).

For the tathāgata might speak such words, and might not speak them.

The meaning is that one who, through the condition of what is spoken, sees the meaning ( attha ) might speak; one who does not see it might not speak.

Thus, the Blessed One ( bhagavā ) crushed the question ( pañha ) that Mahānigaṇṭha had pondered for four months with a single word, like a thunderbolt ( asanipāta ) [crushing] a mountain peak.

“The nigaṇṭhas were destroyed” ( anassuṃ nigaṇṭhā ti ): the nigaṇṭhas were ruined.

Sace, bhante, na sakkuṇeyyaṃ ādikeneva āhattuṃ āharituṃ (syā. kaṃ.), vāmena hatthena sīsaṃ pariggahetvā paggahetvā (sī.) dakkhiṇena hatthena vaṅkaṅguliṃ karitvā salohitampi āhareyyaṃ. Taṃ kissa hetu? Atthi me, bhante, kumāre anukampā’’ti. ‘‘Evameva kho, rājakumāra, yaṃ tathāgato vācaṃ jānāti abhūtaṃ atacchaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ sā ca paresaṃ appiyā amanāpā, na taṃ tathāgato vācaṃ bhāsati. Yañca kho tathāgato vācaṃ jānāti bhūtaṃ tacchaṃ atthasaṃhitaṃ sā ca paresaṃ appiyā amanāpā, tatra kālaññū tathāgato hoti tassā vācāya veyyākaraṇāya. Yaṃ tathāgato vācaṃ jānāti abhūtaṃ atacchaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ sā ca paresaṃ piyā manāpā, na taṃ tathāgato vācaṃ bhāsati. Yampi tathāgato vācaṃ jānāti bhūtaṃ tacchaṃ anatthasaṃhitaṃ sā ca paresaṃ piyā manāpā tampi tathāgato vācaṃ na bhāsati. Yañca tathāgato vācaṃ jānāti bhūtaṃ tacchaṃ atthasaṃhitaṃ sā ca paresaṃ piyā manāpā, tatra kālaññū tathāgato hoti tassā vācāya veyyākaraṇāya. Taṃ kissa hetu? Atthi, rājakumāra, tathāgatassa sattesu anukampā’’ti.
Now on that occasion, a young, tender infant was lying prone on Prince Abhaya’s lap.

Then the Blessed One said to Prince Abhaya: “What do you think, prince? If, while you or your nurse were not attending to him, this child were to put a stick or a pebble in his mouth, what would you do to him?”

“I would take it out, venerable sir. If I could not take it out at once, I would take his head in my left hand, and crooking a finger of my right hand, I would take it out even if it meant drawing blood. Why is that? Because I have compassion for the child, venerable sir.”

“So too, prince, such speech as the Tathāgata knows to be untrue, incorrect, and unbeneficial, and which is also unwelcome and disagreeable to others: such speech the Tathāgata does not utter. Such speech as the Tathāgata knows to be true and correct but unbeneficial, and which is also unwelcome and disagreeable to others: such speech the Tathāgata does not utter. Such speech as the Tathāgata knows to be true, correct, and beneficial, but which is unwelcome and disagreeable to others: the Tathāgata knows the time to use such speech. Such speech as the Tathāgata knows to be untrue, incorrect, and unbeneficial, but which is welcome and agreeable to others: such speech the Tathāgata does not utter. Such speech as the Tathāgata knows to be true and correct but unbeneficial, and which is welcome and agreeable to others: such speech the Tathāgata does not utter. Such speech as the Tathāgata knows to be true, correct, and beneficial, and which is welcome and agreeable to others: the Tathāgata knows the time to use such speech. Why is that? Because the Tathāgata has compassion for beings, prince.”

86. "Sitting on the lap" means he sits on the thighs.

Fraudulent debaters, indeed, establishing a debate, taking some flower, fruit, or book, sit down.

They, when they themselves are victorious, boast, but when another is victorious, they show distraction as if eating fruit, smelling a flower, or reading a book.

Then he thought, "The sammāsambuddha is skilled in ending conflict, a destroyer of others' doctrines.

If victory is mine, that will be skillful.

If it is not, having pierced the boy, I will make him cry.

Then look, sirs, this boy is crying, get up quickly, later we will know" thus, having taken the boy, he sat down.

The Blessed One, however, is much more skilled in debate than the prince, even by a thousand qualities, even by a hundred thousand qualities, thinking, "Having made this boy an example, I will break the debate," he said, beginning with, "What do you think, prince?"

There, "mukhe āhareyyā" means he should place it in his mouth.

"āhareyyassāha" means I would remove it from him.

"ādikenevā" means with the first effort.

"abhūta" means untrue.

"ataccha" means not true.

"anatthasaṃhita" means not connected with benefit, not dependent on increase.

"appiyā amanāpā" means neither dear nor pleasing.

In this way, the meaning should be seen everywhere.

mukhe āhareyyā āhareyyassāha ādikenevā abhūta ataccha anatthasaṃhita appiyā amanāpā

There, on the unpleasant side, "paṭhamavācā" the first speech, is current, saying "He is a thief even though he is not a thief, he is a slave even though he is not a slave, he is misused even though he is not misused."

The Tathāgata does not speak that.

"dutiyavācā" the second speech, is current, with the phrase "He is a thief, indeed, who comes."

The Tathāgata does not speak that either.

"tatiyavācā" the third speech, "'Now, because of not doing merit, he is wretched, ugly, and of little power, even having stayed here, again you do not do merit; in the second thought moment, how will you not be freed from the four woeful courses?'" Thus, that is speech worthy of being spoken with regard to the benefit of the multitude, with regard to dharma, and with regard to instruction.

"tatra kālaññū tathāgato" means in that third explanation, for the sake of the explanation of that speech, the Tathāgata is timely, that is, knowing the time of the multitude's receiving and the time of grasping, he speaks.

paṭhamavācā dutiyavācā tatiyavācā tatra kālaññū tathāgato

On the pleasant side, the first speech is called the "base of establishment" (aṭṭhāniyakathā).

That should be understood thus – thus, it is said that many rogues, having come to a city, desiring to deceive an old villager drinking in a tavern, having stayed in a drinking place, drinking liquor with him, thinking, "We will take all of this one's dwelling, cloak, and goods in his hand," they made an agreement – "Let us each tell a story seen by ourselves; whoever says 'untrue', or does not believe what is said, having made him a slave, we will take him."

They asked that old man, "Does this please you, sir?"

Let it be so, sir.

One rogue said – "Sir, when my mother was pregnant with me, she had a craving for kapiṭṭha fruit.

She, not obtaining another kapiṭṭha gatherer, sent me myself.

I, having gone, being unable to climb the tree, taking myself by my feet, threw myself above the tree like a mallet; then, wandering from branch to branch, having taken the fruits, being unable to descend, having gone home, having brought a ladder, having descended, having gone to my mother's presence, I gave the fruits to my mother; those, however, were large, of the size of cooking pots.

Then, my mother, having sat down on one seat, ate sixty fruits together.

The remainder of the fruits brought by me in one lap, were for the small old people in the village belonging to the family.
Our house was sixteen hatthas, filled up to the roof with kapittha fruits after removing the remaining paraphernalia and goods.
Then, taking the surplus, they made a pile at the door of the house.
It was like a mountain eighty hatthas high.
"Is this believable, sir Sakkā?"

The village elder, sat silently and, at the end of the conversation of all those asked, said: "Yes, father, it will be. It is a large kingdom; because of the greatness of the kingdom, it is possible to believe."
As with him, so also with the others, when such groundless stories were told, he said: "Listen to me too, fathers. It is not only your families [that are great]; our family too is a great family. But our cotton field is much larger than the remaining fields."

In the middle of that cotton field of many hundreds of karīsas, there was one great cotton tree, eighty hatthas high.
It had five branches; the remaining branches did not bear fruit, but the eastern branch had only one fruit, the size of a large bowl.
It had six filaments, and on the six filaments, six cotton bolls had flowered.
Having had my beard trimmed and anointed after bathing, I went to the field and, seeing those cotton bolls in bloom, stood there and stretched out my hand and took them.
Those cotton bolls, endowed with strength, became six slaves.
All of them deserted me alone and fled.
In so much time I did not see them, today they are seen, you are those six people.
You are named Nanda, you are named Puṇṇa, you are named Vaḍḍhamāna, you are named Citta, you are named Maṅgala, you are named Poṭṭhiya," he said, and rising up, having grabbed the hair knots of the ones who were still sitting, he stood there.
They could not even say, "We are not slaves."
Then, dragging them along, bringing them to a judgment, branding them with a mark, he made them slaves for life and enjoyed [their services].
How could the Tathāgata not speak such a thing?

The second speech, the speech of praise of others, of various kinds, based on greed for gifts, flattery, and so on, and also animal talk, which proceeds according to the principle beginning with thief talk, king talk, and so on.
That too, the Tathāgata does not speak.
The third speech [is] talk based on the Noble Truths; wise people never get tired of listening to it even for a hundred years.
Thus, the Tathāgata does not speak all unpleasant speech, nor pleasant speech.
The Tathāgata speaks the third [kind of speech] at the very time when it should be spoken, without transgressing the [proper] time for speaking.
There, it should be understood that the simile of the young boy has come down with reference to the third unpleasant speech.

87. ‘‘Yeme, bhante, khattiyapaṇḍitāpi brāhmaṇapaṇḍitāpi gahapatipaṇḍitāpi samaṇapaṇḍitāpi pañhaṃ abhisaṅkharitvā tathāgataṃ upasaṅkamitvā pucchanti, pubbeva nu kho, etaṃ, bhante , bhagavato cetaso parivitakkitaṃ hoti ‘ye maṃ upasaṅkamitvā evaṃ pucchissanti tesāhaṃ evaṃ puṭṭho evaṃ byākarissāmī’ti, udāhu ṭhānasovetaṃ tathāgataṃ paṭibhātī’’ti? ‘‘Tena hi, rājakumāra, taññevettha paṭipucchissāmi, yathā te khameyya tathā naṃ byākareyyāsi. Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, rājakumāra, kusalo tvaṃ rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgāna’’nti? ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante, kusalo ahaṃ rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgāna’’nti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, rājakumāra, ye taṃ upasaṅkamitvā evaṃ puccheyyuṃ – ‘kiṃ nāmidaṃ rathassa aṅgapaccaṅga’nti? Pubbeva nu kho te etaṃ cetaso parivitakkitaṃ assa ‘ye maṃ upasaṅkamitvā evaṃ pucchissanti tesāhaṃ evaṃ puṭṭho evaṃ byākarissāmī’ti, udāhu ṭhānasovetaṃ paṭibhāseyyā’’ti? ‘‘Ahañhi, bhante, rathiko saññāto kusalo rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgānaṃ. Sabbāni me rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgāni suviditāni. Ṭhānasovetaṃ maṃ paṭibhāseyyā’’ti . ‘‘Evameva kho, rājakumāra, ye te khattiyapaṇḍitāpi brāhmaṇapaṇḍitāpi gahapatipaṇḍitāpi samaṇapaṇḍitāpi pañhaṃ abhisaṅkharitvā tathāgataṃ upasaṅkamitvā pucchanti, ṭhānasovetaṃ tathāgataṃ paṭibhāti. Taṃ kissa hetu? Sā hi, rājakumāra, tathāgatassa dhammadhātu suppaṭividdhā yassā dhammadhātuyā suppaṭividdhattā ṭhānasovetaṃ tathāgataṃ paṭibhātī’’ti. Evaṃ vutte, abhayo rājakumāro bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘abhikkantaṃ, bhante, abhikkantaṃ, bhante…pe… ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gata’’nti. Abhayarājakumārasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.
“Venerable sir, when learned nobles (khattiya), learned Brahmins (brāhmaṇa), learned householders (gahapati), and learned renunciates (samaṇa), after formulating a question (pañhaṃ abhisaṅkharitvā), then go to the Tathāgata and pose it, has there already been in the Blessed One’s (bhagavato) mind the thought (parivitakkitaṃ hoti): ‘If they come to me and ask me thus, I shall answer thus’? Or does that answer occur to the Tathāgata on the spot (ṭhānasovetaṃ tathāgataṃ paṭibhātī)?”

“Therefore, prince (rājakumāra), I will question you in return about that very thing here. You should explain it as it suits you. What do you think, prince? Are you skilled in the parts of a chariot (rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgānaṃ)?”

“Yes, venerable sir (bhante), I am skilled in the parts of a chariot (rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgānaṃ).”

“What do you think, prince? If they were to come to you and ask thus: ‘What is the name of this part of the chariot (rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgaṃ)?’ Has that already been thought out in your mind: ‘If they come to me and ask me thus, I shall answer thus,’ or would the answer occur to you on the spot (ṭhānasovetaṃ paṭibhāseyyā)?”

“Venerable sir, I am known as a chariot-maker (rathiko), skilled in the parts of a chariot (rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgānaṃ). All the parts of a chariot (rathassa aṅgapaccaṅgāni) are well known to me (suviditāni). The answer would occur to me on the spot (ṭhānasovetaṃ maṃ paṭibhāseyyā).”

“So too, prince (rājakumāra), when those learned nobles (khattiya), learned Brahmins (brāhmaṇa), learned householders (gahapati), and learned renunciates (samaṇa), after formulating a question (pañhaṃ abhisaṅkharitvā), then come to the Tathāgata and pose it, the answer occurs to the Tathāgata on the spot (ṭhānasovetaṃ tathāgataṃ paṭibhāti). Why is this so (taṃ kissa hetu)? That element of things (dhammadhātu) has been fully penetrated (suppaṭividdhā) by the Tathāgata, through the full penetration of which (dhammadhātuyā suppaṭividdhattā) the answer occurs to the Tathāgata on the spot (ṭhānasovetaṃ tathāgataṃ paṭibhātī).”

When this was said, Prince Abhaya said this to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ): “Excellent, venerable sir (bhante), excellent, venerable sir (bhante)… from today, I go to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ) for refuge (saraṇaṃ gata), taking refuge for life (pāṇupetaṃ).”

The Abhayarājakumārasutta, the eighth, is finished.

Taṃ bhagavatā suppaṭividdhaṃ, hatthagataṃ bhagavato. Tasmā so yaṃ yaṃ icchati, taṃ taṃ sabbaṃ ṭhānasova paṭibhātīti. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānameva. Ayaṃ pana dhammadesanā neyyapuggalavasena pariniṭṭhitāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Abhayarājakumārasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
87. "udāhu ṭhānasoveta" means, does it arise for the Tathāgata at that very moment through the knowledge of the arising of the basis? This is a question.

"saññāto" means known, understood, manifest.

"dhammadhātū" means the nature of the Dhamma (dharma).

This is a designation for the knowledge of omniscience. It is well-penetrated by the Blessed One, it is in the hand of the Blessed One. Therefore, whatever he wishes, all of that appears to him as a basis.

The rest is clear throughout.

This teaching of the Dhamma, however, is concluded in accordance with persons who need to be led (neyya-puggala).

The commentary on the Abhayarājakumāra Sutta in the Papañcasūdanī, the commentary on the Majjhima Nikāya, is finished.
