Thus.

I have heard that on one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Mithilā, in the Makhādeva Mango Grove. Then in a certain place the Blessed One smiled. Then it occurred to the Venerable Ānanda: “What is the reason, what is the cause, for the Blessed One’s smile? The Tathāgatas do not smile for no reason.”

Then the Venerable Ānanda arranged his upper robe on one shoulder, and extending his hands in reverential salutation towards the Blessed One, he said to him: “What is the reason, Venerable Sir, what is the cause, for the Blessed One’s smile? The Tathāgatas do not smile for no reason.”

“Once, Ānanda, in this same Mithilā there was a king named Maghadeva. He was a righteous king, a king of dharma, a great king established in dharma. He conducted himself according to dharma among the brāhmaṇas, householders, townspeople, and country people. And he observed the uposatha days on the fourteenth, fifteenth, and eighth of the fortnight.

Then, Ānanda, after many years, many hundreds of years, many thousands of years, King Maghadeva addressed his barber: ‘Good barber, when you see grey hairs growing on my head, then tell me.’

‘Yes, sire,’ the barber replied to King Maghadeva.

After many years, many hundreds of years, many thousands of years, the barber saw grey hairs growing on King Maghadeva’s head. Seeing them, he said to King Maghadeva: ‘The divine messengers have appeared, sire; grey hairs are seen growing on your head.’

‘Then, good barber, carefully pluck out those grey hairs with tweezers and place them in my cupped hands.’

‘Yes, sire,’ the barber replied to King Maghadeva, and he carefully plucked out those grey hairs with tweezers and placed them in King Maghadeva’s cupped hands.

308. It is like this: I have heard it said in the Maghadevasutta.

There, "Maghadeva’s Mango Grove" means that formerly, a king named Maghadeva planted that mango grove. When those trees were decaying, other kings planted [others] there as well. However, it came to be reckoned as Maghadeva's Mango Grove, simply by virtue of its initial designation.

"He revealed a smile" means that at eventide, while wandering on a tour of the monastery, he saw a delightful part of the earth and, reflecting, [thought,] "Have I formerly dwelt in this place?" [He recalled,] "Formerly, I, a king named Maghadeva, planted this mango grove; right here, I went forth, cultivated the four Brahmavihāras, and was reborn in the Brahma-world.

"That reason was unknown to the Sangha of monks, [so] I will make it known," showing the tips of his teeth, he revealed a smile.

dharmiko: Because he has Dharma, he is dhārmika.

dharmarājā: Because he became a king through Dharma, he is dharmarājā.

dhamme ṭhito: Because he abides in the ten wholesome courses of action, he abides in the Dharma.

dhammaṃ caratī: He acts evenly.

There, "brāhmaṇagahapatikesū" means that he did not cause the grant previously given to the brāhmaṇas by former kings to be lost; he gave it according to its original regulation, and likewise to the gahapatikas.

This was said with reference to that.

pakkhassā: By this, the pāṭihārikapakkha is also included.

Indeed, the eighth Uposatha, and the seventh and ninth days depending on whether they precede or follow it, and the fourteenth and fifteenth days, and the thirteenth and first days depending on whether they precede or follow them—these days are known as the pāṭihārikapakkha.

Even on those [days], he fasted the Uposatha.

dharmiko dharmarājā.
dhamme ṭhito dhammaṃ caratī.
brāhmaṇagahapatikesū pakkhassā pāṭihārikapakkhā.

Then, Ānanda, King Makhadeva, after giving the best village to his barber and summoning his eldest son, the prince, said to him: ‘The divine messengers, dear prince, have appeared to me; grey hairs are seen grown on my head; human sensual pleasures have been enjoyed by me; it is time to seek divine sensual pleasures.

Come, dear prince, take over this kingdom.

I, however, will shave off my hair and beard, put on the yellow robes, and go forth from the house life into homelessness.

Therefore, dear prince, when you too see grey hairs grown on your head, then, after giving the best village to the barber, and after properly instructing your eldest son, the prince, in the kingship, shave off your hair and beard, put on the yellow robes, and go forth from the house life into homelessness.

Continue this good practice (kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ) instituted by me, and may you not be the last man.

In whatever generation of men, dear prince, the cutting off of this kind of good practice (kalyāṇassa vattassa) occurs, he is the last man among them.

Therefore, dear prince, I say this to you: Continue this good practice (kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ) instituted by me, and may you not be the last man.’

Then, Ānanda, King Makhadeva, after giving the best village to his barber, and properly instructing his eldest son, the prince, in the kingship, in this very Makhadeva Mango Grove, shaved off his hair and beard, put on the yellow robes, and went forth from the house life into homelessness.

He abided pervading one quarter with a mind imbued with loving-kindness (mettāsahagatena cetasā), likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth; so above, below, across, everywhere, and to all as to himself, he abided pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with loving-kindness, abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility and without ill will.

With a mind imbued with compassion (karuṇāsahagatena cetasā)… with a mind imbued with appreciative joy (muditāsahagatena cetasā)… with a mind imbued with equanimity (upekkhāsahagatena cetasā), he abided pervading one quarter, likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth; so above, below, across, everywhere, and to all as to himself, he abided pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with equanimity, abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility and without ill will.

Ānanda, King Makhadeva for eighty-four thousand years played as a child; for eighty-four thousand years he ruled as viceroy; for eighty-four thousand years he governed the kingdom; for eighty-four thousand years in this very Makhadeva Mango Grove, having gone forth from the household life into homelessness, he practiced the holy life (brahmacariyamacari).

Having cultivated these four divine abodes (brahmavihāre), on the dissolution of the body, after death, he was reborn in the Brahma world (brahmalokūpago ahosi).

Devadūta means devo is death, and dūta is the messenger of that, therefore devadūta.

When grey hairs appear on the head, one is as if standing near the king of death, therefore grey hairs are called the messengers of the god of death (maccudeva).

Just as gods are messengers, so too are the devadūtas.

Just as when a decorated and prepared goddess, standing in the sky, says, "On such and such a day, [someone] will die," it happens just as she said; likewise, when grey hairs appear on the head, it is just like a declaration of the deity.

Therefore, grey hairs are called messengers similar to gods.

The purified gods also have messengers, the devadūtas.

Indeed, all Bodhisattvas, having seen the aged, the sick, the dead, and those who have gone forth, become agitated, leave the house, and go forth.

As it was said:

"Having seen the aged, the afflicted, and the sick,

Having seen the dead, their life's duration exhausted;

Having seen one in saffron robes who has gone forth,

Therefore, I, a king, have gone forth."

By this method, grey hairs are called devadūtas, because they are the messengers of the purified gods.

"Giving a choice village to the barber means giving the most excellent village, with an income of one hundred thousand.

Why did he give it? Because his mind was agitated.

Indeed, having seen the grey hairs placed in his joined hands, agitation arose.

Even if there are eighty-four thousand years of life remaining, even so, considering himself as standing near the king of death, agitated, he approves of going forth.

Therefore, it was said:

Giving a choice village to the barber,

"When Maghadeva, lord of the realm, saw grey hairs on his head,

The wise one gained agitation, and approved of going forth."

Moreover, it was said:

"These excellent things have arisen for me, destroyers of youth;

The devadūtas have appeared, it is time for my going forth."

"In the age of men means in a man born in a lineage.

"Having shaved off his hair and beard means even those who go forth into the ascetic life first shave off their hair and beard, and from then on, having bound their grown hair, and becoming wearers of matted locks, they wander about.

The Bodhisattva too went forth into the ascetic life.

But the one who has gone forth, without pursuing wrong means of livelihood, maintaining himself with alms food brought from the royal palace, developed the divine abodes (brahmavihāra).

Therefore, that which begins with "accompanied by loving-kindness" was said.

In the age of men, having shaved off his hair and beard, accompanied by loving-kindness. "Playing the play of a child means he played, being carried around from lap to lap.

Indeed, having lifted him up like a garland, they wandered about.

"The son of King Maghadeva… went forth" means on this day of his going forth, there were five auspicious events.

The funerary offering of King Maghadeva, the auspicious going forth of that king, the auspicious umbrella-raising of that king's son, the auspicious sub-kingship of that king's son, the auspicious name-giving of that king's son—thus, at one time, there were five auspicious events; there was a festival throughout the whole of Jambudvīpa.

Playing the play of a child.

The son of King Maghadeva… went forth.

He abided pervading one quarter with a mind imbued with compassion ( karuṇā)…with a mind imbued with appreciative joy ( muditā)…with a mind imbued with equanimity ( upekṣā), likewise the second, likewise’ the third, likewise the fourth; so above, below, around, and everywhere, and to all as to himself, he abided pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind imbued with equanimity, abundant, exalted, immeasurable, without hostility and without ill will.

Nimi, Ānanda, for eighty-four thousand years the king played childish games; for eighty-four thousand years he acted as viceregent; for eighty-four thousand years he governed the kingdom; for eighty-four thousand years in this very Maghadeva Mango Grove he went forth from the home life into homelessness and practiced the holy life (brahmacarya).

By developing the four divine abodes ( brahmavihāra), on the dissolution of the body, after death, he passed on to the Brahma-world.

Nimi's son was a youth called Kalarajanaka.

He did not go forth from the home life into homelessness.

He broke that good practice.

He was the last man among them.

316. ‘‘Siyā kho pana te, ānanda, evamassa – ‘añño nūna tena samayena rājā maghadevo ahosi, yena taṃ kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihita’nti yo taṃ kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihinīti (sī.). Na kho panetaṃ, ānanda, evaṃ daṭṭhabbaṃ. Ahaṃ tena samayena rājā maghadevo ahosiṃ. (Ahaṃ taṃ kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihiniṃ,) ( ) natthi (ka.) mayā taṃ kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihitaṃ; pacchimā janatā anuppavattesi. Taṃ kho panānanda, kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ na nibbidāya na virāgāya na nirodhāya na upasamāya na abhiññāya na sambodhāya na nibbānāya saṃvattati, yāvadeva brahmalokūpapattiyā. Idaṃ kho panānanda, etarahi mayā kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihitaṃ ekantanibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattati. Katamañcānanda, etarahi mayā kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihitaṃ ekantanibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattati? Ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi, sammāsaṅkappo, sammāvācā, sammākammanto, sammāājīvo, sammāvāyāmo , sammāsati, sammāsamādhi. Idaṃ kho, ānanda, etarahi mayā kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihitaṃ ekantanibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattati. Taṃ vo ahaṃ, ānanda, evaṃ vadāmi – ‘yena me idaṃ kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihitaṃ anuppavatteyyātha, mā kho me tumhe antimapurisā ahuvattha’. Yasmiṃ kho, ānanda, purisayuge vattamāne evarūpassa kalyāṇassa vattassa samucchedo hoti so tesaṃ antimapuriso hoti. Taṃ vo ahaṃ, ānanda, evaṃ vadāmi – ‘yena me idaṃ kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ nihitaṃ anuppavatteyyātha, mā kho me tumhe antimapurisā ahuvatthā’’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā ānando bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Maghadevasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ tatiyaṃ.
“Now, Ānanda, it may be that you think thus: ‘Certainly, someone else was King Maghadeva at that time, by whom that beautiful practice was established,’” do not view it in that way, Ānanda. I was King Maghadeva at that time. By me that beautiful practice was established; the later generations continued that good practice.

But that beautiful practice, Ānanda, does not lead to disenchantment (nirbidā), to dispassion (virāga), to cessation (nirodha), to peace (upaśamā), to direct knowledge (abhijñāya), to enlightenment (saṃbodhāya), to Nibbāna (nirvāṇa), but only to reappearance in the Brahma-world (brahmaloka).

This good practice (kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ), Ānanda, instituted by me now, leads to complete disenchantment (ekantanibbidāya), to dispassion (virāgāya), to cessation (nirodhāya), to peace (upasamāya), to direct knowledge (abhijñāya), to enlightenment (saṃbodhāya), to Nibbāna (nirvāṇa).

And what, Ānanda, is the good practice (kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ) established by me now, which leads to complete disenchantment (ekantanibbidāya), to dispassion (virāgāya), to cessation (nirodhāya), to peace (upasamāya), to direct knowledge (abhijñāya), to enlightenment (saṃbodhāya), to Nibbāna (nirvāṇa)? It is just this noble eightfold path (ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo), that is to say: right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right intention (sammāsaṅkappo), right speech (sammāvācā), right action (sammākammanto), right livelihood (sammāājīvo), right effort (sammāvāyāmo), right mindfulness (sammāsati), right concentration (sammāsamādhi).

This good practice (kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ), Ānanda, instituted by me now, leads to complete disenchantment (ekantanibbidāya), to dispassion (virāgāya), to cessation (nirodhāya), to peace (upasamāya), to direct knowledge (abhijñāya), to enlightenment (saṃbodhāya), to Nibbāna (nirvāṇa).

Ānanda, I say this to you: may you continue this good practice (kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ) established by me, may you not be the last men.

Ānanda, in whatever generation of men, while this kind of beautiful practice is in place, there is utter destruction, he is the last of them.

Ānanda, I say this to you: may you continue this good practice (kalyāṇaṃ vattaṃ) established by me, may you not be the last men.’”

Thus spoke the Blessed One (bhagavā). The venerable Ānanda, delighted, rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words.

The Maghadeva Sutta, the third, is finished.
316.Samucchedo hotīti ettha kalyāṇavattaṃ ko samucchindati, kena samucchinnaṃ, ko pavatteti, kena pavattitaṃ nāma hotīti ayaṃ vibhāgo veditabbo. Tattha sīlavā bhikkhu ‘‘na sakkā mayā arahattaṃ laddhu’’nti vīriyaṃ akaronto samucchindati. Dussīlena samucchinnaṃ nāma hoti. Satta sekhā pavattenti. Khīṇāsavena pavattitaṃ nāma hoti. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Maghadevasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
Here, "utter destruction occurs," who completely cuts off the practice of virtue? By whom is it completely cut off? Who sets it in motion? By whom is it set in motion? This distinction should be understood.

There, a virtuous bhikṣu (monk), not generating vīrya (energy) thinking, " It is not possible for me to attain arhattva (arahantship)," completely cuts it off.

It is said to be completely cut off by immorality.

The seven learners set it in motion.

It is said to be set in motion by one with destroyed āsrava (influxes).

The rest is clear throughout.
