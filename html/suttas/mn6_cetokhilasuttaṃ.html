185. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno pañca cetokhilā appahīnā, pañca cetasovinibandhā cetasovinibaddhā (sī.), cetovinibaddhā (sāratthadīpanīṭīkā) asamucchinnā, so vatimasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjissatīti – netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati. cetasovinibaddhā (sī.), cetovinibaddhā (sāratthadīpanīṭīkā) ‘‘Katamāssa pañca cetokhilā appahīnā honti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satthari kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satthari kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati tassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ paṭhamo cetokhilo appahīno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhamme kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati…pe… evamassāyaṃ dutiyo cetokhilo appahīno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu saṅghe kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati…pe… evamassāyaṃ tatiyo cetokhilo appahīno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sikkhāya kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sikkhāya kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati, tassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ catuttho cetokhilo appahīno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sabrahmacārīsu kupito hoti anattamano āhatacitto khilajāto. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sabrahmacārīsu kupito hoti anattamano āhatacitto khilajāto, tassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ pañcamo cetokhilo appahīno hoti. Imāssa pañca cetokhilā appahīnā honti.
185. Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks: “Bhikkhus”.

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Bhikkhus, it is impossible that any bhikkhu in this Dhamma-Vinaya will achieve growth, increase, or abundance if they have not abandoned five mental blockages (cetokhilā) and have not severed five mental shackles (cetasovinibandhā).

“What are the five mental blockages that they have not abandoned? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is doubtful, uncertain, indecisive, and unconfident about the Teacher ( satthari). When a bhikkhu is doubtful, uncertain, indecisive, and unconfident about the Teacher, his mind does not incline to ardour (ātappāya), devotion (anuyogāya), perseverance (sātaccāya), and exertion (padhānāya). When one’s mind does not incline to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and exertion, that is the first mental blockage that he has not abandoned.

“Again, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is doubtful, uncertain, indecisive, and unconfident about the Dhamma…As his mind does not incline to ardour…that is the second mental blockage that he has not abandoned.

“Again, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is doubtful, uncertain, indecisive, and unconfident about the Sangha…As his mind does not incline to ardour…that is the third mental blockage that he has not abandoned.

“Again, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is doubtful, uncertain, indecisive, and unconfident about the training (sikkhāya). When a bhikkhu is doubtful, uncertain, indecisive, and unconfident about the training, his mind does not incline to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and exertion. When one’s mind does not incline to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and exertion, that is the fourth mental blockage that he has not abandoned.

“Again, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is angry and displeased with his companions in the holy life (sabrahmacārīsu), resentful and callous (āhatacitto khilajāto) towards them. When a bhikkhu is angry and displeased with his companions in the holy life, resentful and callous towards them, his mind does not incline to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and exertion. When one’s mind does not incline to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and exertion, that is the fifth mental blockage that he has not abandoned. These are the five mental blockages that he has not abandoned.
185.Evaṃme sutanti cetokhilasuttaṃ. Tattha cetokhilāti cittassa thaddhabhāvā kacavarabhāvā khāṇukabhāvā. Cetaso vinibandhāti cittaṃ bandhitvā muṭṭhiyaṃ katvā viya gaṇhantīti cetaso vinibandhā. Vuddhintiādīsu sīlena vuddhiṃ, maggena viruḷhiṃ, nibbānena vepullaṃ. Sīlasamādhīhi vā vuddhiṃ, vipassanāmaggehi viruḷhiṃ, phalanibbānehi vepullaṃ. Satthari kaṅkhatīti satthu sarīre vā guṇe vā kaṅkhati. Sarīre kaṅkhamāno dvattiṃsavaralakkhaṇappaṭimaṇḍitaṃ nāma sarīraṃ atthi nu kho natthīti kaṅkhati, guṇe kaṅkhamāno atītānāgatapaccuppannajānanasamatthaṃ sabbaññutaññāṇaṃ atthi nu kho natthīti kaṅkhati. Vicikicchatīti vicinanto kicchati, dukkhaṃ āpajjati, vinicchetuṃ na sakkoti. Nādhimuccatīti evametanti adhimokkhaṃ na paṭilabhati. Na sampasīdatīti guṇesu otaritvā nibbicikicchabhāvena pasīdituṃ, anāvilo bhavituṃ na sakkoti. Ātappāyāti kilesasantāpakavīriyakaraṇatthāya. Anuyogāyāti punappunaṃ yogāya. Sātaccāyāti satatakiriyāya padhānāyāti padahanatthāya. Ayaṃ paṭhamo cetokhiloti ayaṃ satthari vicikicchāsaṅkhāto paṭhamo cittassa thaddhabhāvo, evametassa bhikkhuno appahīno hoti. Dhammeti pariyattidhamme ca paṭivedhadhamme ca. Pariyattidhamme kaṅkhamāno, tepiṭakaṃ buddhavacanaṃ caturāsīti dhammakkhandhasahassānīti vadanti, atthi nu kho etaṃ natthīti kaṅkhati. Paṭivedhadhamme kaṅkhamāno vipassanānissando maggo nāma, magganissando phalaṃ nāma, sabbasaṅkhārapaṭinissaggo nibbānaṃ nāmāti vadanti. Taṃ atthi nu kho natthīti kaṅkhati. Saṅghe kaṅkhatīti suppaṭipannotiādīnaṃ padānaṃ vasena evarūpaṃ paṭipadaṃ paṭipannā cattāro maggaṭṭhā cattāro phalaṭṭhāti aṭṭhannaṃ puggalānaṃ samūhabhūto saṅgho nāma, so atthi nu kho natthīti kaṅkhati. Sikkhāya kaṅkhamāno adhisīlasikkhā nāma adhicittasikkhā nāma adhipaññāsikkhā nāmāti vadanti. Sā atthi nu kho natthīti kaṅkhati. Ayaṃ pañcamoti ayaṃ sabrahmacārīsu kopasaṅkhāto pañcamo cittassa thaddhabhāvo kacavarabhāvo khāṇukabhāvo.
185. Thus have I heard the Cetokhila Sutta.

There, cetokhilā means rigidity of mind, the state of being like rubbish, the state of being like a stake. Cetaso vinibandhā means what seizes the mind, as if making a fist, therefore cetaso vinibandhā.

In vuddhi etc., vuddhiṃ through morality, virūḷhiṃ through the path, vepullaṃ through nirvāṇa. Or, vuddhiṃ through morality and concentration, virūḷhiṃ through the path of insight, vepullaṃ through the fruit of nirvāṇa.

Satthari kaṅkhatī means he doubts the teacher's body or qualities. Doubting the body, he doubts, "Is there indeed a body adorned with the thirty-two marks of a great man, or is there not?" Doubting the qualities, he doubts, "Is there indeed the knowledge of omniscience capable of knowing the past, future, and present, or is there not?"

Vicikicchatī means he is troubled while investigating, he encounters suffering, he is unable to decide. Nādhimuccatī means he does not attain conviction that "This is so." Na sampasīdatī means he is unable to be confident, to become untroubled, by entering into the qualities with a non-wavering attitude.

Ātappāya means for the purpose of making an effort that inflames the defilements. Anuyogāya means for repeated practice. Sātaccāya means for continuous action. Padhānāya means for striving.

Ayaṃ paṭhamo cetokhilo means this first rigidity of the mind, reckoned as doubt in the teacher, is unabandoned in this monk.

Dhamme means in the Dharma of learning and the Dharma of realization. Doubting the Dharma of learning, he doubts, "They say that the Tipiṭaka is the word of the Buddha, eighty-four thousand aggregates of teachings; is this indeed so, or is it not?" Doubting the Dharma of realization, he doubts, "They say that there is a path that is the outflow of insight, a fruit that is the outflow of the path, nirvāṇa that is the relinquishing of all formations; is that indeed so, or is it not?"

Saṅghe kaṅkhatī means he doubts, "Is there indeed a saṅgha, a group of eight individuals, those who have entered the path and those who abide in the fruit, who are practicing such conduct in accordance with the words suppaṭipanno etc., or is there not?"

Sikkhāya kaṅkhamāno means he doubts, "They say that there is the training in higher morality, the training in higher mind, the training in higher wisdom; is that indeed so, or is it not?"

Ayaṃ pañcamo means this fifth rigidity of the mind, the state of being like rubbish, the state of being like a stake, is reckoned as anger towards one's fellow brahmacārīs.

186. ‘‘Katamāssa pañca cetasovinibandhā asamucchinnā honti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāme avītarāgo avigatarāgo (katthaci) hoti avigatacchando avigatapemo avigatapipāso avigatapariḷāho avigatataṇho. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāme avītarāgo hoti avigatacchando avigatapemo avigatapipāso avigatapariḷāho avigatataṇho, tassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ paṭhamo cetasovinibandho asamucchinno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye avītarāgo hoti…pe… evamassāyaṃ dutiyo cetasovinibandho asamucchinno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu rūpe avītarāgo hoti…pe… evamassāyaṃ tatiyo cetasovinibandho asamucchinno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yāvadatthaṃ udarāvadehakaṃ bhuñjitvā seyyasukhaṃ passasukhaṃ middhasukhaṃ anuyutto viharati. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yāvadatthaṃ udarāvadehakaṃ bhuñjitvā seyyasukhaṃ passasukhaṃ middhasukhaṃ anuyutto viharati, tassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ catuttho cetasovinibandho asamucchinno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu aññataraṃ devanikāyaṃ paṇidhāya brahmacariyaṃ carati – ‘imināhaṃ sīlena vā vatena vā tapena vā brahmacariyena vā devo vā bhavissāmi devaññataro vā’ti. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu aññataraṃ devanikāyaṃ paṇidhāya brahmacariyaṃ carati – ‘imināhaṃ sīlena vā vatena vā tapena vā brahmacariyena vā devo vā bhavissāmi devaññataro vā’ti, tassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya . Yassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ pañcamo cetasovinibandho asamucchinno hoti. Imāssa pañca cetasovinibandhā asamucchinnā honti. ‘‘Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno ime pañca cetokhilā appahīnā, ime pañca cetasovinibandhā asamucchinnā, so vatimasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjissatīti – netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati.
186. “And what are the five mental bonds ( cetaso-vinibandha) that he has not severed? Here, monks, a monk is not free of passion (avītarāga), not free of desire (avigata-chanda), not free of affection (avigata-pema), not free of thirst (avigata-pipāsa), not free of fever (avigata-pariḷāha), not free of craving (avigata-taṇhā) for sensual pleasures (kāma).

When a monk is not free of passion, desire, affection, thirst, fever, or craving for sensual pleasures, then his mind doesn’t tend toward ardency (ātappa), commitment (anuyoga), perseverance (sātacca), or exertion (padhāna). As his mind does not incline to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and striving, this is the first mental bond (cetaso-vinibandha) that he has not severed.

“Again, monks, a monk is not free from lust for the body (kāya)… As his mind does not incline to ardour…that is the second mental bond (cetaso-vinibandha) that he has not severed.

“Again, monks, a monk is not free from lust for form (rūpa)… As his mind does not incline to ardour…that is the third mental bond (cetaso-vinibandha) that he has not severed.

“Again, monks, a monk eats as much as he likes until his belly is full (yāvadatthaṃ udarāvadehakaṃ bhuñjitvā) and indulges in the pleasures of sleeping (seyyasukha), lolling (passasukha), and drowsing (middhasukha). When a monk eats as much as he likes until his belly is full and indulges in the pleasures of sleeping, lolling, and drowsing, then his mind doesn’t tend toward ardency, commitment, perseverance, or exertion. As his mind does not incline to ardour…that is the fourth mental bond (cetaso-vinibandha) that he has not severed.

“Again, monks, a monk practices the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ carati) aspiring to some order of gods (aññataraṃ devanikāyaṃ paṇidhāya) thus: ‘By this virtue (sīla) or observance (vata) or asceticism (tapa) or holy life (brahmacariya), I shall become a god (deva) or some lesser god (devaññataro vā)’. When a monk practices the holy life intent on being born in one or another of the deva hosts, ‘By this virtue or practice or abstinence or holy life I will be a deva of one sort or another,’ then his mind doesn’t tend toward ardency, commitment, perseverance, or exertion. As his mind does not incline to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and striving, this is the fifth mental bond (cetaso-vinibandha) that he has not severed. These are the five mental bonds (cetaso-vinibandha) that he has not severed.

“Bhikkhus, that any bhikkhu who has not abandoned these five mental hindrances (ceto-khila) and severed these five mental bonds (cetaso-vinibandha), should come to growth, increase, and fulfilment in this Dhamma and Discipline - that is impossible.

186. In bonds, kāme means both the object of desire (vatthukāma) and the defilement of desire (kilesakāma). Kāye means in one's own body. Rūpe means in external form. Yāvadattha means as much as one wants, that much. Udarāvadehaka means filling the belly. For that belly is called udarāvadehaka because of filling the belly. Seyyasukha means the pleasure of couch and chair, or the pleasure of the seasons. Passasukha means the pleasure that arises when lying down, such as the pleasure of the right side or left side while lying with changing sides. Middhasukha means the pleasure of sleep. Anuyutto means lives engaged and devoted.

Paṇidhāyā means desiring. In sīlenā, etc., sīla means the fourfold purity of morality (catupārisuddhisīlaṃ). Vata means undertaking a vow (vatasamādānaṃ). Tapo means practicing asceticism (tapacaraṇaṃ). Brahmacariya means abstinence from sexual intercourse (methunavirati). Devo vā bhavissāmī means I will become a god of great power (mahesakkhadevo). Devaññataro vā means or one of the gods of little power (appesakkhadevesu).

187. ‘‘Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno pañca cetokhilā pahīnā, pañca cetasovinibandhā susamucchinnā, so vatimasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjissatīti – ṭhānametaṃ vijjati. ‘‘Katamāssa pañca cetokhilā pahīnā honti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satthari na kaṅkhati na vicikicchati adhimuccati sampasīdati. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satthari na kaṅkhati na vicikicchati adhimuccati sampasīdati, tassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ paṭhamo cetokhilo pahīno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhamme na kaṅkhati na vicikicchati adhimuccati sampasīdati…pe… evamassāyaṃ dutiyo cetokhilo pahīno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu saṅghe na kaṅkhati na vicikicchati adhimuccati sampasīdati…pe… evamassāyaṃ tatiyo cetokhilo pahīno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sikkhāya na kaṅkhati na vicikicchati adhimuccati sampasīdati…pe… evamassāyaṃ catuttho cetokhilo pahīno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sabrahmacārīsu na kupito hoti na anattamano attamano (sī. pī.) anāhatacitto akhilajāto. 187. “Bhikkhus, that any bhikkhu who has abandoned five wildernesses of the heart ( pañca cetokhilā pahīnā), and severed five mental bonds (pañca cetasovinibandhā susamucchinnā) should come to growth (vuddhiṃ), increase (virūḷhiṃ), and fulfilment (vepullaṃ āpajjissati) in this Dhamma and Discipline (dhammavinaye) - that is possible (ṭhānametaṃ vijjati).

“What are the five wildernesses of the heart that he has abandoned (katamāssa pañca cetokhilā pahīnā honti)? Here (idha), bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not doubtful (na kaṅkhati) , uncertain (na vicikicchati), decided (adhimuccati), and confident (sampasīdati) about the Teacher (satthari).
When a bhikkhu is not doubtful (na kaṅkhati) , uncertain (na vicikicchati), decided (adhimuccati), and confident (sampasīdati) about the Teacher (satthari), his mind (cittaṃ) inclines (namati) to ardour (ātappāya), devotion (anuyogāya), perseverance (sātaccāya), and striving (padhānāya).
As his mind inclines to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and striving, the first wilderness of the heart (paṭhamo cetokhilo) has been abandoned (pahīno hoti) by him.

“Again (puna caparaṃ), bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not doubtful (na kaṅkhati), uncertain (na vicikicchati), decided (adhimuccati), and confident (sampasīdati) about the Dhamma (dhamme)… As his mind inclines to ardour…the second wilderness of the heart (dutiyo cetokhilo) has been abandoned (pahīno hoti) by him.

“Again (puna caparaṃ), bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not doubtful (na kaṅkhati), uncertain (na vicikicchati), decided (adhimuccati), and confident (sampasīdati) about the Sangha (saṅghe)… As his mind inclines to ardour…the third wilderness of the heart (tatiyo cetokhilo) has been abandoned (pahīno hoti) by him.

“Again (puna caparaṃ), bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not doubtful (na kaṅkhati), uncertain (na vicikicchati), decided (adhimuccati), and confident (sampasīdati) about the training (sikkhāya)… As his mind inclines to ardour…the fourth wilderness of the heart (catuttho cetokhilo) has been abandoned (pahīno hoti) by him.

“Again (puna caparaṃ), bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not angry (na kupito hoti) and displeased (na anattamano) with his companions in the holy life (sabrahmacārīsu), nor resentful (anāhatacitto) and callous (akhilajāto) towards them.
When a bhikkhu is not angry and displeased with his companions in the holy life, nor resentful and callous towards them, his mind (cittaṃ) inclines (namati) to ardour (ātappāya), devotion (anuyogāya), perseverance (sātaccāya), and striving (padhānāya).
As his mind inclines to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and striving, the fifth wilderness in the heart (pañcamo cetokhilo) has been abandoned (pahīno hoti) by him.
These are the five wildernesses in the heart that he has abandoned (imāssa pañca cetokhilā pahīnā honti).

188. ‘‘Katamāssa pañca cetasovinibandhā susamucchinnā honti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāme vītarāgo hoti vigatacchando vigatapemo vigatapipāso vigatapariḷāho vigatataṇho. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāme vītarāgo hoti vigatacchando vigatapemo vigatapipāso vigatapariḷāho vigatataṇho, tassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ paṭhamo cetasovinibandho susamucchinno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye vītarāgo hoti…pe… rūpe vītarāgo hoti…pe… na yāvadatthaṃ udarāvadehakaṃ bhuñjitvā seyyasukhaṃ passasukhaṃ middhasukhaṃ anuyutto viharati. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na yāvadatthaṃ udarāvadehakaṃ bhuñjitvā seyyasukhaṃ passasukhaṃ middhasukhaṃ anuyutto viharati, tassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ catuttho cetasovinibandho susamucchinno hoti. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na aññataraṃ devanikāyaṃ paṇidhāya brahmacariyaṃ carati – ‘imināhaṃ sīlena vā vatena vā tapena vā brahmacariyena vā devo vā bhavissāmi devaññataro vā’ti. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na aññataraṃ devanikāyaṃ paṇidhāya brahmacariyaṃ carati – ‘imināhaṃ sīlena vā vatena vā tapena vā brahmacariyena vā devo vā bhavissāmi devaññataro vā’ti, tassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ pañcamo cetasovinibandho susamucchinno hoti. Imāssa pañca cetasovinibandhā susamucchinnā honti. ‘‘Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno ime pañca cetokhilā pahīnā, ime pañca cetasovinibandhā susamucchinnā, so vatimasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjissatīti – ṭhānametaṃ vijjati.
188. "What, bhikkhus, are the five shackles in the heart that he has severed? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is free from lust, desire, affection, thirst, fever, and craving for sensual pleasures (kāme vītarāgo hoti vigatacchando vigatapemo vigatapipāso vigatapariḷāho vigatataṇho). When a monk is free of passion, desire, love, thirst, fever, or craving for sensual pleasures, then his mind tends toward ardency, commitment, perseverance, or exertion (tassa cittaṃ namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya). As his mind inclines to ardour, devotion, perseverance, and striving, the first shackle in the heart has been severed by him (evamassāyaṃ paṭhamo cetasovinibandho susamucchinno hoti).

“Again, a bhikkhu is free from lust, desire, affection, thirst, fever, and craving for the body… free from lust, desire, affection, thirst, fever, and craving for form… does not eat as much as he likes until his belly is full and indulges in the pleasures of sleeping, lolling, and drowsing (na yāvadatthaṃ udarāvadehakaṃ bhuñjitvā seyyasukhaṃ passasukhaṃ middhasukhaṃ anuyutto viharati). Again, a bhikkhu does not eat as much as he likes until his belly is full and does not indulge in the pleasures of sleeping, lolling, and drowsing, then his mind tends toward ardency, commitment, perseverance, or exertion. As his mind inclines to ardour…the fourth shackle in the heart has been severed by him.

“Again, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not practice the holy life intent on being born in one or another of the deva hosts, ‘By this virtue or practice or abstinence or holy life, may I become a deva of one sort or another.’ (na aññataraṃ devanikāyaṃ paṇidhāya brahmacariyaṃ carati – ‘imināhaṃ sīlena vā vatena vā tapena vā brahmacariyena vā devo vā bhavissāmi devaññataro vā’ti). When a monk does not practice the holy life intent on being born in one or another of the deva hosts…then his mind tends toward ardency, commitment, perseverance, or exertion. As his mind inclines to ardour…the fifth shackle in the heart has been severed by him. These are the five shackles in the heart that he has severed (imāssa pañca cetasovinibandhā susamucchinnā honti).

“Bhikkhus, that any bhikkhu who has abandoned these five wildernesses in the heart and severed these five shackles in the heart should come to growth, increase, and fulfilment in this Dhamma and Discipline - that is possible (so vatimasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjissatīti – ṭhānametaṃ vijjati).

189. ‘‘So chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, vīriyasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, cittasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, vīmaṃsāsamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, ussoḷhīyeva pañcamī. Sa kho so, bhikkhave, evaṃ ussoḷhīpannarasaṅgasamannāgato bhikkhu bhabbo abhinibbidāya, bhabbo sambodhāya, bhabbo anuttarassa yogakkhemassa adhigamāya. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, kukkuṭiyā aṇḍāni aṭṭha vā dasa vā dvādasa vā. Tānassu kukkuṭiyā sammā adhisayitāni sammā pariseditāni sammā paribhāvitāni. Kiñcāpi tassā kukkuṭiyā na evaṃ icchā uppajjeyya – ‘aho vatime kukkuṭapotakā pādanakhasikhāya vā mukhatuṇḍakena vā aṇḍakosaṃ padāletvā sotthinā abhinibbhijjeyyu’nti. Atha kho bhabbāva te kukkuṭapotakā pādanakhasikhāya vā mukhatuṇḍakena vā aṇḍakosaṃ padāletvā sotthinā abhinibbhijjituṃ. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, evaṃ ussoḷhipannarasaṅgasamannāgato bhikkhu bhabbo abhinibbidāya, bhabbo sambodhāya, bhabbo anuttarassa yogakkhemassa adhigamāyā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Cetokhilasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ.
“He cultivates the basis for spiritual power consisting of concentration due to zeal with determined striving (chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti); he cultivates the basis for spiritual power consisting of concentration due to energy with determined striving (vīriyasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti); he cultivates the basis for spiritual power consisting of concentration due to purity of mind with determined striving; he cultivates the basis for spiritual power consisting of concentration due to investigation with determined striving (vīmaṃsāsamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti). And enthusiasm is the fifth.

"So too, a bhikkhu who possesses the fifteen factors including enthusiasm is capable of breaking out, capable of enlightenment, capable of attaining the supreme security from bondage."

“Suppose there were a hen with eight, ten, or twelve eggs. which she had properly covered, warmed, and incubated. Even though she did not wish: ‘Oh, that my chicks might pierce their shells with the points of their claws and beaks and hatch out safely! ‘ yet the chicks are capable of piercing their shells with the points of their claws and beaks and hatching out safely.

“So too, a bhikkhu who thus possesses the fifteen factors including enthusiasm is capable of breaking out, capable of enlightenment, capable of attaining the supreme safety from bondage.”

The Buddha spoke thus.

Gratified, the monks delighted at his words.

The Cetokhila Sutta, the sixth, is finished.
189. Iddhipādesu chandaṃ nissāya pavatto samādhi chandasamādhi. Padhānabhūtā saṅkhārā padhānasaṅkhārā. Samannāgatanti tehi dhammehi upetaṃ. Iddhiyā pādaṃ, iddhibhūtaṃ vā pādanti iddhipādaṃ. Sesesupi eseva nayo, ayamettha saṅkhepo. Vitthāro pana iddhipādavibhaṅge āgato yeva. Visuddhimaggepissa attho dīpito. Iti imehi catūhi iddhipādehi vikkhambhanappahānaṃ kathitaṃ. Ussoḷhīyeva pañcamīti ettha ussoḷhīti sabbattha kattabbavīriyaṃ dasseti. Ussoḷhīpannarasaṅgasamannāgatoti pañca cetokhilappahānāni pañca vinibandhappahānāni cattāro iddhipādā ussoḷhīti evaṃ ussoḷhiyā saddhiṃ pannarasahi aṅgehi samannāgato. Bhabboti anurūpo, anucchaviko. Abhinibbhidāyāti ñāṇena kilesabhedāya. Sambodhāyāti catumaggasambodhāya. Anuttarassāti seṭṭhassa. Yogakkhemassāti catūhi yogehi khemassa arahattassa. Adhigamāyāti paṭilābhāya. Seyyathāti opammatthe nipāto. Pīti sambhāvanatthe. Ubhayenapi seyyathāpi nāma, bhikkhaveti dasseti . Kukkuṭiyāaṇḍāni aṭṭha vā dasa vā dvādasa vāti ettha pana kiñcāpi kukkuṭiyā vuttappakārato ūnādhikānipi aṇḍāni honti, vacanasiliṭṭhatāya pana evaṃ vuttaṃ. Evañhi loke siliṭṭhaṃ vacanaṃ hoti. Tānassūti tāni assu, bhaveyyunti attho. Kukkuṭiyā sammā adhisayitānīti tāya janettiyā kukkuṭiyā pakkhe pasāretvā tesaṃ upari sayantiyā sammā adhisayitāni. Sammā pariseditānīti kālena kālaṃ utuṃ gāhāpentiyā suṭṭhu samantato seditāni usmīkatāni. Sammā paribhāvitānīti kālena kālaṃ suṭṭhu samantato bhāvitāni, kukkuṭagandhaṃ gāhāpitānīti attho. Kiñcāpi tassā kukkuṭiyāti tassā kukkuṭiyā imaṃ tividhakiriyākaraṇena appamādaṃ katvā kiñcāpi na evaṃ icchā uppajjeyya. Atha kho bhabbāva teti atha kho te kukkuṭapotakā vuttanayena sotthinā abhinibbhijjituṃ bhabbāva. Te hi yasmā tāya kukkuṭiyā evaṃ tīhākārehi tāni aṇḍāni paripālīyamānāni na pūtīni honti. Yopi nesaṃ allasineho, sopi pariyādānaṃ gacchati, kapālaṃ tanukaṃ hoti, pādanakhasikhā ca mukhatuṇḍakañca kharaṃ hoti, sayaṃ paripākaṃ gacchati, kapālassa tanuttā bahi āloko anto paññāyati, tasmā ‘‘ciraṃ vata mayaṃ saṅkuṭitahatthapādā sambādhe sayimhā, ayañca bahi āloko dissati, ettha dāni no sukhavihāro bhavissatī’’ti nikkhamitukāmā hutvā kapālaṃ pādena paharanti, gīvaṃ pasārenti, tato taṃ kapālaṃ dvedhā bhijjati. Atha te pakkhe vidhunantā taṅkhaṇānurūpaṃ viravantā nikkhamantiyeva, nikkhamitvā ca gāmakkhettaṃ upasobhayamānā vicaranti. Kukkuṭiyā aṇḍāni aṭṭha vā dasa vā dvādasa vā Tānassū Kukkuṭiyā sammā adhisayitānī Sammā pariseditānī Sammā paribhāvitānī Kiñcāpi tassā kukkuṭiyā Atha kho bhabbāva te Evameva khoti idaṃ opammasampaṭipādanaṃ. Taṃ evaṃ atthena saṃsandetvā veditabbaṃ – tassā kukkuṭiyā aṇḍesu tividhakiriyākaraṇaṃ viya hi imassa bhikkhuno ussoḷhīpannarasehi aṅgehi samannāgatabhāvo. Kukkuṭiyā tividhakiriyāsampādanena aṇḍānaṃ apūtibhāvo viya pannarasaṅgasamannāgatassa bhikkhuno tividhānupassanāsampādanena vipassanāñāṇassa aparihāni. Tassā tividhakiriyākaraṇena aṇḍānaṃ allasinehapariyādānaṃ viya tassa bhikkhuno tividhānupassanāsampādanena bhavattayānugatanikantisinehapariyādānaṃ . Aṇḍakalāpānaṃ tanubhāvo viya bhikkhuno avijjaṇḍakosassa tanubhāvo. Kukkuṭapotakānaṃ pādanakhamutuṇḍakānaṃ thaddhakharabhāvo viya bhikkhuno vipassanāñāṇassa tikkhakharavippasannasūrabhāvo. Kukkuṭapotakānaṃ pariṇāmakālo viya bhikkhuno vipassanāñāṇassa pariṇāmakālo vaḍḍhitakālo gabbhaggahaṇakālo. Kukkuṭapotakānaṃ pādanakhasikhāya vā mukhatuṇḍakena vā aṇḍakosaṃ padāletvā pakkhe papphoṭetvā sotthinā abhinikkhamanakālo viya tassa bhikkhuno vipassanāñāṇagabbhaṃ gaṇhāpetvā vicarantassa tajjātikaṃ utusappāyaṃ vā bhojanasappāyaṃ vā puggalasappāyaṃ vā dhammassavanasappāyaṃ vā labhitvā ekāsane nisinnasseva vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhentassa anupubbādhigatena arahattamaggena avijjaṇḍakosaṃ padāletvā abhiññāpakkhe papphoṭetvā sotthinā arahattappattakālo veditabbo. Yathā pana kukkuṭapotakānaṃ pariṇatabhāvaṃ ñatvā mātāpi aṇḍakosaṃ bhindati, evaṃ tathārūpassa bhikkhuno ñāṇaparipākaṃ ñatvā satthāpi – Evameva kho ‘‘Ucchinda sinehamattano, kumudaṃ sāradikaṃva pāṇinā; Santimaggameva brūhaya, nibbānaṃ sugatena desita’’nti. (dha. pa. 285) – Ādinā nayena obhāsaṃ pharitvā gāthāya avijjaṇḍakosaṃ paharati, so gāthāpariyosāne avijjāṇḍakosaṃ bhinditvā arahattaṃ pāpuṇāti. Tato paṭṭhāya yathā te kukkuṭapotakā gāmakkhettaṃ upasobhayamānā tattha tattha vicaranti, evaṃ ayampi mahākhīṇāsavo nibbānarammaṇaṃ phalasamāpattiṃ appetvā saṅghārāmaṃ upasobhayamāno vicarati. Iti imasmiṃ sutte cattāri pahānāni kathitāni. Kathaṃ? Cetokhilānañhi cetovinibandhānaṃ pahānena paṭisaṅkhānappapahānaṃ kathitaṃ, iddhipādehi vikkhambhanappahānaṃ kathita, magge āgate samucchedappahānaṃ kathitaṃ, phale āgate paṭippassaddhippahānaṃ kathitaṃ. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti. paṭisaṅkhānappapahānaṃ vikkhambhanappahānaṃ samucchedappahānaṃ paṭippassaddhippahānaṃ Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Cetokhilasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
189. Concentration (samādhi) that arises dependent on desire (chanda) in the bases of supernormal power (iddhipāda) is desire-concentration (chandasamādhi).

Formative factors that are dominant are dominant formative factors (padhānasaṅkhārā).

"Endowed" means possessed of these qualities.

The foot (pāda) of supernormal power (iddhi), or what is the basis of supernormal power (iddhi), is supernormal power-foot (iddhipāda).

This is the method in the remainder too; this is the summary here.

But the detailed explanation is in the Vibhaṅga (Division) of the Bases of Supernormal Power (iddhipāda).

Its meaning is explained in the Visuddhimagga (Path of Purification).

Thus, the suppression-abandonment (vikkhambhanappahāna) through these four bases of supernormal power (iddhipāda) has been spoken of.

"Zeal alone is fifth (ussoḷhīyeva pañcamī)"—here, zeal (ussoḷhī) shows energy that should be done everywhere.

"Endowed with zeal and fifteen factors (ussoḷhīpannarasaṅgasamannāgato)" means endowed with five abandonings of mental barrenness (cetokhilappahānāni), five abandonings of bonds (vinibandhappahānāni), four bases of supernormal power (iddhipādā), and zeal (ussoḷhī); thus, endowed with zeal and fifteen factors (aṅga).

"Capable (bhabbo)" means suitable, conforming.

"For penetration (abhinibbhidāyā)" means for the breaking of defilements (kilesa) through knowledge (ñāṇa).

"For full awakening (sambodhāyā)" means for the full awakening to the four paths (catumagga).

"Of the unsurpassed (anuttarassā)" means of the best (seṭṭhassa).

"Of the safety from yokes (yogakkhemassā)" means of the safety from the four yokes (yoga), of the Arahantship (arahatta).

"For attainment (adhigamāyā)" means for gain (paṭilābhāya).

"Just as (seyyathā)" is a particle (nipāta) in the sense of comparison (opamma).

"Indeed (pī)" is in the sense of possibility (sambhāvana).

He shows (dasseti) through both, just as, monks (bhikkhave), by name.

Eggs, eight or ten or twelve: Here, although there are eggs fewer or more than the stated kind of hen, thus it is spoken due to skillfulness of speech.

For in the world, speech is skillful thus.

"May they be (tānassū)" means may those (tāni) be (assu), may they come to be (bhaveyyunti), [this is the] meaning (attho).

"Well incubated by the hen (kukkuṭiyā sammā adhisayitānī)" means well incubated by that mother hen (janettiyā kukkuṭiyā), spreading the wings (pakkha) and lying on top of them (tesaṃ upari sayantiyā).

"Well heated (sammā pariseditānī)" means well heated from all around (samantato seditāni) by causing heat (utuṃ gāhāpentiyā) at the right time (kālena kālaṃ), warmed (usmīkatāni).

"Well fostered (sammā paribhāvitānī)" means well fostered from all around (samantato bhāvitāni) at the right time (kālena kālaṃ), caused to take up the hen-smell (kukkuṭagandhaṃ gāhāpitānīti), [this is the] meaning (attho).

"Although of that hen (kiñcāpi tassā kukkuṭiyā)" means although such a desire (icchā) would not arise even if, making non-negligence (appamādaṃ katvā), by that hen (tassā kukkuṭiyā) the action of doing this threefold activity (imaṃ tividhakiriyākaraṇena).

"But they are capable (atha kho bhabbāva te)" means but those chicks (kukkuṭapotakā) are indeed capable of breaking forth safely (sotthinā abhinibbhijjituṃ bhabbāva) by the stated method (vuttanayena).

Since those eggs (tāni aṇḍāni), being protected (paripālīyamānāni) by that hen (tāya kukkuṭiyā) in such a threefold way (evaṃ tīhākārehi), are not putrid (na pūtīni honti).

Also, that tenderness in them (nesaṃ allasineho) goes to wearing away (pariyādānaṃ gacchati), the shell (kapālaṃ) is thin (tanukaṃ hoti), and the toenail-tips (pādanakhasikhā ca) and the beak-tips (mukhatuṇḍakañca) are hard (kharaṃ hoti), it itself goes to ripening (paripākaṃ gacchati), because of the thinness of the shell (kapālassa tanuttā) the light (āloko) outside (bahi) appears (paññāyati) inside (anto), therefore, "For a long time (ciraṃ vata) we (mayaṃ) have been lying (sayimhā) cramped (saṅkuṭitahatthapādā) in confinement (sambādhe), and this light (āloko) outside (bahi) is seen (dissati), now (dāni) here (ettha) there will be ease (sukhavihāro bhavissatī)", becoming desirous of going out (nikkhamitukāmā hutvā), they strike the shell (kapālaṃ paharanti) with the foot (pādena), they stretch out (pasārenti) the neck (gīvaṃ), then (tato) that (taṃ) shell (kapālaṃ) is split in two (dvedhā bhijjati).

Then (atha) they (te), flapping the wings (pakkhe vidhunantā), sounding the appropriate note (taṅkhaṇānurūpaṃ viravantā), come forth (nikkhamanti); and having come forth (nikkhamitvā ca), beautifying (upasobhayamānā) the village-field (gāmakkhettaṃ), they wander (vicaranti).

Hen: Eggs, eight or ten or twelve, may they be well incubated by the hen, well heated, well fostered, although of that hen, but they are capable. Thus indeed: This is the presentation of the simile.

That should be understood by connecting it to the meaning thus: Just as the activity of the threefold doing by that hen in the eggs, so is the state of having become endowed with the fifteen factors by this monk.

Just as, by the accomplishment of the threefold activity by the hen, there is non-putridity of the eggs, so, by the accomplishment of the threefold contemplation by the monk endowed with the fifteen factors, there is non-decline of the insight-knowledge.

Just as, by that hen's activity of the threefold doing, there is a wearing away of the oily tenderness (allasineha) of the eggs, so, by this monk's accomplishment of the threefold contemplation, there is a wearing away of the desire-tenderness associated with the triple existence.
As the thinness of eggshells, so [is] the thinness of the ignorant bhikkhu's shell of ignorance.

As the firm, hard nature of the feet, nails, and beaks of chicks, so [is] the sharp, hard, clear, and valorous nature of the bhikkhu's insight knowledge.

As the time for the development of chicks, so [is] the time for the development, growth, and grasping of the embryo of the bhikkhu's insight knowledge.

As the time when chicks break open the eggshell with the tip of their feet or the beak of their mouths, and flapping their wings, safely emerge; so should it be understood as the time when that bhikkhu, causing the embryo of insight knowledge to be grasped and wandering about, and having obtained suitable weather, suitable food, a suitable person, or the hearing of suitable Dharma, and sitting in a solitary place, developing insight, by the successively attained path of Arahantship, breaks open the eggshell of ignorance, flaps his wings of direct knowledge, and safely attains Arahantship.

Moreover, just as the mother, knowing the ripened state of the chicks, also breaks the eggshell, so too, the Teacher, knowing the ripened state of the bhikkhu, [speaks thus:]

"Cut off your affection for yourself, as one would a water lily in autumn with one's hand; cultivate only the path to peace: Nibbāna, as taught by the Sugata" (Dhp. 285).

In this way, pervading [him] with light, with the verse, he strikes at the eggshell of ignorance; at the end of the verse, he breaks the eggshell of ignorance and attains Arahantship.

From then on, just as those chicks, adorning the village fields, wander here and there, so too, this great Khīṇāsava, having attained the bliss of fruition, the object of which is Nibbāna, wanders about adorning the Saṅghārāma.

Thus, in this sutta, four kinds of abandoning have been spoken of. How? By the abandoning of the mind's barrenness, for indeed, with the abandoning of the mind's fetters, paṭisaṅkhānappahānaṃ [abandoning by reflective knowledge] is spoken of; with the bases of psychic power, vikkhambhanappahānaṃ [abandoning by suppression] is spoken of; with the path having arisen, samucchedappahānaṃ [abandoning by eradication] is spoken of; with the fruition having arisen, paṭippassaddhippahānaṃ [abandoning by tranquillization] is spoken of. The rest is clear throughout.

Paṭisaṅkhānappahānaṃ, vikkhambhanappahānaṃ, samucchedappahānaṃ, paṭippassaddhippahānaṃ.

The Cetokhilasuttavaṇṇanā in the Papañcasūdanī, commentary to the Majjhimanikāya, is finished.
