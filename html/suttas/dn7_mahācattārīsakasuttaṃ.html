Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Anathapindika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Savatthi.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks (saying): “Monks.”

“Venerable, sir,” they replied.

The Blessed One said this: “Bhikkhus, I shall teach you noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites.

Listen & pay close attention. I will speak.”

‘Yes, Lord’, said the monks,

The Blessed One said this:

“And what, bhikkhus, is noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites? that is, right view sammādiṭṭhi, right intention sammāsaṅkappa, right speech sammāvācā, right action sammākammanto, right livelihood sammāājīvo, right effort sammāvāyāmo, and right mindfulness sammāsati; Unification of mind equipped with these seven factors is called noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites.

“Therein, bhikkhus, right view comes first.

And how, bhikkhus, does right view come first? One understands wrong view as wrong view, and right view as right view: that is one’s right view.

“And what, bhikkhus, is wrong view? ‘There is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed; no fruit or result of good and bad actions; no this world, no other world; no mother, no father; no beings who are reborn spontaneously; no good and virtuous recluses and brahmins in the world who have realised for themselves by direct knowledge and declare this world and the other world. ‘ This, bhikkhus, is wrong view.

“And what, bhikkhus, is right view? Right view, I say, is twofold: there is right view that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment; and there is right view that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

And what, bhikkhus, is right view that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment? ‘There is what is given and what is offered and what is sacrificed; there is fruit and result of good and bad actions; there is this world and the other world; there is mother and father; there are beings who are reborn spontaneously; there are in the world good and virtuous recluses and brahmins who have realised for themselves by direct knowledge and declare this world and the other world. ‘ This is right view affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment.

“And what, bhikkhus, is right view that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path? The wisdom, the faculty of wisdom, the power of wisdom, the investigation-of-states enlightenment factor, the path factor of right view in one whose mind is noble, whose mind is taintless, who possesses the noble path and is developing the noble path: this is right view that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

Mindfully one abandons wrong view, mindfully one enters upon and abides in right view: this is one’s right effort.
Mindfully one abandons wrong view, mindfully one enters upon and abides in right view: this is one’s right mindfulness.
there is right mindfulness.

Thus, these three qualities run and circle around right view, namely, right view (samyagdṛṣṭi), right effort (samyagvyāyāma), and right mindfulness (samyaksmṛti).

137. "Therein, monks, right view comes first.

And how, monks, does right view come first? One understands wrong intention as 'wrong intention,' and right intention as 'right intention.' That is one's right view.

"And what, monks, is wrong intention? It is the intention of sensual pleasure (kāmasaṅkappa), the intention of ill-will (byāpādasaṅkappa), and the intention of violence (vihiṃsāsaṅkappa)—this, monks, is wrong intention.

"And what, monks, is right intention? Right intention, I say, monks, is twofold: there is right intention that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment (sāsavo puññabhāgiyo upadhivepakko); and there is right intention that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path (ariyo anāsavo lokuttaro maggaṅgo).

And what, monks, is right intention that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment? It is the intention of renunciation (nekkhammasaṅkappa), the intention of non-ill-will (abyāpādasaṅkappa), and the intention of non-violence (avihiṃsāsaṅkappa)—this, monks, is right intention that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment."

"And what, monks, is right intention that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path? The thinking (takko), thought (vitakko), intention (saṅkappo), mental absorption (appanā), mental fixity (byappanā), directing of mind (cetaso abhiniropanā), verbal formation (vacīsaṅkhāro) in one whose mind is noble (ariyacittassa), whose mind is taintless (anāsavacittassa), who possesses the noble path (ariyamaggasamaṅgino) and is developing the noble path (ariyamaggaṃ bhāvayato): this, monks, is right intention that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

One makes an effort to abandon wrong intention and to enter upon right intention: this is one’s right effort (sammāvāyāmo).

Mindfully one abandons wrong intention, mindfully one enters upon and abides in right intention: this is one’s right mindfulness (sammāsati).

Thus these three states run and circle around right intention, that is, right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right effort (sammāvāyāmo), and right mindfulness (sammāsati).

138. "Therein, bhikkhus, right view comes first.

And how, bhikkhus, does right view come first? One understands wrong speech as wrong speech, and right speech as right speech; that is one's right view (sammādiṭṭhi).

And what, bhikkhus, is wrong speech (micchāvācā)? False speech (musāvādo), malicious speech (pisuṇā vācā), harsh speech (pharusā vācā), and gossip (samphappalāpo)—this, bhikkhus, is wrong speech (micchāvācā).

And what, bhikkhus, is right speech (sammāvācā)? Right speech (sammāvāca), bhikkhus, I say is of two kinds: there is right speech (sammāvācā) that is affected by taints, on the side of merit, ripening in attachment; and there is right speech (sammāvācā) that is noble (ariyā), taintless (anāsavā), supramundane (lokuttarā), a factor of the path (maggaṅgā).

And what, bhikkhus, is right speech (sammāvācā) that is affected by taints, on the side of merit, ripening in attachment? Abstinence (veramaṇī) from false speech (musāvādā), abstinence (veramaṇī) from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya), abstinence (veramaṇī) from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya), abstinence (veramaṇī) from gossip (samphappalāpā)—this, bhikkhus, is right speech (sammāvācā) that is affected by taints, on the side of merit, ripening in attachment.

And what, bhikkhus, is right speech (sammāvācā) that is noble (ariyā), taintless (anāsavā), supramundane (lokuttarā), a factor of the path (maggaṅgā)? The desisting (ārati), abstaining (virati), refraining (paṭivirati), abstinence (veramaṇī) from the four kinds of verbal misconduct (vacīduccaritehi) in one whose mind is noble (ariyacittassa), whose mind is taintless (anāsavacittassa), who possesses the noble path (ariyamaggasamaṅgino) and is developing the noble path (ariyamaggaṃ bhāvayato): this, bhikkhus, is right speech (sammāvācā) that is noble (ariyā), taintless (anāsavā), supramundane (lokuttarā), a factor of the path (maggaṅgā).

One makes an effort (vāyamati) to abandon wrong speech (micchāvācāya pahānāya) and to enter upon right speech (sammāvācāya upasampadāya): this is one’s right effort (sammāvāyāmo).

Mindful (sato) one abandons wrong speech (micchāvācaṃ pajahati), mindful (sato) one dwells having entered upon right speech (sammāvācaṃ upasampajja viharati): this is one’s right mindfulness (sammāsati).

Thus these three states run and circle around right speech (sammāvācaṃ), that is, right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right effort (sammāvāyāmo), and right mindfulness (sammāsati).

“Therein, bhikkhus, right view (sammādiṭṭhi) comes first.

And how, bhikkhus, does right view come first? One understands wrong action (micchākammanta) as ‘wrong action,’ and right action (sammākammanta) as ‘right action’; that is one's right view.

And what, bhikkhus, is wrong action? Killing living beings (pāṇātipāto), taking what is not given (adinnādānaṃ), and misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācāro) – this, bhikkhus, is wrong action.

And what, bhikkhus, is right action? Right action, I say, is twofold: there is right action that is affected by taints (sāsavo), partaking of merit (puññabhāgiyo), ripening on the side of attachment (upadhivepakko); and there is right action that is noble (ariyo), taintless (anāsavo), supramundane (lokuttaro), a factor of the path (maggaṅgo).

And what, bhikkhus, is right action that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment? Abstinence (veramaṇī) from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā), abstinence from taking what is not given (adinnādānā), abstinence from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā) – this, bhikkhus, is right action that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment.

And what, bhikkhus, is right action that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path? The desisting (ārati), abstaining (virati), refraining (paṭivirati), abstinence (veramaṇī) from the three kinds of bodily misconduct in one whose mind is noble, whose mind is taintless, who possesses the noble path and is developing the noble path: this, bhikkhus, is right action that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

One makes an effort (vāyamati) to abandon wrong action and to enter upon right action: this is one’s right effort (sammāvāyāmo).

Mindfully (sato) one abandons wrong action, mindfully one enters upon and abides in right action: this is one’s right mindfulness (sammāsati).

Thus these three states run and circle around right action, that is, right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right effort (sammāvāyāmo), and right mindfulness (sammāsati).

Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo sāsavo puññabhāgiyo upadhivepakko? Idha, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako micchāājīvaṃ pahāya sammāājīvena jīvikaṃ kappeti – ayaṃ, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo sāsavo puññabhāgiyo upadhivepakko. Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo ariyo anāsavo lokuttaro maggaṅgo? Yā kho, bhikkhave, ariyacittassa anāsavacittassa ariyamaggasamaṅgino ariyamaggaṃ bhāvayato micchāājīvā ārati virati paṭivirati veramaṇī – ayaṃ, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo ariyo anāsavo lokuttaro maggaṅgo. So micchāājīvassa pahānāya vāyamati, sammāājīvassa upasampadāya <pb ed="V" n="3.0122"/>; svāssa hoti sammāvāyāmo. So sato micchāājīvaṃ pajahati, sato sammāājīvaṃ upasampajja viharati; sāssa hoti sammāsati. Itiyime tayo dhammā sammāājīvaṃ anuparidhāvanti <pb ed="T" n="3.0187"/> anuparivattanti, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati.

140. “Therein, bhikkhus, right view comes first. And how, bhikkhus, does right view come first? One understands wrong livelihood as ‘wrong livelihood,’ and right livelihood as ‘right livelihood’; that is one’s right view.

“And what, bhikkhus, is wrong livelihood? It is deception (kuhanā), talkativeness (lapanā), prognosticating (nemittikatā), disparaging (nippesikatā), and the pursuit of gain with gain (lābhena lābhaṃ nijigīsanatā)—this, bhikkhus, is wrong livelihood.

“And what, bhikkhus, is right livelihood? Right livelihood, I say, is twofold: there is right livelihood that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment; and there is right livelihood that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

“And what, bhikkhus, is right livelihood that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment? Here, bhikkhus, a noble disciple abandons wrong livelihood and gains his living by right livelihood: this, bhikkhus, is right livelihood that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening on the side of attachment.

“And what, bhikkhus, is right livelihood that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path? The desisting from wrong livelihood, the abstaining, refraining, abstinence from it in one whose mind is noble, whose mind is taintless, who possesses the noble path and is developing the noble path: this, bhikkhus, is right livelihood that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.

“One makes an effort to abandon wrong livelihood and to undertake right livelihood: this is one’s right effort. Mindfully one abandons wrong livelihood, mindfully one enters upon and abides in right livelihood: this is one’s right mindfulness. Thus these three states run and circle around right livelihood, that is, right view, right effort, and right mindfulness.
141
<p n="141" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">141</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Tatra, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti. Kathañca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti? Sammādiṭṭhissa <pb ed="P" n="3.0076"/>, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo pahoti, sammāsaṅkappassa sammāvācā pahoti, sammāvācassa sammākammanto pahoti, sammākammantassa sammāājīvo pahoti, sammāājīvassa sammāvāyāmo pahoti, sammāvāyāmassa sammāsati pahoti, sammāsatissa sammāsamādhi pahoti, sammāsamādhissa sammāñāṇaṃ pahoti, sammāñāṇassa sammāvimutti pahoti. Iti kho, bhikkhave, aṭṭhaṅgasamannāgato sekkho <note>aṭṭhaṅgasamannāgatā sekhā paṭipadā (sī.), aṭṭhaṅgasamannāgato sekho pāṭipado (pī. ka.) ( ) natthi sī. syā. kaṃ. pī. potthakesu</note>, dasaṅgasamannāgato arahā hoti. (Tatrapi sammāñāṇena aneke pāpakā akusalā dhammā vigatā bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti).

There, monks, right view comes first.

And how, monks, does right view come first? In one of right view, monks, right intention comes into being; in one of right intention, right speech comes into being; in one of right speech, right action comes into being; in one of right action, right livelihood comes into being; in one of right livelihood, right effort comes into being; in one of right effort, right mindfulness comes into being; in one of right mindfulness, right concentration comes into being; in one of right concentration, right knowledge comes into being; and in one of right knowledge, right liberation comes into being.

Thus, monks, the path of the disciple in higher training possesses eight factors (aṣṭāṅga-samannāgataḥ śaikṣaḥ), the arhat possesses ten factors. (There, with right knowledge, many evil, unwholesome things are destroyed and one goes to the perfection of development).

<p n="142" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">142</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Tatra, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti. Kathañca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi pubbaṅgamā hoti? Sammādiṭṭhissa, bhikkhave, micchādiṭṭhi nijjiṇṇā hoti. Ye ca micchādiṭṭhipaccayā aneke pāpakā akusalā dhammā sambhavanti te <pb ed="M" n="3.0121"/> cassa nijjiṇṇā honti. Sammādiṭṭhipaccayā aneke kusalā dhammā bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti. Sammāsaṅkappassa, bhikkhave, micchāsaṅkappo nijjiṇṇo hoti…pe… sammāvācassa, bhikkhave, micchāvācā nijjiṇṇā hoti… sammākammantassa, bhikkhave, micchākammanto nijjiṇṇo hoti… sammāājīvassa, bhikkhave, micchāājīvo nijjiṇṇo hoti… sammāvāyāmassa <pb ed="P" n="3.0077"/>, bhikkhave <pb ed="T" n="3.0188"/>, micchāvāyāmo nijjiṇṇo hoti… sammāsatissa, bhikkhave, micchāsati nijjiṇṇā hoti… sammāsamādhissa, bhikkhave, micchāsamādhi nijjiṇṇo hoti… sammāñāṇassa, bhikkhave, micchāñāṇaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ hoti… sammāvimuttassa, bhikkhave, micchāvimutti nijjiṇṇā hoti. Ye ca micchāvimuttipaccayā aneke pāpakā akusalā dhammā sambhavanti te cassa nijjiṇṇā honti. Sammāvimuttipaccayā ca aneke kusalā dhammā bhāvanāpāripūriṃ gacchanti.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Iti kho, bhikkhave, vīsati kusalapakkhā, vīsati akusalapakkhā – mahācattārīsako <pb ed="V" n="3.0123"/> dhammapariyāyo pavattito appaṭivattiyo samaṇena vā brāhmaṇena vā devena vā mārena vā brahmunā vā kenaci vā lokasmiṃ.

<pb ed="V" n="3.0123"/>
“Therein, bhikkhus, right view comes first. And how, bhikkhus, does right view come first? In one of right view, bhikkhus, wrong view is abolished, and the many evil unwholesome dharmas that originate with wrong view as condition are also abolished, and the many wholesome dharmas that originate with right view as condition come to fulfilment by development. In one of right intention, bhikkhus, wrong intention is abolished… In one of right speech, bhikkhus, wrong speech is abolished… In one of right action, bhikkhus, wrong action is abolished… In one of right livelihood, bhikkhus, wrong livelihood is abolished… In one of right effort, bhikkhus, wrong effort is abolished… In one of right mindfulness, bhikkhus, wrong mindfulness is abolished… In one of right samādhi, bhikkhus, wrong samādhi is abolished… In one of right knowledge, bhikkhus, wrong knowledge is abolished… In one of right deliverance, bhikkhus, wrong deliverance is abolished. The many evil, unskillful qualities that come into play with wrong release as their condition are also abolished, while the many skillful qualities that have right release as their condition go to the culmination of their development.

“Thus, bhikkhus, there are twenty factors on the side of the wholesome (kuśala), and twenty factors on the side of the unwholesome (akuśala). This Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty has been set rolling and cannot be stopped by any śramaṇa or brāhmaṇa or deva or Māra or Brahmā or anyone in the world.

<p n="143" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">143</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Yo hi koci, bhikkhave, samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā imaṃ mahācattārīsakaṃ dhammapariyāyaṃ garahitabbaṃ paṭikkositabbaṃ maññeyya tassa diṭṭheva dhamme dasasahadhammikā vādānuvādā gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchanti – sammādiṭṭhiṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati, ye ca micchādiṭṭhī samaṇabrāhmaṇā te bhoto pujjā, te bhoto pāsaṃsā; sammāsaṅkappaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati <pb ed="P" n="3.0078"/>, ye ca micchāsaṅkappā samaṇabrāhmaṇā te bhoto pujjā, te bhoto pāsaṃsā; sammāvācaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati…pe… sammākammantaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāājīvaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāvāyāmaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāsatiṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāsamādhiṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati… sammāñāṇaṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati <pb ed="T" n="3.0189"/>… sammāvimuttiṃ ce bhavaṃ garahati, ye ca micchāvimuttī samaṇabrāhmaṇā te bhoto pujjā, te bhoto pāsaṃsā. Yo koci, bhikkhave, samaṇo vā brāhmaṇo vā imaṃ mahācattārīsakaṃ dhammapariyāyaṃ garahitabbaṃ paṭikkositabbaṃ maññeyya tassa diṭṭheva dhamme ime dasasahadhammikā vādānuvādā gārayhaṃ ṭhānaṃ āgacchanti. Yepi te, bhikkhave, ahesuṃ okkalā vassabhaññā <note>vayabhiññā (ka.) saṃ. ni. 3.62; a. ni. 4.30 passitabbaṃ</note> ahetuvādā akiriyavādā natthikavādā tepi mahācattārīsakaṃ dhammapariyāyaṃ na garahitabbaṃ <pb ed="M" n="3.0122"/> napaṭikkositabbaṃ amaññiṃsu <note>maññeyyuṃ (ka.)</note>. Taṃ kissa hetu? Nindābyārosaupārambhabhayā’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti.

<p rend="centre">Mahācattārīsakasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ sattamaṃ.

“Bhikkhus, if any recluse or brāhmaṇa thinks that this Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty (mahācattārīsakaṃ dhammapariyāyaṃ) should be censured and rejected, then there are ten legitimate deductions from his assertions that would provide grounds for censuring him here and now. If that worthy one censures right view (sammādiṭṭhiṃ), then he would honour and praise those recluses and brāhmaṇas who are of wrong view (micchādiṭṭhī). If that worthy one censures right intention (sammāsaṅkappaṃ), then he would honour and praise those recluses and brāhmaṇas who are of wrong intention (micchāsaṅkappā). If that worthy one censures right speech... If that worthy one censures right action... If that worthy one censures right livelihood... If that worthy one censures right effort... If that worthy one censures right mindfulness... If that worthy one censures right concentration... If that worthy one censures right knowledge... If that worthy one censures right liberation (sammāvimuttiṃ), then he would honour and praise those recluses and brāhmaṇas who are of wrong liberation (micchāvimuttī).

Bhikkhus, if any recluse or brāhmaṇa thinks that this Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty should be censured and rejected, then these are ten legitimate deductions from his assertions that would provide grounds for censuring him here and now.

Bhikkhus, even those Okkalas Vassabhaññas who were nihilists (ahetuvādā), inactive (akiriyavādā) and deniers (natthikavādā) did not think that this Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty should be censured and rejected. What is the reason for that? [It was] out of fear of blame, criticism, and reproach.”

This is what the Blessed One said. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Great Forty Discourse, the seventh, is finished.
