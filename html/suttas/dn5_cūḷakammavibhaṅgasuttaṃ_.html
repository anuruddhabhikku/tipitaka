<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Ko nu kho, bho gotama, hetu ko paccayo yena manussānaṃyeva sataṃ manussabhūtānaṃ dissanti hīnappaṇītatā? <note>bahvābādhā (syā. kaṃ. ka.)</note> <note>paññāvanto (sī. pī.)</note> <pb ed="P" n="3.0203"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Kammassakā <pb ed="M" n="3.0244"/>, māṇava, sattā kammadāyādā kammayonī kammabandhū <note>kammayoni kammabandhu (sī.)</note> kammappaṭisaraṇā. Kammaṃ satte vibhajati yadidaṃ – hīnappaṇītatāyāti. Na kho ahaṃ imassa bhoto gotamassa saṃkhittena bhāsitassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa vitthārena atthaṃ ājānāmi. Sādhu <pb ed="T" n="3.0377"/> me bhavaṃ gotamo tathā dhammaṃ desetu yathā ahaṃ imassa bhoto gotamassa saṃkhittena bhāsitassa vitthārena atthaṃ avibhattassa vitthārena atthaṃ ājāneyya’’nti.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Savatthi in Jeta’s Grove, Anathapindika’s Park.

Then the brahmin student Subha, Todeyya’s son, went to the Blessed One and exchanged greetings with him. When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

Seated at one side, the brahmin student Subha, Todeyya’s son, said to the Blessed One:

“What is the cause and condition, Master Gotama, why human beings are seen to be inferior and superior? For humans are seen to be short-lived and long-lived; sickly and healthy; ugly and beautiful; uninfluential and influential; poor and rich; low-born and high-born; stupid and wise. What is the cause and condition, Master Gotama, why human beings are seen to be inferior and superior?”

“Sentient beings are the owners of their actions (kammassakā), the heirs of their actions, action is their origin, action is their kinsman, action is their refuge. It is action (karma) that distinguishes beings as inferior and superior.”

“I do not understand in detail the meaning of Master Gotama’s statement, which he spoke in brief without expounding the meaning in detail. It would be good if Master Gotama would teach me the Dhamma so that I might understand in detail the meaning of Master Gotama’s statement.”

290. “Then, student (māṇava), listen and attend closely to what I shall say.”

“Yes, sir (bho),” the brahmin student Subha, son of Todeyya, replied to the Blessed One (bhagavat).

The Blessed One (bhagavat) said this:

“Here, student (māṇava), some man or woman is a killer of living beings (prāṇātipātī), is murderous, bloody-handed (lohitapāṇi), given to blows and violence (hatapahate niviṣṭo), merciless to living beings (pāṇabhūtesu). Because of that action (karma), so completed and undertaken, on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he reappears in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ) . But if on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he does not reappear in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), in hell (nirayaṃ), but instead comes back to the human state (manuṣyattvaṃ āgacchati), then wherever he is reborn he is short-lived (appāyuko hoti). This is the way (paṭipadā), student (māṇava), that leads to short life (alpāyuḥ-saṃvartanī), namely, one kills living beings (prāṇātipātī) and is murderous, bloody-handed (lohitapāṇi), given to blows and violence (hatapahate niviṣṭo), merciless to living beings (pāṇabhūtesu).

“But here, student (māṇava), some man or woman, abandoning the killing of living beings (prāṇātipātaṃ pahāya), abstains from killing living beings (prāṇātipātā paṭivirato); with rod (daṇḍa) and weapon (śastra) laid aside (nihita), gentle (lajjī) and kindly (dayāpanno), he abides compassionate (anukampī) to all living beings (sabba-pāṇabhūta-hita). Because of that action (karma), so completed and undertaken, on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he reappears in a happy destination (sugatiṃ), in the heavenly world (svargaṃ lokaṃ). But if on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he does not reappear in a happy destination (sugatiṃ), in the heavenly world (svargaṃ lokaṃ), but instead comes back to the human state (manuṣyattvaṃ āgacchati), then wherever he is reborn he is long-lived (dīghāyuko hoti). This is the way (paṭipadā), student (māṇava), that leads to long life (dīghāyuḥ-saṃvartanī), namely, abandoning the killing of living beings (prāṇātipātaṃ pahāya), he abstains from killing living beings (prāṇātipātā paṭivirato); with rod (daṇḍa) and weapon (śastra) laid aside (nihita), gentle (lajjī) and kindly (dayāpanno), he abides compassionate (anukampī) to all living beings (sabba-pāṇabhūta-hita)."

291. “Here, student, some woman or man is one who injures beings with the hand, with a clod, with a stick, or with a knife. Because of performing and undertaking such action, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, in perdition, even in hell. But if on the dissolution of the body, after death, he does not reappear in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, in perdition, even in hell, but instead comes back to the human state, then wherever he is reborn he is sickly (bahvābādho hoti). This is the way, student, that leads to sickliness, namely, one is given to injuring beings with the hand, with a clod, with a stick, or with a knife.

“But here, student, some woman or man is one who does not injure beings with the hand, with a clod, with a stick, or with a knife. Because of performing and undertaking such action, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in a happy destination, in the heavenly world (svarga), in the world (loka). But if on the dissolution of the body, after death, he does not reappear in a happy destination, in the heavenly world, in the world, but instead comes back to the human state, then wherever he is reborn he is healthy (appābādho hoti). This is the way, student, that leads to health, namely, one is not given to injuring beings with the hand, with a clod, with a stick, or with a knife.

This is the way, student, that leads to being beautiful, namely, one is not of an angry and irritable character…and does not display anger, hate, and bitterness.

293. “Here, student (māṇava), some man or woman is envious (issāmanako hoti); one who envies (issati), resents (upadussati), and begrudges (issaṃ bandhati) the gains (paralābha), honour (sakkāra), respect (garukāra), reverence (mānana), salutations (vandana), and veneration (pūjanāsu) received by others.

Because of performing and undertaking such action (so tena kammena evaṃ samattena evaṃ samādinnena), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he reappears in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ upapajjati).

But if on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he does not reappear in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ upapajjati), but if instead he comes back to the human state (sace manussattaṃ āgacchati), then wherever (yattha yattha) he is reborn (paccājāyati) he is uninfluential (appesakkho hoti).

This is the way (esā paṭipadā), student (māṇava), that leads to being uninfluential (appesakkhasaṃvattanikā), namely, one is envious (issāmanako hoti)…towards the gains (paralābha), honour (sakkāra), respect (garukāra), reverence (mānana), salutations (vandana), and veneration (pūjanāsu) received by others (issati upadussati issaṃ bandhati).

“But here (idha pana), student (māṇava), some man or woman is not envious (anissāmanako hoti); one who does not envy (na issati), resent (na upadussati), and begrudge (na issaṃ bandhati) the gains (paralābha), honour (sakkāra), respect (garukāra), reverence (mānana), salutations (vandana), and veneration (pūjanāsu) received by others.

Because of performing and undertaking such action (so tena kammena evaṃ samattena evaṃ samādinnena), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he reappears in a happy destination (sugatiṃ), in the heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ upapajjati).

But if on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he does not reappear in a happy destination (sugatiṃ), in the heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ upapajjati), but if instead he comes back to the human state (sace manussattaṃ āgacchati), then wherever (yattha yattha) he is reborn (paccājāyati) he is influential (mahesakkho hoti).

This is the way (esā paṭipadā), student (māṇava), that leads to being influential (mahesakkhasaṃvattanikā), namely, one is not envious (anissāmanako hoti)…towards the gains (paralābha), honour (sakkāra), respect (garukāra), reverence (mānana), salutations (vandana), and veneration (pūjanāsu) received by others (na issati na upadussati na issaṃ bandhati).

295. “Here, student (māṇava), some man or woman is obstinate (thaddha) and arrogant (atimānī); he does not pay homage (abhivādetabbaṃ na abhivādeti) to one who should receive homage, does not rise up (paccuṭṭhātabbaṃ na paccuṭṭheti) for one in whose presence he should rise up, does not offer a seat (āsanārahassa na āsanaṃ deti) to one who deserves a seat, does not make way (maggārahassa na maggaṃ deti) for one for whom he should make way, and does not honour (sakkātabbaṃ na sakkaroti), respect (garukātabbaṃ na garukaroti), revere (mānetabbaṃ na māneti), and venerate (pūjetabbaṃ na pūjeti) one who should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

Because of performing and undertaking such action, on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he reappears in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ upapajjati).

But if instead he comes back to the human state (manussattaṃ āgacchati), then wherever (yattha yattha) he is reborn (paccājāyati) he is low-born (nīcakulīno hoti).

This is the way, student (māṇava), that leads to low birth (nīcakulīnasaṃvattanikā), namely, one is obstinate (thaddha) and arrogant (atimānī)… and does not pay homage (abhivādetabbaṃ na abhivādeti), rise up (paccuṭṭhātabbaṃ na paccuṭṭheti), offer a seat (āsanārahassa na āsanaṃ deti), make way (maggārahassa na maggaṃ deti), honour (sakkātabbaṃ na sakkaroti), respect (garukātabbaṃ na garukaroti), revere (mānetabbaṃ na māneti), and venerate (pūjetabbaṃ na pūjeti) one who should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

“But here (idha), student (māṇava), some man or woman is not obstinate (atthaddha) and arrogant (anatimānī); he pays homage (abhivādetabbaṃ abhivādeti) to one who should receive homage, rises up (paccuṭṭhātabbaṃ paccuṭṭheti) for one in whose presence he should rise up, offers a seat (āsanārahassa āsanaṃ deti) to one who deserves a seat, makes way (maggārahassa maggaṃ deti) for one for whom he should make way, and honours (sakkātabbaṃ sakkaroti), respects (garukātabbaṃ garukaroti), reveres (mānetabbaṃ māneti), and venerates (pūjetabbaṃ pūjeti) one who should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

Because of performing and undertaking such action, on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), a good rebirth occurs (sugatiṃ upapajjati) in a heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ).

But if instead he comes back to the human state (manussattaṃ āgacchati), then wherever (yattha yattha) he is reborn (paccājāyati) he is high-born (uccākulīno hoti).

This is the way, student (māṇava), that leads to high birth (uccākulīnasaṃvattanikā), namely, one is not obstinate (atthaddha) and arrogant (anatimānī)…and pays homage (abhivādetabbaṃ abhivādeti), rises up (paccuṭṭhātabbaṃ paccuṭṭheti), offers a seat (āsanārahassa āsanaṃ deti), makes way (maggārahassa maggaṃ deti), honours (sakkātabbaṃ sakkaroti), respects (garukātabbaṃ garukaroti), reveres (mānetabbaṃ māneti), and venerates (pūjetabbaṃ pūjeti) one who should be honoured, respected, revered, and venerated.

<p n="297" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">297</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Iti kho, māṇava, appāyukasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā appāyukattaṃ upaneti, dīghāyukasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā dīghāyukattaṃ upaneti; bavhābādhasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā bavhābādhattaṃ upaneti, appābādhasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā appābādhattaṃ upaneti; dubbaṇṇasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā <pb ed="T" n="3.0385"/> dubbaṇṇattaṃ upaneti, pāsādikasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā pāsādikattaṃ upaneti; appesakkhasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā appesakkhattaṃ upaneti, mahesakkhasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā mahesakkhattaṃ upaneti; appabhogasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā appabhogattaṃ upaneti, mahābhogasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā mahābhogattaṃ upaneti; nīcakulīnasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā nīcakulīnattaṃ upaneti, uccākulīnasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā uccākulīnattaṃ upaneti; duppaññasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā duppaññattaṃ upaneti, mahāpaññasaṃvattanikā paṭipadā mahāpaññattaṃ upaneti. Kammassakā, māṇava, sattā kammadāyādā kammayonī kammabandhū kammappaṭisaraṇā. Kammaṃ satte vibhajati yadidaṃ – hīnappaṇītatāyā’’ti.

297.” Thus, youth, the practice that leads to short life leads to short life, the practice that leads to long life leads to long life; the practice that leads to much sickness leads to much sickness, the practice that leads to little sickness leads to little sickness; the practice that leads to ugliness leads to ugliness, the practice that leads to loveliness leads to loveliness; the practice that leads to little influence leads to little influence, the practice that leads to great influence leads to great influence; the practice that leads to little wealth leads to little wealth, the practice that leads to great wealth leads to great wealth; the practice that leads to birth in a low family leads to birth in a low family, the practice that leads to birth in a high family leads to birth in a high family; the practice that leads to little wisdom leads to little wisdom, the practice that leads to great wisdom leads to great wisdom.

Beings are the owners of their actions (kammassakā), youth, heirs of their actions (kammadāyādā); they originate from their actions (kammayonī), are bound to their actions (kammabandhū), have their actions as their refuge (kammappaṭisaraṇā).

It is action (kammaṃ) that distinguishes beings (satte) as inferior (hīnappaṇītatāyā) and superior.”

When this was said, Subha, the youth, Todeyya’s son, said this to the Blessed One: “Excellent, venerable Gotama, excellent, venerable Gotama! Just as, venerable Gotama, one might set upright what was overturned, or reveal what was hidden, or show the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark, so those with eyes might see forms, even so the Dhamma has been made clear in many ways by venerable Gotama.

I go to venerable Gotama for refuge, and to the Dharma (dhammañca), and to the monastic community (bhikkhusaṅghañca).

May venerable Gotama accept me as a lay follower (upāsakaṃ) who has gone for refuge, from today onwards, for as long as life lasts.”

The Cūḷakammavibhaṅga Sutta is finished, the fifth.
