55. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā vesāliyaṃ viharati mahāvane kūṭāgārasālāyaṃ. Tena kho pana samayena sambahulehi bhikkhūhi bhagavato santike aññā byākatā hoti – ‘‘‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā’’ti. Assosi kho sunakkhatto licchaviputto – ‘‘sambahulehi kira bhikkhūhi bhagavato santike aññā byākatā hoti – ‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā’’ti. Atha kho sunakkhatto licchaviputto yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho sunakkhatto licchaviputto bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘sutaṃ metaṃ, bhante – ‘sambahulehi kira bhikkhūhi bhagavato santike aññā byākatā – khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā’’ti. ‘‘Ye te, bhante, bhikkhū bhagavato santike aññaṃ byākaṃsu – ‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā’’ti, kacci te, bhante, bhikkhū sammadeva aññaṃ byākaṃsu udāhu santetthekacce bhikkhū adhimānena aññaṃ byākaṃsūti?
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Vesali in the Great Wood in the Hall with the Peaked Roof.

Now on that occasion a number of bhikkhus had declared final knowledge in the presence of the Blessed One thus: “We understand: Birth is destroyed (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā).”

Sunakkhatta, son of the Licchavis, heard: “A number of bhikkhus, it seems, have declared final knowledge in the presence of the Blessed One thus: ‘We understand: Birth is destroyed (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā).”

Then Sunakkhatta, son of the Licchavis, went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, he sat down at one side.

Seated to one side, Sunakkhatta, son of the Licchavis, said to the Blessed One: “I have heard, venerable sir, that a number of bhikkhus have declared final knowledge in the presence of the Blessed One, saying: ‘We understand: Birth is destroyed (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā).”

“Those bhikkhus, venerable sir, who in the presence of the Blessed One declared final knowledge, saying: ‘Birth is destroyed (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā)’—did those bhikkhus rightly declare final knowledge, venerable sir, or are there some bhikkhus here who declared final knowledge out of conceit (adhimānena)?”
55.Evaṃme sutanti sunakkhattasuttaṃ. Tattha aññāti arahattaṃ. Byākatāti khīṇā jātītiādīhi catūhi padehi kathitā. Adhimānenāti appatte pattasaññino, anadhigate adhigatasaññino hutvā adhigataṃ amhehīti mānena byākariṃsu.
55. Evaṃ me suta is the Sunakkhatta Sutta. There, aññā means arhattva (arahatship). Byākatā means spoken with four terms beginning with "destroyed is birth." Adhimānenā means, thinking what is unobtained to be obtained, and what is unachieved to be achieved, they declared out of pride, "We have achieved [it]."

56. “When those bhikkhus, Sunakkhatta, declared final knowledge in my presence, saying, ‘Destroyed is birth, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more for this state of being,’
There were some bhikkhus who declared final knowledge rightly, and there were some bhikkhus who declared final knowledge because they overestimated themselves.
Therein, Sunakkhatta, when bhikkhus declare final knowledge rightly, their declaration is true. But when bhikkhus declare final knowledge because they overestimate themselves, the Tathāgata thinks: ‘Let me teach them the Dhamma.’
Thus it is in this case, Sunakkhatta, that the Tathāgata thinks: ‘Let me teach them the Dhamma.’
But some misguided men here formulate a question, come to the Tathāgata, and ask it.
In that case, Sunakkhatta, though the Tathāgata has thought: ‘Let me teach them the Dhamma,’ he changes his mind.”
“This is the time, Blessed One (Bhagavā), this is the time, Sublime One (Sugata), for the Blessed One to teach the Dhamma.
Having heard it from the Blessed One, the bhikkhus will remember it.”
“Then listen, Sunakkhatta, and attend closely to what I shall say; I will speak.”
“Yes, venerable sir (bhante),” Sunakkhatta, son of the Licchavis, replied to the Blessed One.

Atha ca panidhekacce…pe… tassapi hoti aññathattanti bhagavā paṭipannakānaṃ dhammaṃ deseti. Yattha pana icchācāre ṭhitā ekacce moghapurisā honti, tatra bhagavā passati – ‘‘ime imaṃ pañhaṃ uggahetvā ajānitvāva jānantā viya appatte pattasaññino hutvā gāmanigamādīsu visevamānā vicarissanti, taṃ nesaṃ bhavissati dīgharattaṃ ahitāya dukkhāyā’’ti evamassāyaṃ icchācāre ṭhitānaṃ kāraṇā paṭipannakānampi atthāya ‘‘dhammaṃ desissāmī’’ti uppannassa cittassa aññathābhāvo hoti. Taṃ sandhāyetaṃ vuttaṃ.

Moreover, in this case, Sunakkhatta, the Tathāgata has this thought: Sunakkhatta, in this questioning of the monks, "This place is unmanifest, dark for them, so these are those who perceive themselves as having attained what they have not attained. Come, having purified and made manifest for them, I will teach the Dharma." Thus, the Tathāgata has this thought.

Furthermore, the Blessed One teaches the Dharma to those who are practicing, [saying] "But some here...pe... he also has a different state."

Wherever, moreover, some foolish men abide in arbitrary conduct, there the Blessed One sees: "Having taken up this question, without knowing, they wander about behaving as if they know in the villages and towns, experiencing as attained what is unattained. That will be for their harm and suffering for a long time." Thus, this alteration of mind occurs to him because of those abiding in arbitrary conduct, also for the sake of those who are practicing, thinking, "I will teach the Dharma."

This was said with reference to this.

"Sunakkhatta, there are these five cords of sensual pleasure (pañca kāmaguṇā). Which five? Forms cognizable by the eye (cakṣurvijñeyyā rūpā) that are wished for (iṣṭhā), desired (kāntā), agreeable (manāpā), and likeable (priyarūpā), connected with sensual desire (kāmūpasaṃhitā) and provocative of lust (rajanīyā); sounds cognizable by the ear (śotravijñeyyā śabdā)...odours cognizable by the nose (ghānavijñeyyā gandhā)...flavours cognizable by the tongue (jihvāvijñeyyā rasā)...tangibles cognizable by the body (kāyavijñeyyā spraṣṭavyā) that are wished for (iṣṭhā), desired (kāntā), agreeable (manāpā), and likeable (priyarūpā), connected with sensual desire (kāmūpasaṃhitā) and provocative of lust (rajanīyā). 58. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, sunakkhatta, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco purisapuggalo lokāmisādhimutto assa. Lokāmisādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṃ bhajati, tena ca vittiṃ āpajjati; āneñjapaṭisaṃyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṃ odahati, na aññā cittaṃ upaṭṭhāpeti upaṭṭhapeti (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.), na ca taṃ purisaṃ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṃ āpajjati. Seyyathāpi, sunakkhatta, puriso sakamhā gāmā vā nigamā vā ciravippavuttho assa. So aññataraṃ purisaṃ passeyya tamhā gāmā vā nigamā vā acirapakkantaṃ. So taṃ purisaṃ tassa gāmassa vā nigamassa vā khematañca subhikkhatañca appābādhatañca puccheyya; tassa so puriso tassa gāmassa vā nigamassa vā khematañca subhikkhatañca appābādhatañca saṃseyya. Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, sunakkhatta, api nu so puriso tassa purisassa sussūseyya, sotaṃ odaheyya, aññā cittaṃ upaṭṭhāpeyya, tañca purisaṃ bhajeyya, tena ca vittiṃ āpajjeyyā’’ti? ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Evameva kho, sunakkhatta, ṭhānametaṃ vijjati yaṃ idhekacco purisapuggalo lokāmisādhimutto assa. Lokāmisādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṃ bhajati, tena ca vittiṃ āpajjati; āneñjapaṭisaṃyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṃ odahati, na aññā cittaṃ upaṭṭhāpeti, na ca taṃ purisaṃ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṃ āpajjati. So evamassa veditabbo – ‘āneñjasaṃyojanena hi kho visaṃyutto āneñjasaṃyojanena hi kho visaṃyutto-iti pāṭho sī. syā. kaṃ. pī. potthakesu natthi, aṭṭhakathāsu pana tabbaṇṇanā dissatiyeva lokāmisādhimutto purisapuggalo’’’ti.
58. “It is possible, Sunakkhatta, that some person here may be intent on worldly material things ( lokāmisādhimutto).

When a person is intent on worldly material things ( lokāmisādhimuttassa), only talk concerning that interests him, and his thinking and pondering are in line with that, and he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him; when talk about the imperturbable is going on, he will not listen to it or give it ear or exert his mind to understand it, and he does not associate with that kind of person, and he does not find satisfaction through him.

“Suppose, Sunakkhatta, a man had left his own village or town a long time ago, and he were to see another man who had only recently left that village or town. He would ask that man whether the people of that village or town were safe, prosperous, and healthy, and that man would tell him whether the people of that village or town were safe, prosperous, and healthy.

What do you think, Sunakkhatta? Would that first man listen to him, give him ear, and exert his mind to understand, and associate with that man, and find satisfaction through him?” “Yes, sir.”

“So too, Sunakkhatta, it is possible that some person here may be intent on worldly material things ( lokāmisādhimutto).

When a person is intent on worldly material things ( lokāmisādhimuttassa), only talk concerning that interests him, and his thinking and pondering are in line with that, and he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him; when talk about the imperturbable is going on, he will not listen to it or give it ear or exert his mind to understand it, and he does not associate with that kind of person, and he does not find satisfaction through him.

He should be understood thus: ‘The person intent on worldly material things ( lokāmisādhimutto purisapuggalo) is detached from the bond of the imperturbable.’
58.Lokāmisādhimuttoti vaṭṭāmisa-kāmāmisa-lokāmisabhūtesu pañcasu kāmaguṇesu adhimutto tanninno taggaruko tappabbhāro. Tappatirūpīti kāmaguṇasabhāgā. Āneñjapaṭisaṃyuttāyāti āneñjasamāpattipaṭisaṃyuttāya. Saṃseyyāti katheyya. Āneñjasaṃyojanena hi kho visaṃyuttoti āneñjasamāpattisaṃyojanena visaṃsaṭṭho. Lokāmisādhimuttoti evarūpo hi lūkhacīvaradharo mattikāpattaṃ ādāya attano sadisehi katipayehi saddhiṃ paccantajanapadaṃ gacchati, gāmaṃ piṇḍāya paviṭṭhakāle manussā disvā ‘‘mahāpaṃsukulikā āgatā’’ti yāgubhattādīni sampādetvā sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ denti, bhattakicce niṭṭhite anumodanaṃ sutvā – ‘‘svepi, bhante, idheva piṇḍāya pavisathā’’ti vadanti. Alaṃ upāsakā, ajjāpi vo bahūnaṃ dinnanti. Tena hi, bhante, antovassaṃ idha vaseyyāthāti adhivāsetvā vihāramaggaṃ pucchitvā vihāraṃ gacchanti. Tattha senāsanaṃ gahetvā pattacīvaraṃ paṭisāmenti. Sāyaṃ eko āvāsiko te bhikkhū pucchati ‘‘kattha piṇḍāya caritthā’’ti? Asukagāmeti. Bhikkhāsampannāti? Āma evarūpā nāma manussānaṃ saddhā hoti. ‘‘Ajjeva nu kho ete edisā, niccampi edisā’’ti? Saddhā te manussā niccampi edisā, te nissāyeva ayaṃ vihāro vaḍḍhatīti. Tato te paṃsukulikā punappunaṃ tesaṃ vaṇṇaṃ kathenti , divasāvasesaṃ kathetvā rattimpi kathenti. Ettāvatā icchācāre ṭhitassa sīsaṃ nikkhantaṃ hoti udaraṃ phālitaṃ. Evaṃ lokāmisādhimutto veditabbo.
58. Lokāmisādhimutto means one who is intent on worldly material things, attached to the five sense pleasures ( kāmaguṇa), immersed in them, delighting in them, and given over to them.

Tappatirūpī means sharing the same nature as the sense pleasures (kāmaguṇa).

Āneñjapaṭisaṃyuttāyā means associated with the attainment of imperturbability (āneñjasamāpatti).

Saṃseyyā means he should say.

Āneñjasaṃyojanena hi kho visaṃyutto means detached from the fetter of the attainment of imperturbability (āneñjasamāpatti).

Lokāmisādhimutto means someone who is of this kind: clothed in rough robes (lūkhacīvaradhara), carrying a clay bowl (mattikāpattaṃ), and going with some others like himself to a border area (paccantajanapadaṃ). When they enter a village for alms (piṇḍāya), people see them and, thinking, "The great paṃsukulikas have arrived," they prepare rice gruel and other things (yāgubhattādīni) and respectfully give alms (dānaṃ denti). When the meal is finished, after hearing the thanksgiving (anumodanaṃ), they say, "Venerable sirs (bhante), may you enter here for alms again tomorrow (svepi)."

Enough, lay followers (upāsaka), even today alms have been given to many of you.

Then, having invited them with the words, "Venerable sirs (bhante), may you spend the rainy season (antovassaṃ) here," they ask the way to the monastery (vihāramaggaṃ) and go to the monastery (vihāraṃ).

There, having taken lodging (senāsanaṃ), they put away their bowl and robe (pattacīvaraṃ).

In the evening, one resident monk (āvāsiko) asks those monks (bhikkhū), "Where did you go for alms (piṇḍāya )?" "To such and such a village."

"Are you accomplished in alms-begging (bhikkhāsampannā)?" "Yes, such faith (saddhā) arises in people (manussānaṃ)."

"Are they like this only today, or are they always like this (niccampi)?" "The faith (saddhā) of those people (manussā) is always (niccampi) like this; it is because of them that this monastery (vihāro) thrives (vaḍḍhati)."

Then those paṃsukulikas repeatedly tell their praise (vaṇṇaṃ kathenti), having told it during the remainder of the day (divasāvasesaṃ kathetvā), they tell it even at night (rattimpi kathenti).

To such an extent, the head of one abiding in arbitrary conduct (icchācāre ṭhita) bursts off, and the belly is split open (udaraṃ phālitaṃ).

In this way, one should understand lokāmisādhimutto.

59. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, sunakkhatta, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco purisapuggalo āneñjādhimutto assa. Āneñjādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṃ bhajati, tena ca vittiṃ āpajjati; lokāmisapaṭisaṃyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṃ odahati, na aññā cittaṃ upaṭṭhāpeti, na ca taṃ purisaṃ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṃ āpajjati. Seyyathāpi, sunakkhatta, paṇḍupalāso bandhanā pavutto abhabbo haritattāya; evameva kho, sunakkhatta, āneñjādhimuttassa purisapuggalassa ye lokāmisasaṃyojane se pavutte. So evamassa veditabbo – ‘lokāmisasaṃyojanena hi kho visaṃyutto āneñjādhimutto purisapuggalo’’’ti.
59. Moreover, Sunakkhatta, it is possible that a certain person here is intent on the imperturbable.

When a person is intent on the imperturbable, only talk concerning that interests him, and his thinking and pondering are in line with that, and he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him. But when talk about worldly material things is going on, he will not listen to it or give it ear or exert his mind to understand it. He does not associate with that kind of person, and he does not find satisfaction through him.

“Just as a yellow leaf that has fallen from its stalk is incapable of becoming green again, so too, Sunakkhatta, when a person is intent on the imperturbable he has shed the fetter of worldly material things.

He should be understood as a person not bound by the fetter of worldly material things who is intent on the imperturbable.
59. Idāni āneñjasamāpattilābhiṃ adhimānikaṃ dassento ṭhānaṃ kho panetantiādimāha. Āneñjādhimuttassāti kilesasiñcanavirahitāsu heṭṭhimāsu chasu samāpattīsu adhimuttassa tanninnassa taggaruno tappabbhārassa. Se pavutteti taṃ pavuttaṃ. Cha samāpattilābhino hi adhimānikassa pañcakāmaguṇāmisabandhanā patitapaṇḍupalāso viya upaṭṭhāti. Tenetaṃ vuttaṃ.
Now, showing the conceited person who has gained access to the attainment of immovability, he says, "Indeed, this is the place," and so forth.

"'For one resolved on immovability' means for one resolved on the lower six attainments, which are free from the sprinkling of defilements, for one who inclines toward that, who holds that dear, who is devoted to that.

"'Because it is spoken' means it is spoken.

Indeed, for the conceited person who has obtained the six attainments, the bond of the allurement of the five sense pleasures stands like a fallen yellow leaf.

Therefore, it was said:

60. ‘‘Ṭhānaṃ kho panetaṃ, sunakkhatta, vijjati yaṃ idhekacco purisapuggalo ākiñcaññāyatanādhimutto assa. Ākiñcaññāyatanādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṃ bhajati, tena ca vittiṃ āpajjati ; āneñjapaṭisaṃyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṃ odahati, na aññā cittaṃ upaṭṭhāpeti , na ca taṃ purisaṃ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṃ āpajjati. Seyyathāpi, sunakkhatta, puthusilā dvedhābhinnā appaṭisandhikā hoti; evameva kho, sunakkhatta, ākiñcaññāyatanādhimuttassa purisapuggalassa ye āneñjasaṃyojane se bhinne. So evamassa veditabbo – ‘āneñjasaṃyojanena hi kho visaṃyutto ākiñcaññāyatanādhimutto purisapuggalo’’’ti.
“It is possible, Sunakkhatta, that some person here may be intent on the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanādhimutto).

When a person is intent on the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanādhimuttassa), only talk concerning that interests him, and his thinking and pondering are in line with that, and he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him. But when talk about the imperturbable is going on, he will not listen to it or give it ear or exert his mind to understand it. He does not associate with that kind of person, and he does not find satisfaction through him.

“Just as a thick stone that has split in two cannot be joined together again, so too, Sunakkhatta, when a person is intent on the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanādhimuttassa) his fetter of the imperturbable (āneñjasaṃyojane) has been split.

It is possible, Sunakkhatta, that some bhikkhu here might think thus: ‘Craving (taṇhā) has been called an arrow (salla) by the Recluse; the poisonous humour of ignorance (avijjā) is spread about by desire (chanda), lust (rāga), and ill will (byāpāda). That arrow of craving has been pulled out from me; the poisonous humour of ignorance has been expelled. I am one who is completely intent on Nibbāna.’ Being one who really is completely intent on Nibbāna, he would not pursue those things that are unsuitable (asappāya) for one completely intent on Nibbāna. He would not pursue the sight of unsuitable forms with the eye (cakkhunā rūpadassanaṃ), he would not pursue unsuitable sounds with the ear (sotena saddaṃ), unsuitable odours with the nose (ghānena gandhaṃ), unsuitable flavours with the tongue (jivhāya rasaṃ), unsuitable tangibles with the body (kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ), or unsuitable mind-objects with the mind (manasā dhammaṃ). Because he does not pursue the sight of unsuitable forms with the eye, unsuitable sounds with the ear, unsuitable odours with the nose, unsuitable flavours with the tongue, unsuitable tangibles with the body, or unsuitable mind-objects with the mind, lust (rāga) does not invade his mind (cittaṃ). Because his mind is not invaded by lust, he would not incur death or deadly suffering (maraṇamattaṃ dukkhaṃ).

Suppose, Sunakkhatta, a man were wounded by an arrow thickly smeared with poison. Then his friends and companions, his kinsmen and relatives, would bring a surgeon (sallakattaṃ). The surgeon would cut around the opening of the wound with a knife. After cutting around the opening of the wound with a knife, he would probe for the arrow with a probe (esanī). After probing for the arrow with a probe, he would pull out the arrow and would expel the poisonous humour without leaving a trace of it behind (anupādisesaṃ). Knowing that no trace was left behind, he would say: ‘Good man, the arrow has been pulled out from you; the poisonous humour has been expelled with no trace left behind. And it is incapable of harming you (analañca te antarāyāya). Eat only suitable food (sappāyāni bhojanāni); do not eat unsuitable food or else the wound (vaṇo) may suppurate. From time to time wash the wound and from time to time anoint its opening. so that pus and blood do not cover the opening of the wound. Do not walk around in the wind and sun or else dust and dirt may infect the opening of the wound. Take care of your wound, good man, and see to it that the wound heals.’ The man would think: ‘The arrow has been pulled out from me; the poisonous humour has been expelled with no trace left behind. And it is incapable of harming me.’ He would eat only suitable food. The wound would not suppurate if he eats only suitable food. From time to time he would wash the wound and from time to time he would anoint its opening. Pus and blood would not cover the opening of the wound because he washes the wound and anoints its opening from time to time. He would not walk around in the wind and sun. Dust and dirt would not infect the opening of the wound because he does not walk around in the wind and sun. He would take care of his wound and would see to it that the wound heals. Because of his care and proper treatment, and because the poisonous humour has been expelled without a trace, the wound would heal. Because it had healed and was covered with skin, he would not incur death or deadly suffering.

Just so, Sunakkhatta, it is possible that some bhikkhu here might think thus: ‘Craving has been called an arrow by the Recluse; the poisonous humour of ignorance is spread about by desire, lust, and ill will. That arrow of craving has been pulled out from me; the poisonous humour of ignorance has been expelled. I am one who is completely intent on Nibbāna.’
Being truly intent on Nirvāṇa (Nibbana), he would not pursue those things that are unsuitable (asappāya) for one truly intent on Nirvāṇa. He would not pursue the sight of unsuitable forms (rūpadassana) with the eye (cakkhunā), he would not pursue unsuitable sounds (sadda) with the ear (sotena), unsuitable odors (gandha) with the nose (ghānena), unsuitable flavors (rasa) with the tongue (jivhāya), unsuitable tangibles (phoṭṭhabba) with the body (kāyena), or unsuitable mind-objects (dhamma) with the mind (manasā).

Because he does not pursue the sight of unsuitable forms with the eye, unsuitable sounds with the ear, unsuitable odors with the nose, unsuitable flavors with the tongue, unsuitable tangibles with the body, or unsuitable mind-objects with the mind, lust (rāga) does not invade his mind (cittaṃ).

With his mind not invaded by lust, he would not incur death (maraṇaṃ) or deadly suffering (dukkhaṃ).

65. ‘‘Upamā kho me ayaṃ, sunakkhatta, katā atthassa viññāpanāya. 65. “Sunakkhatta, I have given this simile in order to convey a meaning.

This is the meaning here: ‘Wound’ vaṇo is a term for the six internal bases (ajjhattikānaṃ āyatanānaṃ). ‘Poisonous humour’ visadoso is a term for ignorance (avijjāya). ‘Arrow’ salla is a term for craving (taṇhā). ‘Probe’ esanī is a term for mindfulness (satiyā). ‘Knife’ satthanti is a term for noble wisdom (ariyā paññāya). Surgeon bhisakko sallakatto is a term for the Tathāgata, the Accomplished One (arahato), the Fully Enlightened One (sammāsambuddhassa).

“When, Sunakkhatta, a bhikkhu practises restraint (saṃvutakārī) in the six bases of contact (phassāyatanesu), and having understood that attachment (upadhi) is the root (mūla) of suffering (dukkha), is without attachment (nirupadhi), liberated (vimutto) by the destruction of attachment (upadhisaṅkhaye), it is not possible that he would direct (upasaṃharissati) his body (kāyaṃ) or arouse (uppādessati) his mind (cittaṃ) towards any object of attachment.

“Suppose, Sunakkhatta, there were a bronze cup of beverage (āpānīyakaṃso) possessing a good colour (vaṇṇasampanno), smell (gandhasampanno), and taste (rasasampanno), but it was mixed with poison (visena saṃsaṭṭho),

and a man came who wanted to live (jīvitukāmo), not to die (amaritukāmo), who wanted pleasure (sukhakāmo) and recoiled from pain (dukkhapaṭikūlo).

What do you think, Sunakkhatta, would that man drink (piveyya) from that bronze cup of beverage (āpānīyakaṃsaṃ) knowing: ‘If I drink (pivitvā) this, I will incur (nigacchāmi) death (maraṇaṃ) or deadly suffering (maraṇamattaṃ dukkha)’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“So too, Sunakkhatta, when a bhikkhu practises restraint (saṃvutakārī) in the six bases of contact (phassāyatanesu), and having understood that attachment (upadhi) is the root (mūla) of suffering (dukkha), is without attachment (nirupadhi), liberated (vimutto) by the destruction of attachment (upadhisaṅkhaye), it is not possible that he would direct (upasaṃharissati) his body (kāyaṃ) or arouse (uppādessati) his mind (cittaṃ) towards any object of attachment.

“Suppose, Sunakkhatta, there were a deadly poisonous snake (āsīviso ghoraviso),

and a man came who wanted to live (jīvitukāmo), not to die (amaritukāmo), who wanted pleasure (sukhakāmo) and recoiled from pain (dukkhapaṭikūlo).

What do you think, Sunakkhatta, would that man give that deadly poisonous snake (āsīvisassa ghoravisassa) his hand (hatthaṃ) or his thumb (aṅguṭṭhaṃ), knowing: ‘If I am bitten (daṭṭho) by him, I will incur (nigacchāmi) death (maraṇaṃ) or deadly suffering (maraṇamattaṃ dukkha)’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“So too, Sunakkhatta, when a bhikkhu practises restraint (saṃvutakārī) in the six bases of contact (phassāyatanesu), and having understood that attachment (upadhi) is the root (mūla) of suffering (dukkha), is without attachment (nirupadhi), liberated (vimutto) by the destruction of attachment (upadhisaṅkhaye), it is not possible that he would direct (upasaṃharissati) his body (kāyaṃ) or arouse (uppādessati) his mind (cittaṃ) towards any object of attachment.”

This is what the Blessed One said. Sunakkhatta, son of the Licchavis, was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words (bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti).

65. "It is a designation of mindfulness (satiyāyetaṃ adhivacanaṃ)," here, mindfulness is connected with wisdom (paññā). With mundane wisdom (lokikāya paññāya), it is mundane; with supramundane (lokuttarāya), it is supramundane. "It is a designation of noble wisdom (ariyāyetaṃ paññāya)," [meaning] with pure insight wisdom (parisuddhāya vipassanāpaññāya).

Now, showing the power of one with destroyed influxes (khīṇāsavassa), he said, "So vatā," etc. There, "saṃvutakārī" means one who does what is concealed (pihitakārī). "Having understood thus, without basis (iti viditvā nirupadhī)," having known thus, due to the abandonment of the defilement-basis (kilesupadhi), he is without basis (nirupadhi), meaning without grasping (nirupādānoti attho). "Freed in the destruction of the basis (upadhisaṅkhaye vimutto)," freed in nibbāna, which is the destruction of the basis (upadhīnaṃ saṅkhayabhūte nibbāne), from the perspective of an object (ārammaṇato). "In the basis (upadhismiṃ)," [meaning] in the basis of sense desire (kāmupadhismiṃ). "He will draw the body near (kāyaṃ upasaṃharissatī)," he will cause the body to adhere (kāyaṃ allīyāpessati).

This is what was said: the one with destroyed influxes (khīṇāsavo), freed from the perspective of an object (ārammaṇato) in nibbāna, which is the destruction of craving (taṇhakkhaye nibbāne), [whether] to enjoy the five sense pleasures (pañca kāmaguṇe sevituṃ), or he will draw the body near (kāyaṃ upasaṃharissati), or he will generate the mind (cittaṃ vā uppādessatīti), this is not a possibility (netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati). The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti).

So vatā saṃvutakārī iti viditvā nirupadhī upadhisaṅkhaye vimutto upadhismiṃ kāyaṃ upasaṃharissatī
