390. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvatthī in the Eastern Park in Migāramātā’s mansion.

Then Sakka, ruler of gods, went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, stood to one side. Standing to one side, Sakka, ruler of gods, said to the Blessed One: “In how short a time, venerable sir, is a bhikkhu liberated by the destruction of craving, one who has reached the ultimate end, the ultimate security from bondage, the ultimate holy life, the ultimate goal, one who is foremost among gods and humans?”

“Here, ruler of gods, a bhikkhu has heard that nothing is worth adhering to. Thus, ruler of gods, a bhikkhu has heard that nothing is worth adhering to. He directly knows everything; having directly known everything, he fully understands everything; having fully understood everything, whatever feeling (vedanā) he feels, whether pleasant (sukhaṃ) or painful (dukkhaṃ) or neither-painful-nor-pleasant (adukkhamasukhaṃ), he abides contemplating impermanence (anicānupassī) in those feelings, contemplating fading away (virāgānupassī), contemplating cessation (nirodhānupassī), contemplating relinquishment (paṭinissaggānupassī). Contemplating thus, he does not cling to anything in the world. When he does not cling, he is not agitated. Being unagitated, he personally attains nibbana – ‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the spiritual life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā)’ he understands. Briefly, it is in this way, ruler of gods, that a bhikkhu is liberated by the destruction of craving…one who is foremost among gods and humans.”

Then Sakka, ruler of gods, delighting and rejoicing in the Blessed One’s words, paid homage to the Blessed One, and keeping him on his right, he vanished at once.

390. Thus I have heard: the Cūḷataṇhāsaṅkhayasuttaṃ.

There, Pubbārāme Migāramātupāsāde means in the monastery known as Pūrvārāma, in the palace of Migāramātā.

There, this sequential account [begins]: In the distant past, hundreds of thousands of eons ago, a female lay follower invited the Blessed One Padumuttara and, having given a hundred thousand donations to the Saṃgha of monks with the Buddha at its head, knelt at the feet of the Blessed One, and made the aspiration, “In the future, may I become the foremost attendant of a Buddha like you."

Having transmigrated among gods and humans for hundreds of thousands of eons, at the time of our Blessed One, she took rebirth in Bhaddiyanagara, in the womb of Sumanadevī, the wife of the wealthy merchant Dhanañjaya, son of Meṇḍaka.

At the time of her birth, they gave her the name Visākhā.

When the Blessed One arrived in Bhaddiyanagara, she went to greet the Blessed One with five hundred girls, and at her first sight, she became a stream-enterer (sotāpanna).

Later, she went to the house of Puṇṇavaḍḍhanakumāra, the son of the wealthy merchant Migāra, in Śrāvastī. There, Migāra the merchant placed her in the position of a mother, therefore she is called Migāramātā.

When she went to her husband's family, her father had a Mahālatāpiḷandhana made.

In that Piḷandhana, four diamond pipes were used, eleven pipes of pearls, twenty-two pipes of corals, thirty-three pipes of jewels, thus it reached completion with these and other seven-colored jewels.

When it was placed on her head, it reached down to the surface of her feet; only a woman possessing the strength of five elephants could bear it.

Later, having become the chief attendant of the Ten-Powered One, she spent that adornment, and while having a monastery built for the Blessed One with nine koṭis, she had a palace built on an area equal to a karīsa.

In the upper story of that, there were five hundred chambers; on the lower story, there were five, so it was adorned with a thousand chambers.

Thinking, "A mere palace does not look good," she surrounded it with five hundred two-peaked houses, five hundred smaller palaces, and five hundred long halls.

The monastery festival reached completion in four months.

Mahālatāpiḷandhana

In the state of being a woman, there was no other in the Buddha’s Dispensation like Visākhā who gave donations of wealth, and in the state of being a man, there was no other in the Buddha’s Dispensation like Anāthapiṇḍika who gave donations of wealth.

For he, having spent fifty-four koṭis, had a monastery built called Jetavanamahāvihāra in the southern part of Śrāvastī, in a place similar to the Mahāvihāra of Anurādhapura.

Visākhā had a monastery built called Pūrvārāma in the eastern part of Śrāvastī, in a place similar to the Uttamadevī Monastery.

The Blessed One, out of compassion for these two families, dwelling near Śrāvastī, spent the rainy season in these two monasteries.

One rainy season he dwells in Jetavana, one in Pūrvārāma. In this time, the Blessed One dwells in Pūrvārāma.

Therefore it was said - "In Pūrvārāma, in the palace of Migāramātā."

Pūrvārāma

"How much, O Bhante?" means by how much, O Bhante.

"In brief, he is liberated by the cessation of craving," means by making that cessation of craving, Nirvāṇa, the object [of his meditation], by the liberation of his mind, how much is he liberated by the cessation of craving in brief? With whatever practice he is liberated by the cessation of craving, please teach me that preliminary practice of a monk who has destroyed the āsavas in brief," he asks.

"Accantaniṭṭho" means because it has gone beyond the end defined by decay and destruction, it is accantā.

Because accantā is the niṭṭhā, it is Accantaniṭṭho, Ekantaniṭṭho, Satataniṭṭho, is the meaning.

"Accantaṃ yogakkhemīti" means "Accantayogakkhemī," "niccayogakkhemīti," is the meaning.

"Accantaṃ brahmacārīti" means "Accantabrahmacārī," "niccabrahmacārīti," is the meaning.

"Accantaṃ pariyosānamassāti" means "Accantapariyosāno" by the same method as before.

"Seṭṭho devamanussāna" means "Seṭṭho uttamo" of gods and humans.

"How much is a monk of such a form, quickly tell me about his practice in brief," he asks the Blessed One.

Why does he hasten thus? Because of his desire to experience play.
"Surely, venerable sir, is one succinctly liberated through the destruction of craving," utterly complete, utterly complete, utterly secure from bondage, utterly practicing the holy life, utterly perfect.

The best of gods and humans, this one, having ordered a pleasure garden and having had the four great kings take charge of protection in the four directions, surrounded by a host of deities in the two celestial realms, together with two and a half million troupes of actors, mounted Erāvaṇa, ascended to the garden gate, stood there, and reflected on this question: "By how much is the practice leading to the prior part of the path attainable in summary by one liberated through the destruction of craving, whose āsavas are destroyed?"

Then this occurred to him: "This question is exceedingly splendid. If, without first grasping this question, I enter the pleasure garden, overwhelmed by objects through the six doorways, I will not again reflect on this question.

Let the pleasure garden remain for now. Having gone to the presence of the Teacher, having asked this question, and having understood the question, I will enjoy myself in the pleasure garden." Immediately, having vanished from the elephant's shoulder, he appeared in the presence of the Blessed One.

Those four great kings, too, having taken charge of protection, stood right there in their respective places. The retinues of serving deities, the troupes of actors, Erāvaṇa, and the Nāga king all stood right there at the garden gate. Thus, eager to experience the pleasure, hurrying, he spoke thus:

All phenomena are not worth adhering to," here, all phenomena, namely, are the five aggregates (pañcakkhandhā), the twelve sources (dvādasāyatanāni), and the eighteen elements (aṭṭhārasa dhātuyo).

All of those, too, are not adequate, not sufficient, not capable, not fit for adhering to due to not remaining in the manner they are grasped. Why? Because they do not persist in the way they are grasped.

Those phenomena, even grasped as permanent (nicca), become impermanent (anicca); even grasped as pleasure (sukha), become suffering (dukkha); even grasped as self (attā), become not-self (anattā). Therefore, they are not worth adhering to.

"He understands completely (abhijānātī)," means he understands completely through the knowledge of discernment that they are impermanent (aniccaṃ), suffering (dukkhaṃ), and not-self (anattā).

"He comprehends completely (parijānātī)," means he comprehends completely through the determining comprehension in just that way.

"Whatever feeling (yaṃkiñci vedana)," means whatever feeling, even the slightest, that one experiences, even connected with the five types of sensory consciousness.

By this, the Blessed One, having manifested it through the feeling-aspect of Śakka, the lord of the gods, shows the non-apprehension of form.

If, however, the feeling-contemplation had not been spoken of below, it would be appropriate to speak of it in this place.

However, it was spoken of below; therefore, it should be understood in just the manner spoken of in the mindfulness foundations.

"Observing impermanence (aniccānupassī)," here, impermanence (aniccaṃ) should be understood, the observation of impermanence (aniccānupassanā) should be understood, and the one observing impermanence (aniccānupassī) should be understood.

There, "impermanence (aniccaṃ)" refers to the five aggregates (pañcakkhandhā). They are impermanent due to having the characteristic of arising and passing away (uppādavayaṭṭhena).

"The observation of impermanence (aniccānupassanā)" is the knowledge of seeing the aggregates as decaying and vanishing.

"The one observing impermanence (aniccānupassī)" is the person who is endowed with that knowledge.

Therefore, "he dwells observing impermanence (aniccānupassī viharati)," means he dwells observing impermanence from [the perspective of] impermanence. This is the meaning here.

All phenomena are not worth adhering to.

He understands completely. He comprehends completely. Whatever feeling. He observes impermanence. Impermanence. Observation of impermanence. He observes impermanence.

"Observing dispassion (virāgānupassī)," here, there are two kinds of dispassion: the dispassion of destruction (khayavirāgo) and the ultimate dispassion (accantavirāgo).

There, the observation of the passing away and vanishing of conditioned phenomena (saṅkhārānaṃ), and the knowledge of the path seeing nirvāṇa (nibbānaṃ), the ultimate dispassion, from dispassion, is "observing dispassion (virāgānupassanā)."

A person complete with both of those is called one who observes dispassion (virāgānupassī). With reference to that, it was said, "observing dispassion (virāgānupassī)," meaning he observes dispassion from [the perspective of] dispassion.

The same method applies to "observing cessation (nirodhānupassī)," for cessation (nirodho), too, is just twofold: cessation by destruction (khayanirodho) and ultimate cessation (accantanirodho).

"Observing relinquishment (paṭinissaggānupassī)," here, relinquishment (paṭinissaggo) is said to be abandonment (vossaggo), and that is twofold: abandonment by renunciation (pariccāgavossaggo) and abandonment by ejection (pakkhandanavossaggoti).

There, "abandonment by renunciation (pariccāgavossaggo)" is insight (vipassanā), for that abandons both the defilements (kilese) and the aggregates (khandhe) by way of their respective aspects.

"Abandonment by ejection (pakkhandanavossaggo)" is the path (maggo), for that ejects toward nirvāṇa (nibbānaṃ) as its object (ārammaṇaṃ), from [the perspective of] its object.

Abandonment is indeed [accomplished] by those two causes: by abandoning the aggregates and defilements through the force of complete destruction, and by ejecting toward nirvāṇa.

Therefore, "he relinquishes both the defilements and the aggregates," [this] is abandonment by renunciation (pariccāgavossaggo). "In cessation (nirodhe), consciousness (cittaṃ) ejects toward the element of nirvāṇa (nibbānadhātuyā)," [this] is abandonment by ejection (pakkhandanavossaggoti). Both of these come together in the path (magge).

A person complete with both of those, due to being endowed with this observation of relinquishment (paṭinissaggānupassanāya), is called one who observes relinquishment (paṭinissaggānupassī).

With reference to that, it was said, "observing relinquishment (paṭinissaggānupassī)."

"He clings to nothing in the world (na kiñci loke upādiyatī)," means he does not cling to, grasp, or touch anything at all pertaining to conditioned phenomena (saṅkhāragataṃ) through the force of craving (taṇhāvasena).
Anupādiyaṃ na paritassatīti, because he does not grasp, he is not disturbed by the disturbance of craving.

Paccattaññeva parinibbāyatīti, he himself attains Nirvāṇa by the Nirvāṇa of defilements.

Khīṇā jātītiādinā panassa paccavekkhaṇāva dassitā.

Eso nu te, mārisa, so bhagavā satthāti, mārisa, tvaṃ kuhiṃ gatosīti vutte mayhaṃ satthu santikanti vadesi, imasmiṃ devaloke ekapādakena viya tiṭṭhasi, yaṃ tvaṃ evaṃ vadesi, eso nu te, mārisa, so bhagavā satthāti pucchiṃsu. Sabrahmacārī me esoti ettha kiñcāpi thero anagāriyo abhinīhārasampanno aggasāvako, sakko agāriyo, maggabrahmacariyavasena panete sabrahmacārino honti, tasmā evamāha. Ahonūna te so bhagavā satthāti sabrahmacārī tāva te evaṃmahiddhiko, so pana te bhagavā satthā aho nūna mahiddhikoti satthu iddhipāṭihāriyadassane jātābhilāpā hutvā evamāhaṃsu.
"Here I am, friend," because now, since the Elder, having generated joy and agitation, dispelled the darkness, therefore, reflecting, he spoke thus.

"Is this, friend, your Lord, the Teacher?" When it was said, "Friend, where have you gone?" he replied, "I dwell near my Teacher," in this divine world you stand as if on one foot. Because you speak thus, they asked, "Is this, friend, your Lord, the Teacher?"

"This is my fellow practitioner." Here, although the Elder is homeless, endowed with aspiration, the foremost disciple, Śakka is a householder, yet by way of the path of ব্রহ্মচर्या, these are fellow practitioners, therefore he spoke thus.

"Alas! Surely your Lord is the Teacher!" Since a fellow practitioner of yours is so mighty, and your Lord is the Teacher, alas, surely he is mighty," having become of manifested speech at the sight of the Teacher's magical display, they spoke thus.

Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā mahāmoggallāno bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘abhijānāti no, bhante, bhagavā ahu ahunaññeva (sī. syā. kaṃ.) ñātaññatarassa mahesakkhassa yakkhassa saṃkhittena taṇhāsaṅkhayavimuttiṃ bhāsitā’’ti abhāsitthāti (ka.)? ‘‘Abhijānāmahaṃ, moggallāna, idha sakko devānamindo yenāhaṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā maṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho, moggallāna, sakko devānamindo maṃ etadavoca – ‘kittāvatā nu kho, bhante , bhikkhu saṃkhittena taṇhāsaṅkhayavimutto hoti accantaniṭṭho accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussāna’’nti. Evaṃ vutte ahaṃ, moggallāna, sakkaṃ devānamindaṃ etadavocaṃ ‘‘idha devānaminda bhikkhuno sutaṃ hoti ‘sabbe dhammā nālaṃ abhinivesāyā’ti. Evaṃ cetaṃ devānaminda bhikkhuno sutaṃ hoti ‘sabbe dhammā nālaṃ abhinivesāyā’ti. So sabbaṃ dhammaṃ abhijānāti, sabbaṃ dhammaṃ abhiññāya sabbaṃ dhammaṃ parijānāti , sabbaṃ dhammaṃ pariññāya yaṃ kiñci vedanaṃ vedeti sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā. So tāsu vedanāsu aniccānupassī viharati, virāgānupassī viharati, nirodhānupassī viharati, paṭinissaggānupassī viharati. So tāsu vedanāsu aniccānupassī viharanto, virāgānupassī viharanto, nirodhānupassī viharanto, paṭinissaggānupassī viharanto na kiñci loke upādiyati, anupādiyaṃ na paritassati, aparitassaṃ paccattaññeva parinibbāyati – ‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ , kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāti. Ettāvatā kho, devānaminda, bhikkhu saṃkhittena taṇhāsaṅkhayavimutto hoti accantaniṭṭho accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānanti. Evaṃ kho ahaṃ, moggallāna, abhijānāmi sakkassa devānamindassa saṃkhittena taṇhāsaṅkhayavimuttiṃ bhāsitā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā mahāmoggallāno bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. 395. Then the venerable Mahāmoggallāna approached the Lord; having approached, having greeted the Lord, he sat down at a respectful distance. Seated at a respectful distance, the venerable Mahāmoggallāna said to the Lord: “Venerable sir, does the Blessed One recall stating in brief - to a certain one of the renowned spirits with a great following - deliverance through the destruction of craving?” “I do recall doing so, Moggallana. Here Sakka, ruler of gods, came to me, and after paying homage to me, he stood at one side. As he was standing there, Sakka, ruler of gods, said to me: ‘In what way, venerable sir, is a bhikkhu liberated by the destruction of craving in brief, one who has reached the ultimate end, the ultimate security from bondage, the ultimate holy life, the ultimate goal, one who is foremost among gods and humans?’”

When this was said, I told Sakka, ruler of gods: ‘Here, ruler of gods, a bhikkhu has heard that nothing is worth adhering to. When a bhikkhu has heard that nothing is worth adhering to, he directly knows everything; having directly known everything, he fully understands everything; having fully understood everything, whatever feeling he feels, whether pleasant or painful or neitherpainful- nor-pleasant. he abides contemplating impermanence in those feelings, contemplating fading away, contemplating cessation, contemplating relinquishment. Contemplating thus, he does not cling to anything in the world. When he does not cling, he is not agitated. When he is not agitated, he personally attains Nibbana. He understands: “Birth is destroyed, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more coming to any state of being.” Briefly, it is in this way, ruler of gods, that a bhikkhu is liberated by the destruction of craving, one who has reached the ultimate end, the ultimate security from bondage, the ultimate holy life, the ultimate goal, one who is foremost among gods and humans.’ That is how I recall stating in brief to Sakka, ruler of gods, deliverance through the destruction of craving.”

Thus spoke the Lord. The venerable Maha Moggallana was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Cūḷataṇhāsaṅkhayasuttaṃ, the seventh, is finished.

"ñātaññatarassā" means "paññātaññatarassa," because Sakka is one of the known ones.
