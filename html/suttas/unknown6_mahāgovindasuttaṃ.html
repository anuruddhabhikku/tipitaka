Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Rājagaha, on the Vulture Peak (Gijjhakūṭa) mountain.

Then, late in the night, Pañcasikha (Pañcasikho), son of a Gandharva, illuminating the entire Vulture Peak (Gijjhakūṭa) mountain with surpassing radiance, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and stood to one side.

Standing to one side, Pañcasikha, the Gandharva's son, said this to the Blessed One: "Venerable Sir (bhante), I will announce to the Blessed One what I have heard and directly received in the presence of the gods of the Thirty-three (tāvatiṃsānaṃ)."

"Tell me then, Pañcasikha," said the Blessed One.

293. Thus have I heard: the Mahāgovindasutta.

Here, there is a word-by-word explanation of terms that are not obvious: *pañcasikho* means one with five crests (*pañcacūḷo*), one with five earrings (*pañcakuṇḍaliko*). It is said that in the path of humans, while doing meritorious deeds, when he was young, as a boy with five crests, he was the eldest of the cowherds. Taking other boys, outside the village, at the crossroads, constructing halls, digging ponds, building bridges, leveling uneven roads, and removing trees that obstructed the axles of vehicles, he wandered about doing such meritorious deeds and passed away while still young. That existence of his was desired, lovely, and pleasing. After passing away, he was reborn in the heaven of the Four Great Kings, having taken a lifespan of ninety hundred thousand years. His form was three *gāvuta*s in size, like a golden mountain peak. Adorning himself with ornaments worth a thousand cartloads, anointing himself with nine pots of perfume, wearing divine red clothes, wearing red golden earrings, he wanders about with an entourage like that of a five-crested boy, with five earrings circling around his back. Therefore, they recognize him as "Pañcasikha."

*abhikkantāya rattiyā*: *abhikkantāya khīṇāya rattiyā*, meaning, after one portion of the night has passed. *abhikkantavaṇṇo*: of exceedingly desired, lovely, pleasing color. By nature, he was of lovely color, but because he came adorned, he was of exceedingly lovely color. *kevalakappaṃ*: without remainder, completely. Here, the word *kevala* has the meaning of "without remainder." Like in *kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ*. The word *kappa* has the meaning of "completely," as in *kevalakappaṃ jetavanaṃtiādīsu*. *obhāsetvā*: having pervaded with radiance (*ābhāya pharitvā*), like the moon or the sun, having made it all one glow, all one illumination.

*Vaiśravaṇa*, "*atha pacchā amhākaṃ āsanaṃ hotī*" means after them, we get the opportunity to sit down. After that, we are unable to enter or see. The cause of assembly here has been previously stated as only fourfold. Among them, the rain-retreat collection has been expanded. Just as for the rain-retreat collection, so too for the great *Pravāraṇā*, having assembled on the full-moon day, they contemplate, "Where shall we go today, near whom shall we end the retreat?". There, Śakra, the lord of the gods, mostly ends the retreat in the Piyaṅgudīpa great monastery itself. The remaining deities, taking divine flowers and divine sandalwood powders etc. from the Pāricchattaka tree, go to their own agreeable places and end the retreat. Thus, they assemble for the sake of the *Pravāraṇā* collection.

"*atha pacchā amhākaṃ āsanaṃ hotī*"

320. “I understand “Spuming possessive thoughts” as follows.

This means that one renounces one’s possessions, small or great, leaves one’s relatives, few or many, and, shaving off hair and beard, puts on the yellow robe, and goes forth from the household life into homelessness. This is how I understand “Spuming possessive thoughts”.

I understand “Alone, intent” as follows.

That means that one goes off on one’s own and chooses a lodging in the forest (*araññaṃ*), at the foot of a tree (*rukkhamūlaṃ*), in a mountain glen (*pabbataṃ kandaraṃ*), in a rocky cave (*giriguhaṃ*), a charnel-ground (*susānaṃ*), in the jungle (*vanapatthaṃ*) or on a heap of grass in the open (*abbhokāsaṃ palālapuñjaṃ*). This is how I understand “Alone, intent”.

I understand “Compassion-filled” as follows.

That means that one dwells suffusing one quarter with a mind filled with compassion (*karuṇāsahagatena cetasā*), then a second, then a third and a fourth quarter.

Appesakkhā ca appalābhā cā: "Monastics are those of little expectation and little gain," for indeed, from the time of ordination, they despise possessions and, by repeatedly afflicting the ordained one, they declare them base and helpless.

Appalābhā ca: And little gain, even wandering in every village, what is suitable to be eaten is difficult to obtain.

Idaṃ pana brahmaññaṃ: But this is Brahman-hood.

Mahesakkhañca mahāyasattā, mahālābhañca lābhasakkārasampannattā: And those great beings of great power and great gain are accomplished in gain, honor, and reverence.

Bhavañhi etarahi sakalajambudīpe aggapurohito sabbattha aggāsanaṃ aggodakaṃ aggabhattaṃ aggagandhaṃ aggamālaṃ labhatīti: "For you now, in all of Jambudvīpa, are the foremost priest, everywhere obtaining the foremost seat, the foremost water, the foremost food, the foremost perfume, and the foremost garland."

Rājāva rañña: "Like a king among kings," for I am now among ordinary kings like a *cakravartin* king.

Brahmāva brahmāna: "Like Brahmā among Brahmans," for among ordinary Brahmans, I am like Mahābrahmā.

Devatāva gahapatikāna: "Like a god among householders," for among the remaining householders, I am like Śakra, the king of the gods.

Rājāva rañña brahmāva brahmāna devatāva gahapatikāna: Like a king among kings, like Brahmā among Brahmans, like a god among householders.

Bhariyānaṃ āmantanāvaṇṇanā: Description of the invitation to the wives.
