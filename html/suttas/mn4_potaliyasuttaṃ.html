31. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the country of the Aṅguttarāpas at a town of theirs named Āpaṇa, a market town of the Aṅguttarāpas.

Then, in the morning, the Blessed One dressed, and taking his bowl and robe, went into Āpaṇa for alms.

When he had wandered for alms in Āpaṇa and had returned from his almsround, after his meal he went to a certain grove (vanasaṇḍa) for the day’s abiding.

Having entered that grove (vanasaṇḍa), he sat down at the foot of a tree for the day's abiding.

Potaliya the householder, while walking and wandering for exercise, wearing full dress with parasol and sandals, also went to the grove (vanasaṇḍa), and having entered the grove, he went to the Blessed One and exchanged greetings with him.

After an exchange of friendly greetings & courtesies, he stood to one side.

The Blessed One said to Potaliya the householder as he stood to one side: “There are seats, householder (gahapati), sit down if you like.”

When this was said, the householder Potaliya thought: “The recluse Gotama addresses me as ‘householder (gahapati)’,” and angry and displeased, he remained silent.

A second time the Blessed One…pe… A third time the Blessed One said to Potaliya the householder: “There are seats, householder (gahapati), sit down if you like.”

When this was said, the householder Potaliya thought: “The recluse Gotama addresses me as ‘householder (gahapati)’,” and angry and displeased, he said to the Blessed One: “Master Gotama, it is neither fitting nor proper that you address me as ‘householder (gahapati)’.”

“Householder (gahapati), you have the aspects, marks (liṅga), and signs of a householder (gahapati).”

“Nevertheless, Master Gotama, I have given up all my works and cut off all my affairs.”

“But how, householder (gahapati), have you given up all your works and cut off all your affairs?” “Here, Master Gotama, whatever wealth or grain or silver or gold I had, all that I have bequeathed to my sons, and there I live without admonishing, without reproving, devoted to food and clothing.

That is how I have given up all my works and cut off all my affairs.”

“Householder (gahapati), the cutting off of affairs as you describe it is one thing, but in the Noble One’s Discipline the cutting off of affairs is different.”

“But how, venerable sir (bhante), does the cutting off of affairs occur in the Noble One’s Discipline? It would be good, venerable sir (bhante), if the Blessed One would teach me the Dhamma in such a way that the cutting off of affairs occurs in the Noble One’s Discipline.”

“Then listen, householder (gahapati), and attend closely to what I shall say.”

“Yes, venerable sir (bhante),” Potaliya the householder replied.

Tatthāyaṃ āvibhāvakathā – ayaṃ kira jambudīpo dasasahassayojanaparimāṇo. Tattha ca catusahassayojanappamāṇo padeso udakena ajjhotthaṭo samuddoti saṅkhaṃ gato . Tisahassayojanappamāṇe manussā vasanti. Tisahassayojanappamāṇe himavā patiṭṭhito ubbedhena pañcayojanasatiko caturāsītikūṭasahassapaṭimaṇḍito samantato sandamānapañcasatanadīvicitto, yattha āyāmavitthārena ceva gambhīratāya ca paṇṇāsapaṇṇāsayojanā diyaḍḍhayojanasataparimaṇḍalā anotattadaho kaṇṇamuṇḍadaho rathakāradaho chaddantadaho kuṇāladaho mandākinīdaho sīhapapātadahoti satta mahāsarā patiṭṭhitā. Tesu anotattadaho sudassanakūṭaṃ citrakūṭaṃ kāḷakūṭaṃ gandhamādanakūṭaṃ kelāsakūṭanti imehi pañcahi pabbatehi parikkhitto. Tattha sudassanakūṭaṃ sovaṇṇamayaṃ dviyojanasatubbedhaṃ antovaṅkaṃ kākamukhasaṇṭhānaṃ tameva saraṃ paṭicchādetvā ṭhitaṃ. Citrakūṭaṃ sabbaratanamayaṃ. Kāḷakūṭaṃ añjanamayaṃ. Gandhamādanakūṭaṃ sānumayaṃ abbhantare muggavaṇṇaṃ, mūlagandho sāragandho pheggugandho tacagandho papaṭikagandho rasagandho pattagandho pupphagandho phalagandho gandhagandhoti imehi dasahi gandhehi ussannaṃ nānappakāraosadhasañchannaṃ, kāḷapakkhauposathadivase ādittamiva aṅgāraṃ jalantaṃ tiṭṭhati. Kelāsakūṭaṃ rajatamayaṃ. Sabbāni sudassanena samānubbedhasaṇṭhānāni, tameva saraṃ paṭicchādetvā ṭhitāni. Tāni sabbāni devānubhāvena nāgānubhāvena ca vassanti, nadiyo ca tesu sandanti. Taṃ sabbampi udakaṃ anotattameva pavisati. Candimasūriyā dakkhiṇena vā uttarena vā gacchantā pabbatantarena tattha obhāsaṃ karonti, ujuṃ gacchantā na karonti, tenevassa anotattanti saṅkhā udapādi. Tattha manoharasilātalāni nimmacchakacchapāni phalikasadisanimmaludakāni nhānatitthāni supaṭiyattāni honti, yesu buddhapaccekabuddhakhīṇāsavā ca iddhimanto ca isayo nhāyanti, devayakkhādayo uyyānakīḷakaṃ kīḷanti. Tassa catūsu passesu sīhamukhaṃ hatthimukhaṃ assamukhaṃ usabhamukhanti cattāri mukhāni honti, yehi catasso nadiyo sandanti. Sīhamukhena nikkhantanadītīre sīhā bahutarā honti. Hatthimukhādīhi hatthiassausabhā. Puratthimadisato nikkhantanadī anotattaṃ tikkhattuṃ padakkhiṇaṃ katvā itarā tisso nadiyo anupagamma pācīnahimavanteneva amanussapathaṃ gantvā mahāsamuddaṃ pavisati. Pacchimadisato ca uttaradisato ca nikkhantanadiyopi tatheva padakkhiṇaṃ katvā pacchimahimavanteneva uttarahimavanteneva ca amanussapathaṃ gantvā mahāsamuddaṃ pavisanti. Dakkhiṇadisato nikkhantanadī pana taṃ tikkhattuṃ padakkhiṇaṃ katvā dakkhiṇena ujukaṃ pāsāṇapiṭṭheneva saṭṭhiyojanāni gantvā pabbataṃ paharitvā vuṭṭhāya parikkhepena tigāvutappamāṇā udakadhārā ca hutvā ākāsena saṭṭhiyojanāni gantvā tiyaggaḷe nāma pāsāṇe patitā, pāsāṇo udakadhārāvegena bhinno. Tattha paññāsayojanappamāṇā tiyaggaḷā nāma pokkharaṇī jātā, pokkharaṇiyā kūlaṃ bhinditvā pāsāṇaṃ pavisitvā saṭṭhiyojanāni gatā. Tato ghanapathaviṃ bhinditvā umaṅgena saṭṭhiyojanāni gantvā viñjhuṃ nāma tiracchānapabbataṃ paharitvā hatthatale pañcaṅgulisadisā pañcadhārā hutvā pavattanti. Sā tikkhattuṃ anotattaṃ padakkhiṇaṃ katvā gataṭṭhāne āvaṭṭagaṅgāti vuccati. Ujukaṃ pāsāṇapiṭṭhena saṭṭhiyojanāni gataṭṭhāne kaṇhagaṅgāti, ākāsena saṭṭhiyojanāni gataṭṭhāne ākāsagaṅgāti, tiyaggaḷapāsāṇe paññāsayojanokāse ṭhitā tiyaggaḷapokkharaṇīti, kūlaṃ bhinditvā pāsāṇaṃ pavisitvā saṭṭhiyojanāni gataṭṭhāne bahalagaṅgāti, umaṅgena saṭṭhiyojanāni gataṭṭhāne umaṅgagaṅgāti vuccati. Viñjhuṃ nāma tiracchānapabbataṃ paharitvā pañcadhārā hutvā pavattaṭṭhāne pana gaṅgā yamunā aciravatī sarabhū mahīti pañcadhā saṅkhaṃ gatā. Evametā pañca mahānadiyo himavantato pabhavanti. Thus have I heard. This is the Potaliyasutta.

Here, aṅguttarāpesū means that district is indeed Aṅga. But because the waters that are to the north of that land are not far away, it is called Uttarāpotipi.

Which land has waters to the north? The great land.

Here is the story of its manifestation: This Jambudvīpa is approximately ten thousand yojanas.

There, approximately four thousand yojanas of the region are covered with water, which has the designation "ocean."

Humans live in approximately three thousand yojanas.

The Himavā is established in approximately three thousand yojanas, and is a hundred leagues in height; it is adorned with eighty-four thousand peaks; it is characterized all around by a continuous flow of five hundred rivers. There, with a length and width, and also with a depth of fifty by fifty yojanas, and one and a half hundred yojanas in circumference, are the Anotatta lake, the Kaṇṇamuṇḍa lake, the Rathakāra lake, the Chaddanta lake, the Kuṇāla lake, the Mandākinī lake, and the Sīhapapāta lake, which are the seven great lakes that are established.

Among those, the Anotatta lake is surrounded by these five mountains: the Sudassana peak, the Citrakūṭa peak, the Kāḷakūṭa peak, the Gandhamādana peak, and the Kelāsa peak.

There, the Sudassana peak is made of gold, two hundred yojanas in height, with a concave interior, with a crow-mouth shape, and stands covering that very lake.

The Citrakūṭa peak is made of all gems.

The Kāḷakūṭa peak is made of añjana.

The Gandhamādana peak is made of summits, with the color of mudga beans on the interior. From these ten smells—root smell, core smell, pheggu smell, bark smell, papaṭika smell, sap smell, leaf smell, flower smell, fruit smell, and fragrance smell—it is overgrown and covered over with various kinds of medicinal herbs, and on the day of the black-fortnight uposatha, it stands like a burning coal that is blazing.

The Kelāsa peak is made of silver.

All are of equal height and shape to the Sudassana peak, and stand covering that very lake.

All of those are raining down by the power of the gods and by the power of the nāgas, and rivers flow down in those.

That entire water enters only the Anotatta.

The moon and sun, going either to the south or to the north, make light there through the mountain interior; they do not make [light] going straight, and so its name, Anotatta, arose.

There, the delightful stone surfaces, free from flies and turtles, bathing places with pure water like crystal, are well-prepared; there, Buddhas, Pratyekabuddhas, Khīṇāsavas, powerful ones, and ṛṣis bathe, and gods, yakṣas, and others play garden sports.

On its four sides are the lion-mouth, elephant-mouth, horse-mouth, and bull-mouth, these are the four mouths from which the four rivers flow.

On the river bank issuing from the lion-mouth, there are many lions.

Elephants, horses, and bulls [are on the banks issuing] from the elephant-mouth and the others.

The river issuing from the eastern direction, having circumambulated the Anotatta three times, without going to the other three rivers, having gone to the path of non-humans by means of the eastern Himavant, enters the great ocean.

The rivers issuing from the western direction and from the northern direction also, having circumambulated in that way and having gone to the path of non-humans by means of the western Himavant and by means of the northern Himavant, enter the great ocean.

But the river issuing from the southern direction, having circumambulated that three times, and going directly south by means of a stone surface, having gone sixty yojanas, having struck the mountain, rising up and becoming a stream of water approximately three gāvutas in circumference, and having gone sixty yojanas through the sky, fell upon a stone named Tiyaggaḷa; the stone was broken by the force of the water stream.

There, a pond named Tiyaggaḷā, approximately fifty yojanas in extent, arose; having broken the bank of the pond, having entered the stone, it went sixty yojanas.

From there, having broken the solid earth, having gone sixty yojanas by means of a tunnel, having struck the mountain named Viñjhuṃ, five streams similar to fingers in the palm of the hand, flow out.

That, having circumambulated the Anotatta three times, in the place it went, is called Āvaṭṭagaṅgā.

In the place where it went sixty yojanas directly by means of a stone surface, [it is called] Kaṇhagaṅgā; in the place where it went sixty yojanas through the sky, [it is called] Ākāsagaṅgā; the Tiyaggaḷapokkharaṇī, standing in a fifty-yojana space on the Tiyaggaḷa stone; having broken the bank, having entered the stone, in the place where it went sixty yojanas, [it is called] Bahalagaṅgā; in the place where it went sixty yojanas by means of a tunnel, [it is called] Umaṅgagaṅgā.

But in the place where, having struck the mountain named Viñjhuṃ, having become five streams, it flowed out, it has the designation fivefold, as the Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Aciravatī, Sarabhū, and Mahī.

Thus, these five great rivers originate from the Himavant.
Among them, that which is called the fifth earth (pañcamī mahī) is what is intended here as the great earth (mahāmahī).

To the north of that are the waters (āpo), and not far from them is that district (janapada), which should be known as Aṅguttarāpa.

In that district of Aṅguttarāpa.

Thus, because of the thriving of the shops (āpaṇānaṃ), it went to the designation of shop (āpaṇaṃ).

Even a branch breaking (sākhābhaṅgampi) is called a seat (āsananteva).

gahapativādenāti: gahapatīti iminā vacanena, meaning by this word, householder (gahapati).

samudācaratīti: voharati, meaning he speaks.

nappatirūpanti: na sāruppaṃ, meaning not conforming.

ākārātiādīni sabbāneva kāraṇavevacanāni: ākārātiādīni are all words for cause.

dīghadasavatthadhāraṇa-kesamassunakhaṭhapanādīni hi sabbāneva gihibyañjanāni tassa gihibhāvaṃ pākaṭaṃ karontīti ākārā, gihisaṇṭhānena saṇṭhitattā liṅgā, gihibhāvassa sañjānananimittatāya nimittāti vuttā: Because the wearing of long-fringed robes, the keeping of hair, beards, and nails, and so on, are all characteristics of a householder, they make his state as a householder apparent; therefore, they are called form (ākārā), sign (liṅgā) because they are arranged in the manner of a householder, and cause (nimittā) because they are the cause of the arising of the state of being a householder.

bhagavantaṃ etadavocā nacchanna nappatirūpa ākārā ākārā, liṅgā, nimittā yathā taṃ gahapatissā niyyātanti niyyātitaṃ.

‘‘tumhe na kasatha, na vapatha, na vaṇippathaṃ payojetha, kathaṃ jīvissatha, puttadāraṃ vā bharissathā’’tiādinā nayena pana upavadanto upavādī nāma hoti: But he who censures, saying, "You do not cultivate, you do not sow, you do not engage in trade, how will you live, or support your children and wife?" is called a censurer (upavādī).

ahaṃ pana ubhayampi taṃ na karomi: But I do not do either of those things.

tenāhaṃ tattha anovādī anupavādīti dasseti: Therefore, he shows that he is neither an advisor nor a censurer there.

ghāsacchādanaparamo viharāmīti: ghāsamattañceva acchādanamattañca paramaṃ katvā viharāmi, tato paraṃ natthi, na ca patthemīti dīpeti: He lives with only food and clothing as the highest goal, and he reveals that there is nothing beyond that, nor does he desire anything.

The Blessed One said this: “Householder, there are these eight things in the Noble One’s Discipline that lead to the cutting off of affairs.

Which eight? With the support of the non-killing of living beings, the killing of living beings is to be abandoned. With the support of taking only what is given, the taking of what is not given is to be abandoned. With the support of truthful speech, false speech is to be abandoned. With the support of unmalicious speech, malicious speech is to be abandoned. With the support of refraining from rapacious greed, rapacious greed is to be abandoned. With the support of refraining from spiteful scolding, spiteful scolding is to be abandoned. With the support of refraining from anger and despair, anger and despair are to be abandoned. With the support of refraining from excessive pride, excessive pride is to be abandoned. These are the eight things, stated in brief without being expounded in detail, that lead to the cutting off of affairs in the Noble One’s Discipline.”

“Venerable sir, it would be good if the Blessed One would expound in detail these eight things that you have stated in brief without being expounded in detail, which lead to the cutting off of affairs in the Noble One’s Discipline, out of compassion.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” Potaliya the householder replied.

(ma. ni. 2.457) – Ayaṃ byavahāravohāro nāma. ‘‘Saṅkhā samaññā paññatti vohāro’’ti (dha. sa. 1313-1315) ayaṃ paṇṇattivohāro nāma. ‘‘Tathā tathā voharati aparāmasa’’nti (ma. ni. 3.332) ayaṃ vacanavohāro nāma. ‘‘Aṭṭha ariyavohārā aṭṭha anariyavoharā’’ti (a. ni. 8.67) ayaṃ cetanāvohāro nāma, ayamidhādhippeto. Nindāroso means irritation is blaming. Vohārasamucchedāyā means here vohāro means conduct, also designation, speech, and thought.

There –

"He who lives dependent on conduct among humans;

Thus, Vāseṭṭha, know that a merchant is not a brāhmaṇa."

(Majjhima Nikāya 2.457) –

This is called practical conduct.

“Calculation (saṃkhā), designation (samaññā), description (paññatti), conduct (vohāro)” (Dhammasaṃgani 1313-1315) This is called descriptive conduct.

“Thus, thus he speaks without adhering to that expression” (Majjhima Nikāya 3.332) This is called verbal conduct.

“Eight noble conducts (ariyavohārā) and eight ignoble conducts (anariyavoharā)” (Anguttara Nikāya 8.67) This is called intentional conduct, this is what is intended here.

Since from the time of going forth, the thought, "I am a householder ( gihī)" does not occur, but the thought "I am a renunciate (śramaṇa)" occurs;

The statement, "I am a householder" does not occur, but the statement "I am a renunciate" occurs;

The designation "I am a householder" does not occur, but the designation "I am a renunciate" occurs;

The practice "I am a householder" does not occur, but the practice "I am a renunciate" or "I am a pravrajita" occurs;

Therefore, all these are obtainable.

33. ‘‘‘Apāṇātipātaṃ nissāya pāṇātipāto pahātabbo’ti iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ ? Idha, gahapati, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘yesaṃ kho ahaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ hetu pāṇātipātī assaṃ, tesāhaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ pahānāya samucchedāya paṭipanno. Ahañceva ahañce (?) kho pana pāṇātipātī assaṃ, attāpi maṃ upavadeyya pāṇātipātapaccayā, anuviccāpi maṃ viññū anuvicca viññū (sī. syā. pī.) garaheyyuṃ pāṇātipātapaccayā, kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā duggati pāṭikaṅkhā pāṇātipātapaccayā. Ye ca pāṇātipātapaccayā uppajjeyyuṃ āsavā vighātapariḷāhā, pāṇātipātā paṭiviratassa evaṃsa te āsavā vighātapariḷāhā na honti’. ‘Apāṇātipātaṃ nissāya pāṇātipāto pahātabbo’ti – iti yantaṃ vuttaṃ idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ.
33. “‘With the support of non-killing (apāṇātipātaṃ), the killing of living beings (pāṇātipāto) is to be abandoned (pahātabbo)’—so it was said. And with reference to what was this said? Here, householder (gahapati), a noble disciple (ariyasāvako) considers thus: ‘I am practicing the way to the abandoning and cutting off of those fetters (saṃyojanānaṃ) because of which I might kill living beings (pāṇātipātī). If I were to kill living beings (pāṇātipātī), I would blame myself (attāpi) for doing so; the wise (viññū), having investigated, would censure me for doing so; and on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), an unhappy destination (duggati) would be expected because of killing living beings (pāṇātipātapaccayā). But this killing of living beings (pāṇātipāto) is itself a fetter (saṃyojanaṃ) and a hindrance (nīvaraṇaṃ). And while taints (āsavā), vexation (vighāta), and fever (pariḷāhā) might arise through the killing of living beings (pāṇātipātapaccayā), there are no taints (āsavā), vexation (vighāta), and fever (pariḷāhā) in one who abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭiviratassa).’ ‘With the support of non-killing (apāṇātipātaṃ), the killing of living beings (pāṇātipāto) is to be abandoned (pahātabbo)’—so it was said. So it is with reference to this that it was said.
33.Yesaṃ kho ahaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ hetu pāṇātipātīti ettha pāṇātipātova saṃyojanaṃ. Pāṇātipātasseva hi hetu pāṇātipātapaccayā pāṇātipātī nāma hoti. Pāṇātipātānaṃ pana bahutāya ‘‘yesaṃ kho aha’’nti vuttaṃ. Tesāhaṃsaṃyojanānanti tesaṃ ahaṃ pāṇātipātabandhanānaṃ. Pahānāya samucchedāya paṭipannoti iminā apāṇātipātasaṅkhātena kāyikasīlasaṃvarena pahānatthāya samucchedanatthāya paṭipanno. Attāpi maṃ upavadeyyāti kunthakipillikampi nāma jīvitā avoropanakasāsane pabbajitvā pāṇātipātamattatopi oramituṃ na sakkomi, kiṃ mayhaṃ pabbajjāyāti evaṃ attāpi maṃ upavadeyya. Anuviccāpi maṃ viññū garaheyyunti evarūpe nāma sāsane pabbajitvā pāṇātipātamattatopi oramituṃ na sakkoti, kiṃ etassa pabbajjāyāti evaṃ anuvicca tulayitvā pariyogāhetvā aññepi viññū paṇḍitā garaheyyuṃ. Etadeva kho pana saṃyojanametaṃ nīvaraṇanti dasasu saṃyojanesu pañcasu ca nīvaraṇesu apariyāpannampi ‘‘aṭṭha nīvaraṇā’’ti desanāvasenetaṃ vuttaṃ. Vaṭṭabandhanaṭṭhena hi hitapaṭicchādanaṭṭhena ca saṃyojanantipi nīvaraṇantipi vuttaṃ. Āsavāti pāṇātipātakāraṇā eko avijjāsavo uppajjati. Vighātapariḷāhāti vighātā ca pariḷāhā ca. Tattha vighātaggahaṇena kilesadukkhañca vipākadukkhañca gahitaṃ, pariḷāhaggahaṇenapi kilesapariḷāho ca vipākapariḷāho ca gahito. Iminā upāyena sabbattha attho veditabbo.
33. "Because of what saṃyojanas [fetters] am I a killer of living beings (pāṇātipātī)?" Here, killing of living beings is itself a saṃyojana.

Indeed, it is because of the cause of killing living beings, due to the condition of killing living beings, that one is called a killer of living beings.

But it is said, "Because of what saṃyojanas am I?" with reference to the abundance of killing living beings.

"Of those saṃyojanas" means of those bonds of killing living beings.

"Committed to abandoning and destroying" means committed to abandoning and destroying through this restraint of bodily morality (kāyikasīlasaṃvara) known as abstaining from killing living beings (apāṇātipāta).

"Even myself would reproach me," [thinking,] "Even someone as insignificant as a kunthakipillika [ant] cannot, after going forth in a teaching that prevents the taking of life, refrain even from the smallest act of killing living beings. What is the use of my going forth?" Thus, even myself would reproach me.

"Even the wise would despise me," [thinking,] "Having gone forth in such a teaching, he cannot refrain even from the smallest act of killing living beings. What is the use of his going forth?" Thus, having investigated, weighed, and examined, other wise and learned people would despise him.

"This very thing is a saṃyojana, this is a nīvaraṇa [hindrance]"—although not included among the ten saṃyojanas and the five nīvaraṇas, this was said in the context of the teaching, "Eight nīvaraṇas."

Indeed, it is said to be both a saṃyojana and a nīvaraṇa by way of binding together and by way of concealing what is beneficial.

"Āsavas [influxes]"—because of the cause of killing living beings, one avijjāsava [influx of ignorance] arises.

"Vighāta [affliction] and pariḷāha [torment]" means affliction and torment.

There, by the term "vighāta," both the suffering of defilements (kilesadukkha) and the suffering of consequences (vipākadukkha) are included; and by the term "pariḷāha," both the torment of defilements (kilesapariḷāha) and the torment of consequences (vipākapariḷāha) are included.

The meaning should be understood everywhere by this method.

42. In the detailed explanation, the connection of the phrase "skilled in that" (tamenaṃ dakkho), with the word "should approach" (upasumbheyyā) should be understood in this way.

This means: one should approach that dog, one should throw [food] near it; this is the meaning.

"A skeleton" (aṭṭhikaṅkala) means a breast bone or a backbone or a head bone.

Because it is without flesh, it is called a skeleton.

"Well-scraped," "scraped" (sunikkantaṃ nikkantaṃ): Just as it is well-scraped, so it is scraped and made smooth, and whatever clinging flesh there is, all of that is scraped away, only the bones remain; this is the meaning.

Therefore, he said, "without flesh" (nimmaṃsa).

But blood having been smeared remains. Therefore, it is said, "smeared with blood" (lohitamakkhita).

"With much suffering, with much distress" (bahudukkhā bahupāyāsā) means with much suffering due to suffering in this very life and in the life to come, with much distress due to distress and defilements.

"Whatever equanimity is diverse, dependent on diversity" (yāyaṃ upekkhā nānattā nānattasitā) means whatever equanimity is of diverse natures through the force of the five sense-qualities (pañcakāmaguṇa), and because those objects are dependent, it is called "dependent on diversity" (nānattasitā). Therefore, one should abandon equanimity with the five sense-qualities (pañcakāmaguṇa).

"Unified, dependent on unity" (ekattā ekattasitā) is the equanimity of the fourth jhāna; for that is of one nature, since it arises in a single object even for a day, and because it depends on that single object, it is called "dependent on unity" (ekattasitā).

"Where all grasping at worldly bait ceases without remainder" (yattha sabbaso lokāmisūpādānā aparisesā nirujjhantī) means wherever, in the equanimity of the fourth jhāna, that equanimity is reached, and because of that, all the objects designated as worldly bait, the sense-qualities (pañcakāmaguṇa) as bait, cease without remainder.

"The sense-qualities as bait" (pañcakāmaguṇāmisā) are desire and passion for objects of the sense-qualities, and they themselves are also called grasping due to their characteristic of seizing.

"He cultivates that same equanimity" (tamevūpekkhaṃ bhāvetī) means he develops only the equanimity of the fourth jhāna, which is the opposite of the grasping at those worldly baits.

With much suffering, with much distress, whatever equanimity is diverse, dependent on diversity, unified, dependent on unity, where all grasping at worldly bait ceases without remainder, the sense-qualities as bait, he cultivates that same equanimity.

Evameva kho, gahapati, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘supinakūpamā kāmā vuttā bhagavatā bahudukkhā bahupāyāsā, ādīnavo ettha bhiyyo’ti…pe… tamevūpekkhaṃ bhāveti.
“Householder, suppose a man might see in a dream lovely parks (ārāma), lovely groves (vana), lovely meadows (bhūmi), and lovely lakes (pokkharaṇī). But upon awakening, he would perceive nothing. So too, householder, a noble disciple reflects thus: ‘Sensual pleasures (kāma) have been compared to dreams; they are said by the Blessed One to be the source of much suffering (dukkha) and much despair (upāyāsa), while the danger (ādīnava) in them is great.’ …and so on… He cultivates equanimity (upekkhā) regarding them.

47. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, gahapati, puriso yācitakaṃ bhogaṃ yācitvā yānaṃ vā yānaṃ (syā. kaṃ. pī.) poriseyyaṃ poroseyyaṃ (sī. pī. ka.), oropeyya (syā. kaṃ.) pavaramaṇikuṇḍalaṃ. So tehi yācitakehi bhogehi purakkhato parivuto antarāpaṇaṃ paṭipajjeyya. Tamenaṃ jano disvā evaṃ vadeyya – ‘bhogī vata, bho, puriso, evaṃ kira bhogino bhogāni bhuñjantī’ti. Tamenaṃ sāmikā yattha yattheva passeyyuṃ tattha tattheva sāni hareyyuṃ. Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, gahapati, alaṃ nu kho tassa purisassa aññathattāyā’’ti? ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Taṃ kissa hetu’’? ‘‘Sāmino hi, bhante, sāni harantī’’ti. ‘‘Evameva kho, gahapati, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘yācitakūpamā kāmā vuttā bhagavatā bahudukkhā bahupāyāsā, ādīnavo ettha bhiyyo’ti…pe… tamevūpekkhaṃ bhāveti.
“Householder, suppose a man, having borrowed goods on loan, obtained a vehicle or excellent jeweled earrings. Preceded and surrounded by those borrowed goods, he would go to the marketplace. Seeing him, people would say, ‘Sirs, that is a rich man! That is how the rich enjoy their wealth!’ Then the owners, whenever and wherever they saw him, would take back their things. What do you think, householder? Would that be enough to make that man unhappy?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Why is that?”

“Because, venerable sir, the owners would be taking back their things.”

“In the same way, householder, a noble disciple reflects thus: ‘Sensual pleasures have been described by the Blessed One as like borrowed goods, as bringing much suffering and much despair, and as having great danger in them.’ … He develops equanimity with regard to that.

48. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, gahapati, gāmassa vā nigamassa vā avidūre tibbo vanasaṇḍo. Tatrassa rukkho sampannaphalo ca upapannaphalo uppannaphalo (syā.) ca, na cassu kānici phalāni bhūmiyaṃ patitāni. Atha puriso āgaccheyya phalatthiko phalagavesī phalapariyesanaṃ caramāno. So taṃ vanasaṇḍaṃ ajjhogāhetvā taṃ rukkhaṃ passeyya sampannaphalañca upapannaphalañca. Tassa evamassa – ‘ayaṃ kho rukkho sampannaphalo ca upapannaphalo ca, natthi ca kānici phalāni bhūmiyaṃ patitāni. Jānāmi kho panāhaṃ rukkhaṃ ārohituṃ āruhituṃ (sī.). Yaṃnūnāhaṃ imaṃ rukkhaṃ ārohitvā yāvadatthañca khādeyyaṃ ucchaṅgañca pūreyya’nti. So taṃ rukkhaṃ ārohitvā yāvadatthañca khādeyya ucchaṅgañca pūreyya. Atha dutiyo puriso āgaccheyya phalatthiko phalagavesī phalapariyesanaṃ caramāno tiṇhaṃ kuṭhāriṃ kudhāriṃ (syā. kaṃ. ka.) ādāya. So taṃ vanasaṇḍaṃ ajjhogāhetvā taṃ rukkhaṃ passeyya sampannaphalañca upapannaphalañca. Tassa evamassa – ‘ayaṃ kho rukkho sampannaphalo ca upapannaphalo ca, natthi ca kānici phalāni bhūmiyaṃ patitāni. Na kho panāhaṃ jānāmi rukkhaṃ ārohituṃ. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ imaṃ rukkhaṃ mūlato chetvā yāvadatthañca khādeyyaṃ ucchaṅgañca pūreyya’nti. So taṃ rukkhaṃ mūlatova chindeyya. Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, gahapati, amuko asu (sī. pī.) yo so puriso paṭhamaṃ rukkhaṃ ārūḷho sace so na khippameva oroheyya tassa so rukkho papatanto hatthaṃ vā bhañjeyya pādaṃ vā bhañjeyya aññataraṃ vā aññataraṃ vā aṅgapaccaṅgaṃ bhañjeyya, so tatonidānaṃ maraṇaṃ vā nigaccheyya maraṇamattaṃ vā dukkha’’nti? ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Evameva kho, gahapati, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘rukkhaphalūpamā kāmā vuttā bhagavatā bahudukkhā bahupāyāsā, ādīnavo ettha bhiyyo’ti. Evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya disvā yāyaṃ upekkhā nānattā nānattasitā taṃ abhinivajjetvā yāyaṃ upekkhā ekattā ekattasitā yattha sabbaso lokāmisūpādānā aparisesā nirujjhanti tamevūpekkhaṃ bhāveti.
48. “Householder, suppose there were a dense grove not far from some village or town.

Within it, there was a tree laden with fruit (sampannaphalo ca upapannaphalo), and none of its fruits had fallen to the ground.

Then a man came, needing fruit (phalatthiko), seeking fruit (phalagavesī), wandering in search of fruit (phalapariyesanaṃ caramāno).

Having entered the grove, he saw the tree laden with fruit (sampannaphalañca upapannaphalañca).

He thought, ‘This tree is laden with fruit (sampannaphalo ca upapannaphalo), and none of its fruits have fallen to the ground.

I know how to climb a tree.’

So, he climbed the tree, ate as much as he wanted, and filled his bag.

Then, a second man came, needing fruit (phalatthiko), seeking fruit (phalagavesī), wandering in search of fruit (phalapariyesanaṃ caramāno), and taking a sharp axe (tiṇhaṃ kuṭhāriṃ).

Having entered the grove, he saw the tree laden with fruit (sampannaphalañca upapannaphalañca).

He thought, ‘This tree is laden with fruit (sampannaphalo ca upapannaphalo), and none of its fruits have fallen to the ground.

I do not know how to climb a tree.

So, let me cut this tree down at its root, eat as much fruit as I want, and fill my bag.’

And he cut that tree down at its root.

What do you think, householder? If that first man who had climbed the tree didn’t come down quickly, when the tree falls, wouldn’t he break his hand or his foot or some other part of his body, so that he might incur death or deadly suffering?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“So too, householder, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘Sensual pleasures have been compared to a fruit tree (rukkhaphalūpamā kāmā) by the Blessed One; they provide much suffering (bahudukkhā) and much despair (bahupāyāsā), while the danger (ādīnavo) in them is great.’

Having seen this thus as it actually is with proper wisdom, he avoids the equanimity that is diversified, based on diversity (yāyaṃ upekkhā nānattā nānattasitā taṃ abhinivajjetvā), and develops the equanimity that is unified, based on unity (yāyaṃ upekkhā ekattā ekattasitā), where clinging to the material things of the world utterly ceases without remainder (yattha sabbaso lokāmisūpādānā aparisesā nirujjhanti tamevūpekkhaṃ bhāveti).
48.Sampannaphalanti madhuraphalaṃ. Upapannaphalanti phalūpapannaṃ bahuphalaṃ.

upapannaphalanti: fruit endowed with qualities (phalūpapannaṃ), abundant fruit (bahuphalaṃ).

49. ‘‘Sa kho so, gahapati, ariyasāvako imaṃyeva anuttaraṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ āgamma anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati, seyyathidaṃ – ekampi jātiṃ dvepi jātiyo…pe… iti sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati. ‘‘Sa kho so, gahapati, ariyasāvako imaṃyeva anuttaraṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ āgamma dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passati cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe sugate duggate…pe… yathākammūpage satte pajānāti. ‘‘Sa kho so, gahapati, ariyasāvako imaṃyeva anuttaraṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ āgamma āsavānaṃ khayā anāsavaṃ cetovimuttiṃ paññāvimuttiṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati. Ettāvatā kho, gahapati, ariyassa vinaye sabbena sabbaṃ sabbathā sabbaṃ vohārasamucchedo hoti.
“And indeed, householder, when the noble disciple has arrived at that unsurpassed purity of mindfulness due to equanimity, he recollects his manifold past lives, that is, one birth, two births...and so on... Thus with their aspects and particulars he recollects his manifold past lives.

“And indeed, householder, when the noble disciple has arrived at that unsurpassed purity of mindfulness due to equanimity, with the divine eye, which is purified and surpasses the human, he sees beings passing away and reappearing, inferior and superior, fair and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate…and so on… he understands how beings pass on according to their actions (yathākammūpage satte pajānāti).

“And indeed, householder, when the noble disciple has arrived at that unsurpassed purity of mindfulness due to equanimity, by realizing for himself with direct knowledge, here and now enters upon and abides in the deliverance of mind and deliverance by wisdom that are taintless with the destruction of the taints (āsavānaṃ khayā anāsavaṃ cetovimuttiṃ paññāvimuttiṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati).

“At this point, householder, the cutting off of affairs in the Noble One’s Discipline has been achieved entirely and in all ways (ettāvatā kho, gahapati, ariyassa vinaye sabbena sabbaṃ sabbathā sabbaṃ vohārasamucchedo hoti).”

50. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, gahapati, yathā ariyassa vinaye sabbena sabbaṃ sabbathā sabbaṃ vohārasamucchedo hoti, api nu tvaṃ evarūpaṃ vohārasamucchedaṃ attani samanupassasī’’ti? ‘‘Ko cāhaṃ, bhante, ko ca ariyassa vinaye sabbena sabbaṃ sabbathā sabbaṃ vohārasamucchedo! Ārakā ahaṃ, bhante, ariyassa vinaye sabbena sabbaṃ sabbathā sabbaṃ vohārasamucchedā. Mayañhi, bhante, pubbe aññatitthiye paribbājake anājānīyeva samāne ājānīyāti amaññimha, anājānīyeva samāne ājānīyabhojanaṃ bhojimha, anājānīyeva samāne ājānīyaṭhāne ṭhapimha; bhikkhū pana mayaṃ, bhante, ājānīyeva samāne anājānīyāti amaññimha, ājānīyeva samāne anājānīyabhojanaṃ bhojimha, ājānīyeva samāne anājānīyaṭhāne ṭhapimha; idāni pana mayaṃ, bhante, aññatitthiye paribbājake anājānīyeva samāne anājānīyāti jānissāma, anājānīyeva samāne anājānīyabhojanaṃ bhojessāma, anājānīyeva samāne anājānīyaṭhāne ṭhapessāma. Bhikkhū pana mayaṃ, bhante, ājānīyeva samāne ājānīyāti jānissāma ājānīyeva samāne ājānīyabhojanaṃ bhojessāma, ājānīyeva samāne ājānīyaṭhāne ṭhapessāma. Ajanesi vata me, bhante, bhagavā samaṇesu samaṇappemaṃ, samaṇesu samaṇappasādaṃ, samaṇesu samaṇagāravaṃ. Abhikkantaṃ, bhante, abhikkantaṃ, bhante ! Seyyathāpi, bhante, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya, cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantīti; evamevaṃ kho, bhante, bhagavatā anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Esāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsakaṃ maṃ bhagavā dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gata’’nti. Potaliyasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ catutthaṃ.
50. What do you think, householder? In the Noble One’s Discipline, is there a complete cutting off of all affairs, in every way? Do you see in yourself such a cutting off of affairs?” “Venerable sir, who am I, and what is the complete cutting off of all affairs in every way in the Noble One’s Discipline! Venerable sir, I am far removed from the complete cutting off of all affairs in every way in the Noble One’s Discipline.

For, venerable sir, though the wanderers of other sects are not thoroughbreds, we imagined that they are thoroughbreds; though they are not thoroughbreds, we fed them the food of thoroughbreds; though they are not thoroughbreds, we set them in the place of thoroughbreds. But though the bhikkhus are thoroughbreds, we imagined that they are not thoroughbreds; though they are thoroughbreds, we fed them the food of those who are not thoroughbreds; though they are thoroughbreds, we set them in the place of those who are not thoroughbreds. But now, venerable sir, though the wanderers of other sects are not thoroughbreds, we shall understand that they are not thoroughbreds; though they are not thoroughbreds, we shall feed them the food of those who are not thoroughbreds; though they are not thoroughbreds, we shall set them in the place of those who are not thoroughbreds.

But as the bhikkhus are thoroughbreds, we shall understand that they are thoroughbreds; as they are thoroughbreds, we shall feed them the food of thoroughbreds; as they are thoroughbreds, we shall set them in the place of those who are thoroughbreds.

Venerable sir, the Blessed One Bhagavā has inspired in me love for ascetics (samaṇa), confidence in ascetics, reverence for ascetics.

Excellent, venerable Sir! Excellent, venerable Sir! Just as, venerable Sir, one might set upright what has been overturned, or open up what has been closed, or show a path to one who is lost, or carry an oil lamp into the darkness, 'those with vision will see forms', just so has the Dharma been explained by the Blessed One Bhagavata in countless ways.

I go to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ) for refuge, and to the Dharma (dhammañca), and to the Saṃgha (bhikkhusaṅghañca), the community of monks.

May the Blessed One (bhagavā) accept me as a lay follower (upāsakaṃ) who has gone for refuge (saraṇaṃ gataṃ) from this day forward, for as long as life lasts."

50. “I am far away, venerable sir,” [is] like the sky from the earth, like the far shore from the near shore of the ocean, I am very, very far.

“Not knowing,” [means] the cause of the destruction of the householder's affairs, to one who does not know.

“Knowing food” means food that should be eaten by those who know the cause.

“Not knowing food” means food that should be eaten by those who do not know the cause.
