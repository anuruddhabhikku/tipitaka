453. Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the land of Gajaṅgala, in the Suveḷu Grove.

Then Uttara, a student of the Pārāsariya school, went to where the Blessed One was. Having arrived, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One. After an exchange of courteous and amiable greetings, he sat down at one side.

Seated at one side, Uttara, the student of the Pārāsariya school, the Blessed One asked this: "Uttara, does the Brahmin Pārāsiriya teach his disciples about the development of the faculties (indriyabhāvana)?" "He does, Master Gotama, the Brahmin Pārāsiriya teaches his disciples about the development of the faculties."

"But how, Uttara, does the Brahmin Pārāsiriya teach his disciples about the development of the faculties?" "Here, Master Gotama, one does not see a form with the eye, one does not hear a sound with the ear – in this way, Master Gotama, the Brahmin Pārāsiriya teaches his disciples about the development of the faculties."

"If that is so, Uttara, then a blind man will have developed faculties, a deaf man will have developed faculties, according to the Brahmin Pārāsiriya's teaching. For a blind man, Uttara, does not see a form with the eye, and a deaf man does not hear a sound with the ear."

When this was said, Uttara, the student of the Pārāsariya school, sat silent, dismayed, with shoulders drooping, head down, glum, and without response.

Then the Blessed One, knowing that Uttara, the student of the Pārāsariya school, was silent, dismayed, with drooping shoulders, head down, glum, and without response, addressed the venerable Ānanda: "Ānanda, the Brahmin Pārāsiriya teaches the development of the faculties in one way, but in the discipline of the Noble One, the supreme (anuttarā) development of the faculties is another way."

"This is the time, Blessed One; this is the time, Sugata, for the Blessed One to teach the supreme development of the faculties in the discipline of the Noble One. Having heard it from the Blessed One, the monks will remember it."

"Then listen, Ānanda, and pay close attention; I will speak."

"Yes, venerable sir," replied the venerable Ānanda to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

454. Now, Ānanda, how does the supreme development of the faculties (indriyabhāvanā) occur in the Noble One’s Discipline (ariyassa vinaye)? Here, Ānanda, when a bhikkhu sees a form (rūpaṃ) with the eye (cakkhunā), there arises what is agreeable (manāpaṃ), there arises what is disagreeable (amanāpaṃ), there arises what is both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ).

He understands thus (so evaṃ pajānāti): ‘There has arisen in me what is agreeable (manāpaṃ), there has arisen what is disagreeable (amanāpaṃ), there has arisen what is both agreeable and disagreeable.’

But that is conditioned (saṅkhataṃ), gross (oḷārikaṃ), dependently arisen (paṭiccasamuppannaṃ).

This is peaceful (etaṃ santaṃ), this is sublime (etaṃ paṇītaṃ), that is, equanimity (upekkhā).’

The agreeable (manāpaṃ) that arose, the disagreeable (amanāpaṃ) that arose, and the both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ) that arose cease in him and equanimity (upekkhā) is established (saṇṭhāti).

Just as a man with good sight (cakkhumā puriso), having opened his eyes (ummīletvā) might shut them (nimīleyya) or having shut his eyes (nimīletvā) might open them (ummīleyya); so too concerning anything at all, the agreeable (manāpaṃ) that arose, the disagreeable (amanāpaṃ) that arose, and the both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ) that arose cease just as quickly, just as rapidly, just as easily, and equanimity (upekkhā) is established (saṇṭhāti). This is called, Ānanda, in the Noble One’s Discipline (ariyassa vinaye) the supreme development of the faculties (indriyabhāvanā) regarding forms (rūpesu) cognizable by the eye (cakkhuviññeyyesu).

459. Again, Ānanda, when a bhikkhu cognizes a dhamma (mind-object) with the mind, there arises in him what is agreeable (manāpaṃ), there arises what is disagreeable (amanāpaṃ), there arises what is both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ).

He understands thus: “There has arisen in me what is agreeable (manāpaṃ), there has arisen what is disagreeable (amanāpaṃ), there has arisen what is both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ)."

But that is conditioned (saṅkhataṃ), gross (oḷārikaṃ), dependently arisen (paṭiccasamuppannaṃ);

This is peaceful (santaṃ), this is sublime (paṇītaṃ), that is, equanimity (upekkhā).'

The agreeable (manāpaṃ) that arose, the disagreeable (amanāpaṃ) that arose, and the both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ) that arose cease in him and equanimity (upekkhā) is established.

Just as if, Ānanda, a strong man were to let two or three drops of water (udakaphusitāni) fall onto an iron plate heated for a whole day,

the falling of the water drops (udakaphusitānaṃ nipāto) might be slow but they would quickly vaporise and vanish, so too concerning anything at all, the agreeable (manāpaṃ) that arose, the disagreeable (amanāpaṃ) that arose, and the both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ) that arose cease just as quickly, just as rapidly, just as easily, and equanimity (upekkhā) is established. This is called in the Noble One’s Discipline the supreme (anuttarā) development (bhāvanā) of the faculties (indriya) regarding ideas (dhammesu) cognizable by the mind (manoviññeyyesu).

That is how, Ānanda, there is the supreme (anuttarā) development (bhāvanā) of the faculties (indriya) in the Noble One’s Discipline (ariyassa vinaye).

Idhānanda, bhikkhuno cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā uppajjati manāpaṃ, uppajjati amanāpaṃ, uppajjati manāpāmanāpaṃ. So <pb ed="M" n="3.0351"/> tena uppannena manāpena uppannena amanāpena uppannena manāpāmanāpena aṭṭīyati harāyati jigucchati. Sotena saddaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0301"/> sutvā…pe… ghānena gandhaṃ ghāyitvā…, jivhāya rasaṃ sāyitvā… kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ phusitvā… manasā dhammaṃ viññāya uppajjati manāpaṃ, uppajjati amanāpaṃ, uppajjati manāpāmanāpaṃ. So tena uppannena manāpena uppannena amanāpena uppannena manāpāmanāpena aṭṭīyati harāyati jigucchati. Evaṃ kho, ānanda, sekho hoti pāṭipado.

“And how, Ānanda, is one a disciple in higher training, one who has entered upon the way? Here, Ānanda, when a bhikkhu sees a form with the eye, there arises in him what is agreeable (manāpaṃ), there arises what is disagreeable (amanāpaṃ), there arises what is both agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpaṃ). He is ashamed, humiliated and disgusted by the agreeable that arose, by the disagreeable that arose, and by the both agreeable and disagreeable that arose. He hears a sound with the ear…smells an odour with the nose…tastes a flavour with the tongue…touches a tangible with the body…cognizes a mind-object with the mind, there arises in him what is agreeable, there arises what is disagreeable, there arises what is both agreeable and disagreeable. He is ashamed, humiliated and disgusted by the agreeable that arose, by the disagreeable that arose, and by the both agreeable and disagreeable that arose. That is how, Ānanda, one is a disciple in higher training, one who has entered upon the way.
461
<p n="461" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">461</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Kathañcānanda, ariyo hoti bhāvitindriyo? Idhānanda, bhikkhuno cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā uppajjati manāpaṃ, uppajjati amanāpaṃ, uppajjati manāpāmanāpaṃ. So sace ākaṅkhati – ‘paṭikūle <note>paṭikkūle (sabbattha)</note> appaṭikūlasaññī vihareyya’nti, appaṭikūlasaññī tattha viharati. Sace ākaṅkhati – ‘appaṭikūle paṭikūlasaññī vihareyya’nti, paṭikūlasaññī tattha viharati. Sace ākaṅkhati – ‘paṭikūle ca appaṭikūle ca appaṭikūlasaññī <pb ed="T" n="3.0547"/> vihareyya’nti, appaṭikūlasaññī tattha viharati. Sace ākaṅkhati – ‘appaṭikūle ca paṭikūle ca paṭikūlasaññī vihareyya’nti, paṭikūlasaññī tattha viharati. Sace ākaṅkhati – ‘paṭikūlañca appaṭikūlañca tadubhayaṃ abhinivajjetvā upekkhako vihareyyaṃ sato sampajāno’ti, upekkhako tattha viharati sato sampajāno.

Ananda, how is one a noble one with developed faculties? Here, Ānanda, when a bhikkhu sees a form with the eye, agreeable things arise, disagreeable things arise, agreeable-disagreeable things arise.

And if he wishes: ‘May I abide perceiving the unrepulsive in the repulsive (paṭikūle appaṭikūlasaññī vihareyya)’’, he abides perceiving the unrepulsive in that.

If he should wish: ‘May I abide perceiving the repulsive in the unrepulsive (appaṭikūle paṭikūlasaññī vihareyya)’’, he abides perceiving the repulsive in that.

If he should wish: ‘May I abide perceiving the unrepulsive in the repulsive and the unrepulsive (paṭikūle ca appaṭikūle ca appaṭikūlasaññī vihareyya)’’, he abides perceiving the unrepulsive in that.

If he should wish: ‘May I abide perceiving the repulsive in the unrepulsive and the repulsive (appaṭikūle ca paṭikūle ca paṭikūlasaññī vihareyya)’’, he abides perceiving the repulsive in that.

If he should wish: ‘May I, avoiding both the repulsive and unrepulsive (paṭikūlañca appaṭikūlañca tadubhayaṃ abhinivajjetvā), abide in equanimity (upekkhako vihareyyaṃ), mindful (sato) and fully aware (sampajāno)’’, he abides in equanimity towards that, mindful and fully aware.
462
<p n="462" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">462</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, ānanda, bhikkhuno sotena saddaṃ sutvā…pe… ghānena gandhaṃ ghāyitvā… jivhāya rasaṃ sāyitvā… kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ phusitvā… manasā dhammaṃ viññāya uppajjati manāpaṃ, uppajjati amanāpaṃ, uppajjati manāpāmanāpaṃ. So sace ākaṅkhati – ‘paṭikūle appaṭikūlasaññī vihareyya’nti, appaṭikūlasaññī tattha viharati. Sace ākaṅkhati – ‘appaṭikūle paṭikūlasaññī vihareyya’nti, paṭikūlasaññī tattha viharati. Sace ākaṅkhati – ‘paṭikūle ca appaṭikūle ca appaṭikūlasaññī vihareyya’nti, appaṭikūlasaññī tattha viharati. Sace ākaṅkhati – ‘appaṭikūle ca paṭikūle ca paṭikūlasaññī vihareyya’nti, paṭikūlasaññī tattha viharati. Sace ākaṅkhati – ‘paṭikūlañca appaṭikūlañca tadubhayampi <pb ed="P" n="3.0302"/> abhinivajjetvā upekkhako vihareyyaṃ sato sampajāno’ti, upekkhako tattha viharati sato sampajāno. Evaṃ kho, ānanda, ariyo hoti bhāvitindriyo.

"Furthermore, Ānanda, when a monk hears a sound with the ear...smells an odor with the nose...tastes a flavor with the tongue...touches a tangible object with the body...cognizes a mental object with the mind, there arises what is agreeable, there arises what is disagreeable, there arises what is both agreeable and disagreeable.

If he should wish, 'May I abide perceiving the unrepulsive in the repulsive (paṭikūle appaṭikūlasaññī vihareyya),' he abides perceiving the unrepulsive in that.

If he should wish, 'May I abide perceiving the repulsive in the unrepulsive (appaṭikūle paṭikūlasaññī vihareyya),' he abides perceiving the repulsive in that.

If he should wish, 'May I abide perceiving the unrepulsive in the repulsive and the unrepulsive (paṭikūle ca appaṭikūle ca appaṭikūlasaññī vihareyya),' he abides perceiving the unrepulsive in that.

If he should wish, 'May I abide perceiving the repulsive in the unrepulsive and the repulsive (appaṭikūle ca paṭikūle ca paṭikūlasaññī vihareyya),' he abides perceiving the repulsive in that.

If he should wish, 'May I, abandoning both the repulsive and the unrepulsive (paṭikūlañca appaṭikūlañca tadubhayampi abhinivajjetvā), abide in equanimity, mindful and fully aware (upekkhako vihareyyaṃ sato sampajāno),' he abides in equanimity towards that, mindful and fully aware (upekkhako tattha viharati sato sampajāno).

Thus, Ānanda, a noble one is one with developed faculties (evaṃ kho, ānanda, ariyo hoti bhāvitindriyo)."

Yaṃ kho, ānanda, satthārā karaṇīyaṃ sāvakānaṃ hitesinā anukampakena anukampaṃ upādāya, kataṃ vo taṃ mayā. Etāni, ānanda, rukkhamūlāni, etāni suññāgārāni, jhāyathānanda, mā pamādattha, mā pacchā vippaṭisārino ahuvattha. Ayaṃ vo amhākaṃ anusāsanī’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā ānando bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti.

463. Thus, Ānanda, the unsurpassed cultivation of the faculties (indriya-bhāvanā) has been taught by me in the Dharma (vinaya) of the noble ones (ariya). The training for the adept (sekha) has been taught, and the noble one with cultivated faculties (bhāvitindriya) has been taught.

Whatever should be done by a teacher (sattharā) out of compassion for his disciples, seeking their welfare, that I have done for you, Ānanda. These are the roots of trees, these are empty huts. Meditate, Ānanda, do not be heedless, or else you will regret it later. This is our instruction to you."
