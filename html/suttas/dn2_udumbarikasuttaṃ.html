49. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Rājagaha on the Vulture Peak (Gijjhakūṭa) Mountain.

At that time, the wanderer Nigrodha was staying at the Udumbarika rest-house for wanderers, with a large company of some three hundred wanderers.

Then Sandhāna, the householder (gahapati), one morning, left Rājagaha in order to see the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ) .

Then Sandhāna, the householder, thought: "It is not the proper time to see the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ). The Blessed One (bhagavā) is in retreat. It is not the proper time to see even the monks worthy of esteem. The monks worthy of esteem are in retreat. Perhaps I should go to the Udumbarika rest-house for wanderers, and visit Nigrodha, the wanderer."

Then Sandhāna, the householder, went to the Udumbarika rest-house for wanderers.

49. Thus, I have heard: the Uḍumbarika Sūtra.

Here follows a word-by-word explanation of the beginning: Paribbājaka means a wandering mendicant. Udumbarikāya paribbājakārāme means in the monastery of the wandering mendicants belonging to Uḍumbarikā. Sandhāno means his name.

Now, this great and powerful Sandhāna, surrounded by and attending to five hundred lay followers, the foremost, a non-returner, was thus praised by the Blessed One in the great assembly:

"Sandhāna, householder, is endowed with six qualities, has put down his anchor in the Tathāgata, and fares on in the true Dhamma. Which six? He has unwavering faith in the Buddha ( buddhe aveccappasādena), unwavering faith in the Dhamma ( dhamme aveccappasādena), unwavering faith in the Saṅgha ( saṅghe aveccappasādena), noble morality ( ariyena sīlena), noble knowledge ( ariyena ñāṇena), and noble liberation ( ariyāya vimuttiyā). Sandhāna, householder, endowed with these six qualities, has put down his anchor in the Tathāgata, and fares on in the true Dhamma" (A.
iii.
120-139).

In the morning, he would undertake the uposatha vows, and in the forenoon, having given alms to the Saṅgha, with the Buddha at its head, after the monks had gone to the monastery, he, overwhelmed by the noise of the young and old children in the house, would leave, thinking, "I will hear the Dhamma," to the presence of the Teacher.

Therefore, it was said: "He went out from Rājagṛha in the day."

There, divā divassā means during the day, namely after the passing of noon; the meaning is that he went out at midday, when the day had already become day.

Paṭisallīno means having withdrawn his mind from this and that, from objects such as forms, having secluded himself, having gone to a state of oneness through cultivating the pleasure of meditation. Manobhāvanīyāna means of those who increase the mind. And those who cultivate and apply their minds, the mind becomes free from hindrances, rises up, and increases.

He went out from Rājagṛha in the day. During the day. Secluded. Of those who increase the mind.

At that time, Nigrodha the wanderer was sitting with a large assembly of wanderers, who were making an uproar, loudly and noisily talking many kinds of pointless talk ( tiracchānakathaṃ ). Namely: talk of kings (rājakathaṃ), talk of robbers (corakathaṃ), talk of high officials (mahāmattakathaṃ), talk of armies (senākathaṃ), talk of fear (bhayakathaṃ), talk of war (yuddhakathaṃ), talk of food (annakathaṃ), talk of drinks (pānakathaṃ), talk of clothing (vatthakathaṃ), talk of beds (sayanakathaṃ), talk of garlands (mālākathaṃ), talk of perfumes (gandhakathaṃ), talk of relatives (ñātikathaṃ), talk of vehicles (yānakathaṃ), talk of villages (gāmakathaṃ), talk of towns (nigamakathaṃ), talk of cities (nagarakathaṃ), talk of countries (janapadakathaṃ), talk of women (itthikathaṃ), talk of heroes (sūrakathaṃ), talk of street gossip (visikhākathaṃ), talk of wells (kumbhaṭṭhānakathaṃ), talk of deceased ancestors (pubbapetakathaṃ), talk of various things (nānattakathaṃ), world stories (lokakkhāyikaṃ), sea stories (samuddakkhāyikaṃ), talk of thus it is or thus it is not (itibhavābhavakathaṃ), and so on.

Disvā sakaṃ parisaṃ saṇṭhāpesi – ‘‘appasaddā bhonto hontu, mā bhonto saddamakattha. Ayaṃ samaṇassa gotamassa sāvako āgacchati sandhāno gahapati. Yāvatā kho pana samaṇassa gotamassa sāvakā gihī odātavasanā rājagahe paṭivasanti, ayaṃ tesaṃ aññataro sandhāno gahapati. Evaṃ vutte te paribbājakā tuṇhī ahesuṃ.

Then Nigrodha the wanderer saw the householder Sandhana approaching from afar. Seeing him, he called his followers to order, saying: "Be quiet, gentlemen, don’t make a noise, gentlemen! The householder Sandhana, a follower of the ascetic Gotama, is approaching. He is one of the number of white-robed householder followers of the ascetic Gotama in Rajagaha. These venerable ones are fond of quiet, trained in quiet, and speak in praise of quiet. Maybe, if he perceives our group as quiet, he will consider it worth his while to come our way.” When this was said, the wanderers fell silent.

<p n="52" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">52</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Atha kho sandhāno gahapati yena nigrodho paribbājako tenupasaṅkami, upasaṅkamitvā nigrodhena paribbājakena saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho sandhāno gahapati nigrodhaṃ paribbājakaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘aññathā kho ime bhonto aññatitthiyā paribbājakā saṅgamma samāgamma unnādino uccāsaddamahāsaddā anekavihitaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0038"/> tiracchānakathaṃ anuyuttā viharanti. Seyyathidaṃ – rājakathaṃ…pe… itibhavābhavakathaṃ iti vā. Aññathā kho <note>ca (sī. pī.)</note> pana so bhagavā araññavanapatthāni pantāni senāsanāni paṭisevati appasaddāni appanigghosāni vijanavātāni manussarāhasseyyakāni paṭisallānasāruppānī’’ti.

Then Sandhāna, the householder, approached Nigrodha the wanderer, and having approached, exchanged greetings with Nigrodha the wanderer. When the courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

As he sat down at one side, Sandhāna the householder said to Nigrodha the wanderer: ‘Reverend sirs, the way the wanderers of another faith conduct themselves when they come together is one thing: they are noisy and loud, given to all kinds of frivolous talk, such as talk about kings (rājakathaṃ), …and talk about being and non-being (itibhavābhavakathaṃ).

The Blessed Lord’s way is different: he seeks a lodging in the forest (araññavanapatthāni), in the depths of the jungle, free from noise, with little sound, far from the madding crowd, undisturbed by men, well fitted for seclusion.’

52. "Having said, `etadavocā,'" [it means that] while they were coming along the road, because of the conversation among themselves, they were excited, and he spoke this statement, "`aññathā kho ime,' etc."

There, "aññatitthiyā" means that by appearance, by manner, by fraud, by conduct, by dwelling, and by deportment, they are other tīrthikas, therefore, they are "aññatitthiyā."

"saṅgamma samāgammā" means having come together, having assembled, having become a heap, at a place where they were sitting.

"araññavanapatthānī" means forest and woodland resting places, remote dwellings, free from village vicinity.

"pantānī" means very distant, devoid of human resort.

"appasaddānī" means with little sound, due to the sound of those who go to the monastery vicinity, [and] even for the sake of the sound of travelers, they are places with subdued sound.

"appanigghosānī" means with indistinct noise, with indistinct sound, places with subdued noise.

"vijanavātānī" means the winds of people who travel inside, the winds that are free from wind.

"manussarāhasseyyakānī" means suitable and appropriate for people's secret deeds.

"paṭisallānasāruppānī" means corresponding to solitude.

Thus, Sandhāno the householder, thinking, "Oh, my teacher frequents such dwellings!" raised his joined palms (añjali) and having placed them on the top of his head, sat down uttering this solemn utterance (udāna).

Having spoken thus, Nigrodha the wanderer said this to Sandhāna the householder: "Come now, householder, would that you might know with whom the śramaṇa Gautama converses, with whom he engages in discussion, and through what he attains proficiency in wisdom (prajñā). The wisdom (prajñā) of the śramaṇa Gautama is ruined by solitary dwelling; the śramaṇa Gautama is unused to assemblies and is no good at conversation. He frequents only the outskirts."

"Just as bison circling around keep to the fringes, even so, the wisdom (prajñā) of the śramaṇa Gautama is ruined by solitary dwelling; the śramaṇa Gautama is unused to assemblies; he is no good at conversation. He frequents only the outskirts. Come now, householder, should the śramaṇa Gautama come to this assembly, we would confound him with a single question, we would, I think, knock him over like an empty pot."

"Thus spoken" means thus spoken by the householder, with such a combination of words, uttering a solemn utterance.

Nigrodha the wandering ascetic [thought:] "This householder, even sitting near me, praises and exalts his own teacher. He does not even acknowledge that we exist. "Indeed!" is a particle indicating incitement.

"You should know" means you should understand, you should see.

"With whom does the śramaṇa Gotama converse?" means with what reason, with what individual does the śramaṇa Gotama converse, speak, and address.

What is said is this: "If there should be any cause for conversation, or if any conversationalist should go to the śramaṇa Gotama's presence, he would converse. Indeed! You should know! With whom does the śramaṇa Gotama converse?

"Having conversation" means association.

"Manifestation of wisdom" means the state of making wisdom clear through the method of question and answer.

"Habituated to solitary dwellings" means ruined in solitary dwellings. For the wisdom that the śramaṇa Gotama attained at the foot of the bodhi tree, though he was conscientious, was ruined when he lived alone in solitary dwellings. He shows that if he were to sit with a group and an assembly, as we do, his wisdom would not be ruined.

"Not frequenting assemblies" means he is unable to enter an assembly due to lack of confidence.

"Not competent for conversation" means not capable of making conversation.

"Only to the very ends" means because he is afraid that someone will ask him a question, being fearful of questions, he resorts only to remote forest resting places.

"Cow with a defect" means a one-eyed, injured, blind cow.

That cow, it is said, is accustomed to wandering on the periphery, she resorts only to the very ends.

That cow, it is said, is unable to face the forest edge because of being one-eyed.

Why? Because she fears being struck by a leaf or a branch or a thorn.

She is also unable to face the herds.

Why? Because she fears being struck by a horn or an ear or a tail.

"Indeed!" is a particle indicating incitement.

"We should cause him to be depressed" means by asking a single question, we should cause him to be dejected and to meet with misfortune.

"Like an empty pot, no" means like an empty vessel, no.

"We should obstruct" means we should ruin.

For a full pot, having been turned this way and that, is not easily ruined.

An empty one, having been turned as one pleases, is able to be ruined. Just so, with the ruin of wisdom, we will completely ruin the śramaṇa Gotama, who is like an empty pot, with the ruin of argument, so he says.

Having conversation, manifestation of wisdom, habituated to solitary dwellings, not frequenting assemblies, not competent for conversation, only to the very ends, cow with a defect, indeed!, we should cause him to be depressed, like an empty pot, no, we should obstruct.

74. "In this way, Nigrodha, the abhorrence of austerity is both the highest attainment and the attainment of essence." Thus spoke the Blessed One in reference to the ascetics.

The higher knowledges ending with the knowledge of former abodes are like the pith. These people take the divine eye and go about thinking it is arahantship. Therefore, that for them is like the heartwood of the tree.

In the Dispensation, gain and honor are like branches and leaves. Accomplishment in virtue is like bark. Meditative attainments are like sapwood. Mundane higher knowledges are like the pith. The path and its fruit are the heartwood (sāro). Thus, the Blessed One compared his own Dispensation to a tree laden with the weight of fruits that are bent down and bowed low.

Because of skill in teaching and because of that attainment of the essence of the sapwood, my Dispensation is even more excellent and refined. When will you know this?"

"These wanderers" means these wanderers, who are his retinue, numbering thirty hundred.

Uddesā no cāvetukāmo means, having made us accept his teaching, he wants to remove us from our assignment. So eva vo uddeso hotū means, whatever is naturally your assignment, let that be yours; we do not need your assignment. Ājīvā means from living. Akusalasaṅkhātā means having attained to the state of unwholesome things. Akusalā dhammā means the twelve unwholesome states of mind, or especially craving. For it is called "leading to renewed existence" because it causes renewed existence. Sadarathā means associated with the anxieties of defilements. Jātijarāmaraṇiyā means being the cause of birth, old age and death. Saṃkilesikā dhammā means the twelve unwholesome states of mind. Vodāniyā means the qualities of serenity and insight. For they purify beings, therefore they are called "vodāniyā." Paññāpāripūri means the fulfillment of wisdom on the path. Vepullattañcā means the abundance of the wisdom of fruition, or both of these are indeed synonyms for each other. This is what was said: "Then you yourselves, in this very life, having realized and attained the wisdom of the path and the wisdom of fruition through direct knowledge, will abide therein." In this way, the Blessed One, in connection with the wandering ascetics, showing the fruit of his own instruction, concluded the teaching with the pinnacle of Arahantship.

79. At these words, the wanderers sat silent, upset, their shoulders drooping, they hung their heads and sat there downcast and bewildered, their minds so possessed by Māra.

Then the Lord thought: “Every one of these foolish men is possessed by the evil one.

So that not a single one of them thinks: “Let us now follow the holy life, brahmacarya, proclaimed by the ascetic Gotama, that we may learn it—for what do seven days matter?”’ Then the Lord, after uttering a lion’s roar sīhanāda in the alms-hall of the wanderers at Udumbarikā, rose into the sky and reappeared on Vulture Peak Gijjhakūṭe pabbate.

And the householder Sandhāna also returned to Rājagaha.

The Udumbarika Discourse is finished, the second.

79. yathā taṃ mārenāti: Just as they sit with their minds overcome by Māra, so too, they were silent… speechless.

Māra, it seems, having greatly roared, displaying the power of the Buddha, teaches the Dharma to these wanderers, [thinking,] "Perhaps there might be a realization of the Dharma." So, he overcame their minds.

Indeed, the mind of one who has not abandoned distorted perceptions is easily manipulated by Māra.

They too, with their minds overcome by Māra, sat silently, their bodies stiff and their limbs frozen, as if speechless.

Then, the Teacher, knowing that these wanderers were sitting very silently, reflecting, understood that they were overcome by Māra.

If, however, there were a cause for the arising of the path and its fruit in them, the Blessed One, even having repelled Māra, would teach the Dharma, but that does not exist in them.

"All of them are empty men to me," he realized.

Therefore, it was said, "Then, it occurred to the Blessed One, 'All of them are vain men to me,'" and so on.

There, phuṭṭhā pāpimatāti: touched by Māra, the evil one.

yatra hi nāmāti: in whom [there is] a name.

aññāṇatthampīti: For the sake of ignorance also… for the sake of knowledge.

kiṃ karissati sattāhoti: What will the week do? [This means:] What will the week, defined by the ascetic Gotama, do to us?

This is what was said: "The ascetic Gotama said, 'Having directly experienced the higher knowledge himself, he will abide for a week,' what discomfort will that week cause us?"

Now, we are able to realize this Dharma within a week, or not able. Even for the sake of ignorance, we will live the holy life.

Or, we know his Dhamma for a moment, for one day, once; even for the sake of ignorance, this thought did not arise in them. What will the week do for these lazy ones? What will they be able to complete in a week? This is the meaning here.

sīhanādanti: Having roared the lion's roar, [which is] the shattering of the doctrines of others and the raising up of one's own doctrine, [a roar] free from fear.

paccupaṭṭhāsīti: Established.

tāvadevāti: In that very moment.

rājagahaṃ pāvisīti: He entered Rājagṛha itself.

Although no special [insight] arose in those wanderers from hearing this discourse, in the future, however, it will be a cause for their habit-energy.

The rest is clear throughout.
