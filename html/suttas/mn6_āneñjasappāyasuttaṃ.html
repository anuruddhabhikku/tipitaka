66. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā kurūsu viharati kammāsadhammaṃ nāma kurūnaṃ nigamo. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Ye ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmā, ye ca samparāyikā kāmā; yā ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmasaññā, yā ca samparāyikā kāmasaññā – ubhayametaṃ māradheyyaṃ, mārassesa mārasseva (ka.) visayo, mārassesa nivāpo, mārassesa gocaro. Etthete pāpakā akusalā mānasā abhijjhāpi byāpādāpi sārambhāpi saṃvattanti. Teva ariyasāvakassa idhamanusikkhato antarāyāya sambhavanti. Tatra, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ye ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmā, ye ca samparāyikā kāmā; yā ca diṭṭhadhammikā kāmasaññā, yā ca samparāyikā kāmasaññā – ubhayametaṃ māradheyyaṃ, mārassesa visayo, mārassesa nivāpo, mārassesa gocaro. Etthete pāpakā akusalā mānasā abhijjhāpi byāpādāpi sārambhāpi saṃvattanti, teva ariyasāvakassa idhamanusikkhato antarāyāya sambhavanti. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ vipulena mahaggatena cetasā vihareyyaṃ abhibhuyya lokaṃ adhiṭṭhāya manasā. Vipulena hi me mahaggatena cetasā viharato abhibhuyya lokaṃ adhiṭṭhāya manasā ye pāpakā akusalā mānasā abhijjhāpi byāpādāpi sārambhāpi te na bhavissanti. Tesaṃ pahānā aparittañca me cittaṃ bhavissati appamāṇaṃ subhāvita’nti. Tassa evaṃpaṭipannassa tabbahulavihārino āyatane cittaṃ pasīdati. Sampasāde sati etarahi vā āneñjaṃ samāpajjati paññāya vā adhimuccati kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā. Ṭhānametaṃ vijjati yaṃ taṃsaṃvattanikaṃ viññāṇaṃ assa āneñjūpagaṃ. Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, paṭhamā āneñjasappāyā paṭipadā akkhāyati’’.
Thus have I heard.

On one occasion the Blessed One was staying among the Kurus, at a town of the Kurus named Kammāsadhamma. There the Blessed One addressed the monks: "Monks!" "Venerable sir," those monks replied.

The Blessed One said this: "Impermanent, monks, are sensual pleasures, hollow, false, deceptive. They are illusory, monks, the prattle of fools.

Sensual pleasures here and now and sensual pleasures in lives to come, sensual perceptions here and now and sensual perceptions in lives to come - both alike are Māra’s realm, Māra’s domain, Māra’s bait, Māra’s hunting ground. On account of them, these evil unwholesome mental states such as covetousness ( abhijjhā), ill will (byāpāda), and presumption (sārambhāpi) arise, and they constitute an obstruction to a noble disciple in training here.

Therein, monks, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘Sensual pleasures here and now and sensual pleasures in lives to come, sensual perceptions here and now and sensual perceptions in lives to come - both alike are Māra’s realm, Māra’s domain, Māra’s bait, Māra’s hunting ground. On account of them, these evil unwholesome mental states such as covetousness (abhijjhā), ill will (byāpāda), and presumption (sārambhāpi) arise, and they constitute an obstruction to a noble disciple in training here.

Suppose I were to abide with a mind abundant and exalted, having transcended the world and made a firm resolution with the mind. When I do so, there will be no more evil unwholesome mental states such as covetousness (abhijjhā), ill will (byāpāda), and presumption (sārambhāpi) in me. And with the abandoning of them my mind will be unlimited, immeasurable, and well developed.’

When he practises in this way and frequently abides thus, his mind acquires confidence in this base. Once there is full confidence, he either attains to the imperturbable (āneñja) now or else he decides upon perfecting wisdom. On the dissolution of the body, after death, it is possible that this consciousness of his, leading to rebirth, may pass on to rebirth in the imperturbable (āneñjūpagaṃ).

This, monks, is declared to be the first way directed to the imperturbable (āneñjasappāyā).

66. Thus have I heard. This is the Āneñjasappāyasutta.

There, aniccā means impermanent due to being the opposite of permanence.

Kāmā means both the vatthukāmā (desires for objects) and the kilesakāmā (desires that are defilements).

Tucchā means empty because it is devoid of permanence, essence, lasting essence, and self essence; it is hollow. But it is not to be taken as non-existent.

For if it is said "empty fist," it is not said that there is no fist.

But if there is nothing inside, it is called empty.

Musā means destructive.

Mosadhammā means of a nature to vanish. Like a field, like an object, like gold and silver, it cannot be perceived. Even in a short time, like things seen in a dream, they vanish and are not perceived.

Therefore it was said, "Mosadhammā." Māyākatameta means just as water is shown as a jewel by means of illusion, or a jujube leaf is shown as a coin, or anything else of this kind is perceived as such only as long as one is in the sphere of illusion; from the moment one transcends that sphere, it is perceived as its natural self.

Thus, kāmāpi is said to be ‘‘māyākata’’ because of their fleeting nature.

And just as an illusionist deceives by showing water and other things as jewels and so on, so also, the kāmāpi deceive by showing impermanent things and so on as having a permanent nature and so on; therefore, they are said to be ‘‘māyākata’’ even in the sense of being deceptive.

Bālalāpana means the babbling of fools, such as "my son, my daughter, my gold, my silver"; thus, bālalāpanaṃ is the babbling of fools.

Diṭṭhadhammikā kāmā means the five human sensual qualities.

Samparāyikā means the remaining ones, having set those aside.

Diṭṭhadhammikā. Kāmasaññā means the perception that arises in connection with human sense pleasures.

Ubhayameṭaṃ māradheyya means both these, namely the sense pleasures and the perception of sense pleasures, are the domain of Māra.

Those by whom both of these are grasped—over them Māra wields his power.

With reference to that, it was said, ‘‘Ubhayameṭaṃ māradheyya.’’

Mārassesa visayo etc. Just as the territory of the Cholas is the Chola territory, the territory of a eunuch is the eunuch territory, the territory of restraints is the territory of restraints, so the sphere of activity is called territory; likewise, those by whom these sense pleasures are grasped—over them Māra wields his power.

With reference to that, it was said, Mārassesa visayo.

But scattering the seed of craving in the five sensual qualities, Māra departs.

Those by whom those are grasped—over them Māra wields his power.

With reference to that, it was said, Mārassesa nivāpo.

And just as where elephants and so on dwell, that is called elephant pasture, horse pasture, goat pasture, so likewise, those by whom these sense pleasures are grasped—in them Māra wields his power.

With reference to that, it was said, Mārassesa gocaro.

The territory of Māra. The territory of Māra. The oblation to Māra.

The territory of Māra.

Etthā means in these sense pleasures.

Mānasā means born of the mind.

There, it may be asked: since craving, characterized by covetousness, and violence, characterized by aggressiveness, arise in connection with the two kinds of sense pleasures, how does ill will arise? They grieve even when the thing they consider theirs is not lost, they grieve even when it is being lost, they grieve even when they suspect it might be lost; thus, ill will arises, being an affliction of the mind of this kind.

Teva ariyasāvakassā means to that noble disciple.
The letter va has only the function of euphonic combination (āgamasandhi).

"Here, for one training" means that for one training in this teaching, those three defilements are obstacles.

"Having overcome the world" means having overcome the world of desire ( kāmaloka).

"Having resolved with the mind" means having resolved with a mind focused on the object of jhāna.

"Limited" (aparitta) means that a mind belonging to the desire realm (kāmāvacara-citta) is called limited (paritta); by rejecting that, the exalted (mahaggata) is called unlimited (aparitta).

"Boundless" (pramāṇa) also refers to only the desire realm; the form realm (rūpāvacara) and formless realm (arūpāvacara) are "immeasurable" (appamāṇa).

"Well developed" means that this term is not a designation for the desire realm etc.; this term is only for the supramundane (lokuttara).

Therefore, based on that, "limited," "immeasurable," and "well developed" all apply only to the supramundane.

Here, "mental," "truly," "for the noble disciple," "here, for one training," "having overcome the world," "having resolved with the mind," "limited," "measurable," "immeasurable," "well developed..."

"Habitually dwelling thus" means for one dwelling habitually, having made that very practice the constant one, by rejecting desire.

"The mind is calmed in the base" means the mind is calmed by the cause.

What is the cause here? Either arhatship, or insight (vipaśyanā) into arhatship, or the fourth jhāna, or access (upacāra) to the fourth jhāna.

"When there is serenity" means that here there are two kinds of serenity: serenity of conviction (adhimokkha-sampasāda) and serenity of attainment (paṭilābha-sampasāda).

Indeed, for one dwelling, having established insight into arhatship, when the great elements etc. are present, the great elements are present by means of that outflow; the appropriated form is present, name and form are present, the conditions are present in every way, insight with characteristic objects is present, and the hope stands, "Today I will attain arhatship," even without attainment, he obtains conviction.

Or, for one making the third jhāna the basis and performing the preliminary work (kasiṇa-parikamma) for the sake of the fourth jhāna, for one contemplating the suppression of the hindrances etc., by means of that outflow, the hindrances are suppressed, the defilements subside, mindfulness stands firm, or the formations (saṅkhāra) having become distinct and manifest, appear like the other world to one with divine vision; the arising of the mind, like sticking to a lump of clay, is concentrated by access; the hope stands, "Today I will generate the fourth jhāna," even without attainment, he obtains conviction.

This is called serenity of conviction (adhimokkha-sampasāda).

When there is that serenity.

But whoever attains arhatship or the fourth jhāna, his mind is indeed very clear.

Here, however, because of the saying "the mind is calmed in the base," one should understand that the attainment of insight into arhatship and also access to the fourth jhāna is serenity of attainment (paṭilābha-sampasāda).

Indeed, insight is the cause of conviction in wisdom, and access [is the cause] of imperturbable attainment.

Habitually dwelling thus, the mind is calmed in the base, when there is serenity.

Serenity of conviction, serenity of attainment. He attains imperturbability right now, or else he is convinced by wisdom.

"Or he attains imperturbability right now, or he is convinced by wisdom": here, having made a word-permutation in this way, the meaning should be understood.

For this is said: "When there is that serenity, either he is convinced by wisdom right now, or he realizes arhatship."

Not fully comprehending that, either he attains imperturbability, or else "he is convinced by wisdom," he cultivates the path of arhatship; not fully comprehending that, either he attains imperturbability.

Not being able to cultivate the path of arhatship, either he realizes the Four Noble Truths right now.

Not fully comprehending that, either he attains imperturbability.

Either he attains imperturbability right now.

Or he is convinced by wisdom, or he is convinced by wisdom. In that case, this is the method: here, a monk makes the third jhāna the basis and performs the preliminary work for the fourth jhāna.

His hindrances are suppressed, mindfulness stands firm, the mind is concentrated by access.

He grasps form and the formless, he grasps the condition, he determines insight with characteristic objects; it occurs to him thus: "By access, may my jhāna have a superior share; let the having a superior share stand firm, I will make it have a penetrating share," having increased insight, he realizes arhatship.

By this much, the task is said to be done.

But not being able to realize arhatship, then, with a mind that has drawn back from that, he does not stand in between; he attains the fourth jhāna itself.

But for the three monks who, having established the realm of infinite consciousness as a basis, remain, falling away is spoken of, rebirth is spoken of, arahantship is spoken of.

Likewise, for the monk who, having established the realm of nothingness as a foundation, remains.

However, for the one who, having established the realm of neither perception nor non-perception as a foundation, remains, there is no falling away, but rebirth and arahantship are spoken of.

Only the arahantship of the dry-insightful one is spoken of.

Thus, it should be understood that falling away is spoken of in seven places, rebirth in eight places, and arahantship in nine places.

By relating this falling away in seven places, rebirth in eight places, and arahantship in nine places, this Āneñjasappāyasutta is said to be well-spoken.

The Commentary on the Āneñjasappāyasutta is Finished.
