Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Tathāgatena, bhikkhave, arahatā sammāsambuddhena bārāṇasiyaṃ isipatane migadāye anuttaraṃ dhammacakkaṃ pavattitaṃ appaṭivattiyaṃ samaṇena vā brāhmaṇena vā devena vā mārena vā brahmunā vā kenaci vā lokasmiṃ, yadidaṃ – catunnaṃ ariyasaccānaṃ ācikkhanā desanā paññāpanā paṭṭhapanā vivaraṇā vibhajanā uttānīkammaṃ. Katamesaṃ catunnaṃ? Dukkhassa ariyasaccassa ācikkhanā desanā paññāpanā paṭṭhapanā vivaraṇā vibhajanā uttānīkammaṃ, dukkhasamudayassa ariyasaccassa ācikkhanā desanā paññāpanā paṭṭhapanā vivaraṇā vibhajanā uttānīkammaṃ, dukkhanirodhassa ariyasaccassa ācikkhanā desanā paññāpanā paṭṭhapanā vivaraṇā vibhajanā uttānīkammaṃ, dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya ariyasaccassa ācikkhanā desanā paññāpanā paṭṭhapanā vivaraṇā vibhajanā uttānīkammaṃ. Tathāgatena, bhikkhave, arahatā sammāsambuddhena bārāṇasiyaṃ isipatane migadāye anuttaraṃ dhammacakkaṃ pavattitaṃ appaṭivattiyaṃ samaṇena vā brāhmaṇena vā devena vā mārena vā brahmunā vā kenaci vā lokasmiṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0450"/>, yadidaṃ – imesaṃ catunnaṃ ariyasaccānaṃ ācikkhanā desanā paññāpanā paṭṭhapanā vivaraṇā vibhajanā uttānīkammaṃ.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0450"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Sevatha, bhikkhave, sāriputtamoggallāne; bhajatha, bhikkhave, sāriputtamoggallāne. Paṇḍitā bhikkhū anuggāhakā sabrahmacārīnaṃ. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, janetā <note>janetti (sī. pī.)</note>, evaṃ sāriputto; seyyathāpi jātassa āpādetā, evaṃ moggallāno. Sāriputto, bhikkhave, sotāpattiphale vineti, moggallāno uttamatthe. Sāriputto, bhikkhave, pahoti cattāri ariyasaccāni vitthārena ācikkhituṃ desetuṃ paññāpetuṃ paṭṭhapetuṃ vivarituṃ vibhajituṃ uttānīkātu’’nti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Idaṃ vatvāna sugato uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṃ pāvisi.

371. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Bārāṇasī in the Deer Park at Isipatana. There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: “Bhikkhus.” “Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“At Bārāṇasī, bhikkhus, in the Deer Park at Isipatana, the Tathāgata, the worthy one, the fully enlightened one, set in motion the unexcelled Wheel of the Dharma ( dhammacakkaṃ pavattitaṃ appaṭivattiyaṃ), which cannot be turned back by any ascetic or Brahmin or god or Māra or Brahmā or anyone in the world; that is to say, the announcement, teaching, describing, establishing, revealing, expounding, and elucidating of the Four Noble Truths (catunnaṃ ariyasaccānaṃ ācikkhanā desanā paññāpanā paṭṭhapanā vivaraṇā vibhajanā uttānīkammaṃ). Which four? The announcement, teaching, describing, establishing, revealing, expounding, and elucidating of the Noble Truth of suffering (dukkha), the announcement, teaching, describing, establishing, revealing, expounding, and elucidating of the Noble Truth of the origin of suffering (dukkhasamudaya), the announcement, teaching, describing, establishing, revealing, expounding, and elucidating of the Noble Truth of the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodha), the announcement, teaching, describing, establishing, revealing, expounding, and elucidating of the Noble Truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadā). At Bārāṇasī, bhikkhus, in the Deer Park at Isipatana, the Tathāgata, the worthy one, the fully enlightened one, set in motion the unexcelled Wheel of the Dharma, which cannot be turned back by any ascetic or Brahmin or god or Māra or Brahmā or anyone in the world; that is to say, the announcement, teaching, describing, establishing, revealing, expounding, and elucidating of these Four Noble Truths.

“Associate with Sāriputta and Moggallāna, bhikkhus; befriend Sāriputta and Moggallāna. They are wise monks who assist those living the spiritual life (sabrahmacārīnaṃ). Sāriputta is like a mother, bhikkhus; Moggallāna is like a nurse. Sāriputta trains others for the fruit of stream-entry (sotāpattiphale), Moggallāna for the supreme goal. Sāriputta, bhikkhus, is able to announce, teach, describe, establish, reveal, expound, and elucidate the Four Noble Truths in detail.”

That is what the Blessed One said. Having said this, the Fortunate One got up from his seat and entered his dwelling.

Then, soon after the Blessed One had gone, the venerable Sāriputta addressed the bhikkhus thus: “Friends, bhikkhus.”

—“Friend,” the bhikkhus replied to the venerable Sāriputta. The venerable Sāriputta said this:

“At Benares, friends, in the Deer Park at Isipatana the Tathāgata, accomplished and perfectly awakened (arhat sammāsambuddha), set rolling the unexcelled Wheel of the Dharma (dhammacakkaṃ), which cannot be stopped by any ascetic (śramaṇa) or Brahmin (brāhmaṇa) or god (deva) or Māra or Brahmā or anyone in the world - that is, the announcing (ācikkhanā), teaching (desanā), describing (paññāpanā), establishing (paṭṭhapanā), revealing (vivaraṇā), expounding (vibhajanā), and making manifest (uttānīkammaṃ) of the Four Noble Truths (catunnaṃ ariyasaccānaṃ). Which four? The announcing, teaching, describing, establishing, revealing, expounding, and making manifest of the noble truth of suffering (duḥkha; dukkhassa ariyasaccassa). The announcing, teaching, describing, establishing, revealing, expounding, and making manifest of the noble truth of the origin of suffering (dukkhassa samudayassa ariyasaccassa)… of the noble truth of the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhassa ariyasaccassa)… of the noble truth of the way leading to the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya ariyasaccassa)."

<p n="373" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">373</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Katamañcāvuso, dukkhaṃ ariyasaccaṃ? Jātipi dukkhā, jarāpi dukkhā, maraṇampi dukkhaṃ, sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsāpi dukkhā, yampicchaṃ na labhati tampi dukkhaṃ; saṃkhittena pañcupādānakkhandhā dukkhā.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katamā cāvuso, jāti? Yā tesaṃ tesaṃ sattānaṃ tamhi tamhi sattanikāye jāti sañjāti okkanti abhinibbatti khandhānaṃ pātubhāvo āyatanānaṃ paṭilābho, ayaṃ vuccatāvuso – ‘jāti’’’.

373. "And what, friends, is the noble truth of suffering (duḥkha āryasatya)? Birth (jāti) is suffering (duḥkha); aging (jarā) is suffering (duḥkha); death (maraṇa) is suffering (duḥkha); sorrow (śoka), lamentation (parideva), pain (duḥkha), grief (daurmanasya), and despair (upāyāsa) are suffering (duḥkha); not to obtain what one wants is suffering (duḥkha); in short, the five aggregates subject to grasping (pañcupādānakkhandhā) are suffering (duḥkha)."

"And what, friends, is birth (jāti)? The birth (jāti), coming into existence (sañjāti), descent (okkranti), production (abhinibbatti) of beings in the various orders of beings, the manifestation of the aggregates (khandhānaṃ pātubhāvo), the obtaining of the sense bases (āyatanānaṃ paṭilābho)—this, friends, is called birth (jāti)."

"And what, friends, is aging (jarā)? The aging (jarā), wearing out (jīraṇatā), broken teeth (khaṇḍiccaṃ), gray hair (pāliccaṃ), wrinkled skin (valittacatā), decline of life (āyuno saṃhāni), weakening of the faculties (indriyānaṃ paripāko) of beings in the various orders of beings—this, friends, is called aging (jarā)."

"And what, friends, is death (maraṇa)? The passing away (cuti), deceasing (cavanatā), breaking up (bhedo), disappearance (antaradhānaṃ), dying (maccu), death (maraṇa), completion of time (kālaṃkiriyā), the breaking up of the aggregates (khandhānaṃ bhedo), the discarding of the corpse (kaḷevarassa nikkhepo), the interruption of the life faculty (jīvitindriyassupacchedo) of beings in the various orders of beings—this, friends, is called death (maraṇa)."

"And what, friends, is sorrow (śoka)? Whatever sorrow (śoko), sorrowing (socanā), state of sorrow (socitattaṃ), inner sorrow (antosoko), inner anguish (antoparisoko) of one afflicted by one or another misfortune, touched by one or another painful condition—this, friends, is called sorrow (śoka)."

"And what, friends, is lamentation (paridevo)? Whatever lamenting (ādevo), lamentation (paridevo), act of lamenting (ādevanā), lamenting (paridevanā), state of lamenting (ādevitattaṃ), condition of lamenting (paridevitattaṃ) of one afflicted by one or another misfortune, touched by one or another painful condition—this, friends, is called lamentation (paridevo)."

"And what, friends, is pain (duḥkhaṃ)? Whatever bodily pain (kāyikaṃ dukkhaṃ), bodily discomfort (kāyikaṃ asātaṃ), painful (duḥkhaṃ) uncomfortable (asātaṃ) feeling (vedayitaṃ) born of bodily contact (kāyasamphassajaṃ)—this, friends, is called pain (duḥkhaṃ)."

"And what, friends, is grief (daurmanasyaṃ)? Whatever mental pain (cetasikaṃ dukkhaṃ), mental discomfort (cetasikaṃ asātaṃ), painful (duḥkhaṃ) uncomfortable (asātaṃ) feeling (vedayitaṃ) born of mental contact (manosamphassajaṃ)—this, friends, is called grief (daurmanasyaṃ)."

"And what, friends, is despair (upāyāso)? Whatever anguish (āyāso), despair (upāyāso), state of anguish (āyāsitattaṃ), condition of despair (upāyāsitattaṃ) of one afflicted by one or another misfortune, touched by one or another painful condition—this, friends, is called despair (upāyāso)."

"And what, friends, is not to obtain what one wants as suffering (duḥkha)? In beings subject to birth (jātidhammānaṃ), friends, the wish arises: ‘Oh, that we were not subject to birth (jātidhammā); that birth (jāti) would not come to us!’ But this is not to be obtained by wishing. This too is ‘not to obtain what one wants as suffering (duḥkha)’. In beings subject to aging (jarādhammānaṃ), friends… In beings subject to sickness (vyādhidhammānaṃ), friends… In beings subject to death (maraṇadhammānaṃ), friends… In beings subject to sorrow (śoka), lamentation (parideva), pain (duḥkha), grief (daurmanasya), and despair (upāyāsa) the wish arises: ‘Oh, that we were not subject to sorrow (śoka), lamentation (parideva), pain (duḥkha), grief (daurmanasya), and despair (upāyāsa); that sorrow (śoka), lamentation (parideva), pain (duḥkha), grief (daurmanasya), and despair (upāyāsa) would not come to us!’ But this is not to be obtained by wishing. This too is ‘not to obtain what one wants as suffering (duḥkha)’."

"And what, friends, are in brief the five aggregates subject to grasping (pañcupādānakkhandhā) that are suffering (duḥkha)? They are: the form aggregate subject to grasping (rūpupādānakkhandho), the feeling aggregate subject to grasping (vedanupādānakkhandho), the perception aggregate subject to grasping (saññupādānakkhandho), the mental formation aggregate subject to grasping (saṅkhārupādānakkhandho), the consciousness aggregate subject to grasping (viññāṇupādānakkhandho). These, friends, are called in brief the five aggregates subject to grasping (pañcupādānakkhandhā) that are suffering (duḥkha). This, friends, is called the noble truth of suffering (duḥkhaṃ āryasatyaṃ)."

Yāyaṃ taṇhā ponobbhavikā <note>ponobhavikā (sī. pī.)</note> nandīrāgasahagatā <note>nandirāgasahagatā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note> tatratatrābhinandinī, seyyathidaṃ – kāmataṇhā <pb ed="M" n="3.0294"/> bhavataṇhā vibhavataṇhā <pb ed="P" n="3.0251"/>, idaṃ vuccatāvuso – ‘dukkhasamudayaṃ <note>dukkhasamudayo (syā. kaṃ.)</note> ariyasaccaṃ’’’.

374. "And what, friend, is the noble truth of the origin of suffering (duḥkhasamudaya)? It is this craving (taṇhā) which leads to rebirth (ponobbhavikā), combined with pleasure and lust (nandīrāgasahagatā), finding delight here and there. That is to say, craving for sensual pleasures (kāmataṇhā), craving for existence (bhavataṇhā), and craving for non-existence (vibhavataṇhā). This, friend, is called the noble truth of the origin of suffering (duḥkhasamudaya)."

"And what, friend, is the noble truth of the cessation of suffering (duḥkhanirodha)? It is the complete fading away and cessation of that very craving (taṇhā), its abandonment, relinquishment, liberation, and detachment. This, friend, is called the noble truth of the cessation of suffering (duḥkhanirodha)."

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katamo cāvuso, sammāvāyāmo? Idhāvuso, bhikkhu anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, uppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, anuppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0252"/> uppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, uppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ṭhitiyā <pb ed="M" n="3.0295"/> asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, ayaṃ vuccatāvuso – ‘sammāvāyāmo’’’.

<pb ed="P" n="3.0252"/> <pb ed="M" n="3.0295"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katamā cāvuso, sammāsati? Idhāvuso, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ. Vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati…pe… citte cittānupassī viharati… dhammesu dhammānupassī <pb ed="T" n="3.0455"/> viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, ayaṃ vuccatāvuso – ‘sammāsati’’’.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0455"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Katamo cāvuso, sammāsamādhi? Idhāvuso, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati, vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati, pītiyā ca virāgā upekkhako ca viharati…pe… tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ… <pb ed="V" n="3.0302"/> viharati, ayaṃ vuccatāvuso – ‘sammāsamādhi’. 375. "And what, friends, is the noble truth of the practice that leads to the cessation of suffering? It is just this Noble Eightfold Path; that is, right view (sammādiṭṭhi), right intention (sammāsaṅkappo), right speech (sammāvācā), right action (sammākammanto), right livelihood (sammāājīvo), right effort (sammāvāyāmo), right mindfulness (sammāsati), and right concentration (sammāsamādhi)."

"And what, friends, is right view? It is, friends, the knowledge of suffering (dukkhe ñāṇaṃ), the knowledge of the arising of suffering (dukkhasamudaye ñāṇaṃ), the knowledge of the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhe ñāṇaṃ), and the knowledge of the practice that leads to the cessation of suffering (dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya ñāṇaṃ). This is called right view."

"And what, friends, is right intention? It is the intention of renunciation (nekkhammasaṅkappo), the intention of non-ill will (abyāpādasaṅkappo), and the intention of non-cruelty (avihiṃsāsaṅkappo). This is called right intention."

"And what, friends, is right speech? It is abstaining from false speech (musāvādā veramaṇī), abstaining from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya veramaṇī), abstaining from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya veramaṇī), and abstaining from idle chatter (samphappalāpā veramaṇī). This is called right speech."

"And what, friends, is right action? It is abstaining from killing (pāṇātipātā veramaṇī), abstaining from stealing (adinnādānā veramaṇī), and abstaining from sexual misconduct (kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇī). This is called right action."

"And what, friends, is right livelihood? Here, a noble disciple, having abandoned wrong livelihood, makes a living by right livelihood (sammāājīvena jīvikaṃ kappeti). This is called right livelihood."

"And what, friends, is right effort? Here, a bhikkhu generates desire (chandaṃ janeti), endeavors (vāyamati), activates persistence (vīriyaṃ ārabhati), upholds & exerts his mind (cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati) for the non-arising of unarisen evil unwholesome states; generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his mind for the abandoning of arisen evil unwholesome states; generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his mind for the arising of unarisen wholesome states; generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his mind for the maintenance (ṭhitiyā), non-confusion (asammosāya), increase (bhiyyobhāvāya), plenitude (vepullāya), development (bhāvanāya), & culmination (pāripūriyā) of arisen wholesome states. This is called right effort."

"And what, friends, is right mindfulness? Here, a bhikkhu abides contemplating the body as the body (kāye kāyānupassī viharati), ardent (ātāpī), fully aware (sampajāno), and mindful (satimā), having put away covetousness and grief for the world (vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ).
He abides contemplating feelings as feelings... He abides contemplating mind as mind... He abides contemplating mind-objects as mind-objects, ardent, fully aware, and mindful, having put away covetousness and grief for the world. This is called right mindfulness."

"And what, friends, is right concentration? Here, quite secluded from sensual pleasures (kāmehi), secluded from unwholesome states (akusalehi dhammehi), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought (savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ), with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion (vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ). With the stilling of applied and sustained thought, he enters upon and abides in the second jhāna, which has self-confidence and singleness of mind (ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ) without applied and sustained thought (avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ), with rapture and pleasure born of concentration (samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ). With the fading away as well of rapture, he remains equanimous... the third jhāna... abides. This is called right concentration. This is called the noble truth of the practice that leads to the cessation of suffering."

"At Benares, friends, in the Deer Park at Isipatana, the Tathāgata, the worthy one, the rightly self-awakened one, set in motion the unexcelled Wheel of the Dharma (dhammacakkaṃ pavattitaṃ) that cannot be turned back by any recluse or Brahmin or god or Māra or Brahmā or anyone in the world—that is, the declaring (ācikkhanā), teaching (desanā), making known (paññāpanā), establishing (paṭṭhapanā), opening up (vivaraṇā), analyzing (vibhajanā), and making plain (uttānīkamma) of these Four Noble Truths."

This is what the Venerable Sāriputta said. The bhikkhus were satisfied and delighted in the Venerable Sāriputta’s words.

The Saccavibhaṅgasutta , the eleventh [sutta], is finished.
