THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the Magadhan country and eventually arrived at Rājagaha. There he went to the potter Bhaggava and said to him: “If it is not inconvenient for you, Bhaggava, I will stay one night in your workshop."

“It is not inconvenient for me, venerable sir. There is a renunciate already staying here, who arrived first. If he gives permission, venerable sir, stay as long as you like.”

Then, at that time, a clansman named Pukkusāti, having faith in the Blessed One, had gone forth from the house life into homelessness. He was the first to stay in that potter's workshop.

Then the Blessed One went to the venerable Pukkusāti and said to him: “If it is not inconvenient for you, bhikṣu, I will stay one night in the workshop.”

“It is spacious, friend, the potter’s workshop. Venerable one, stay as long as you like.”

342.Evaṃme sutanti dhātuvibhaṅgasuttaṃ. Tattha cārikanti turitagamanacārikaṃ. Sace te bhaggava agarūti sace tuyhaṃ bhāriyaṃ aphāsukaṃ kiñci natthi. Sace so anujānātīti bhaggavassa kira etadahosi – ‘‘pabbajitā nāma nānāajjhāsayā, eko gaṇābhirato hoti, eko ekābhirato. Sace so ekābhirato bhavissati, ‘āvuso, mā pāvisi, mayā sālā laddhā’ti vakkhati. Sace ayaṃ ekābhirato bhavissati, ‘āvuso, nikkhama, mayā sālā laddhā’ti vakkhati. Evaṃ sante ahaṃ ubhinnaṃ vivādakāretā nāma bhavissāmi, dinnaṃ nāma dinnameva vaṭṭati, kataṃ katamevā’’ti. Kulaputto Vāsūpagato Tatrāyaṃ anupubbikathā – majjhimappadese kira rājagahanagare bimbisāre rajjaṃ kārente paccante takkasīlanagare pukkusāti rājā rajjaṃ kāresi. Atha takkasīlato bhaṇḍaṃ gahetvā vāṇijā rājagahaṃ āgatā paṇṇākāraṃ gahetvā rājānaṃ addasaṃsu. Rājā te vanditvā ṭhite ‘‘katthavāsino tumhe’’ti pucchi. Takkasīlavāsino devāti. Atha ne rājā janapadassa khemasubhikkhatādīni nagarassa ca pavattiṃ pucchitvā ‘‘ko nāma tumhākaṃ rājā’’ti pucchi. Pukkusāti nāma devāti. Dhammikoti? Āma deva dhammiko. Catūhi saṅgahavatthūhi janaṃ saṅgaṇhāti, lokassa mātāpitiṭṭhāne ṭhito, aṅge nipannadārakaṃ viya janaṃ tosetīti. Katarasmiṃ vaye vattatīti? Athassa vayaṃ ācikkhiṃsu. Vayesupi bimbisārena samavayo jāto. Atha te rājā āha – ‘‘tātā tumhākaṃ rājā dhammiko, vayena ca me samāno, sakkuṇeyyātha tumhākaṃ rājānaṃ mama mittaṃ kātu’’nti. Sakkoma devāti. (khu. pā. 6.6; su. ni. 229) – Evaṃ ekadesena saṅghaguṇe likhitvā – ‘‘bhagavato sāsanaṃ svākkhātaṃ niyyānikaṃ, sace mayhaṃ sahāyo sakkoti, nikkhamitvā pabbajatū’’ti likhitvā suvaṇṇapaṭṭaṃ saṃharitvā sukhumakambalena veṭhetvā sārasamugge pakkhipitvā taṃ samuggaṃ suvaṇṇamaye, suvaṇṇamayaṃ, rajatamaye rajatamayaṃ maṇimaye, maṇimayaṃ pavāḷamaye, pavāḷamayaṃ lohitaṅkamaye, lohitaṅkamayaṃ masāragallamaye, masāragallamayaṃ phalikamaye, phalikamayaṃ dantamaye, dantamayaṃ sabbaratanamaye, sabbaratanamayaṃ kilañjamaye, kilañjamayaṃ samuggaṃ sārakaraṇḍake ṭhapesi. Puna sārakaraṇḍakaṃ suvaṇṇakaraṇḍaketi purimanayeneva haritvā sabbaratanamayaṃ karaṇḍakaṃ kilañjamaye karaṇḍake ṭhapesi. Tato kilañjamayaṃ karaṇḍakaṃ sāramayapeḷāyāti puna vuttanayeneva haritvā sabbaratanamayaṃ peḷaṃ kilañjamayapeḷāya ṭhapetvā bahi vatthena veṭhetvā rājamuddikāya lañchetvā amacce āṇāpesi – ‘‘mama āṇāpavattiṭṭhāne maggaṃ alaṅkārāpetha maggo aṭṭhusabhavitthato hotu, catuusabhaṭṭhānaṃ sodhitamattakameva hotu, majjhe catuusabhaṃ rājānubhāvena paṭiyādethā’’ti. Tato maṅgalahatthiṃ alaṅkārāpetvā tassa upari pallaṅkaṃ paññapetvā setacchattaṃ ussāpetvā nagaravīthiyo sittasammaṭṭhā samussitaddhajapaṭākā kadalipuṇṇaghaṭagandhadhūmapupphādīhi suppaṭimaṇḍitā kāretvā ‘‘attano attano visayappadese evarūpaṃ pūjaṃ kārentū’’ti antarabhogikānaṃ javanadūte pesetvā sayaṃ sabbālaṅkārena alaṅkaritvā – ‘‘sabbatāḷāvacarasammissabalakāyaparivuto paṇṇākāraṃ pesemī’’ti attano visayapariyantaṃ gantvā amaccassa mukhasāsanaṃ adāsi – ‘‘tāta mayhaṃ sahāyo pukkusāti imaṃ paṇṇākāraṃ paṭicchanto orodhamajjhe apaṭicchitvā pāsādaṃ āruyha paṭicchatū’’ti. Evaṃ sāsanaṃ datvā paccantadesaṃ satthā gacchatīti pañcapatiṭṭhitena vanditvā nivatti. Antarabhogikā teneva niyāmena maggaṃ paṭiyādetvā paṇṇākāraṃ nayiṃsu. Pukkusātipi attano rajjasīmato paṭṭhāya teneva niyāmena maggaṃ paṭiyādetvā nagaraṃ alaṅkārāpetvā paṇṇākārassa paccuggamanaṃ akāsi. Paṇṇākāro takkasīlaṃ pāpuṇanto uposathadivase pāpuṇi, paṇṇākāraṃ gahetvā gataamaccopi rañño vuttasāsanaṃ ārocesi. Rājā taṃ sutvā paṇṇākārena saddhiṃ āgatānaṃ kattabbakiccaṃ vicāretvā paṇṇākāraṃ ādāya pāsādaṃ āruyha ‘‘mā idha koci pavisatū’’ti dvāre ārakkhaṃ kāretvā sīhapañjaraṃ vivaritvā paṇṇākāraṃ uccāsane ṭhapetvā sayaṃ nīcāsane nisinno lañchanaṃ bhinditvā nivāsanaṃ apanetvā kilañjapeḷato paṭṭhāya anupubbena vivaranto sāramayaṃ samuggaṃ disvā cintesi – ‘‘mahāparihāro nāyaṃ aññassa ratanassa bhavissati, addhā majjhimadese sotabbayuttakaṃ ratanaṃ uppanna’’nti. Atha taṃ samuggaṃ vivaritvā rājalañchanaṃ bhinditvā sukhumakambalaṃ ubhato viyūhitvā suvaṇṇapaṭṭaṃ addasa. So taṃ pasāretvā – ‘‘manāpāni vata akkharāni samasīsāni samapantīni caturassānī’’tiādito paṭṭhāya vācetuṃ ārabhi. Tassa – ‘‘idha tathāgato loke uppanno’’ti buddhaguṇe vācentassa balavasomanassaṃ uppajji, navanavutilomakūpasahassāni uddhaggalomāni ahesuṃ. Attano ṭhitabhāvaṃ vā nisinnabhāvaṃ vā na jānāti. Athassa – ‘‘kappakoṭisatasahassehipi etaṃ dullabhasāsanaṃ sahāyaṃ nissāya sotuṃ labhāmī’’ti bhiyyo balavapīti udapādi. So hi upari vācetuṃ asakkonto yāva pītivegapassaddhiyā nisīditvā parato – ‘‘svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo’’ti dhammaguṇe ārabhi. Tatrāpissa tatheva ahosi. So puna yāva pītivegapassaddhiyā nisīditvā parato ‘‘suppaṭipanno’’ti saṅghaguṇe ārabhi . Tatrāpissa tatheva ahosi. Atha sabbapariyante ānāpānassatikammaṭṭhānaṃ vācetvā catukkapañcakajjhānāni nibbattesi, so jhānasukheneva vītināmesi. Añño koci daṭṭhuṃ na labhati, ekova cūḷupaṭṭhāko pavisati. Evaṃ addhamāsamattaṃ vītināmesi. Nāgarā rājaṅgaṇe sannipatitvā ukkuṭṭhiṃ akaṃsu ‘‘paṇṇākāraṃ paṭicchitadivasato paṭṭhāya baladassanaṃ vā nāṭakadassanaṃ vā natthi, vinicchayadānaṃ natthi, rājā sahāyena pahitaṃ paṇṇākāraṃ yassicchati tassa dassetu, rājāno nāma ekaccassa paṇṇākāravasenapi vañcetvā rajjaṃ attano kātuṃ vāyamanti. Kiṃ nāma amhākaṃ rājā karotī’’ti? Rājā ukkuṭṭhisaddaṃ sutvā – ‘‘rajjaṃ nu kho dhāremi, udāhu satthāra’’nti cintesi. Athassa etadahosi – ‘‘rajjakāritaattabhāvo nāma neva gaṇakena, na gaṇakamahāmattena gaṇetuṃ sakko. Satthusāsanaṃ dhāressāmī’’ti sayane ṭhapitaṃ asiṃ gahetvā kese chinditvā sīhapañjaraṃ vivaritvā – ‘‘etaṃ gahetvā rajjaṃ kārethā’’ti saddhiṃ cūḷāmaṇinā kesakalāpaṃ parisamajjhe pātesi, mahājano taṃ ukkhipitvā – ‘‘sahāyakasantikā laddhapaṇṇākārā nāma rājāno tumhādisā honti devā’’ti ekappahāreneva viravi. Raññopi dvaṅgulamattaṃ kesamassu ahosi. Bodhisattassa pabbajjāsadisameva kira jātaṃ. Tato cūḷupaṭṭhākaṃ pesetvā antarāpaṇā dve kāsāyavatthāni mattikāpattañca āharāpetvā – ‘‘ye loke arahanto, te uddissa mayhaṃ pabbajjā’’ti satthāraṃ uddissa ekaṃ kāsāvaṃ nivāsetvā ekaṃ pārupitvā pattaṃ vāmaaṃsakūṭe katvā kattaradaṇḍaṃ gahetvā – ‘‘sobhati nu kho me pabbajjā no vā’’ti mahātale katipayavāre aparāparaṃ caṅkamitvā – ‘‘sobhati me pabbajjā’’ti dvāraṃ vivaritvā pāsādā otari. Otarantaṃ pana naṃ tīsu dvāresu ṭhitanāṭakādīni disvāpi na sañjāniṃsu. ‘‘Eko paccekabuddho amhākaṃ rañño dhammakathaṃ kathetuṃ āgato’’ti kira cintayiṃsu. Uparipāsādaṃ pana āruyha rañño ṭhitanisinnaṭṭhānāni disvā rājā gatoti ñatvā samuddamajjhe osīdamānāya nāvāya jano viya ekappahāreneva viraviṃsu. Kulaputtaṃ bhūmitalaṃ otiṇṇamattaṃ aṭṭhārasaseniyo sabbe nāgarā balakāyā ca parivāretvā mahāviravaṃ viraviṃsu. Amaccāpi taṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘deva majjhimadesarājāno nāma bahumāyā, sāsanaṃ pesetvā buddharatanaṃ nāma loke uppannaṃ vā no vāti ñatvā gamissatha , nivattatha devā’’ti. Saddahāmahaṃ mayhaṃ sahāyakassa, tassa mayā saddhiṃ dvejjhavacanaṃ nāma natthi, tiṭṭhatha tumheti. Te anugacchantiyeva. Kulaputto kattaradaṇḍena lekhaṃ katvā – ‘‘idaṃ rajjaṃ kassā’’ti āha? Tumhākaṃ devāti. Yo imaṃ lekhaṃ antaraṃ karoti, rājāṇāya kāretabboti. Mahājanakajātake bodhisattena katalekhaṃ sīvalidevī antaraṃ kātuṃ avisahantī vivattamānā agamāsi. Tassā gatamaggena mahājano agamāsi. Taṃ pana lekhaṃ mahājano antaraṃ kātuṃ na visahi, lekhaṃ ussīsakaṃ katvā vivattamānā viraviṃsu. Kulaputto ‘‘ayaṃ me gataṭṭhāne dantakaṭṭhaṃ vā mukhodakaṃ vā dassatī’’ti antamaso ekaceṭakampi aggahetvā pakkāmi. Evaṃ kirassa ahosi ‘‘mama satthā ca mahābhinikkhamanaṃ nikkhamitvā ekakova pabbajito’’ti ekakova agamāsi. ‘‘Satthu lajjāmī’’ti ca – ‘‘satthā kira me pabbajitvā yānaṃ nāruḷho’’ti ca antamaso ekapaṭalikampi upāhanaṃ nāruhi, paṇṇacchattakampi na dhāresi. Mahājano rukkhapākāraṭṭālakādīni āruyha esa amhākaṃ rājā gacchatīti olokesi. Kulaputto – ‘‘dūraṃ gantabbaṃ, na sakkā ekena maggo nittharitu’’nti ekaṃ satthavāhaṃ anubandhi. Sukhumālassa kulaputtassa kaṭhinatattāya pathaviyā gachantassa pādatalesu phoṭā uṭṭhahitvā bhijjanti, dukkhā vedanā uppajjanti. Satthavāhe khandhāvāraṃ bandhitvā nisinne kulaputto maggā okkamma ekasmiṃ rukkhamūle nisīdati. Nisinnaṭṭhāne pādaparikammaṃ vā piṭṭhiparikammaṃ vā kattā nāma natthi, kulaputto ānāpānacatutthajjhānaṃ samāpajjitvā maggadarathakilamathapariḷāhaṃ vikkhambhetvā jhānaratiyā vītināmeti. Punadivase uṭṭhite aruṇe sarīrapaṭijagganaṃ katvā puna satthavāhaṃ anubandhati. Pātarāsakāle kulaputtassa pattaṃ gahetvā khādanīyaṃ bhojanīyaṃ patte pakkhipitvā denti. Taṃ uttaṇḍulampi hoti kilinnampi samasakkharampi aloṇātiloṇampi, kulaputto pavisanaṭṭhānaṃ paccavekkhitvā amataṃ viya paribhuñjitvā etena niyāmena aṭṭhahi ūnakāni dve yojanasatāni gato. Jetavanadvārakoṭṭhakassa pana samīpena gacchantopi – ‘‘kahaṃ satthā vasatī’’ti nāpucchi. Kasmā? Satthugāravena ceva rañño pesitasāsanavasena ca. Rañño hi – ‘‘idha tathāgato loke uppajjatī’’ti satthāraṃ rājagahe uppannaṃ viya katvā sāsanaṃ pesitaṃ, tasmā naṃ apucchitvāva pañcacattālīsayojanamattaṃ maggaṃ atikkanto. So sūriyatthaṅgamanavelāya rājagahaṃ patvā satthā kahaṃ vasatīti pucchi. Kuto nu, bhante, āgatoti? Ito uttaratoti. Satthā tuyhaṃ āgatamagge ito pañcacattālīsayojanamatte sāvatthi nāma atthi, tattha vasatīti. Kulaputto cintesi – ‘‘idāni akālo na sakkā gantuṃ, ajja idheva vasitvā sve satthu santikaṃ gamissāmī’’ti. Tato – ‘‘vikāle sampattapabbajitā kahaṃ vasantī’’ti pucchi. Imāya kumbhakārasālāya, bhanteti. Atha so taṃ kumbhakāraṃ yācitvā tattha vāsatthāya pavisitvā nisīdi. Bhagavāpi taṃdivasaṃ paccūsakāle lokaṃ volokento pukkusātiṃ disvā cintesi – ‘‘ayaṃ kulaputto sahāyena pesitaṃ sāsanamattakaṃ vācetvā atirekatiyojanasatikaṃ mahārajjaṃ pahāya maṃ uddissa pabbajitvā aṭṭhahi ūnakāni dve yojanasatāni atikkamma rājagahaṃ pāpuṇissati, mayi agacchante pana tīṇi sāmaññaphalāni appaṭivijjhitvā ekarattivāsena anāthakālakiriyaṃ karissati, mayi pana gate tīṇi sāmaññaphalāni paṭivijjhissati. Janasaṅgahatthāyeva pana mayā satasahassakappādhikāni cattāri asaṅkhyeyyāni pāramiyo pūritā, karissāmi tassa saṅgaha’’nti pātova sarīrapaṭijagganaṃ katvā bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya caritvā pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātappaṭikkanto gandhakuṭiṃ pavisitvā muhuttaṃ attadarathakilamathaṃ paṭipassambhetvā – ‘‘kulaputto mayi gāravena dukkaraṃ akāsi, atirekayojanasataṃ rajjaṃ pahāya antamaso mukhadhovanadāyakampi ceṭakaṃ aggahetvā ekakova nikkhanto’’ti sāriputtamahāmoggallānādīsu kañci anāmantetvā sayameva attano pattacīvaraṃ gahetvā ekakova nikkhanto. Gacchanto ca neva ākāse uppati, na pathaviṃ saṃkhipi, – ‘‘kulaputto mama lajjamāno hatthiassarathasuvaṇṇasivikādīsu ekayānepi anisīditvā antamaso ekapaṭalikaṃ upāhanampi anāruyha paṇṇacchattakampi aggahetvā nikkhanto, mayāpi padasāva gantuṃ vaṭṭatī’’ti pana cintetvā padasāva agamāsi. So asīti anubyañjanāni byāmappabhā bāttiṃsa mahāpurisalakkhaṇānīti imaṃ buddhasiriṃ paṭicchādetvā valāhakapaṭicchanno puṇṇacando viya aññatarabhikkhuvesena gacchanto ekapacchābhatteneva pañcacattālīsa yojanāni atikkamma sūriyatthaṅgamalīvelāya kulaputte paviṭṭhamatteyeva taṃ kumbhakārasālaṃ pāpuṇi. Taṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ – ‘‘tena kho pana samayena, pukkusāti, nāma kulaputto bhagavantaṃ uddissa saddhāya agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito, so tasmiṃ kumbhakārāvesane paṭhamaṃ vāsūpagato hotī’’ti. Evaṃ gantvāpi pana bhagavā – ‘‘ahaṃ sammāsambuddho’’ti pasayha kumbhakārasālaṃ apavisitvā dvāre ṭhitova kulaputtaṃ okāsaṃ kārento sace te bhikkhūtiādimāha. Urundanti vivittaṃ asambādhaṃ. Viharatāyasmā yathāsukhanti yena yena iriyāpathena phāsu hoti, tena tena yathāsukhaṃ āyasmā viharatūti okāsaṃ akāsi. Atirekatiyojanasatañhi rajjaṃ pahāya pabbajito kulaputto parassa chaḍḍitapatitaṃ kumbhakārasālaṃ kiṃ aññassa sabrahmacārino maccharāyissati. Ekacce pana moghapurisā sāsane pabbajitvā āvāsamacchariyādīhi abhibhūtā attano vasanaṭṭhāne mayhaṃ kuṭi mayhaṃ pariveṇanti aññesaṃ avāsāya parakkamanti. Nisīdīti accantasukhumālo lokanātho devavimānasadisaṃ gandhakuṭiṃ pahāya tattha tattha vippakiṇṇachārikāya bhinnabhājanatiṇapalāsakukkuṭasūkaravaccādisaṃkiliṭṭhāya saṅkāraṭṭhānasadisāya kumbhakārasālāya tiṇasanthāraṃ santharitvā paṃsukūlacīvaraṃ paññapetvā devavimānasadisaṃ dibbagandhasugandhaṃ gandhakuṭiṃ pavisitvā nisīdanto viya nisīdi. sace te bhikkhū Urunda Viharatāyasmā yathāsukha Nisīdī Iti bhagavāpi asambhinnamahāsammatavaṃse uppanno, kulaputtopi khattiyagabbhe vaḍḍhito. Bhagavāpi abhinīhārasampanno, kulaputtopi abhinīhārasampanno. Bhagavāpi rajjaṃ pahāya pabbajito, kulaputtopi. Bhagavāpi suvaṇṇavaṇṇo, kulaputtopi. Bhagavāpi samāpattilābhī, kulaputtopi. Iti dvepi khattiyā dvepi abhinīhārasampannā dvepi rājapabbajitā dvepi suvaṇṇavaṇṇā dvepi samāpattilābhino kumbhakārasālaṃ pavisitvā nisinnāti tehi kumbhakārasālā ativiya sobhati, dvīhi sīhādīhi paviṭṭhaguhādīhi āharitvā dīpetabbaṃ. Tesu pana dvīsu bhagavā – ‘‘sukhumālo ahaṃ paramasukhumālo ekapacchābhattena pañcacattālīsa yojanāni āgato, muhuttaṃ tāva sīhaseyyaṃ kappetvā maggadarathaṃ paṭipassambhemī’’ti cittampi anuppādetvā nisīdantova phalasamāpattiṃ samāpajji. Kulaputtopi – ‘‘dvānavutiyojanasataṃ āgatomhi, muhuttaṃ tāva nipajjitvā maggadarathaṃ vinodemī’’ti cittaṃ anuppādetvā nisīdamānova ānāpānacatutthajjhānaṃ samāpajji. Idaṃ sandhāya atha kho bhagavā bahudeva rattintiādi vuttaṃ. atha kho bhagavā bahudeva ratti Nanu ca bhagavā kulaputtassa dhammaṃ desessāmīti āgato, kasmā na desesīti? Kulaputtassa maggadaratho appaṭipassaddho, na sakkhissati dhammadesanaṃ sampaṭicchituṃ, so tāvassa paṭipassambhatūti na desesi. Apare – ‘‘rājagahaṃ nāma ākiṇṇamanussaṃ avivittaṃ dasahi saddehi, so saddo diyaḍḍhayāmamattena sannisīdati, taṃ āgamento na desesī’’ti vadanti. Taṃ akāraṇaṃ, brahmalokappamāṇampi hi saddaṃ bhagavā attano ānubhāvena vūpasametuṃ sakkoti, maggadarathavūpasamaṃ āgamentoyeva pana na desesi. Tattha bahudeva rattinti diyaḍḍhayāmamattaṃ. Etadahosīti bhagavā phalasamāpattito vuṭṭhāya suvaṇṇavimāne maṇisīhapañjaraṃ vivaranto viya pañcapasādappaṭimaṇḍitāni akkhīni ummīletvā olokesi, athassa hatthakukkuccapādakukkuccasīsakampanavirahitaṃ sunikhātaindakhīlaṃ viya niccalaṃ avibbhantaṃ suvaṇṇapaṭimaṃ viya nisinnaṃ kulaputtaṃ disvā etaṃ – ‘‘pāsādikaṃ kho’’tiādi ahosi. Tattha pāsādikanti pasādāvahaṃ. Bhāvanapuṃsakaṃ panetaṃ, pāsādikena iriyāpathena iriyati. Yathā iriyato iriyāpatho pāsādiko hoti, evaṃ iriyatīti ayamettha attho. Catūsu hi iriyāpathesu tayo iriyāpathā na sobhanti. Gacchantassa hi bhikkhuno hatthā calanti, pādā calanti, sīsaṃ calati, ṭhitassa kāyo thaddho hoti, nipannassāpi iriyāpatho amanāpo hoti, pacchābhatte pana divāṭṭhānaṃ sammajjitvā cammakhaṇḍaṃ paññapetvā sudhotahatthapādassa catusandhikapallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā nipannasseva iriyāpatho sobhati. Ayañca kulaputto pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā ānāpānacatutthajjhānaṃ appetvā nisīdi. Itissa iriyāpatheneva pasanno bhagavā – ‘‘pāsādikaṃ kho’’ti parivitakkesi. bahudeva ratti Etadahosī pāsādika Yaṃnūnāhaṃ puccheyyanti kasmā pucchati? 343. Cha dhāturo ayanti: The Bhagavān, without elaborating on the preliminary practice of a noble son, immediately commenced teaching the characteristic of insight (vipaśyanā), which is the ultimate emptiness (atyanta-śūnyatā) that serves as the foundation (pada-sthāna-bhūta) for arhat-ship.

If the preliminary practice is impure, he first teaches moral restraint (śīla-saṃvara), guarding the senses (indriyesu guttadvārataṃ), moderation in food (bhojane mattaññutaṃ), engagement in wakefulness (jāgariyānuyogaṃ), the seven good qualities (satta saddhamme), and the four dhyānas—thus, he teaches this preliminary practice.

But if that is pure, he does not elaborate on that; he teaches only insight (vipaśyanā) as the foundation (pada-sthāna-bhūtaṃ) for arhat-ship.

And the preliminary practice of the noble son is pure.

Thus, by causing him to recite the teaching (śāsanaṃ vācetvā) while remaining in the upper story of the palace (pāsādavaragatena eva), the fourth jhāna of mindfulness of breathing (ānāpāna-catutthajjhānaṃ nibbattitaṃ) was produced; that which accomplished the task of travel (yāna-kiccaṃ sādhesi) for him who was coming a distance of ninety-two yojanas; his monastic precepts (śrāmaṇera-śīlam), too, were complete (paripūrṇam).

Therefore, without elaborating on the preliminary practice, he immediately commenced teaching the characteristic of insight (vipaśyanā), which is the ultimate emptiness (atyanta-śūnyatā) that serves as the foundation (pada-sthāna-bhūta) for arhat-ship.

There, cha dhāturoti means that the elements (dhātuyo) are present, but the person (puriso) is not present.

The Bhagavān shows the non-existent (avijjamānaṃ) by means of the existent (vijjamānena), the existent (vijjamānaṃ) by means of the non-existent (avijjamānena), the existent (vijjamānaṃ) by means of the existent (vijjamānena), and the non-existent (avijjamānaṃ) by means of the non-existent (avijjamānaṃ). This should be expanded in accordance with the method stated in the discourse on all the defilements (sabbāsave vuttanayeneva vitthāretabbaṃ).

Here, however, showing the non-existent (avijjamānaṃ) by means of the existent (vijjamānena), he speaks thus:

"If, indeed, the Bhagavān, having rejected the designation (paṇṇattiṃ vissajjetvā) 'person (puriso),' and saying only 'elements (dhātuyo)', were to establish the mind (cittaṃ upaṭṭhāpeyya), the noble son would be in doubt (sandehaṃ kareyya), would fall into confusion (sammohaṃ āpajjeyya), and would not be able to accept the teaching (desanaṃ sampaṭicchituṃ na sakkuṇeyya).

This means: "That which you recognize as a person (puriso), that is cha-dhātuko. But there is no person (puriso) in reality (paramārthato). A person (puriso), however, is merely a designation (paṇṇattimattameva)."

Having turned away from this very thing (so etto va vivaṭṭitvā), while taking hold of arhat-ship (arahattaṃ gaṇhamāno), which is the supreme accomplishment (uttama-siddhi-bhūtaṃ), he takes hold by standing on these four grounds (imesu catūsu ṭhānesu patiṭṭhāya gaṇhāti)." Yo tāva imasmiṃ sāsane pabbajitvā vejjakammādivasena ekavīsatividhāya anesanāya jīvikaṃ kappento pabbajjānurūpena cittuppādaṃ ṭhapetuṃ na sakkoti, ayaṃ paññaṃ pamajjati nāma. Imasmiṃ pana sutte dhātukammaṭṭhānavasena esa paññāya appamādo vutto. Dhātukammaṭṭhāne panettha yaṃ vattabbaṃ, taṃ heṭṭhā hatthipadopamasuttādīsu vuttameva.
348. Now, according to the nikkhittamātikā, it should be stated, "Where, if he remains, thoughts do not arise," but upon attaining arhatship, there is no further need for things like "one should not neglect wisdom" and so forth.

Thus, the Blessed One, having set aside the mātikā with its reversed order of elements, spoke as follows while distinguishing the distinctions in accordance with the nature of the dharma: "One should not neglect wisdom" and so forth.

Therein, who neglects wisdom, and who does not neglect wisdom? He who, having gone forth in this dispensation (sāsana), makes a living by means of twenty-one kinds of improper livelihood like medical practice, is unable to establish a mind-moment (cittuppāda) in accordance with his ordination; this one is said to neglect wisdom.

But he who, having gone forth in the dispensation, being established in morality (sīla), having learned the Buddha's word (buddhavacana), having undertaken suitable ascetic practices (dhutaṅga), having taken up a practice (kammaṭṭhāna) pleasing to the mind, relying on a secluded dwelling (senāsana), having performed the preliminary exercises (parikamma) with kasina, having attained samāpatti, thinking "today itself [I will attain] arhatship," cultivates insight (vipaśyanā) and wanders about, this one is said not to neglect wisdom.

In this sūtra, this non-neglect of wisdom (apramāda) is spoken of in terms of the element-practice (dhātukammaṭṭhāna).

And herein, whatever should be said about the element-practice, that has already been said below in the Hatthipadopama Sutta and so forth.

354. Furthermore, "then only consciousness remains" (athāparaṃ viññāṇaṃyeva avasissatīti) is in this context a connection that pertains to a specific text. Below, the rūpakammaṭṭhāna has been discussed. Now, this teaching (desanā) is begun in order to show, by producing the arūpakammaṭṭhāna through the perspective of feeling (vedanāvasena). There, "remains" (avasissatīti): for what purpose does it remain? It remains for the purpose of the teacher's statement (satthu kathanatthāya), and for the clansman's (kulaputtassa) penetration (paṭivijjhanatthāya). "Pure" (parisuddhaṃ) means without defilements (nirupakkilesaṃ). "Bright" (pariyodātaṃ) means radiant (pabhassaraṃ). "He understands 'pleasant' (sukhantipi vijānātīti)" means experiencing a pleasant feeling (sukhavedanaṃ), he understands, "I am experiencing a pleasant feeling" (sukhavedanaṃ vedayāmīti pajānāti). But it was spoken of in the satipaṭṭhāna; it should be understood according to the method spoken of there. "Leading to a pleasant feeling (sukhavedaniyaṃti)" etc. is said for the purpose of showing arising, cessation, and going away, in terms of conditions. There, "leading to a pleasant feeling" (sukhavedaniyaṃti) means the condition for a pleasant feeling (sukhavedanāya paccayabhūtaṃ). The same method (seva nayo) applies to the remaining phrases (sesapadesupi).

367.Pubbeti puthujjanakāle. Upadhīhontīti khandhūpadhi kilesūpadhi abhisaṅkhārūpadhi pañcakāmaguṇūpadhīti ime upadhayo honti. Samattā samādinnāti paripūrā gahitā paramaṭṭhā. Tasmāti yasmā ādito samathavipassanāvasena kilesapariccāgato, sotāpattimaggādīhi ca kilesapariccāgato arahattamaggeneva kilesapariccāgo uttaritaro, tasmā. Evaṃ samannāgatoti iminā uttamena cāgādhiṭṭhānena samannāgato.
Pubbe means at the time of being an ordinary person.

Upadhi hontī means these are the upadhis: the upadhi of the aggregates (skandha), the upadhi of the defilements (kilesa), the upadhi of the [karmic] formations (abhisaṅkhāra), and the upadhi of the five strands of sense pleasure (pañcakāmaguṇa).

Samattā samādinnā means completely grasped, fully taken up, ultimate.

Tasmā means since, from the beginning, through abandoning the defilements by way of serenity and insight, and through abandoning the defilements by way of the path of stream-entry, etc., the abandoning of the defilements by the path of arahantship alone is superior; therefore.

Evaṃ samannāgato means endowed with this supreme basis of generosity.

368. ‘‘Tasseva kho pana pubbe aviddasuno abhijjhā hoti chando sārāgo. Svāssa pahīno hoti ucchinnamūlo tālāvatthukato anabhāvaṃkato āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo. Tasseva kho pana pubbe aviddasuno āghāto hoti byāpādo sampadoso. Svāssa pahīno hoti ucchinnamūlo tālāvatthukato anabhāvaṃkato āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo. Tasmā evaṃ samannāgato bhikkhu iminā paramena upasamādhiṭṭhānena samannāgato hoti. Eso hi, bhikkhu, paramo ariyo upasamo yadidaṃ – rāgadosamohānaṃ upasamo. ‘Paññaṃ nappamajjeyya, saccamanurakkheyya, cāgamanubrūheyya, santimeva so sikkheyyā’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ.
368. “Formerly, when he was ignorant (aviddasuno), he experienced covetousness (abhijjhā), desire (chando), and lust (sārāgo).

But now, these are abandoned (pahīno hoti), cut off at the root (ucchinnamūlo), made like a palm stump (tālavatthukato), obliterated (anabhāvaṃkato) so that it is no longer subject to future arising (āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo).

Formerly, when he was ignorant, he experienced anger (āghāto), ill will (byāpādo), and hate (sampadoso).

But now, these are abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated so that it is no longer subject to future arising.

Formerly, when he was ignorant, he experienced ignorance (avijjā) and delusion (sammoho).

But now, these are abandoned, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, obliterated so that it is no longer subject to future arising.

Therefore, a bhikkhu possessing this possesses the supreme foundation of peace (upasamādhiṭṭhānena samannāgato hoti).

For this, bhikkhu, is the supreme noble peace (paramo ariyo upasamo), namely, the pacification (upasamo) of lust (rāga), hate (dosa), and delusion (mohānam).

“One should not neglect wisdom (paññaṃ nappamajjeyya), should preserve truth (saccamanurakkheyya), should cultivate relinquishment (cāgamanubrūheyya), and should train for peace (santimeva so sikkheyyā’ti)’—so it was with reference to this that it was said (iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ).
368.Āghātotiādīsu āghātakaraṇavasena āghāto, byāpajjanavasena byāpādo, sampadussanavasena sampadosoti tīhi padehi dosākusalamūlameva vuttaṃ. Tasmāti yasmā ādito samathavipassanāvasena kilesavūpasamato, sotāpattimaggādīhi kilesavūpasamato ca arahattamaggeneva kilesavūpasamo uttaritaro, tasmā. Evaṃ samannāgatoti iminā uttamena upasamādhiṭṭhānena samannāgato.
368. Āghāto etc.: āghāta due to the manner of doing harm, byāpāda due to the manner of malevolence, sampadosa due to the manner of corruption, thus, by these three terms, only the root of hatred and unwholesomeness is stated.

Tasmā because from the beginning, through the means of śamatha and vipaśyanā, from the subsiding of the afflictions (kleśa), the subsiding of the afflictions by means of the path of stream-entry (srotāpatti-mārga) etc., and the subsiding of the afflictions through the path of arhattva alone is superior, therefore.

Evaṃ samannāgato: thus, endowed, i.e., endowed with this excellent basis of tranquility (upaśamādhiṣṭhāna).

369. ‘‘‘Yattha ṭhitaṃ maññassavā nappavattanti, maññassave kho pana nappavattamāne muni santoti vuccatī’ti – iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ. Kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? ‘Asmī’ti, bhikkhu, maññitametaṃ, ‘ayamahamasmī’ti maññitametaṃ, ‘bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘na bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘rūpī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘arūpī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘saññī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘asaññī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘nevasaññīnāsaññī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ. Maññitaṃ, bhikkhu, rogo maññitaṃ gaṇḍo maññitaṃ sallaṃ. Sabbamaññitānaṃ tveva, bhikkhu, samatikkamā muni santoti vuccati. Muni kho pana, bhikkhu, santo na jāyati, na jīyati, na mīyati, na kuppati, na piheti. Tañhissa, bhikkhu, natthi yena jāyetha, ajāyamāno kiṃ jīyissati, ajīyamāno kiṃ mīyissati, amīyamāno kiṃ kuppissati, akuppamāno kissa kiṃ (ka.) pihessati? ‘Yattha ṭhitaṃ maññassavā nappavattanti, maññassave kho pana nappavattamāne muni santoti vuccatī’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ. Imaṃ kho me tvaṃ, bhikkhu, saṃkhittena chadhātuvibhaṅgaṃ dhārehī’’ti.
369. ‘Where the tides of conceiving (maññassavā) do not sweep over one who stands firm (ṭhitaṃ), and when the tides of conceiving (maññassave) no longer sweep over him, he is called a sage (muni) at peace (santo).’ So it was said.

With reference to what was this said? ‘I am’ (asmī’ti), bhikkhu, this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ); ‘I am this’ (ayamahamasmī’ti), this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ); ‘I will be’ (bhavissa’nti), this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ); ‘I will not be’ (na bhavissa’nti), this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ); ‘I will be with form (rūpī)’ (rūpī bhavissa’nti), this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ); ‘I will be without form (arūpī)’ (arūpī bhavissa’nti), this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ); ‘I will be with perception (saññī)’ (saññī bhavissa’nti), this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ); ‘I will be without perception (asaññī)’ (asaññī bhavissa’nti), this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ); ‘I will be neither with nor without perception (nevasaññīnāsaññī)’ (nevasaññīnāsaññī bhavissa’nti), this is conceiving (maññitametaṃ).

Conceiving (maññitaṃ), bhikkhu, is a disease (rogo), conceiving (maññitaṃ) is a tumor (gaṇḍo), conceiving (maññitaṃ) is a dart (sallaṃ).

By overcoming all conceivings (sabbamaññitānaṃ), bhikkhu, one is called a sage (muni) at peace (santo).

And the sage (muni) at peace (santo), bhikkhu, is not born (na jāyati), does not age (na jīyati), does not die (na mīyati), is not shaken (na kuppati), and is not agitated (na piheti).

For there is nothing present in him by which he might be born (jāyetha). Not being born (ajāyamāno), how could he age (jīyissati)? Not aging (ajīyamāno), how could he die (mīyissati)? Not dying (amīyamāno), how could he be shaken (kuppissati)? Not being shaken (akuppamāno), why should he be agitated (pihessati)? 'Where the tides of conceiving (maññassavā) do not sweep over one who stands firm (ṭhitaṃ), and when the tides of conceiving (maññassave) no longer sweep over him, he is called a sage (muni) at peace (santo).' So it was with reference to this that it was said (idaṃetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ).

Bhikkhu, bear in mind this brief exposition (saṃkhittena) of the six elements (chadhātuvibhaṅgaṃ).
369.Maññitametanti taṇhāmaññitaṃ mānamaññitaṃ diṭṭhimaññitanti tividhampi vaṭṭati. Ayamahamasmīti ettha pana ayamahanti ekaṃ taṇhāmaññitameva vaṭṭati. Rogotiādīsu ābādhaṭṭhena rogo, antodosaṭṭhena gaṇḍo, anupaviṭṭhaṭṭhena sallaṃ. Muni santoti vuccatīti khīṇāsavamuni santo nibbutoti vuccati. Yattha ṭhitanti yasmiṃ ṭhāne ṭhitaṃ. Saṃkhittenāti buddhānaṃ kira sabbāpi dhammadesanā saṃkhittāva, vitthāradesanā nāma natthi, samantapaṭṭhānakathāpi saṃkhittāyeva. Iti bhagavā desanaṃ yathānusandhiṃ pāpesi. Ugghāṭitaññūtiādīsu pana catūsu puggalesu pukkusāti kulaputto vipañcitaññū, iti vipañcitaññuvasena bhagavā imaṃ dhātuvibhaṅgasuttaṃ kathesi.
369. "Maññitametaṃ" means that it is fitting as three kinds: craving-conceit ( taṇhāmaññitaṃ), pride-conceit (mānamāññitaṃ), and view-conceit (diṭṭhimaññitaṃ).

"Ayamahamasmi" Here, "ayamahaṃ" refers to only one thing: craving-conceit (taṇhāmaññitameva vaṭṭati).

"Rogo" etc., rogo [is explained] in the sense of affliction (ābādhaṭṭhena), gaṇḍo in the sense of internal defect (antodosaṭṭhena), and sallaṃ in the sense of not entering (anupaviṭṭhaṭṭhena).

"Muni santo'ti vuccati" means a khīṇāsava muni is called peaceful, extinguished (nibbutoti vuccati).

"Yattha ṭhitaṃ" means in whatever place (yasmiṃ ṭhāne) one is situated (ṭhitaṃ).

"Saṃkhittenā" means that all the teachings of the Buddhas are concise ( saṃkhittāva); there is no extensive teaching (vitthāradesanā nāma natthi). Even the samantapaṭṭhānakathā is concise (saṃkhittāyeva).

Thus, the Blessed One brought the teaching into accordance with what was suitable (yathānusandhiṃ pāpesi).

Among the four types of individuals beginning with ugghāṭitaññū, Pukkusāti, the son of a good family (kulaputto), is one who understands what is explained in detail (vipañcitaññū). Thus, through understanding what is explained in detail (vipañcitaññuvasena), the Blessed One taught this Dhātuvibhaṅgasuttaṃ.

Rising from his seat, he arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, prostrated himself with his head at the Blessed One's feet, and said to him, "Venerable Sir, a transgression (accayo) overcame me, in that, like a fool, confused and unskilled, I considered it proper to address the Blessed One as 'friend' (āvusovādena). This, bhikkhu, is growth in the discipline (vinaye) of the noble (ariyassa) ones, when one sees one's transgression (accayaṃ) as such (accayato), makes amends in accordance with the Dhamma (yathādhammaṃ), and undertakes restraint (saṃvaraṃ) in the future."

370. “But venerable sir, my bowl and robe (pattacīvara) are not complete.” Why are the bowl and robe made of spiritual power (iddhimayapattacīvara) not produced for a noble one (kulaputta)?

Because of not having given the eight requisites (aṭṭhannaṃ parikkhārānaṃ adinnattā) formerly.

It should not be said that the noble one (kulaputta) is a giver of gifts (dinnadāna) and has made an aspiration (katābhinīhāra), [but] not a giver (dinna).

However, the bowl and robe made of spiritual power (iddhimayapattacīvaraṃ) are produced only for those in their final existence (pacchimabhavikānaṃyeva), and this one is subject to rebirth (punapaṭisandhiko); therefore, they are not produced.

Then, why did the Blessed One (bhagavā) not ordain him after searching for them himself?

Because of the lack of opportunity (okāsābhāvato).

The noble one’s (kulaputtassa) life was exhausted (āyu parikkhīṇaṃ); the pure abodes (suddhāvāsiko), a non-returner (anāgāmī), the great Brahmā (mahābrahmā) was as if sitting after coming to the potter’s shed (kumbhakārasālaṃ āgantvā nisinno viya ahosi).

Therefore, he did not search for them himself.

“He departed in search of a bowl and robe (pattacīvarapariyesanaṃ pakkāmī),” at what time did he depart (kāya velāya pakkāmi)? When the dawn had risen (uṭṭhite aruṇe).

It is said that for the Blessed One (bhagavato) the completion of the teaching of the Dharma (dhammadesanāpariniṭṭhānañca), the rising of the dawn (aruṇuṭṭhānañca), and the emission of rays (rasmivissajjanañca) occurred at one moment (ekakkhaṇe ahosi).

It is said that the Blessed One (bhagavā) emitted the six-colored rays (chabbaṇṇarasmiyo) just after finishing the teaching (desanaṃ niṭṭhapetvāva); the entire potter’s dwelling (sakalakumbhakāranivesanaṃ) was a single illumination (ekapajjotaṃ ahosi); the six-colored rays (chabbaṇṇarasmiyo) having become masses of nets (jālajālā puñjapuñjā hutvā), running (vidhāvantiyo), and in every direction (sabbadisābhāge) were as if covered with golden cloth (suvaṇṇapaṭapariyonaddhe viya ca), and they made [it] as if exceedingly bright with various colored jewel lotuses (nānāvaṇṇakusumaratanavisarasamujjale viya ca akaṃsu).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) resolved, “Let the city-dwellers see me (nagaravāsino maṃ passantū).”

The city-dwellers (nagaravāsino), having seen the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ) said to each other, “It is said that the teacher (satthā) has come, it is said that he is sitting in the potter’s shed (kumbhakārasālāya),” and they announced it to the king (rañño ārocesuṃ).

He departed in search of a bowl and robe (pattacīvarapariyesanaṃ pakkāmī)

The king (rājā) having come, worshiped the teacher (satthāraṃ vanditvā), [and] asked, “Venerable sir (bhante), at what time did you come (kāya velāya āgatत्था)?”

“Yesterday, at sunset, great king (mahārājāti).”

“By what action, Blessed One (bhagavāti)?” The king [replied], “Pukkusāti, your friend (tumhākaṃ sahāyo), having heard the message (sāsanaṃ) sent by you (tumhehi pahitaṃ), having gone out (nikkhamitvā), having gone forth (pabbajitvā), coming (āgacchanto) aiming at me (maṃ uddissa), having passed beyond Śrāvastī (sāvatthiṃ atikkamma), having come forty-five yojanas (pañcacattālīsa yojanāni āgantvā), having entered this potter’s shed (imaṃ kumbhakārasālaṃ pavisitvā nisīdi), I, having come for the sake of helping him (tassa saṅgahatthaṃ āgantvā), spoke the Dharma talk (dhammakathaṃ kathesiṃ); the noble one (kulaputto) penetrated the three fruits (tīṇi phalāni paṭivijjhi), great king (mahārājāti).”

“Where [is he] now, venerable sir (idāni kahaṃ, bhanteti)?” “Having begged for ordination (upasampadaṃ yācitvā), for the sake of searching for a bowl and robe (pattacīvarapariyesanatthaṃ) because of the incompleteness of the bowl and robe (aparipuṇṇapattacīvaratāya), he has gone, great king (gato mahārājāti).”

The king (rājā) went in the direction in which the noble one (kulaputtassa) had gone (gatadisābhāgena agamāsi).

The Blessed One (bhagavāpi) also, having come through the sky (ākāsenāgantvā), appeared (pāturahosi) in the Jeta Grove’s Fragrant Cottage (jetavanagandhakuṭimhiyeva).

The noble one (kulaputto) also, searching for a bowl and robe (pattacīvaraṃ pariyesamāno), did not go to the presence (santikaṃ agamāsi) of either King Bimbisāra (bimbisārarañño) or the Taxila merchants of the leg (takkasīlakānaṃ jaṅghavāṇijānaṃ).

Thus, it occurred to him (evaṃ kirassa ahosi) – “It is not proper (na kho me yuttaṃ) to search for a bowl and robe (pattacīvaraṃ pariyesituṃ) gleaning (vicinitvā) only what is agreeable and disagreeable (manāpāmanāpameva) there and there (tattha tattha) like a chicken (kukkuṭassa viya); having avoided a great city (mahantaṃ nagaraṃ vajjitvā), I will search in the water-crossing places, cemeteries, refuse heap places, and inner streets (udakatitthasusānasaṅkāraṭṭhānaantaravīthīsu pariyesissāmi),” thus, in an inner street (antaravīthiyaṃ), he began (āraddho) to search for rags (pilotikaṃ pariyesituṃ) in a heap of refuse (saṅkārakūṭesu tāva).

"He deprives [one] of life" (jīvitā voropesī) – in that refuse heap (etasmiṃ saṅkārakūṭe), while looking at a rag (pilotikaṃ olokentaṃ), a confused (vibbhantā) young calf cow (taruṇavacchā gāvī) having run up (upadhāvitvā), pierced [him] with [its] horn (siṅgena vijjhitvā), [and] killed [him] (ghātesi).

The noble one (kulaputto) tormented by hunger (chātakajjhatto), having reached the destruction of life (āyukkhayaṃ patvā) in the sky itself (ākāseyeva), fell (patito).

In the refuse heap (saṅkāraṭṭhāne) he was like a golden image (suvaṇṇapaṭimā viya ahosi) placed face down (adhomukhaṭṭhapitā), and having made time (kālaṅkato ca), he was born (nibbatti) in the Aviha Brahmā world (avihābrahmaloke), and just upon being born (nibbattamattova) he attained (pāpuṇi) arhattaṃ.

It is said that just upon being born (nibbattamatāva) in the Aviha Brahmā world (avihābrahmaloke) seven people (satta janā) attained arhattaṃ.

This was said –

"He deprives [one] of life" (jīvitā voropesī)

‘‘Having approached Aviha (avihaṃ), the seven monks (satta bhikkhavo) are liberated (vimuttā);

Having exhausted passion and hatred (rāgadosaparikkhīṇā), they have crossed over attachment (visattikaṃ) in the world (loke).

And which ones crossed the mud (paṅkaṃ), exceedingly difficult to cross, the domain of death (maccudheyyaṃ suduttaraṃ)?

Who, having abandoned the human body (mānusaṃ dehaṃ hitvā), went beyond the divine union (dibbayogaṃ upaccaguṃ)?
