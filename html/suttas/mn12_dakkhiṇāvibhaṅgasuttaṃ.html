376. Thus have I heard: the Dakkhiṇāvibhaṅgasutta.

There, Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī: Gotamī is the clan name.

On the naming day, Brahmins who had received honors predicted, having seen the signs of good fortune: "If she has a daughter, she will be the chief queen of a cakkavatti king. If she has a son, she will be a cakkavatti ruler." Thus, in both ways, a great progeny will be hers.

Then they gave her the name Mahāpajāpatī.

Here, however, having combined it with the clan name, it is said Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī.

Nava means unwashed.

Sāmaṃ vāyita means it was not woven by her own hands alone; however, one day, surrounded by a group of nurses, having gone to the weaving place of the weavers, taking the end of the loom, she made the gesture of weaving.

This was said with reference to that.

When did Gotamī’s thought arise to give a pair of cloths to the Blessed One?

At the time when, having attained complete awakening, he arrived at Kapilapura by the first journey.

Then, indeed, Suddhodana Mahārāja, having taken the Teacher who had entered for alms, led him into his own dwelling. Then, having seen the beauty of the Blessed One’s form, Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī thought: “How beautiful, indeed, is the state of my son!”

Then a strong joy arose in her.

Then she thought: “For twenty-nine years my son, while living in the house, at least even the amount of a banana fruit had been given only by me. Now I will give him a set of monastic robes.”

“In this royal house, there are many expensive cloths, but those do not please me. Only that which is made with my own hands pleases me. Having made it with my own hands, I will give it,” she generated the thought.

Then, in between, she had cotton brought, and with her own hands she pounded, threshed, spun fine thread, had a loom set up in the inner courtyard itself, summoned weavers, and giving the weavers only her own requisites, edibles, and food, she had it woven. From time to time, surrounded by a group of nurses, she went and held the end of the loom.

At the end of the weaving, having made a great offering to the weavers, having placed the pair of cloths in a perfume box, having taken a dwelling, she announced to the king: “Having taken a set of monastic robes for my son, I will go.”

The king had the road prepared, having swept the streets, having placed full water pots, having raised flags and banners, having had the road prepared from the gate of the royal palace as far as the Banyan Tree Park, they made it strewn with flowers.

Mahāpajāpatī also, having adorned herself with all ornaments, surrounded by a group of nurses, having placed the box on her head, having gone to the presence of the Blessed One, said, beginning with, “This is my new pair of cloths, O Blessed One.”

“This is my new pair of cloths, O Blessed One.”

The second time, when he said, “Give it to the Saṅgha, Gotamī,” she said: “I am able, O Blessed One, to give monastic robes from a storehouse of cloths to even a hundred monks, even a thousand monks, even a hundred thousand monks. However, this, having intended it for the Blessed One, I spun myself, I wove myself. May the Blessed One accept that from me, O Blessed One,” she invited him.

Thus, she requested up to the third time, but the Blessed One refused.

The second time, why did the Blessed One have what was being given to himself given to the Saṅgha? Out of compassion for his mother.

Thus it occurred to him: “For her, having been begun with me, three thoughts have arisen: a prior thought, a releasing thought, and a subsequent thought. Let them also arise, having been begun with the Saṅgha. Thus, her six thoughts, having become one, will conduce to well-being and happiness for a long time.”

The debater then said: “What is given to the Saṅgha is of great fruit. Therefore, it was said thus.”

He should be told: “Do you say that what is given to the Teacher is of less fruit than what is given to the Saṅgha?” “Yes, I say so.”

He brings the sutta.

“Give to the Saṅgha, Gotamī. When it is given to the Saṅgha by you, both I will be honored, and the Saṅgha also.”

Is this itself the meaning of this sutta? Yes, this itself.

If so, “Then, Ānanda, give the leftover food first,” and also (Pāci.

269) “Then, Kaccāna, give the leftover food, the sugar lumps,” (Mahāva.
284) And because of the statement, giving leftovers is more greatly fruitful.

Even so, [they say,] "The Teacher makes [others] give what belongs to himself."

Even kings, chief ministers, and others cause their acquired tribute to be given to elephant drivers and others. These would be greater than the kings, etc.

Therefore, do not take it thus –

‘‘There is not in this world or in another,

a superior or equal to the Buddha;

having gone to the path of those worthy of offerings,

for those who desire merit, he is the one who gives abundant fruit.’’

–

Because of the statement, there is no one called a dakṣiṇīya superior to the Teacher.

Having considered that these six thoughts, being unified, will be for long-term benefit and happiness, intending this, having prevented it up to the third time, he caused it to be given to the saṃgha.

For the sake of generating respect in the saṃgha in the later generation, he also said this:

Thus it occurred to him: “I am not long-lasting, but my teaching will be established in the community of monks. May the later generation generate respect in the saṃgha!” Having prevented it up to the third time, he caused it to be given to the saṃgha.

Because it is thus, [he thought,] “The Teacher makes [others] give even what belongs to himself to the saṃgha. The saṃgha is called worthy of offerings." Having generated respect in the saṃgha, the later generation will think that the four requisites should be given. The saṃgha, not tiring with the four requisites, having mastered the Buddha's word, will do the ascetic life.

Thus, my teaching will last for five thousand years.

"May the Blessed One accept [this]," even from this statement, it should be understood that there is no dakṣiṇīya superior to the Teacher.

For Ānanda Thera does not have hatred or animosity towards Mahāpajāpatī.

The Thera does not desire, "May her offering not be greatly fruitful."

For the wise Thera, being very learned, having attained the sekha-paṭisambhidā, seeing the greatly fruitful nature of what was given by the Teacher, requested it for the purpose of acceptance, saying, "May the Blessed One accept [this], Venerable Sir."

Again, the disputatious one said, “Because of the statement, ‘When it is given to you in the saṃgha, I myself will be honored, and so will the saṃgha,’ the Teacher is included in the saṃgha.”

He should be told, "Do you know how many refuges there are, how many unshakable faiths there are?" Knowing, he will say, "Three." Then he should be told, "According to your doctrine, because the Teacher is included in the saṃgha, there are only two."

When it is thus, and – "I allow, monks, the pravrajyā and upasampadā with these three going-for-refuges" (Mahāva.

34) Thus, the permitted pravrajyā and upasampadā do not grow.

Therefore, you are neither a pravrajita nor a householder.

And when the monks are sitting in the gandhakuṭī of the Perfectly Awakened One, they perform the uposatha, the pavāraṇa, and the saṃghakarmas. These, because the Teacher is included in the saṃgha, would be ruined, but they are not.

Therefore, it should not be said that "the Teacher is included in the saṃgha."

When this had been said, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may the Blessed One accept this new pair of cloths from Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī.

Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī has been very helpful to the Blessed One, venerable sir. As the Blessed One’s maternal aunt, she was his nurse, his foster mother, the one who gave him milk; she suckled the Blessed One when his own mother died. The Blessed One too has been very helpful to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, venerable sir.

It is owing to the Blessed One that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī has gone for refuge to the Buddha (buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā), has gone for refuge to the Dharma (dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā), and has gone for refuge to the Saṅgha (saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gatā).

It is owing to the Blessed One that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭiviratā), abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭiviratā), abstains from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā paṭiviratā), abstains from false speech (musāvādā paṭiviratā), and abstains from wine, liquor, and intoxicants, which are the basis of negligence (surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā paṭiviratā).

It is owing to the Blessed One that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī possesses perfect confidence in the Buddha (buddhe aveccappasādena samannāgatā), possesses perfect confidence in the Dharma (dhamme aveccappasādena samannāgatā), possesses perfect confidence in the Saṅgha (saṅghe aveccappasādena samannāgatā), and possesses the virtues loved by noble ones (ariyakantehi sīlehi samannāgatā).

It is owing to the Blessed One that Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī is free from doubt about suffering (dukkhe nikkaṅkhā), about the origin of suffering (dukkhasamudaye nikkaṅkhā), about the cessation of suffering ( dukkhanirodhe nikkaṅkhā), and about the way leading to the cessation of suffering ( dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya nikkaṅkhā). The Blessed One too has been very helpful to Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, venerable sir.”

The second and third, too, are the highest offerings (paramadakkhiṇāyeva). The remaining ones do not attain (na pāpuṇanti) the state (bhāvaṃ) of the highest offering (paramadakkhiṇabhāvaṃ).

381. There are these four kinds of purification of offering, Ānanda.

Which four? There is the offering, Ānanda, that is purified by the giver (dāyaka), not by the receiver (paṭiggāhaka).

There is the offering, Ānanda, that is purified by the receiver, not by the giver.

There is the offering, Ānanda, that is purified neither by the giver nor by the receiver.

There is the offering, Ānanda, that is purified both by the giver and by the receiver.

How is the offering, Ānanda, purified by the giver, not by the receiver? Here, Ānanda, the giver is virtuous (sīlavā), of good qualities (kalyāṇadhammo), and the receivers are immoral (dussīlā), of evil qualities (pāpadhammā). Thus, Ānanda, the offering is purified by the giver, not by the receiver.

How is the offering, Ānanda, purified by the receiver, not by the giver? Here, Ānanda, the giver is immoral, of evil qualities, and the receivers are virtuous, of good qualities. Thus, Ānanda, the offering is purified by the receiver, not by the giver.

How is the offering, Ānanda, purified neither by the giver nor by the receiver? Here, Ānanda, the giver is immoral and of evil qualities, and the receivers are also immoral and of evil qualities. Thus, Ānanda, the offering is purified neither by the giver nor by the receiver.

How is the offering, Ānanda, purified both by the giver and by the receiver? Here, Ānanda, the giver is virtuous and of good qualities, and the receivers are also virtuous and of good qualities. Thus, Ānanda, the offering is purified both by the giver and by the receiver.

These, Ānanda, are these four purifications of offerings.

Thus spoke the Blessed One (Bhagavā).

382. When the virtuous (sīlavā) gives a gift (dānaṃ) to the immoral (dussīlesu),

Having obtained it righteously (dhammena laddhaṃ), with a very clear mind (supasannacitto);

Believing in an excellent result of action (kammaphalaṃ uḷāraṃ),

That offering is purified by the giver (dāyakato visujjhati).

When the immoral gives a gift to the virtuous,

Having obtained it unrighteously (adhammena laddhaṃ), with an unclear mind (appasannacitto);

Not believing in an excellent result of action,

That offering is purified by the receiver (paṭiggāhakato visujjhati).

When the immoral gives a gift to the immoral,

Having obtained it unrighteously, with an unclear mind;

Not believing in an excellent result of action,

That offering, I say, is not of great fruit (vipulapphalanti).

When the virtuous gives a gift to the virtuous,

Having obtained it righteously, with a very clear mind;

Believing in an excellent result of action,

That gift, I say, is of great fruit.

When one free from passion (vītarāgo) gives a gift to those free from passion (vītarāgesu),

Having obtained it righteously, with a very clear mind;

Believing in an excellent result of action,

That gift is the best of material gifts.
I declare that gift to be abundant.

381. "Being purified by the giver" means being purified by having great fruit, meaning it is of great fruit.

"Auspicious Dhamma" means pure Dhamma, not evil Dhamma.

Regarding "Being purified by the giver," the great king Vessantara should be mentioned. Indeed, he, having given his children to the Jūjaka Brahmin, caused the earth to tremble.

Regarding "Being purified by the receiver," the fisherman residing at the mouth of the Kalyāṇī river should be mentioned. It is said that he, having given alms food to the elder Dīghasoma even three times, lying on his deathbed said, "May the alms food given by me to the elder Dīghasoma uplift me."

Regarding "Being purified by the receiver," Vaḍḍhamānavāsiluddako should be mentioned. It is said that while giving the petadakkhiṇa, he gave it to an immoral person three times. The third time, he cried out, "A non-human, immoral person is robbing me!" Having given it to a virtuous bhikkhu, it reached him at the very moment it was given.

Regarding "Not purified by the giver," the unparalleled gift should be mentioned.

Regarding "Being purified by the giver," just as a skillful farmer, having obtained even an unproductive field, plows it at the right time, removes the dust, plants the best seeds, and without neglecting protection day and night, obtains more grain than another from a productive field, likewise, a virtuous person, even having given to an immoral person, obtains great fruit. By this method, purification should be understood in all instances.

Regarding "The one without passion [giving] to those without passion," here, "one without passion" (vītarāga) means a non-returner (anāgāmī), but an arhat is entirely without passion; therefore, giving to an arhat by an arhat is the highest. Why? Because of the absence of a dwelling place of becoming, of a desire for becoming. Is it not true that a khīṇāsava does not believe in the fruit of giving? Those who believe in the fruit of giving are not like the khīṇāsavas. However, action done by a khīṇāsava, being without desire and passion, is neither wholesome nor unwholesome; it stands in the place of mere action (kiriyaṭṭhāne); therefore, they say that his giving is the highest.

But is the gift given by the Perfectly Awakened One to the elder Sāriputta of great fruit, or the gift given by the elder Sāriputta to the Perfectly Awakened One? They say the gift given by the Perfectly Awakened One to the elder Sāriputta is of great fruit. For, except for the Perfectly Awakened One, there is no other who is capable of knowing the result of giving. Indeed, giving gives its result in that very existence to one who is able to give with four kinds of accomplishment. Here are these accomplishments: the given object is acquired lawfully, equally, without oppressing others; the greatness of intention by way of preliminary intention, etc.; the excellence of qualities by way of being a khīṇāsava; and the perfection of the object by way of having arisen from cessation that very day.
