Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Anāthapindika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Sāvatthī.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: “Monks!”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied to the Fortunate One.

The Blessed One said this: “Monks, I will teach you the summary and exposition of one who has had an auspicious day.

Listen & pay close attention. I will speak.”

“As you say, lord,” the monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said:

“One should not pursue the past, nor hope for the future;

What is past is left behind; the future has not yet been reached.

But whoever sees insightfully here and now each arising state,

unshaken, unperturbed, knows it in their heart, teachable."

“Ardently doing what should be done today, for — who knows? — tomorrow death.

There is no bargaining with Mortality & his mighty horde.

Whoever lives thus ardently, untiringly, day and night,

that one, truly, is called auspicious, said the silent sage.”

273.
How, monks, does one revive the past? Thinking, ‘I had such material form (rūpa) in the past (atītamaddhāna),’ one finds delight (nandi) in that. Thinking, ‘I had such feeling (vedanā) in the past,’ one finds delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such perception (saṃjñā) in the past,’ one finds delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such formations (saṃskāra) in the past,’ one finds delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such consciousness (vijñāna) in the past’ one finds delight in that. That is how, monks, one revives the past.

How, monks, does one not revive the past? Thinking, ‘I had such material form in the past,’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such feeling in the past,’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such perception in the past,’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such formations in the past,’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such consciousness in the past’ one does not find delight in that. That is how, monks, one does not revive the past.

275. "And how, monks, is one entangled in present phenomena? Here, monks, an uninstructed ordinary person, who has no regard for the noble ones, is unskilled and undisciplined in the Dhamma of the noble ones, who has no regard for true men, is unskilled and undisciplined in the Dhamma of true men, regards material form ( rūpa) as self (ātta), or self as possessed of material form, or material form as in self, or self as in material form. He regards feeling (vedanā)…perception (saññā)…volitional formations (saṅkhāre)…consciousness (viññāṇa) as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness – that is how, monks, one is entangled in present phenomena.

"And how, monks, is one not entangled in present phenomena? Here, monks, a well-taught noble disciple, who has regard for the noble ones, is skilled and disciplined in their Dhamma, who has regard for true men, is skilled and disciplined in their Dhamma, does not regard material form as self, or self as possessed of material form, or material form as in self, or self as in material form. He does not regard feeling…not perception…not volitional formations…not consciousness as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness – that is how, monks, one is not entangled in present phenomena.

“One should not pursue the past, nor set one’s hopes on the future;

for the past is dead and gone, and the future is yet to come.

“But whoever sees clearly the present reality, in each and every condition,

unshaken, unperturbed, knowing it, let him cultivate it.

“Today the effort must be made; who knows, death may come tomorrow?

No bargain can be made with Death and his mighty host!

“One who lives thus ardently, tirelessly, day and night,

is truly called ‘one living in auspicious solitude’ by the peaceful sage.”

“‘I will teach you, monks, the summary and the detailed explanation of the bhaddekaratta’ – it was with reference to this that this was said."

This is what the Blessed One said. Elated, those monks delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Bhaddekaratta Sutta, the first, is concluded.
