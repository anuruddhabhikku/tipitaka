317. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ āyasmā mahākaccāno madhurāyaṃ viharati gundāvane. Assosi kho rājā mādhuro avantiputto – ‘‘samaṇo khalu, bho, kaccāno madhurāyaṃ mathurāyaṃ (ṭīkā) viharati gundāvane. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ kaccānaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato – ‘paṇḍito viyatto medhāvī bahussuto cittakathī kalyāṇapaṭibhāno vuddho ceva arahā ca’. Sādhu kho pana tathārūpānaṃ arahataṃ dassanaṃ hotī’’ti. Atha kho rājā mādhuro avantiputto bhadrāni bhadrāni yānāni yojāpetvā bhadraṃ yānaṃ abhiruhitvā bhadrehi bhadrehi yānehi madhurāya niyyāsi mahaccarājānubhāvena āyasmantaṃ mahākaccānaṃ dassanāya. Yāvatikā yānassa bhūmi yānena gantvā yānā paccorohitvā pattikova yenāyasmā mahākaccāno tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā mahākaccānena saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho rājā mādhuro avantiputto āyasmantaṃ mahākaccānaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘brāhmaṇā, bho kaccāna, evamāhaṃsu – ‘brāhmaṇova seṭṭho vaṇṇo, hīno añño vaṇṇo; brāhmaṇova sukko vaṇṇo, kaṇho añño vaṇṇo; brāhmaṇāva sujjhanti, no abrāhmaṇā; brāhmaṇāva brahmuno puttā orasā mukhato jātā brahmajā brahmanimmitā brahmadāyādā’ti. Idha bhavaṃ kaccāno kimakkhāyī’’ti? ‘‘Ghosoyeva kho eso, mahārāja, lokasmiṃ – ‘brāhmaṇova seṭṭho vaṇṇo, hīno añño vaṇṇo; brāhmaṇova sukko vaṇṇo, kaṇho añño vaṇṇo; brāhmaṇāva sujjhanti, no abrāhmaṇā; brāhmaṇāva brahmuno puttā orasā mukhato jātā brahmajā brahmanimmitā brahmadāyādā’ti. Tadamināpetaṃ, mahārāja, pariyāyena veditabbaṃ yathā ghosoyeveso lokasmiṃ – ‘brāhmaṇova seṭṭho vaṇṇo, hīno añño vaṇṇo…pe… brahmadāyādā’’’ti.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the venerable Mahākaccāna was living at Madhurā in the Gunda Grove.

King Mādhurā Avantiputta heard: “The recluse Kaccāna is living at Madhurā in the Gunda Grove.”

Now a good report of Master Kaccāna has been spread to this effect: ‘He is wise, discerning, sagacious, learned, articulate, and perspicacious; he is aged and he is an arhat (arahā ca).’

“It is good to see such a worthy one.”

Then King Mādhurā Avantiputta had a number of state carriages made ready, and mounting a state carriage, he drove out from Madhurā with the full pomp of royalty in order to see the venerable Mahākaccāna.

He went thus as far as the road was passable for carriages, and then he got down from his carriage and went forward on foot to the venerable Mahākaccāna; having approached, he exchanged greetings with the venerable Mahākaccāna.

When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side.

Having sat down to one side, King Mādhurā Avantiputta said to the venerable Mahākaccāna: “The brahmins (brāhmaṇā), Master Kaccāna, say thus: ‘Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) are the highest caste (varṇa), those of any other caste are inferior; brahmins (brāhmaṇā) are the fairest caste, those of any other caste are dark; only brahmins (brāhmaṇā) are purified, not non-brahmins (abrāhmaṇā); brahmins (brāhmaṇā) alone are the sons of Brahmā, the offspring of Brahmā, born of his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā, heirs of Brahmā.’

What do you say about this?”

“It is just a saying in the world, great king, that ‘Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) are the highest caste (varṇa), those of any other caste are inferior; brahmins (brāhmaṇā) are the fairest caste, those of any other caste are dark; only brahmins (brāhmaṇā) are purified, not non-brahmins (abrāhmaṇā); brahmins (brāhmaṇā) alone are the sons of Brahmā, the offspring of Brahmā, born of his mouth, born of Brahmā, created by Brahmā, heirs of Brahmā.’

And there is a way whereby it can be understood how that statement of the brahmins (brāhmaṇā) is just a saying in the world.
317.Evaṃme sutanti madhurasuttaṃ. Tattha mahākaccānoti gihikāle ujjenikarañño purohitaputto abhirūpo dassanīyo pāsādiko suvaṇṇavaṇṇo ca. Madhurāyanti evaṃnāmake nagare. Gundāvaneti kaṇhakagundāvane . Avantiputtoti avantiraṭṭhe rañño dhītāya putto. Vuddho ceva arahā cāti daharaṃ arahantampi na tathā sambhāventi yathā mahallakaṃ, thero pana vuddho ceva ahosi arahā ca. Brāhmaṇā, bho kaccānāti so kira rājā brāhmaṇaladdhiko, tasmā evamāha. Brāhmaṇova seṭṭho vaṇṇotiādīsu jātigottādīnaṃ paññāpanaṭṭhāne brāhmaṇāva seṭṭhāti dasseti. Hīno añño vaṇṇoti itare tayo vaṇṇā hīnā lāmakāti vadati. Sukkoti paṇḍaro. Kaṇhoti kāḷako. Sujjhantīti jātigottādipaññāpanaṭṭhānesu sujjhanti. Brahmuno puttāti mahābrahmuno puttā. Orasā mukhato jātāti ure vasitvā mukhato nikkhantā, ure katvā saṃvaddhitāti vā orasā. Brahmajāti brahmato nibbattā. Brahmanimmitāti brahmunā nimmitā. Brahmadāyādāti brahmuno dāyādā. Ghosoyeva kho esoti vohāramattamevetaṃ.
317. Thus, I heard, [is] the Madhura Sutta.

There, Mahākaccāna means, in the householder period, the son of the chaplain of Ujjeni King, handsome, beautiful, pleasing, golden-colored, and.

Madhurā means in a city of that name.

Gundāvana means in Kaṇhakagundāvana.

Avantiputta means the son of the daughter of the king in the Avanti country.

Vuddha and Arahā means they do not honor a young Arahant as much as an old man; but a Thera was old and an Arahant.

Brāhmaṇas, bho Kaccānā means that king was one who obtained [his position] from Brahmins, therefore he spoke thus.

Brāhmaṇova seṭṭho vaṇṇo, etc., means that in the designation of jāti, gotta, etc., he shows that only the brāhmaṇas are the best.

Hīno añño vaṇṇo means the other three vaṇṇas are low and wretched, he says.

Sukko means paṇḍaro.

Kaṇho means kāḷako.

Sujjhantī means in the designation of jāti, gotta, etc., they are purified.

Brahmuno puttā means the sons of Mahābrahmā.

Orasā mukhato jātā means having lived in the breast, having emerged from the mouth, or having made [him] in [his] breast, he was raised, or [they are] orasa.

Brahmajā means born from Brahmā.

Brahmanimmitā means created by Brahmā.

Brahmadāyādā means the heirs of Brahmā.

Ghosoyeva kho eso means it is merely a convention.

318. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, khattiyassa cepi ijjheyya dhanena vā dhaññena vā rajatena vā jātarūpena vā khattiyopissāssa pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṃkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī… brāhmaṇopissāssa… vessopissāssa… suddopissāssa pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṃkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī’’ti? ‘‘Khattiyassa cepi, bho kaccāna, ijjheyya dhanena vā dhaññena vā rajatena vā jātarūpena vā khattiyopissāssa pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṃkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī… brāhmaṇopissāssa… vessopissāssa… suddopissāssa pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṃkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, brāhmaṇassa cepi ijjheyya dhanena vā dhaññena vā rajatena vā jātarūpena vā brāhmaṇopissāssa pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṃkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī… vessopissāssa… suddopissāssa … khattiyopissāssa pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṃkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī’’ti? ‘‘Suddassa cepi, bho kaccāna, ijjheyya dhanena vā dhaññena vā rajatena vā jātarūpena vā suddopissāssa pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṃkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādīti… khattiyopissāssa… brāhmaṇopissāssa… vessopissāssa pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṃkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, yadi evaṃ sante, ime cattāro vaṇṇā samasamā honti no vā? 318. “What do you think, great king? If a noble (kṣatriya) prospers in wealth (dhana), grain (dhānya), silver (rajata), or gold (jātarūpa), will there be nobles who rise before him and retire after him, who are eager to serve him, who seek to please him and speak sweetly to him, and will there also be brahmins (brāhmaṇa), merchants (vaiśya), and workers (śūdra) who do likewise?”

“What do you think, great king? If a brahmin prospers in wealth, grain, silver, or gold, will there be brahmins who rise before him and retire after him, who are eager to serve him, who seek to please him and speak sweetly to him, and will there also be merchants, workers, and nobles who do likewise?”

“What do you think, great king? If a merchant prospers in wealth, grain, silver, or gold, will there be merchants who rise before him and retire after him, who are eager to serve him, who seek to please him and speak sweetly to him, and will there also be workers, nobles, and brahmins who do likewise?”

“What do you think, great king? If a worker prospers in wealth, grain, silver, or gold, will there be workers who rise before him and retire after him, who are eager to serve him, who seek to please him and speak sweetly to him, and will there also be nobles, brahmins, and merchants who do likewise?”

“What do you think, great king? If that is so, then are these four castes (varṇa) all the same, or are they not? What do you say about this?”

“Surely if that is so, Master Kaccana, then these four castes are all the same; there is no difference between them at all that I see!”

“That is also a way, great king, whereby it can be understood how that statement of the brahmins is just a saying in the world: ‘The brahmin is the best caste, and any other caste is inferior…and they are the heirs of Brahma’."
318.Ijjheyyāti samijjheyya, yattakāni dhanādīni pattheyya, tattakehissa manoratho pūreyyāti attho. Khattiyopissāssāti khattiyopi assa issariyasampattassa pubbuṭṭhāyī assa. Nesaṃ ettha kiñcīti na etesaṃ ettha kiñci.
"Ijjheyyā" means may be successful, whatever wealth etc. is desired, in that way may his wish be fulfilled, is the meaning.

"Khattiyopissāssā" means even a noble may be an early riser in the attainment of sovereignty.

"Nesaṃ ettha kiñcī" means nothing of theirs is here.

319. “What do you think, great king? Suppose a noble (kṣatriya) were to kill living beings (prāṇātipātī), take what is not given (adinnādāyī), misconduct himself in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārī), speak falsely (musāvādī), speak maliciously (pisuṇavāco), speak harshly (pharusavāco), gossip (samphappalāpī), be covetous (abhijjhālu), have a mind of ill will (byāpannacitto), and hold wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi). On the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), would he be likely to reappear in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ), or not? How does it appear to you in this case?”

“Whether it be a noble (kṣatriyo), Master Kaccāna, who kills living beings (prāṇātipātī), takes what is not given (adinnādāyī), misconducts himself in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārī), speaks falsely (musāvādī), speaks maliciously (pisuṇavāco), speaks harshly (pharusavāco), gossips (samphappalāpī), is covetous (abhijjhālu), has a mind of ill will (byāpannacitto), and holds wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he is likely to reappear in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ). That is how it appears to me in this case, and thus I have heard from the arahants.”

“Excellent, excellent, great king! It is good that this appears to you thus, great king, and good that you have heard this from the arahants. What do you think, great king? Suppose a Brahmin…a merchant… a worker were to kill living beings, take what is not given…and hold wrong view. On the dissolution of the body, after death, would he be likely to reappear in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, in perdition, even in hell, or not? How does it appear to you in this case?”

“Whether it be a worker (śūddo), Master Kaccāna, who kills living beings (prāṇātipātī), takes what is not given (adinnādāyī)…and holds wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), he is likely to reappear in a state of deprivation (apāyaṃ), in an unhappy destination (duggatiṃ), in perdition (vinipātaṃ), even in hell (nirayaṃ). That is how it appears to me in this case, and thus I have heard from the arhats.”

“Excellent, excellent, great king! It is good that this appears to you thus, great king, and good that you have heard this from the arhats. What do you think, great king? If that is so, then are these four castes (varṇā) all the same, or are they not? How does it appear to you in this case?”

“Surely if that is so, Master Kaccāna, then these four castes (varṇā) are all the same; I see no difference among them.”

“That is also a way, great king, whereby it can be understood how that statement of the Brahmins is just a saying in the world – ‘The Brahmin is the best caste (varṇa), the other caste (varṇa) is inferior…heirs of Brahmā.’”

320. “What do you think, great king? Suppose a noble (kṣatriya) here abstains from killing living beings (prāṇātipātā paṭivirato), abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato), abstains from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato), abstains from false speech (musāvādā paṭivirato), abstains from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato), abstains from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato), abstains from gossip (samphappalāpā paṭivirato), is uncovetous (anabhijjhālu), has a mind without ill will (abyāpannacitto), and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi). On the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), would he be likely to reappear in a happy destination (sugatiṃ), even in the heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ), or not (upapajjeyya no vā)? What do you think about this?”

“Even a noble (khattiyopi hi), venerable Kaccāna, who abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato), abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato), abstains from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato), abstains from false speech (musāvādā paṭivirato), abstains from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato), abstains from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato), abstains from gossip (samphappalāpā paṭivirato), is uncovetous (anabhijjhālu), has a mind without ill will (abyāpannacitto), and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), is likely to reappear in a happy destination (sugatiṃ), even in the heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ). Thus it appears to me here, and thus I have heard from the worthy ones (arahataṃ sutaṃ).”

“Excellent (sādhu)! Excellent (sādhu), great king (mahārāja)! It is excellent (sādhu), great king (mahārāja), that it appears thus to you, and it is excellent (sādhu) that you have heard thus from the worthy ones (arahataṃ sutaṃ). What do you think, great king (mahārāja)? Suppose a brāhmaṇa, a merchant (vaiśya), or a worker (śūdra) here abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato), abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato)… and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), is likely to reappear in a happy destination (sugatiṃ), even in the heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ), or not (upapajjeyya no vā)? What do you think about this?”

“Even a worker (śūdra), venerable Kaccāna, who abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato), abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato)… and holds right view (sammādiṭṭhi), on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), is likely to reappear in a happy destination (sugatiṃ), even in the heavenly world (saggaṃ lokaṃ). Thus it appears to me here, and thus I have heard from the worthy ones (arahataṃ sutaṃ).”

“Excellent (sādhu)! Excellent (sādhu), great king (mahārāja)! It is excellent (sādhu), great king (mahārāja), that it appears thus to you, and it is excellent (sādhu) that you have heard thus from the worthy ones (arahataṃ sutaṃ). What do you think, great king (mahārāja)? If that is so (yadi evaṃ sante), are these four castes (cattāro varṇā) all the same (samasamā honti) or not (no vā)? What do you think about this?”

“Surely (addhā), venerable Kaccāna, if that is so (evaṃ sante), these four castes (cattāro varṇā) are all the same (samasamā honti). I see no difference (nānākaraṇaṃ samanupassāmī) between them at all.”

“That is also a way, great king (mahārāja), whereby it can be understood that the statement in the world is just a saying (ghoso yeveso lokasmiṃ)—‘The brāhmaṇa is the highest caste (brāhmaṇova seṭṭho varṇo), other castes are inferior (hīno añño varṇo)… descendants of Brahmā (brahmadāyādā)’.”

321. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, idha khattiyo sandhiṃ vā chindeyya, nillopaṃ vā hareyya, ekāgārikaṃ vā kareyya, paripanthe vā tiṭṭheyya, paradāraṃ vā gaccheyya, tañce te purisā gahetvā dasseyyuṃ – ‘ayaṃ te, deva, coro āgucārī. Imassa yaṃ icchasi taṃ daṇḍaṃ paṇehī’ti. Kinti naṃ kareyyāsī’’ti? ‘‘Ghāteyyāma vā, bho kaccāna, jāpeyyāma vā pabbājeyyāma vā yathāpaccayaṃ vā kareyyāma. Taṃ kissa hetu? Yā hissa , bho kaccāna, pubbe ‘khattiyo’ti samaññā sāssa antarahitā; corotveva saṅkhyaṃ saṅkhaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) gacchatī’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, idha brāhmaṇo, idha vesso, idha suddo sandhiṃ vā chindeyya, nillopaṃ vā hareyya, ekāgārikaṃ vā kareyya, paripanthe vā tiṭṭheyya, paradāraṃ vā gaccheyya, tañce te purisā gahetvā dasseyyuṃ – ‘ayaṃ te, deva, coro āgucārī. Imassa yaṃ icchasi taṃ daṇḍaṃ paṇehī’ti. Kinti naṃ kareyyāsī’’ti? ‘‘Ghāteyyāma vā, bho kaccāna, jāpeyyāma vā pabbājeyyāma vā yathāpaccayaṃ vā kareyyāma. Taṃ kissa hetu? Yā hissa, bho kaccāna, pubbe ‘suddo’ti samaññā sāssa antarahitā; corotveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchatī’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, yadi evaṃ sante, ime cattāro vaṇṇā samasamā honti no vā? Kathaṃ vā te ettha hotī’’ti? ‘‘Addhā kho, bho kaccāna, evaṃ sante, ime cattāro vaṇṇā samasamā honti. Nesaṃ ettha kiñci nānākaraṇaṃ samanupassāmī’’ti. ‘‘Imināpi kho etaṃ, mahārāja, pariyāyena veditabbaṃ yathā ghoso yeveso lokasmiṃ – ‘brāhmaṇova seṭṭho vaṇṇo, hīno añño vaṇṇo…pe… brahmadāyādā’’’ti.
321. “What do you think, great king? Suppose a noble (kṣatriya) were to break into houses (sandhiṃ chindeyya), plunder wealth (nillopaṃ hareyya), commit burglary (ekāgārikaṃ kareyya), ambush highways (paripanthe vā tiṭṭheyya), or seduce another’s wife (paradāraṃ vā gaccheyya), and if your men arrested him and produced him, saying: ‘Sire, this is the culprit; this is a criminal (coro āgucārī).
Command what punishment you wish for him (imassa yaṃ icchasi taṃ daṇḍaṃ paṇehī’ti).’ What would you do to him?” “We would have him executed (ghāteyyāma), Master Kaccāna, or we would have him fined (jāpeyyāma), or we would have him exiled (pabbājeyyāma), or we would do with him as he deserved (yathāpaccayaṃ vā kareyyāma).
What is the reason for that (taṃ kissa hetu)? Because his former designation ‘noble’ (khattiyo’ti samaññā) would have disappeared (antarahitā); he would simply go by the name of thief (corotveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchatī’’ti).”

“What do you think, great king? Suppose a brahmin (brāhmaṇa), a merchant (vessa), a worker (śūddo) were to break into houses, plunder wealth, commit burglary, ambush highways, or seduce another’s wife, and if your men arrested him and produced him, saying: ‘Sire, this is the culprit; this is a criminal.
Command what punishment you wish for him.’ What would you do to him?” “We would have him executed, Master Kaccāna, or we would have him fined, or we would have him exiled, or we would do with him as he deserved.
What is the reason for that? Because his former designation ‘worker’ (śūddo’ti samaññā) would have disappeared; he would simply go by the name of thief.”

“What do you think, great king? If that is so, then are these four castes (cattāro varṇā) all the same (samasamā honti) or are they not? How do you see it?” “Indeed, Master Kaccāna, if that is so, then these four castes are all the same.
There is no difference between them at all that I see (nesaṃ ettha kiñci nānākaraṇaṃ samanupassāmī’’ti).”
“That is also a way, great king, whereby it can be understood how that statement is just a saying in the world: ‘A brahmin is the highest caste (brāhmaṇova seṭṭho varṇo), another caste is inferior (hīno añño varṇo)…heirs of Brahmā (brahmadāyādā).’”

322. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, idha khattiyo kesamassuṃ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito assa virato pāṇātipātā, virato adinnādānā, virato musāvādā, rattūparato, ekabhattiko, brahmacārī, sīlavā, kalyāṇadhammo? Kinti naṃ kareyyāsī’’ti? ‘‘Abhivādeyyāma vā pi (dī. ni. 1.184, 187 sāmaññaphale), bho kaccāna, paccuṭṭheyyāma vā āsanena vā nimanteyyāma abhinimanteyyāma vā naṃ cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārehi dhammikaṃ vā assa rakkhāvaraṇaguttiṃ saṃvidaheyyāma. Taṃ kissa hetu? Yā hissa, bho kaccāna, pubbe ‘khattiyo’ti samaññā sāssa antarahitā; samaṇotveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchatī’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, idha brāhmaṇo, idha vesso, idha suddo kesamassuṃ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito assa virato pāṇātipātā, virato adinnādānā virato musāvādā, rattūparato, ekabhattiko, brahmacārī, sīlavā, kalyāṇadhammo? Kinti naṃ kareyyāsī’’ti? ‘‘Abhivādeyyāma vā, bho kaccāna, paccuṭṭheyyāma vā āsanena vā nimanteyyāma abhinimanteyyāma vā naṃ cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārehi dhammikaṃ vā assa rakkhāvaraṇaguttiṃ saṃvidaheyyāma. Taṃ kissa hetu? Yā hissa, bho kaccāna, pubbe ‘suddo’ti samaññā sāssa antarahitā; samaṇotveva saṅkhyaṃ gacchatī’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, mahārāja, yadi evaṃ sante, ime cattāro vaṇṇā samasamā honti no vā? Kathaṃ vā te ettha hotī’’ti? ‘‘Addhā kho, bho kaccāna, evaṃ sante, ime cattāro vaṇṇā samasamā honti. Nesaṃ ettha kiñci nānākaraṇaṃ samanupassāmī’’ti. ‘‘Imināpi kho etaṃ, mahārāja, pariyāyena veditabbaṃ yathā ghoso yeveso lokasmiṃ – ‘brāhmaṇova seṭṭho vaṇṇo, hīno añño vaṇṇo; brāhmaṇova sukko vaṇṇo, kaṇho añño vaṇṇo; brāhmaṇāva sujjhanti, no abrāhmaṇā; brāhmaṇāva brahmuno puttā orasā mukhato jātā brahmajā brahmanimmitā brahmadāyādā’’’ti.
322. “What do you think, great king? Suppose a noble (kṣatriya) here shaves off his hair and beard, puts on the saffron robes, and goes forth from the household life into homelessness, abstaining from killing living beings (prāṇātipātā), abstaining from taking what is not given (adinnādānā), abstaining from false speech (musāvādā), refraining from nocturnal meals, eating only one meal a day, living a life of chastity (brahmacārī), virtuous (śīlavā), and of good character (kalyāṇadhammo). What would you do for him?” “We would bow down to him, Master Kāccāna, or rise up to greet him, or offer him a seat, or invite him to accept robes, alms-food, lodgings, or medicinal requisites for curing illness; or we would arrange for him lawful guarding, defense, and protection. Why is that? Because his former designation, ‘a noble,’ has vanished; he is simply reckoned as a renunciate (śramaṇa).”

“What do you think, great king? Suppose a Brahmin (brāhmaṇa) here, or a merchant (vaiśya), or a worker (śūdra) shaves off his hair and beard, puts on the saffron robes, and goes forth from the household life into homelessness, abstaining from killing living beings (prāṇātipātā), abstaining from taking what is not given (adinnādānā), abstaining from false speech (musāvādā), refraining from nocturnal meals, eating only one meal a day, living a life of chastity (brahmacārī), virtuous (śīlavā), and of good character (kalyāṇadhammo). What would you do for him?” “We would bow down to him, Master Kāccāna, or rise up to greet him, or offer him a seat, or invite him to accept robes, alms-food, lodgings, or medicinal requisites for curing illness; or we would arrange for him lawful guarding, defense, and protection. Why is that? Because his former designation, ‘a worker,’ has vanished; he is simply reckoned as a renunciate (śramaṇa).”

“What do you think, great king? If that is so, then are these four castes (varṇā) all the same, or are they not? How do you see it?” “Indeed, Master Kāccāna, if that is so, then these four castes (varṇā) are all the same. I see no difference between them at all.”

“That is also a way, great king, whereby it can be understood how that statement in the world is just a saying: ‘The Brahmin (brāhmaṇa) is the highest caste (varṇa), other castes (varṇa) are inferior; the Brahmin (brāhmaṇa) is the fair caste (varṇa), other castes (varṇa) are dark; only Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) are purified, not non-Brahmins; Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) are the legitimate sons of Brahmā, born from his mouth, offspring of Brahmā, created by Brahmā, heirs of Brahmā."
322.Āsanena vā nimanteyyāmāti nisinnāsanaṃ papphoṭetvā idha nisīdāti vadeyyāma. Abhinimanteyyāmapi nanti abhiharitvā taṃ nimanteyyāma. Tattha duvidho abhihāro vācāya ceva kāyena ca. ‘‘Tumhākaṃ icchiticchitakkhaṇe mamaṃ cīvarādīhi vadeyyātha yenattho’’ti vadanto hi vācāya abhiharitvā nimanteti nāma. Cīvarādivekallaṃ sallakkhetvā ‘‘idaṃ gaṇhathā’’ti tāni dento pana kāyena abhiharitvā nimanteti nāma. Tadubhayampi sandhāya ‘‘abhinimanteyyāmapi na’’nti āha. Rakkhāvaraṇaguttinti rakkhāsaṅkhātañceva āvaraṇasaṅkhātañca guttiṃ. Yā panesā āvudhahatthe purise ṭhapentena rakkhā, sā dhammikā nāma saṃvihitā na hoti. Yathā pana avelāya kaṭṭhahārikāpaṇṇahārikādayo vihāraṃ na pavisanti, migaluddakādayo vihārasīmāya mige vā macche vā na gaṇhanti, evaṃ saṃvidahantena dhammikā nāma saṃvihitā hoti. Taṃ sandhāyāha ‘‘dhammika’’nti. Evaṃsanteti evaṃ catunnampi vaṇṇānaṃ pabbajitānaṃ pabbajitasakkārena same samāne. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Evaṃ sante Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Madhurasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
322. "Should we invite [him] with a seat?" Saying this, having snapped the prepared seat, one should say, "Sit here."
"Even if we over-invite [him], no," [meaning] having brought it near, we should invite him.
There, the bringing near is twofold: by speech and by body.
"At whatever moment you desire, you should speak to me with robes etc., by which there is a purpose," thus, speaking, indeed, by speech, he invites, it is said.
Having considered the lack of robes etc., "Take this," giving those, however, by body, he invites, it is said.
Having considered both of these, he said, "Even if we over-invite [him], no."
rakṣāvaraṇagutti means protection, guarding, and preservation, namely, something that is counted as protection and something that is counted as guarding.
That protection by which a person is stationed with a weapon in hand, that is not called a dhārmikā (righteous) arrangement.
Just as, however, at the wrong time, firewood carriers, leaf carriers, etc., do not enter the monastery, hunters etc., do not seize deer or fish within the boundary of the monastery, thus, by arranging, it is called a dhārmikā arrangement.
Having considered that, he said, "dhārmikā."

"Thus, while it exists," thus, in this way, for all four castes, for those who have gone forth, with the honor of those who have gone forth, it is the same, equal.
The rest is clear throughout.

Thus, while it exists, the Papañcasūdanī commentary on the Majjhimanikāya, the Madhurasuttavaṇṇanā, is finished.

323. Evaṃ vutte, rājā mādhuro avantiputto āyasmantaṃ mahākaccānaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘abhikkantaṃ, bho kaccāna, abhikkantaṃ, bho kaccāna! Seyyathāpi, bho kaccāna, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya – ‘cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantī’ti; evamevaṃ bhotā kaccānena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Esāhaṃ bhavantaṃ kaccānaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca . Upāsakaṃ maṃ bhavaṃ kaccāno dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gata’’nti. ‘‘Mā kho maṃ tvaṃ, mahārāja, saraṇaṃ agamāsi. Tameva tvaṃ tametaṃ tvaṃ (syā. kaṃ.), tametaṃ (ka.) bhagavantaṃ saraṇaṃ gaccha yamahaṃ saraṇaṃ gato’’ti. ‘‘Kahaṃ pana, bho kaccāna, etarahi so bhagavā viharati arahaṃ sammāsambuddho’’ti? ‘‘Parinibbuto kho, mahārāja, etarahi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho’’ti. ‘‘Sacepi mayaṃ, bho kaccāna, suṇeyyāma taṃ bhagavantaṃ dasasu yojanesu, dasapi mayaṃ yojanāni gaccheyyāma taṃ bhagavantaṃ dassanāya arahantaṃ sammāsambuddhaṃ. Sacepi mayaṃ, bho kaccāna, suṇeyyāma taṃ bhagavantaṃ vīsatiyā yojanesu, tiṃsāya yojanesu, cattārīsāya yojanesu, paññāsāya yojanesu, paññāsampi mayaṃ yojanāni gaccheyyāma taṃ bhagavantaṃ dassanāya arahantaṃ sammāsambuddhaṃ. Yojanasate cepi mayaṃ bho kaccāna, suṇeyyāma taṃ bhagavantaṃ, yojanasatampi mayaṃ gaccheyyāma taṃ bhagavantaṃ dassanāya arahantaṃ sammāsambuddhaṃ. Yato ca, bho kaccāna, parinibbuto so bhagavā, parinibbutampi mayaṃ bhagavantaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāma dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsakaṃ maṃ bhavaṃ kaccāno dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gata’’nti. Madhurasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ catutthaṃ.
When this was said, King Avantiputta of Madhura said to the venerable Mahā-Kaccāna: “Magnificent, Master Kaccāna! Magnificent, Master Kaccāna! Just as, Master Kaccāna, one might set upright what was overturned, or reveal what was hidden, or point out the path to one who was lost, or hold up an oil lamp in the dark, so that those with eyes might see forms, even so has the Dhamma been revealed in many ways by Master Kaccāna.

I go to Master Kaccāna for refuge, and to the Dharma, and to the monastic community ( bhikkhusaṃgha ). May Master Kaccāna regard me as a lay follower ( upāsaka ) who, from this day forward, has gone for refuge for life.”

“Do not go to me for refuge, great king. Go to the Blessed One ( bhagavant ) for refuge, to whom I have gone for refuge.”

“But where, Master Kaccāna, does that Blessed One ( bhagavā ), the worthy one, the fully enlightened one, reside now?”

“That Blessed One ( bhagavā ), great king, the worthy one, the fully enlightened one, has attained final parinirvāṇa.”

“If we were to hear, Master Kaccāna, that that Blessed One ( bhagavant ) was within ten yojanas, we would go ten yojanas in order to see that Blessed One ( bhagavant ), the worthy one, the fully enlightened one. If we were to hear, Master Kaccāna, that that Blessed One ( bhagavant ) was within twenty yojanas…thirty yojanas…forty yojanas…fifty yojanas, we would go fifty yojanas in order to see that Blessed One ( bhagavant ), the worthy one, the fully enlightened one. If we were to hear, Master Kaccāna, that that Blessed One ( bhagavant ) was within a hundred yojanas, we would go a hundred yojanas in order to see that Blessed One ( bhagavant ), the worthy one, the fully enlightened one.

And since, Master Kaccāna, that Blessed One ( bhagavā ) has attained final parinirvāṇa, we go to that Blessed One ( bhagavant ) for refuge, and to the Dharma, and to the monastic community ( bhikkhusaṃgha ). May Master Kaccāna regard me as a lay follower ( upāsaka ) who, from this day forward, has gone for refuge for life.”

The Madhura Sutta, the fourth, is finished.
