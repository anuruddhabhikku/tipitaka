<p n="313" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">313</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0411"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0271"/><pb ed="P" n="3.0223"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘uddesavibhaṅgaṃ vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Tathā tathā, bhikkhave, bhikkhu upaparikkheyya yathā yathā <note>yathā yathāssa (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note> upaparikkhato bahiddhā cassa viññāṇaṃ avikkhittaṃ avisaṭaṃ, ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhitaṃ anupādāya na paritasseyya. Bahiddhā, bhikkhave, viññāṇe avikkhitte avisaṭe sati ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhite anupādāya aparitassato āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇadukkhasamudayasambhavo na hotī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Idaṃ vatvāna sugato uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṃ pāvisi.

<note>yathā yathāssa (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note>
Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One (Bhagavā) was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park (Jetavane Anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: “Monks (bhikkhavo)”.

“Venerable sir (bhadante),” those monks replied to the Blessed One (bhagavato).

The Blessed One said this: “Bhikkhus, I shall teach you a summary and an exposition (uddesavibhaṅgaṃ).

Listen & pay close attention (taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha). I will speak.”

“As you say, lord (evaṃ, bhante),” the monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Monks (bhikkhave), a bhikkhu should examine things in such a way that as he is examining them, his consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) is not distracted and scattered externally (bahiddhā) nor stuck internally (ajjhattaṃ), and by not clinging (anupādāya) he does not become agitated (na paritasseyya).

Monks (bhikkhave), if consciousness (viññāṇe) is not distracted and scattered externally (bahiddhā), when there is internal stability (ajjhattaṃ asaṇṭhite) , from lack of clinging (anupādāya), he is unagitated (aparitassato), and there is no coming into being of the origination of future birth (jāti), aging (jarā), death (maraṇa), or stress (dukkha) (dukkhSamudayasambhavo na hoti).”

This is what the Buddha said (idamavoca bhagavā).

Having said this, the Happy One (sugato) rose from his seat (uṭṭhāyāsanā) and went into his living quarters (vihāraṃ pāvisi).

315. It is just as, friends, a man seeking heartwood, searching for heartwood, wandering in search of heartwood, might overlook the root of a large tree that is standing and full of heartwood; overlooking the trunk, branches and foliage, he would think that heartwood is to be sought in the branches and foliage. So too, even though the venerable ones have the Teacher right in front of them, you think that you should ask me about this matter, passing over that Blessed One.

For knowing, the Blessed One knows; seeing, he sees; he is vision, he is knowledge, he is the dharma, he is the holy one; he is the sayer, the proclaimer, the elucidator of meaning (artha), the giver of the Deathless (amata), the lord of the dharma, the tathāgata.

That was the time when you should have asked the Blessed One the meaning. As he told you, so you should have remembered it.’

‘Surely, friend Kaccāna, knowing, the Blessed One knows; seeing, he sees; he is vision, he is knowledge, he is the dharma, he is the holy one; he is the sayer, the proclaimer, the elucidator of meaning, the giver of the Deathless, the lord of the dharma, the tathāgata.

That was the time when we should have asked the Blessed One the meaning. As he told us, so we should have remembered it.

Yet the venerable Mahākaccāna is praised by the Teacher and esteemed by his wise companions in the holy life.

The venerable Mahākaccāna is capable of expounding the detailed meaning of this summary given in brief by the Blessed One without expounding the detailed meaning.

Let the venerable Mahākaccāna expound it without finding it troublesome.’

“Then listen, friends, and attend closely to what I shall say.”

“Yes, friend,” the monks replied to the venerable Mahākaccāna.

The venerable Mahākaccāna said this:

“That point, friends, that the Blessed One, after giving a summary and teaching a brief teaching, arose from his seat and entered the monastery without explaining the meaning in detail—in that case, monks, a monk should examine it thus: ‘In what way, as he examines, would his consciousness (vijñāna) not be scattered externally, not be diffused, be established internally, and by not clinging, would he not be agitated? For, monks, when consciousness is not scattered or diffused externally, it is established internally, and by not clinging, he is not agitated. In the future, there is no arising of birth, aging, death, suffering, and the origin of suffering.’

I understand the detailed meaning of this summary to be thus.

"And how, friend, is the mind called ‘stuck internally’?" Here, friend, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with rapture (pīti) and pleasure (sukha) born of seclusion (vivekaja). If his consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) follows after the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, is tied and shackled by gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, then his mind is called ‘stuck internally.’

"Again, friend, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the second jhāna, which has self-confidence and singleness of mind without applied and sustained thought, with rapture (pīti) and pleasure (sukha) born of concentration (samādhija). If his consciousness follows after the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, is tied and shackled by gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, then his mind is called ‘stuck internally.’

"Again, friend, with the fading away as well of rapture, a bhikkhu abides in equanimity (upekkhā), and mindful and fully aware, still feeling pleasure (sukha) with the body (kāya), he enters upon and abides in the third jhāna, on account of which noble ones announce: ‘He has a pleasant abiding who has equanimity (upekkhā) and is mindful.’ If his consciousness follows after the equanimity, is tied and shackled by gratification in the equanimity and pleasure, then his mind is called ‘stuck internally.’

Again, friend, with the abandoning of pleasure (sukha) and pain (dukkha), and with the previous disappearance of joy (somanassa) and grief (domanassa), a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna, which has neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukha) and purity of mindfulness (sati) due to equanimity (upekkhā). If his consciousness follows after the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, is tied and shackled by gratification in the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, then his mind is called ‘stuck internally.’

That is how the mind is called ‘stuck internally.’

319. How, friends, is the mind called ‘not stuck internally’? Here, friends, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna. If his consciousness does not follow after the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, is not tied and shackled by gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, then his mind is called ‘not stuck internally.’

Again, friends, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the second jhāna. If his consciousness does not follow after the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, is not tied and shackled by gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, then his mind is called ‘not stuck internally.’

Again, friends, with the fading away as well of rapture, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the third jhāna. If his consciousness does not follow after the equanimity, is not tied and shackled by gratification in the equanimity, is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the equanimity, then his mind is called ‘not stuck internally.’

Again, friends, with the abandoning of pleasure and pain, and with the previous disappearance of joy and grief, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna, which has neither-pain-nor-pleasure and purity of mindfulness due to equanimity. If his consciousness does not follow after the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, is not tied and shackled by gratification in the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, is not fettered by the fetter of gratification in the neither-pain-nor-pleasure, then his mind is called ‘not stuck internally.’

That is how the mind is called ‘not stuck internally.’

320. “But how, friend, is there agitation without clinging? Here, friend, an untaught ordinary person who has no regard for noble ones, and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, who has no regard for true men and is unskilled and undisciplined in their Dhamma, regards material form (rūpa) as self (ātman), or self as possessed of material form, or material form as in self, or self as in material form.

That material form of his changes and becomes otherwise.

With the change and becoming otherwise of that material form, his consciousness (vijñāna) is preoccupied with the change of material form.

Agitated mental states born of preoccupation with the change of material form arise together and remain obsessing his mind (citta).

Because his mind is obsessed, he is frightened, distressed, and anxious, and through clinging he becomes agitated.

He regards feeling (vedanā) …pe… perception (saṃjñā)… formations (saṃskāre)… consciousness (vijñāṇa) as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness.

That consciousness of his changes and becomes otherwise.

With the change and becoming otherwise of that consciousness, his consciousness is preoccupied with the change of consciousness.

Agitation and a constellation of mental states born of preoccupation with the change of consciousness remain obsessing his mind.

Because his mind is obsessed, he is frightened, distressed, and anxious, and through clinging he becomes agitated.

That is how, friend, there is agitation without clinging.

321. "But how, friend, does non-clinging lead to non-agitation? Here, friend, an instructed noble disciple, a seer of the noble ones, skilled in the Dhamma of the noble ones, well-trained in the Dhamma of the noble ones, a seer of true men, skilled in the Dhamma of true men, well-trained in the Dhamma of true men, does not regard material form (rūpa) as self (ātman), or self as possessed of material form, or material form as in self, or self as in material form.

That material form of his changes and becomes otherwise.

With the change and becoming otherwise of that material form, his consciousness (vijñāṇa) is not preoccupied with the change of material form.

Agitated mental states born of preoccupation with the change of material form do not arise together and remain obsessing his mind.

Because his mind is not obsessed, he is not anxious, distressed, and concerned, and due to non-clinging (anupādāna) he does not become agitated (aparitassanā).

He does not regard feeling (vedanā)... perception (saṃjñā)... formations (saṃskhāra)... consciousness (vijñāṇa) as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness.

That consciousness of his changes and becomes otherwise.

With the change and becoming otherwise of that consciousness, his consciousness is not preoccupied with the change of consciousness.

Agitated mental states born of preoccupation with the change of consciousness do not arise together and remain obsessing his mind.

Because his mind is not obsessed, he is not anxious, distressed, and concerned, and through non-clinging (anupādāna) he does not become agitated (aparitassanā).

That is how, friend, there is non-agitation due to non-clinging (anupādā aparitassanā hoti).

"Friends, when the Blessed One (bhagavā) rose from his seat and went into his dwelling after giving a summary in brief without expounding the detailed meaning, that is: ‘Monks, a monk should investigate in such a way that–his consciousness (vijñāṇa) neither externally scattered & diffused, nor internally positioned– and by not clinging he does not become agitated.

If his consciousness is not distracted and scattered externally nor stuck internally, and if by not clinging he does not become agitated, then for him there is no origination of suffering - of birth (jāti), ageing (jarā), and death (maraṇa)- or stress (dukkha) in the future,’

I understand the detailed meaning of it to be as follows.

Now, friends, if you wish, go to the Blessed One and ask him about the meaning of this. As the Blessed One explains it to you, so you should remember it.”
