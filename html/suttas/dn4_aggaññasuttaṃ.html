Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvatthī in the Eastern Park in Migāramātā’s mansion.

And at that time Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja were living among the monks, hoping to become monks themselves.

And in the evening, the Blessed One rose from his secluded meditation and came out of the mansion, and started walking up and down in the open space in the shade of the mansion.

111. It is like this.

This is what I have heard: the Aggaññasutta.

Here is an explanation of the words with obvious meaning: "In the Pūrvārāma (Eastern Garden), in the palace of Migāramātā," this is the sequential account.

In the distant past, at the culmination of one hundred thousand kappas, there was a female lay follower (upāsikā) who invited the Blessed One Padumuttara, and after giving alms to a hundred thousand monks headed by the Buddha, she knelt at the feet of the Blessed One and made the aspiration, "In the future, may I be foremost among the female attendants of a Buddha like you."

Having transmigrated for a hundred thousand kappas among gods and humans, in the time of our Blessed One, in the city of Bhaddiya, in the home of Dhanañcaya, the wealthy merchant, the son of Meṇḍaka, the wealthy merchant, she took rebirth in the womb of Sumanadevī.

At the time of her birth, they gave her the name Visākhā.

When the Blessed One arrived in the city of Bhaddiya, she, together with five hundred slave girls, went to greet the Blessed One, and at the very first sight, she became a sotāpanna (stream-enterer).

Later, in Śrāvastī, she went to the house of Puṇṇavaḍḍhana-kumāra, the son of Migāra, the wealthy merchant.

There, Migāra, the wealthy merchant, placed her in the position of a mother.

Therefore, she is called Migāramātā.

When she was going to her husband's family, her father had a great jeweled ornament (mahālatāpiḷandhana) made.

In that ornament, four diamond pipes (vajiranāḷiyo) were used, eleven pipes (nāḷiyo) of pearls (muttānaṃ), twenty-two pipes (nāḷiyo) of coral (pavāḷassa), and thirty-three pipes (nāḷiyo) of jewels (maṇīnaṃ).

Thus, it was completed with these seven jewels and other jewels.

When placed on her head, it would hang down to the instep of her feet.

Only a woman possessing the strength of five elephants was able to bear it.

Later, having become the chief attendant of the One with the Ten Powers (dasabala), she gave away that ornament, and while building a monastery (vihāra) for the Blessed One with nine koṭīs, she had a palace (pāsādaṃ) built on a plot of land equal to a karīsa.

In the upper story of that, there were five hundred chambers (gabbhasatāni), and in the lower story, there were five hundred, so it was adorned with a thousand chambers (gabbhasahassappaṭimaṇḍito).

She thought, "The pure palace is not beautiful," so surrounding it, she had five hundred two-and-a-half storied houses, five hundred small palaces, and five hundred long halls built.

The dedication ceremony (vihāramaho) was completed in four months.

In the state of being a woman, there is no giving of wealth (dhanapariccāgo) in the Buddha's Dispensation like that of Visākhā, and in the state of being a man, there is no giving like that of Anāthapiṇḍika.

For he, having given away fifty-four koṭīs, had the Jetavanamahāvihāra, resembling the Mahāvihāra of Anurādhapura, built in the southern part of Śrāvastī.

Visākhā had the Pūrvārāma (Eastern Garden) built in the eastern part of Śrāvastī, in a place resembling the monastery of Uttaradevi.

Out of compassion for these two families, the Blessed One, relying on Śrāvastī, while dwelling there, constantly resided in these two monasteries.

He spent one rainy season in Jetavana, and one in Pūrvārāma.

At that time, the Blessed One was dwelling in Pūrvārāma.

Therefore, it was said, "In the Pūrvārāma, in the palace of Migāramātā."

"Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja" means Vāseṭṭha and the novice Bhāradvāja.

"Living among the monks" means they do not live the probationary period (parivāsa) of the sectarians (titthiya), nor the probationary period (parivāsa) for offenses (āpatti).

But, because their years are not complete (aparipuṇṇavassattā), they live desiring the state of a monk (bhikkhubhāvaṃ).

Therefore, he said, "Desiring the state of a monk (bhikkhubhāvaṃ ākaṅkhamānā)."

Both of them were born in noble families of Udicca Brahmins, possessing forty koṭīs each, were experts in the three Vedas, and having heard the Vāseṭṭhasutta in the Majjhimanikāya, they had gone for refuge. Having heard the Tevijjasutta, having gone forth, at this time, desiring the state of a monk (bhikkhubhāvaṃ ākaṅkhamānā), they are living the probationary period (parivasanti).

113. Then Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja approached the Blessed One; having approached, having greeted the Blessed One, they followed the Blessed One as he was walking back and forth.

Then the Blessed One addressed Vāseṭṭha, "From what group do you come, Vāseṭṭha? You have gone forth from the household life into homelessness from the Brahmin (brāhmaṇa) caste, from a Brahmin family, from the Brahmin clan. Do the Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) not revile or speak disparagingly of you, Vāseṭṭha?"

"Indeed, they do, venerable sir. The Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) revile and speak disparagingly of us with complete and utter disparagement."

"But in what way, Vāseṭṭha, do the Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) revile and speak disparagingly of you with complete and utter disparagement?"

"Venerable sir, the Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) say thus: 'Only the Brahmins (brāhmaṇova) are the highest class (varṇa); other classes (vaṇṇā) are inferior. Only the Brahmins (brāhmaṇova) are the fair class (vaṇṇa); other classes (vaṇṇā) are dark. Only the Brahmins (brāhmaṇāva) are purified; non-Brahmins are not. The Brahmins (brāhmaṇāva) are the true children of Brahmā, born of his mouth, offspring of Brahmā, created by Brahmā, heirs of Brahmā.

You have abandoned the highest class (seṭṭhaṃ varṇaṃ) and have taken to the inferior class (hīnamattha vaṇṇaṃ), namely these shavelings, these ascetics, these servants, these dark fellows, these descendants of Bandhu's feet.

This is not right; this is not proper; you have abandoned the highest class (seṭṭhaṃ varṇaṃ) and have taken to the inferior class (hīnamattha vaṇṇaṃ), namely these shavelings, these ascetics, these servants, these dark fellows, these descendants of Bandhu's feet.’

In this way, venerable sir, the Brahmins (brāhmaṇā) revile and speak disparagingly of us with complete and utter disparagement."

132. Then, Vāseṭṭha, some of those beings thought: “Evil things have appeared among beings, such as taking what is not given (adinnādāna), censure (garahā), lying (musāvāda), punishment (daṇḍādāna) and banishment (pabbājana).

“Now These Do Not Meditate” is the meaning of ajjhāyakā "non-meditators," which is the third regular title to be introduced.

‘And a Kṣatriya, Vāseṭṭha, who performs deeds of both kinds through body (kāyena), speech (vācāya), and mind (manasā); whose view is mixed (vimissadiṭṭhiko), whose undertaking of deeds is based on mixed views (vimissadiṭṭhikammasamādāno), and whose cause for undertaking deeds is mixed views, will, at the breaking up of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), experience both pleasure (sukha) and pain (dukkha).

A Brāhmaṇa, Vāseṭṭha… a merchant, Vāseṭṭha… a Śūdra, Vāseṭṭha… a renunciate, Vāseṭṭha, who performs deeds of both kinds through body, speech, and mind; whose view is mixed, whose undertaking of deeds is based on mixed views, and whose cause for undertaking deeds is mixed views, will, at the breaking up of the body, after death, experience both pleasure and pain.

Cultivating the Qualities Conducive to Awakening (bodhipakkhiyabhāvanā).

<p rend="subhead">Bodhipakkhiyabhāvanāvaṇṇanā

137. "A doer of both [good and evil]" means that at times one does what is wholesome (kuśala), and at times one does what is unwholesome (akuśala); thus, one is a doer of both.

"Experiencing pleasure and pain" means there is no such thing as a place for giving two results, wholesome and unwholesome, in a single moment.

Moreover, someone who has done much that is unwholesome (akuśala) and little that is wholesome (kuśala), is reborn, relying on that wholesome karma, in a kṣatriya family or a brāhmaṇa family.

Then, unwholesome karma makes them blind (kāṇa), hunchbacked (khujja), or a "stool-serpent" (pīṭhasappin).

They are unworthy of kingship (rājya), or, at the time of consecration (abhiṣeka-kāla), being of such a nature, they are unable to enjoy possessions (bhoga).

At the time of death of another, two strong wrestlers, as it were, two wholesome and unwholesome actions (kuśalākuśalakarmāṇi) attend to them.

Among these, the unwholesome (akuśala) is stronger; having repelled the wholesome (kuśala), it causes rebirth in the animal realm (tiracchānayoni).

A wholesome action (kuśalakarma) is also something to be experienced as it matures.

They make them either an auspicious elephant (maṅgalahastin), an auspicious horse (maṅgalaassa), or an auspicious bull (maṅgalausabha).

They experience prosperity (sampatti).

This was said with reference to "experiencing pleasure and pain."

138. sattannaṃ bodhipakkhiyāna means of the seven—as in, "the four establishments of mindfulness ( cattāro satipaṭṭhānā )"—in terms of the initial divisions; however, in order, of the thirty-seven qualities pertaining to enlightenment (bodhipakkhiyānaṃ dhammānaṃ). bhāvanamanvāyā means having followed after cultivation (bhāvanaṃ anugantvā), that is, having practiced. parinibbāyatī means attains final nirvāṇa (parinibbāna) through the nirvāṇa of defilements (kilesaparinibbānena). Thus, the Blessed One, having shown the four castes and having dismissed them, showed that only the one whose four truths have been penetrated, the one whose outflows are ended (khīṇāsava), is the best among gods and humans.
