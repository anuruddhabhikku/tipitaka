On one occasion the Blessed One was staying near Campā on the banks of the Gaggarā Lake with a large community of monks, approximately five hundred monks in all.

There the venerable Śāriputra addressed the monks: "Friends, monks!" "Friend," those monks replied to the venerable Śāriputra. The venerable Śāriputra said this:

"I will explain the ten ascending qualities, the Dharma leading to the attainment of nirvāṇa,

Ekadhammavaṇṇanā
350. Thus have I heard: the Dasuttara Sutta.

Here begins the word-by-word commentary: "Āvuso bhikkhave," this is an address to the disciples. Buddhas, when addressing an assembly, say "bhikkhave". Disciples, thinking "we will place the Teacher in a high position," address the Teacher not with the Teacher's form of address, but with "āvuso".

"Te bhikkhū" means those monks who were sitting surrounding the general of the Dharma.

Who are those monks? Monks who do not have a fixed residence, monks who can come from any direction.

In the Buddha's time, monks would gather on two occasions: at the time for approaching the rains retreat, and at the time for the Pavāraṇā ceremony.

For the approaching rains retreat, groups of ten, twenty, thirty, forty, or fifty monks would come, group by group, for the purpose of [receiving] a meditation subject.

The Blessed One, having greeted them, would ask, "Why, monks, do you wander for the approaching rains retreat?"

Then they would request, "Blessed One, we have come for a meditation subject; give us a meditation subject."

The Teacher would give them an unsuitable meditation subject according to their disposition.

To one of lustful disposition, he would give an impure meditation subject.

To one of hateful disposition, he would give a meditation subject of loving-kindness; to one of deluded disposition, instruction and questioning: "Listening to the Dharma at the right time, discussion of the Dharma at the right time, this is suitable for you," he would say.

To one of discursive disposition, he would give a meditation subject of mindfulness of breathing.

To one of faithful disposition, he would reveal the Buddha's good enlightenment, the Dharma's good nature, and the Sangha's good practice in the Pāsādanīya Suttanta.

To one of wise disposition, he would speak of profound suttas connected with impermanence and so on.

Having taken the meditation subject, if it is suitable, they would stay right there.

If it is not, they would ask about a suitable dwelling and leave.

Staying there, taking up the three-month practice, striving and endeavoring, they would become stream-enterers, once-returners, non-returners, or arahants.

Then, having completed the rains retreat, having performed the Pavāraṇā ceremony, going to the Teacher's presence, they would announce, "Blessed One, having taken a meditation subject in your presence, I have attained the fruit of stream-entry...pe...I have attained the highest fruit, arahantship," they would announce the qualities they had regained.

There, these monks had come for the approaching rains retreat.

When these monks had come and were leaving, the Blessed One sent [them] to the presence of the chief disciples, as he said, "Consult Sāriputta and Moggallāna, monks."

And the monks would say, "What should we consult Sāriputta and Moggallāna about, venerable sir?" (Saṃ. Ni. 3.2).

Then the Blessed One urged them to see them.

"Associate with Sāriputta and Moggallāna, monks; keep company with Sāriputta and Moggallāna.

They are wise monks who assist those living the spiritual life.

Sāriputta is just like one who gives birth, monks.

Moggallāna is just like a wet nurse to the one new-born, monks;

Sāriputta trains others for the fruit of stream-entry, Moggallāna for the supreme goal" (Ma. Ni. 3.
371).

Even then, the Blessed One, after exchanging courteous greetings with these monks, examining the inclinations of those monks, saw that "these monks are capable of being guided by disciples (sāvakavineyyā)."

Sāvakavineyyā means those who understand the Dharma teaching of the Buddhas and also of the disciples.

However, those capable of being guided by Buddhas (buddhavineyyā) are not able to be awakened by disciples.

Knowing their state of being capable of being guided by disciples, considering which monk's teaching they would understand, seeing that it was Sāriputta's, he sent [them] to the elder's presence.

The elder asked those monks, "Have you gone to the Teacher's presence, friends?"

"Yes, we have gone, but the Teacher has sent us to your presence."

Then the elder, thinking, "These monks will understand my teaching; what kind of teaching is suitable for them?" decided, "These monks delight in harmony; a teaching that illuminates the essence of harmony is suitable for them," and desiring to teach such a teaching, he said, beginning with "dasuttaraṃ pavakkhāmī."

There, dasuttara, because he established the matrix in ten ways and divided [it]; dasuttara also because [he] went from the one to the tens; dasuttara also because ten questions were distinguished in each section; that dasuttaraṃ.

Pavakkhāmī means I will tell.

Dhamma means discourse (sutta).

Nibbānapattiyā means for the purpose of attaining nirvāṇa.

Dukkhassantakiriyāyā means for the purpose of making an end to all the suffering of the cycle [of rebirth] (vaṭṭa).

Sabbaganthappamocana means the freeing from all bonds (gantha), beginning with the bond of covetousness (abhijjhā).

sāvakavineyyā, buddhavineyyā, dasuttaraṃ pavakkhāmī, dasuttaraṃ pavakkhāmī, dhamma, nibbānapattiyā, dukkhassantakiriyāyā, sabbaganthappamocana

Thus, the elder, raising his voice in teaching, generating affection for the monks there, thinking, "They will grasp this, learn it, remember it, recite it," described the qualities with four phrases, like the Blessed One regarding those discourses in the manner beginning with "This is the one way, monks."

351. “One dhamma, friends, is very helpful. One dhamma should be developed. One dhamma should be comprehended. One dhamma should be abandoned. One dhamma is on the side of decline. One dhamma is on the side of distinction. One dhamma is hard to penetrate. One dhamma should be made to arise. One dhamma should be directly known. One dhamma should be realized.

(ka) “What is the one dhamma that is very helpful (bahukāro)? It is diligence (appamāda) in wholesome qualities (kusalesu dhammesu). This one dhamma is very helpful.

(kha) “What is the one dhamma that should be developed (bhāvetabbo)? Mindfulness occupied with the body (kāyagatāsati) accompanied by pleasure (sātasahagatā). This one dhamma should be developed.

(ga) “What is the one dhamma that should be comprehended (pariññeyyo)? Contact (phasso) that has cankers (sāsavo) and is connected with clinging (upādāniyo). This one dhamma should be comprehended.

(gha) “What is the one dhamma that should be abandoned (pahātabbo)? The conceit ‘I am’ (asmimāno). This one dhamma should be abandoned.

(ṅa) “What is the one dhamma that is on the side of decline (hānabhāgiyo)? Unwise attention (ayoniso manasikāro). This one dhamma is on the side of decline.

(ca) “What is the one dhamma that is on the side of distinction (visesabhāgiyo)? Wise attention (yoniso manasikāro). This one dhamma is on the side of distinction.

(cha) “What is the one dhamma that is hard to penetrate (duppaṭivijjho)? The concentration of mind that is immediately succeeding (ānantariko cetosamādhi). This one dhamma is hard to penetrate.

(ja) “What is the one dhamma that should be made to arise (uppādetabbo)? Unshakable knowledge (akuppaṃ ñāṇaṃ). This one dhamma should be made to arise.

(jha) “What is the one dhamma that should be directly known (abhiññeyyo)? All beings subsist on nutriment (sabbe sattā āhāraṭṭhitikā). This one dhamma should be directly known.

(ña) “What is the one dhamma that should be realized (sacchikātabbo)? Unshakable liberation of mind (akuppā cetovimutti). This one dhamma should be realized.

“Thus, these ten dhammas are real, true, actual, not false, not otherwise, rightly awakened to by the Tathāgata.

360. “Ten dhammas, friends, are very helpful… Ten dhammas should be realized.”

(a) “Which ten dhammas are very helpful (bahukārā)? Ten dhammas that create a protector (nāthakaraṇa)—Here, friends, a monk is virtuous (sīlavā), dwells restrained by the restraint of the Pātimokkha, possessing good conduct and proper domain, seeing danger in the slightest faults, undertaking and training himself in the training rules. And the fact that the monk is virtuous…trains himself in the training rules: this dhamma is one that creates a protector (nāthakaraṇa).

“Furthermore (puna ca paraṃ), friends, the monk has heard much (bahussuto)…is well-penetrated in terms of his views. And the fact that he has heard much…this dhamma is one that creates a protector.

“Furthermore, friends, the monk has admirable friends (kalyāṇamitto), admirable comrades, admirable companions. And the fact that he has admirable friends…admirable companions: this dhamma is one that creates a protector.

“Furthermore, friends, the monk is easy to speak to (suvaco), endowed with qualities that make him easy to speak to, patient, respectful to instruction (anusāsaniṃ). And the fact that he is easy to speak to…respectful to instruction: this dhamma is one that creates a protector.

“Furthermore, friends, the monk is adept at the various affairs involving his companions in the holy life (sabrahmacārīnaṃ), is vigorous, quick-witted in the techniques involved in them, is up to doing them or arranging to get them done. And the fact that he is adept at…doing them or arranging to get them done: this dhamma is one that creates a protector.

“Furthermore, friends, the monk is one who desires the dharma (dharmakāmo), endearing in his conversation (piyasamudāhāro), greatly rejoicing in the higher dharma (abhidhamma) & higher Vinaya (abhivinaye). And the fact that the monk…greatly rejoicing: this dhamma is one that creates a protector.

“Furthermore, friends, the monk is content (santuṭṭho) with any old robe cloth at all, any old alms food, any old lodging, any old medicinal requisites for curing sickness at all. And the fact that the monk…this dhamma is one that creates a protector.

“Furthermore, friends, the monk keeps his persistence aroused (āraddhavīriyo)…with regard to skillful qualities (kusalesu dhammesu). And the fact that the monk…this dhamma is one that creates a protector.

“Furthermore, friends, the monk is mindful (satimā), endowed with excellent proficiency in mindfulness (satinepakkena), remembering & recollecting what was done & said a long time ago. And the fact that the monk…this dhamma is one that creates a protector.

“Furthermore, friends, the monk is discerning (paññavā), endowed with the discernment related to arising & passing away (udayatthagāminiyā paññāya)—noble (ariyāya), penetrating (nibbedhikāya), leading to the right ending of stress (sammā dukkhakkhayagāminiyā). And the fact that the monk…this dhamma is one that creates a protector.
These ten dhammas are very helpful.

One perceives the water-kasiṇa (āpokasiṇa)… the fire-kasiṇa (tejokasiṇa)… the wind-kasiṇa (vāyokasiṇa)… the blue-kasiṇa (nīlakasiṇa)… the yellow-kasiṇa (pītakasiṇa)… the red-kasiṇa (lohitakasiṇa)… the white-kasiṇa (odātakasiṇa)… the space-kasiṇa (ākāsakasiṇa)… the consciousness-kasiṇa (viññāṇakasiṇa) above, below, all around, non-dual, immeasurable. These ten dhammas should be developed.

(ga) What ten dhamma should be fully understood (pariññeyyā)? These ten dhamma should be fully understood.

(gha) What ten dhamma should be abandoned (pahātabbā)? They are the ten wrongnesses (dasa micchattā): wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi), wrong intention (micchāsaṅkappo), wrong speech (micchāvācā), wrong action (micchākammanto), wrong livelihood (micchāājīvo), wrong effort (micchāvāyāmo), wrong mindfulness (micchāsati), wrong concentration (micchāsamādhi), wrong knowledge (micchāñāṇaṃ), and wrong liberation (micchāvimutti). These ten dhamma should be abandoned.

(ṅa) What ten dhamma are on the side of decline (hānabhāgiyā)? They are the ten unskillful courses of action (dasa akusalakammapathā): killing (pāṇātipāto), taking what is not given (adinnādānaṃ), sexual misconduct (kāmesumicchācāro), lying (musāvādo), divisive speech (pisuṇā vācā), harsh speech (pharusā vācā), idle chatter (samphappalāpo), covetousness (abhijjhā), ill will (byāpādo), and wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi). These ten dhamma are on the side of decline.

(ca) What ten dhamma are on the side of distinction (visesabhāgiyā)? They are the ten skillful courses of action (dasa kusalakammapathā): abstaining from killing (pāṇātipātā veramaṇī), abstaining from taking what is not given (adinnādānā veramaṇī), abstaining from sexual misconduct (kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇī), abstaining from lying (musāvādā veramaṇī), abstaining from divisive speech (pisuṇāya vācāya veramaṇī), abstaining from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya veramaṇī), abstaining from idle chatter (samphappalāpā veramaṇī), non-covetousness (anabhijjhā), non-ill will (abyāpādo), and right view (sammādiṭṭhi). These ten dhamma are on the side of distinction.

(cha) What ten dhamma are difficult to penetrate (duppaṭivijjhā)? They are the ten noble abodes (dasa ariyavāsā): Here, a bhikkhu has abandoned five factors (pañcaṅgavippahīno), is endowed with six (chaḷaṅgasamannāgato), guards one (ekārakkho), is supported in four (caturāpasseno), has shaken off idiosyncratic truths (paṇunnapaccekasacco), has thoroughly given up searching (samavayasaṭṭhesano), is undisturbed in his resolves (anāvilasaṅkappo), is calmed in his bodily fabrication (passaddhakāyasaṅkhāro), is well-released in mind (suvimuttacitto), and is well-released in discernment (suvimuttapañño).

How, friend, is a bhikkhu one who has abandoned five factors (pañcaṅgavippahīno)? Here, friend, a bhikkhu has abandoned sensual desire (kāmacchando), has abandoned ill will (byāpādo), has abandoned sloth and torpor (thinamiddhaṃ), has abandoned restlessness and anxiety (uddhaccakukkuccaṃ), and has abandoned uncertainty (vicikicchā). This is how a bhikkhu has abandoned five factors.

How, friend, is a bhikkhu one who is endowed with six? Here, friend, having seen a form with the eye, a bhikkhu is neither joyful nor saddened, but dwells equanimous, mindful, and clearly comprehending. Having heard a sound with the ear… Having smelled an odor with the nose… Having tasted a flavor with the tongue… Having felt a tangible object with the body… Having cognized a mental phenomenon with the mind, he is neither joyful nor saddened, but dwells equanimous, mindful, and clearly comprehending. This is how a bhikkhu is endowed with six.

How, friend, is a bhikkhu one who guards one? Here, friend, a bhikkhu is endowed with a mind guarded by mindfulness. This is how a bhikkhu guards one.

How, friend, is a bhikkhu one who is supported in four? Here, friend, a bhikkhu, considering, makes use of one thing; considering, he endures one thing; considering, he avoids one thing; considering, he gets rid of one thing. This is how a bhikkhu is supported in four.

How, friend, is a bhikkhu one who has shaken off idiosyncratic truths (paṇunnapaccekasacco)? Here, friend, whatever idiosyncratic truths are held by various ascetics and brāhmaṇas, all of them are rejected, dispelled, given up, relinquished, released, abandoned, and forsaken. This is how a bhikkhu has shaken off idiosyncratic truths.

How, friend, is a bhikkhu one who has thoroughly given up searching (samavayasaṭṭhesano)? Here, friend, a bhikkhu has abandoned the search for sensual pleasures (kāmesanā), has abandoned the search for existence (bhavesanā), and his search for the spiritual life (brahmacariyesanā) is calmed. This is how a bhikkhu has thoroughly given up searching.

How, friend, is a bhikkhu one who is undisturbed in his resolves (anāvilasaṅkappā)? Here, friend, a bhikkhu has abandoned his resolve for sensuality (kāmasaṅkappo), has abandoned his resolve for ill will (byāpādasaṅkappo), and has abandoned his resolve for harmfulness (vihiṃsāsaṅkappo). This is how a bhikkhu is undisturbed in his resolves.

And the explanation (vaṇṇanā) of the Pāthikavagga is finished (niṭṭhitā).

The Pāthikavaggaṭṭhakathā is finished (niṭṭhitā).

Concluding Remarks (nigamanakathā)

Thus far…

Requested by Thīraguṇena, dwelling in Pariveṇa Sumaṅgala;

By Dāṭhānāgasaṅghatthera, in accordance with the lineage of the elders.

The commentary (aṭṭhakathaṃ) on the ten powers (dasabala) of the excellent Dīghāgama, the illuminator of the multitude of qualities (guṇagaṇaparidīpanassa);

That which I began, named Sumaṅgala, Vilāsinī by name.

That indeed, having taken the essence (sāramādāya) of the Great Commentary (mahāṭṭhakathāya), is finished (niṭṭhitā);

This is eighty-one in measure (ekāsītipamāṇāya), by recitational portions (bhāṇavārehi) of the Pāḷi.

Sixty-nine in measure (ekūnasaṭṭhimatto), so too the Visuddhimagga by recitational portions (bhāṇavārehi);

Because it was made for the purpose of revealing the meaning (atthappakāsanatthāya) of the Āgama.

Therefore, together with that, this commentary (aṭṭhakathā) by the calculation of recitational portions (bhāṇavāragaṇanāya);

Being well-measured and well-defined (suparimitaparicchinnaṃ), is four thousand (cattālīsasataṃ hoti).

All, being a hundred with forty more, the measure (parimāṇaṃ) is thus from the recitational portions (bhāṇavārato evaṃ);

Revealing (pakāsayantiṃ) the occasion (samayaṃ), dwelling in the Great Monastery (mahāvihāre nivāsinaṃ).

Having taken the essence (sāra) of the Commentary on the Root (mūlakaṭṭhakathā), by me, while doing this (imaṃ karontena);

Whatever merit (puññam) has been accumulated (upacitaṃ), by that, may the whole world be happy (sabbo sukhī lokoti).

By the elder (therena) named Buddhaghosa, whose name was taken by the teachers, endowed with vast and pure intelligence, an ornament of the lineage (vaṃsālaṅkārabhūtena) of the elders (therānaṃ), a lamp of the lineage (theravaṃsappadīpānaṃ), dwelling in the Great Monastery (mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ), whose minds are well-established in the qualities of superior humanity (uttarimanussadhamme suppatiṭṭhitabuddhīnaṃ), adorned with qualities such as the six direct knowledges (chaḷabhiññādippabhedaguṇa paṭimaṇḍite), surrounded by a retinue of distinct discriminations (pabhinnapaṭisambhidāparivāre), an excellent speaker (vādīvarena), a great poet (mahākavinā), speaking words that are connected and released (yuttamuttavādinā), endowed with the loveliness (lāvaṇṇayuttena) of sweet and noble words (madhurodāravacana), issuing from the joy born of the accomplishment of the means (karaṇasampattijanitasukha viniggata), a great grammarian (mahāveyyākaraṇena), whose power of knowledge (ñāṇappabhāvena) is unimpeded (appaṭihata) in the Teaching of the Teacher (satthusāsane), with its divisions of the transmitted word of the Three Baskets (tipiṭaka pariyatti ppabhede), together with the commentary (sāṭṭhakathe), endowed with skill in understanding (paññāveyyattiyasamannāgatena), capable of plunging into the depths (gahanajjhogāhaṇa samatthena) of his own school (sakasamaya) and other schools (samayantara), abounding in the arising of qualities (guṇasamudaya samuditena) such as morality, conduct, uprightness, gentleness, etc. (sīlācārajjavamaddavādi), adorned with the most pure faith, intelligence, and energy (paramavisuddhasaddhābuddhivīriya paṭimaṇḍitena), this commentary on the Long Discourses (dīghanikāyaṭṭhakathā) named Sumaṅgalavilāsinī was made.

May Buddhaghosa and Sumaṅgalavilāsinī long endure in the world (lokasmiṃ tiṭṭhatu), for those seeking to cross over the world (lokanittharaṇesinaṃ).
Showing the method of purification of views to sons of good families.

As long as even the name "Buddha" of the pure-minded, steadfast one, the great seer, exists in the world, the world's best.

The Sumangalavilāsinī named

Commentary on the Dīgha Nikāya is finished.
