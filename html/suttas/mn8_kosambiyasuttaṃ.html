Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Kosambi in Ghosita’s Park.

Now on that occasion the monks at Kosambi had taken to quarrelling and brawling and were deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers.

They could neither convince each other nor be convinced by others; they could neither persuade each other nor be persuaded by others.

Then a certain monk went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, he sat down at one side.

Seated to one side, that monk said to the Blessed One: “Here, venerable sir, at Kosambi the monks have taken to quarrelling and brawling and are deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers; they can neither convince each other nor be convinced by others, nor can they persuade each other nor be persuaded by others.”

Then the Blessed One addressed a certain monk: “Go, monk, tell those monks in my name that the Teacher summons the venerable ones.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” replied that monk to the Blessed One, and he went to those monks; on arrival, he told the monks: “The Teacher summons the venerable ones.”

“Very well, friend,” replied those monks to that monk, and they went to the Blessed One; on arrival, having paid homage to the Blessed One, they sat down at one side.

Seated to one side, the Blessed One said to those monks: “Is it true, as is said, that you, monks, have taken to quarrelling and brawling and are deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers; that you can neither convince each other nor be convinced by others, that you can neither persuade each other nor be persuaded by others?” “Yes, venerable sir.”

“What do you think, monks, when you have taken to quarrelling and brawling and are deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers, do you on that occasion maintain loving-kindness through bodily action towards your companions in the holy life, both in public and in private; and loving-kindness through speech…and… loving-kindness through mental action towards your companions in the holy life, both in public and in private?” “No, venerable sir.”

“So, monks, when you have taken to quarrelling and brawling and are deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers, on that occasion you do not maintain loving-kindness through bodily action towards your companions in the holy life, both in public and in private; nor loving-kindness through speech…and… nor loving-kindness through mental action towards your companions in the holy life, both in public and in private.

Misguided men, what can you possibly know, what can you see, that you take to quarrelling and brawling and are deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers? That you can neither convince each other nor be convinced by others, that you can neither persuade each other nor be persuaded by others? This will lead to your harm and suffering for a long time.”

491. Thus have I heard: the Kosambiya Sūtra.

There, "Kosambī" refers to a city of that name.

It is said that in that city, in various places such as parks and ponds, Kosamba trees grew densely. Therefore, it came to be known as Kosambī.

Some say [it was named] after the hermitage of a sage named Kusamba, not far from [the city].

Ghoṣitārāma refers to a park caused to be made by Ghoṣita the merchant.

Formerly, it was known as Addilarattha.

Then, a poor man named Kotūhalaka, fearing starvation, was going to a prosperous country with his wife and child. Unable to carry his son, he abandoned him and went away.

The mother returned, took the child, and left.

They entered a village of cowherds. At that time, milk-rice mixed with pounded grain had been prepared for the cowherds. Having obtained the milk-rice, they ate it.

Then that man, having eaten a large amount of milk-rice, was unable to digest it. At night, he died and was reborn as a dog in the womb of a bitch in that very place.

He was dear and pleasing to the cowherd, and the cowherd attended upon a solitary Buddha.

The solitary Buddha also gave the dog a morsel of food after the meal was finished.

Having developed affection for the solitary Buddha, he went with the cowherd to the leaf hut as well.

When the cowherd was away, at mealtime, he himself would go and bark at the door of the leaf hut to announce the time. Even on the road in between, having seen a fierce animal, he would bark and chase it away.

Having passed away from the solitary Buddha with a tender mind, he was reborn in the world of the gods.

There, his name was Ghosakadevaputta.

Having passed away from the world of the gods, he was reborn in a respectable family in Kosambī.

A childless merchant, having given wealth to his parents, adopted him as his son.

Then, when his own sons were born, he attempted to kill him seven times.

Due to his merit, not meeting death in even seven places, at the end, having obtained a livelihood through the shrewdness of a merchant’s daughter, and having obtained the position of merchant upon the death of his father, he became known as Ghoṣita the merchant.

Moreover, in Kosambī, there were two other merchants: Kukkuṭa the merchant and Pāvārika the merchant.

With these, there were three.

Ghoṣita the merchant

And at that time, five hundred ascetics who were family priests of those merchants lived at the foot of a mountain.

From time to time, they would come to the path of humans for the sake of obtaining salt and sour gruel.

Then, on one occasion, in the summer season, while coming to the path of humans, having crossed a great wilderness without water, and having seen a large banyan tree at the end of the wilderness, they thought, "Since this tree is like this, surely a deity of great power must live here. It would be good if he would give us drinking water or food."

The deity, having known the intention of the ascetics, [thought,] "I will do a favor for these," and by his own power, he caused a stream of water the size of a ploughshare to flow from within a branch.

The group of ascetics, having seen the stream of water resembling a silver trunk, having taken the water with their own vessels, and having used it, they thought, "The deity has given us water for use. However, this is a great forest without villages. It would be good if he would give us food as well."

The deity, according to the thought of the ascetics, gave divine rice gruel, cakes, and the like, and satisfied them.

The ascetics thought, "The deity has given us all things—water for use, and food. It would be good if he would show us himself."

The deity, having known their intention, showed half of his body.
They said, "O goddess, great is your prosperity. What karma did you do to attain this prosperity?" "Venerable sir, I did not do a very great or trifling karma."

That prosperity was obtained by the goddess on account of undertaking the half-observance of the uposatha.

It seems that this devaputta was a worker in Anāthapiṇḍika's house.

Indeed, in the treasurer's house, on the uposatha days, all the people, even including the slave workers, observe the uposatha.

One day this worker, alone, early in the morning, went to work.

The great treasurer, considering the men who deserved an oblation, knowing that he alone had gone to the forest, gave him an oblation for his evening meal.

In this place, [there are] six memorable dharmas that have come, thus this Kosambiya Sutta accords with what follows.

There, sāraṇīyā means proper to be remembered, not to be forgotten even when gone far away.

"He fulfills these dharmas, he makes [himself] dear to his fellow practitioners," [this is] piyakaraṇā.

They make [him] heavy [with respect], [this is] garukaraṇā.

Saṅgahāyā means for the purpose of harmony.

Avivādāyā means for the purpose of non-dispute.

Sāmaggiyā means for the purpose of harmonious being.

Ekībhāvāyā means for the sake of unity, for the sake of non-differentiation.

Saṃvattantī means they become.

Mettaṃ kāyakamma means bodily action to be done with a mind of loving-kindness.

Vacīkammamanokammesupi—the same is the method for verbal and mental action.

These have come in dependence on the monks, they are also obtained by householders.

For monks, the fulfillment of the practice of conduct that accords with loving-kindness with a mind of loving-kindness is called mettaṃ kāyakamma.

For householders, going for the purpose of venerating a cetiya, going for the purpose of venerating the bodhi tree, going for the purpose of inviting the saṅgha, seeing monks who have entered the village for alms, going out to meet [them], receiving [their] bowls, preparing seats, following after—such things as these are called mettaṃ kāyakamma.


For monks, with a mind of loving-kindness, the code of monastic discipline, the telling of the place for meditation, a dharma talk, even the Three Baskets, the Buddha's word, is called mettaṃ vacīkamma.

And for householders, [saying,] "Let us go for the purpose of venerating a cetiya, let us go for the purpose of venerating a bodhi tree, let us perform the hearing of the dharma, let us make an offering of a garland of lamps and flowers, let us undertake and maintain the three kinds of good conduct, let us give food offered by ticket," and so on, "let us give robes for the rainy season retreat, today let us give the four requisites to the saṅgha," inviting the saṅgha, preparing consumable things and so on, preparing seats, providing water, going out to meet the saṅgha and bringing [them] in, having seated [them] on prepared seats, at the time of speaking about things like being born from desire, being born from enthusiasm, doing service—this is called mettaṃ vacīkamma.


mettaṃ vacīkamma
For monks, rising up early in the morning, having attended to [their] bodies, and having made the practice of the cetiya courtyard and so on, and having sat down on a secluded seat, thinking, "May the monks in this monastery be happy, free from enmity, free from oppression," this thought is called mettaṃ manokamma.

For householders, the thought, "May the noble ones be happy, free from enmity, free from oppression," is called mettaṃ manokamma.


mettaṃ manokamma
Āvi ceva raho cā means both in public and in private.

There, going for companionship to new monks in things like robe-making and so on, is called sammukhā mettaṃ kāyakamma.

But for elder monks, even the distinction of washing the feet, venerating, giving fans, and so on, all this proper action is called sammukhā mettaṃ kāyakamma.

Even for both, not making light of wooden utensils and so on that are badly put away, but [rather] arranging what has been badly put away by oneself is called parammukhā mettaṃ kāyakamma.

Saying things like "Devatthera, Tissatthera," and so on, with respect, is called sammukhā mettaṃ vacīkamma.

But in the monastery, to one who asks about one who is not there, [saying,] "Where is our Devatthera? When will our Tissatthera come?" Such speech of possessiveness is called parammukhā mettaṃ vacīkamma.

But opening the eyes, which are sticky with loving affection, and looking with a very clear face, is called sammukhā mettaṃ manokamma.

The thought, "Devatthera, Tissatthera, may [they] be free from disease, free from affliction," is called parammukhā mettaṃ manokamma.

Lābhā means requisites that have been obtained, like robes and so on.

Dhammikā means arisen from proper conduct by avoiding wrong livelihood which is different from hypocrisy and so on, by [means of] the alms-begging life, which is even and in accordance with the dharma.

Antamaso pattapariyāpannamattampī means even a little food that has been completely filled in a bowl at the last edge, that is contained within the bowl, two or three spoonfuls.

Appaṭivibhattabhogī—here, there are two kinds of sharing: sharing of material things and sharing with individuals.
Therein, the distribution with the thought, “I will give this much; I will not give this much,” is called distribution based on material (āmisapaṭivibhatta).

The distribution with the thought, “I will give to this person; I will not give to that person,” is called distribution based on the individual (puggalapaṭivibhatta).

But one who, without doing either of those two, enjoys [something] undivided—this one is called an enjoyer of the undivided (appaṭivibhattabhogī).

The gains that are righteous (lābhā dhammikā)—even as little as would fill a bowl—[belong to] the enjoyer of the undivided (appaṭivibhattabhogī). An enjoyer of the undivided…

…[is] one who shares in common with his fellow-practitioners (sabrahmacārīhi sādhāraṇabhogī). Here is the characteristic of the one who shares in common: whatever is excellent that he obtains, that he does not give to householders with a desire to conquer gain with gain in a deceiving way, nor does he enjoy it himself; but receiving it, thinking, “Let it be shared in common by the saṅgha,” having taken it, struck the gong, he sees [it] as if [it were] the property of the saṅgha to be enjoyed.

Who fulfills this practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhamma), and who does not fulfill it? A person of immoral character, of course, does not fulfill it.

For virtuous people do not accept what belongs to him.

But a person of completely pure virtue, not breaking his observance, fulfills it.

[Is] one who shares in common with his fellow-practitioners (sabrahmacārīhi sādhāraṇabhogī). Therein, this is the observance: the one who, indeed, having made a specific allocation (odissaka), gives to his mother, or father, or teachers, preceptors, etc., this one gives what should be given. The practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhamma)

does not exist for him; it is called an impediment of worldliness (palibodhajagganaṃ nāma hoti).

The practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhamma) is suitable only for one freed from the impediments of worldliness (muttapalibodha). Therefore, through giving a specific allocation, it should be given to the sick, to those who attend to the sick, to visitors, to travelers, and also to a newly ordained monk who does not know the robe, bowl, and acceptance.

Having given to these, and not giving the remainder little by little from the seat of the elders onward, the one who takes whatever amount, that much should be given to him.

If there is no remainder, having again gone for alms, having given whatever is excellent from the seat of the elders onward, the remainder should be enjoyed, “Because of the saying, ‘by virtuous ones,’” it is also suitable not to give to an immoral person.

This practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhamma), however, is easily fulfilled by a well-trained community, not by an untrained community.

For in a well-trained community, the one who obtains [something] from elsewhere, this one does not take [it]; even though not obtaining [it] from elsewhere, he takes only what is in accordance with measure, not an excess.

And this practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhamma), even for one who, having thus repeatedly gone for alms, gives what he has obtained, is fulfilled in twelve years; not less than that.

If, indeed, even in the twelfth year, one fulfilling the practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhammapūrako), having filled his bowl with alms food, places [it] in the alms hall and goes to bathe, and the saṅgha elder asks, “Whose bowl is this?” if it is said, “Of the one fulfilling the practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhammapūraka),” [they will say] “Bring it here!” and all…

…having reflected upon the alms food, and having eaten, puts away the empty bowl.

Then that monk, having seen the empty bowl, if he produces displeasure, [thinking], “They have enjoyed [it] all, without leaving anything for me!” the practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhamma) is broken; again, twelve years must be fulfilled.

It is like the probationary period of members of other sects (titthiya); that is how it is.

Once a break has occurred, it must be fulfilled again.

But the one who generates joy, [thinking], “It is a gain for me, it is well gained for me, that my fellow-practitioners enjoy what has gone into my bowl without asking me!” this one is said to be full (puṇṇo).

The one whose practice conducive to amiability (sāraṇīyadhamma) is thus fulfilled, however, has neither envy nor stinginess; that one is dear to people, [and has] easily obtained requisites. Even though [something] is being given to the one into whose bowl [food] has gone, it does not diminish; in the place of disposable goods, he obtains excellent goods; when fear or hunger has befallen [him], the gods become eager.

497.Uccāvacānīti uccanīcāni. Kiṃ karaṇīyānīti kiṃ karomīti evaṃ vatvā kattabbakammāni. Tattha uccakammaṃ nāma cīvarassa karaṇaṃ rajanaṃ cetiye sudhākammaṃ uposathāgāracetiyagharabodhigharesu kattabbakammanti evamādi. Avacakammaṃ nāma pādadhovanamakkhanādikhuddakakammaṃ, atha vā cetiye sudhākammādi uccakammaṃ nāma. Tattheva kasāvapacanaudakānayanakucchakaraṇa niyyāsabandhanādi avacakammaṃ nāma. Ussukkaṃ āpanno hotīti ussukkabhāvaṃ kattabbataṃ paṭipanno hoti. Tibbāpekkho hotīti bahalapatthano hoti. Thambañcaālumpatīti tiṇañca ālumpamānā khādati. Vacchakañca apacinātīti vacchakañca apaloketi. Taruṇavacchā hi gāvī araññe ekato āgataṃ vacchakaṃ ekasmiṃ ṭhāne nipannaṃ pahāya dūraṃ na gacchati, vacchakassa āsannaṭṭhāne caramānā tiṇaṃ ālumpitvā gīvaṃ ukkhipitvā ekantaṃ vacchakameva ca viloketi, evameva sotāpanno uccāvacāni kiṃ karaṇīyāni karonto tanninno hoti, asithilapūrako tibbacchando bahalapatthano hutvāva karoti. Tatridaṃ vatthu – mahācetiye kira sudhākamme kariyamāne eko ariyasāvako ekena hatthena sudhābhājanaṃ, ekena kucchaṃ gahetvā sudhākammaṃ karissāmīti cetiyaṅgaṇaṃ āruḷho. Eko kāyadaḷhibahulo bhikkhu gantvā therassa santike aṭṭhāsi. Thero aññasmiṃ sati papañco hotīti tasmā ṭhānā aññaṃ ṭhānaṃ gato. Sopi bhikkhu tattheva agamāsi. Thero puna aññaṃ ṭhānanti evaṃ katipayaṭṭhāne āgataṃ, – ‘‘sappurisa mahantaṃ cetiyaṅgaṇaṃ kiṃ aññasmiṃ ṭhāne okāsaṃ na labhathā’’ti āha. Na itaro pakkāmīti.
497. "Uccāvacānī" means high and low.
"Kiṃ karaṇīyānī" means saying "What shall I do?" are the things to be done.

There, "uccakammaṃ" means making robes, dyeing, whitewashing the cetiya, things to be done in the uposatha hall, cetiyaghara, and bodhighara, and so on.

"Avacakammaṃ" means minor tasks such as washing feet and anointing, or whitewashing the cetiya, etc., is called "uccakammaṃ."

Right there, tasks such as setting out astringent medicine, fetching water, making a kuccha, applying resin, etc., are called "avacakammaṃ".

"Ussukkaṃ āpanno hotī" means he has undertaken the state of eagerness, the duty.

"Tibbāpekkho hotī" means he is one with intense longing, being very desirous.

"Thambañca ālumpatī" means she grazes on grass, chewing as she grazes.

"Vacchakañca apacinātī" means she observes the calf.

Indeed, a young cow, having left her calf who has come to the forest from one side and is lying down in one place, does not go far away. While wandering in a place near the calf, having grazed on grass, having raised her neck, she only looks at the calf, in the same way, a stream-enterer, while doing various things to be done, is inclined to that, he does [them] having become one with unfailing effort, intense desire, and great longing.

Here is the story: While whitewashing was being done at the great cetiya, a certain noble disciple, having taken a whitewash vessel in one hand and a kuccha in the other, ascended the cetiyaṅgaṇa, thinking "I will do the whitewashing".

A certain bhikkhu, strong and stout in body, went and stood near the elder.

The elder thought, "If there is association, there will be proliferation," therefore, he went from that place to another place.

That bhikkhu also went right there.

The elder again [went] to another place. Thus, [they] came to several places. [The elder] said, "Good sir, why don't you find a place in another place, [this] great cetiyaṅgaṇa?"

The other one did not leave.

498. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘yathārūpāya balatāya diṭṭhisampanno puggalo samannāgato, ahampi tathārūpāya balatāya samannāgato’ti. Kathaṃrūpāya ca, bhikkhave, balatāya diṭṭhisampanno puggalo samannāgato? Balatā esā, bhikkhave, diṭṭhisampannassa puggalassa yaṃ tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye desiyamāne aṭṭhiṃkatvā manasikatvā sabbacetasā sabbacetaso (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.), sabbaṃ cetasā (ka.) samannāharitvā ohitasoto dhammaṃ suṇāti. So evaṃ pajānāti – ‘yathārūpāya balatāya diṭṭhisampanno puggalo samannāgato, ahampi tathārūpāya balatāya samannāgato’ti. Idamassa chaṭṭhaṃ ñāṇaṃ adhigataṃ hoti ariyaṃ lokuttaraṃ asādhāraṇaṃ puthujjanehi.
“Again, O monks, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘I possess the strength of a person who possesses right view ( dṛṣṭisampanno puggalaḥ ).’

“And how, O monks, does a person who possesses right view possess strength? This is the strength, O monks, of a person who possesses right view: when the dharmavinaya (teachings) proclaimed by the tathāgata is being taught, he heeds it, gives it attention, engages it with all his mind (sabbacetasā), hears the Dharma with eager ears (ohitasoto dhammaṃ suṇāti).

“He understands thus: ‘I possess the strength of a person who possesses right view.’

This is the sixth knowledge attained by him that is noble (ariyaṃ), supramundane (lokuttaraṃ), not shared by ordinary people (puthujjanehi).
498.Balatāya samannāgatoti balena samannāgato. Aṭṭhiṃ katvāti atthikabhāvaṃ katvā, atthiko hutvāti attho. Manasikatvāti manasmiṃ karitvā. Sabbacetasā samannāharitvāti appamattakampi vikkhepaṃ akaronto sakalacittena samannāharitvā. Ohitasototi ṭhapitasoto. Ariyasāvakā hi piyadhammassavanā honti, dhammassavanaggaṃ gantvā niddāyamānā vā yena kenaci saddhiṃ sallapamānā vā vikkhittacittā vā na nisīdanti, atha kho amataṃ paribhuñjantā viya atittāva honti dhammassavane, atha aruṇaṃ uggacchati. Tasmā evamāha.
498. "balatāya samannāgato" means samannāgato with strength.

"aṭṭhiṃ katvā" means having done atthikabhāvaṃ, atthiko hutvāti is the meaning.

"manasikatvā" means having done manasmiṃ.

"sabbacetasā samannāharitvā" means samannāharitvā with sakalacittena, not doing even a little vikkhepaṃ.

"ohitasoto" means ṭhapitasoto.

Ariyasāvakās are indeed fond of hearing the Dhamma. Having gone to a place for hearing the Dhamma, they do not sit down drowsing, or conversing with anyone, or with distracted minds; rather, they are as if enjoying amata, not being satiated in hearing the Dhamma, and then aruṇaṃ rises.

Therefore, he said this.

499. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘yathārūpāya balatāya diṭṭhisampanno puggalo samannāgato, ahampi tathārūpāya balatāya samannāgato’ti. Kathaṃrūpāya ca, bhikkhave, balatāya diṭṭhisampanno puggalo samannāgato? Balatā esā, bhikkhave, diṭṭhisampannassa puggalassa yaṃ tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye desiyamāne labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. So evaṃ pajānāti – ‘yathārūpāya balatāya diṭṭhisampanno puggalo samannāgato, ahampi tathārūpāya balatāya samannāgato’ti. Idamassa sattamaṃ ñāṇaṃ adhigataṃ hoti ariyaṃ lokuttaraṃ asādhāraṇaṃ puthujjanehi.
Again, O monks, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘Do I possess the strength (balatā) of a person who possesses right view (dṛṣṭisampanna)?’

What is the strength (balatā) with which a person possessing right view (dṛṣṭisampanna) is endowed, O monks? This is the strength (balatā), O monks, of a person possessing right view (dṛṣṭisampanna): when the dharma and vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata are being taught, he gains inspiration in the meaning (arthaveda), gains inspiration in the dharma (dharmaveda), gains gladness connected with the dharma (dharmūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ).

He understands thus: ‘I possess the strength (balatā) of a person who possesses right view (dṛṣṭisampanna).’

This is the seventh knowledge (jñāna) attained by him that is noble (ārya), supramundane (lokottara), not shared by ordinary people (pṛthagjana).

500. ‘‘Evaṃ sattaṅgasamannāgatassa kho, bhikkhave, ariyasāvakassa dhammatā susamanniṭṭhā hoti sotāpattiphalasacchikiriyāya. Evaṃ sattaṅgasamannāgato kho, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako sotāpattiphalasamannāgato hotī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Kosambiyasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.
"Bhikkhus, when a noble disciple is thus possessed of seven factors, it is fitting that he has well sought the character for the realization of the fruit of stream-entry. Bhikkhus, when a noble disciple is thus possessed of seven factors, he possesses the fruit of stream-entry."

The Blessed One said this. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Kosambiya Discourse, the eighth, is concluded.
500.Dhammatāsusamanniṭṭhā hotīti sabhāvo suṭṭhu samannesito hoti. Sotāpattiphalasacchikiriyāyāti karaṇavacanaṃ , sotāpattiphalasacchikatañāṇenāti attho. Evaṃ sattaṅgasamannāgatoti evaṃ imehi sattahi mahāpaccavekkhaṇañāṇehi samannāgato. Ayaṃ tāva ācariyānaṃ samānakathā. Lokuttaramaggo hi bahucittakkhaṇiko nāma natthi. Vitaṇḍavādī pana ekacittakkhaṇiko nāma maggo natthi, ‘‘evaṃ bhāveyya satta vassānī’’ti hi vacanato sattapi vassāni maggabhāvanā honti. Kilesā pana lahu chijjantā sattahi ñāṇehi chijjantīti vadati. So suttaṃ āharāti vattabbo, addhā aññaṃ suttaṃ apassanto, ‘‘idamassa paṭhamaṃ ñāṇaṃ adhigataṃ hoti, idamassa dutiyaṃ ñāṇaṃ…pe… idamassa sattamaṃ ñāṇaṃ adhigataṃ hotī’’ti imameva āharitvā dassessati. Tato vattabbo kiṃ panidaṃ suttaṃ neyyatthaṃ nītatthanti. Tato vakkhati – ‘‘nītatthatthaṃ, yathāsuttaṃ tatheva attho’’ti. So vattabbo – ‘‘dhammatā susamanniṭṭhā hoti sotāpattiphalasacchikiriyāyāti ettha ko attho’’ti? Addhā sotāpattiphalasacchikiriyāyatthoti vakkhati. Tato pucchitabbo, ‘‘maggasamaṅgī phalaṃ sacchikaroti, phalasamaṅgī’’ti. Jānanto, ‘‘phalasamaṅgī sacchikarotī’’ti vakkhati. Tato vattabbo, – ‘‘evaṃ sattaṅgasamannāgato kho, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako sotāpattiphalasamannāgato hotīti idha maggaṃ abhāvetvā maṇḍūko viya uppatitvā ariyasāvako phalameva gaṇhissati. Mā suttaṃ me laddhanti yaṃ vā taṃ vā avaca. Pañhaṃ vissajjentena nāma ācariyasantike vasitvā buddhavacanaṃ uggaṇhitvā attharasaṃ viditvā vattabbaṃ hotī’’ti. ‘‘Imāni satta ñāṇāni ariyasāvakassa paccavekkhaṇañāṇāneva, lokuttaramaggo bahucittakkhaṇiko nāma natthi, ekacittakkhaṇikoyevā’’ti saññāpetabbo. Sace sañjānāti sañjānātu. No ce sañjānāti, ‘‘gaccha pātova vihāraṃ pavisitvā yāguṃ pivāhī’’ti uyyojetabbo. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Kosambiyasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
500. Dhammatā susamanniṭṭhā hotī means that the nature is well ascertained.

Sotāpattiphalasacchikiriyāyā means the statement of the cause, the meaning is the knowledge of realizing the fruit of stream-entry ( sotāpatti-phala).

Evaṃ sattaṅgasamannāgato means thus, endowed with these seven great knowledges of retrospection.

This, firstly, is the similar talk of the teachers.

There is certainly no supramundane path ( lokuttara-maggo) that is of many moments of consciousness.

But the sophist says, "There is no path that is a single moment of consciousness, because according to the statement, 'Thus he should cultivate for seven years,' even seven years are the cultivation of the path."

But the defilements, quickly being cut off, he speaks of being cut off by seven knowledges.

He should be told to bring the sutra, for sure, not seeing another sutra, he will show by bringing this very one, "This is his first knowledge attained, this is his second knowledge...pe...this is his seventh knowledge attained."

Then he should be told, "What then is this sutra, is it interpretable or definitive?"

Then he will say - "It is of definitive meaning, the meaning is just as in the sutra."

He should be told - "What is the meaning of dhammatā susamanniṭṭhā hoti sotāpattiphalasacchikiriyāyāti ettha ko attho?" Surely, he will say, "The meaning is the realization of the fruit of stream-entry ( sotāpatti-phala)."

Then he should be asked, "The one endowed with the path realizes the fruit, the one endowed with the fruit?"

Knowing, he will say, "The one endowed with the fruit realizes it."

Then he should be told - "Thus, endowed with seven factors, monks, the noble disciple is endowed with the fruit of stream-entry ( sotāpatti-phala), here, without cultivating the path, like a frog having jumped up, the noble disciple will only take hold of the fruit."

"May you not have found the sutra for me," or whatever he said.

The one releasing the question, having lived near the teacher, having learned the word of the Buddha, having known the taste of the meaning, should be told.

"These seven knowledges are indeed the knowledges of retrospection of the noble disciple, there is certainly no supramundane path ( lokuttara-maggo) that is of many moments of consciousness, it is only of one moment of consciousness," he should be made to understand.

If he understands, let him understand.

If he does not understand, he should be dismissed, "Go early in the morning, having entered the monastery, drink rice gruel."

The rest is clear throughout.

The Kosambiyasutta Commentary is finished in the Papañcasūdanī, a commentary on the Majjhimanikāya.
