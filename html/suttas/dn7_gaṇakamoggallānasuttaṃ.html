<p n="74" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">74</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0081"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0050"/><pb ed="P" n="3.0001"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde. Atha kho gaṇakamoggallāno <note>gaṇakamoggalāno (ka.)</note> brāhmaṇo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ <pb ed="M" n="3.0053"/> sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho gaṇakamoggallāno brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca –

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, imassa migāramātupāsādassa dissati anupubbasikkhā anupubbakiriyā anupubbapaṭipadā yadidaṃ – yāva pacchimasopānakaḷevarāः imesampi hi, bho gotama, brāhmaṇānaṃ dissati anupubbasikkhā anupubbakiriyā anupubbapaṭipadā yadidaṃ – ajjheneः imesampi hi, bho gotama, issāsānaṃ dissati anupubbasikkhā anupubbakiriyā anupubbapaṭipadā yadidaṃ – issatthe <note>issatte (ka.)</note>. Amhākampi hi, bho gotama, gaṇakānaṃ gaṇanājīvānaṃ dissati anupubbasikkhā anupubbakiriyā anupubbapaṭipadā yadidaṃ – saṅkhāne. Mayañhi, bho gotama, antevāsiṃ labhitvā paṭhamaṃ evaṃ gaṇāpema – ‘ekaṃ ekakaṃ, dve dukā, tīṇi tikā, cattāri catukkā, pañca pañcakā, cha chakkā, satta sattakā, aṭṭha aṭṭhakā, nava navakā, dasa dasakā’ti; satampi mayaṃ, bho gotama, gaṇāpema, bhiyyopi gaṇāpema. Sakkā nu kho, bho gotama, imasmimpi dhammavinaye evameva anupubbasikkhā anupubbakiriyā anupubbapaṭipadā paññapetu’’nti?

<note>issatte (ka.)</note>
Thus I have heard: At one time the Blessed One was staying near Śrāvastī, in the Eastern Park, at the palace of Migāramāta.

Then Gaṇakamoggallāna, the Brahmin, went to the Blessed One; having gone, he exchanged greetings with the Blessed One. After exchanging courteous and amiable talk, he sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, Gaṇakamoggallāna, the Brahmin, said this to the Blessed One:

“Just as, Master Gotama, in this Migāramāta’s palace a gradual training, a gradual doing, a gradual practice is evident, that is, as far as the final step of the staircase; so too, Master Gotama, among Brahmins a gradual training, a gradual doing, a gradual practice is evident, that is, study; so too, Master Gotama, among archers a gradual training, a gradual doing, a gradual practice is evident, that is, archery.

Likewise, Master Gotama, among us accountants who live by calculation a gradual training, a gradual doing, a gradual practice is evident, that is, counting. For, Master Gotama, having obtained a pupil, we first teach them to count thus: ‘One is one, two are twos, three are threes, four are fours, five are fives, six are sixes, seven are sevens, eight are eights, nine are nines, ten are tens’; even a hundred we teach, and even more we teach.

Is it possible, Master Gotama, in this Dharma-Vinaya also to establish a gradual training, a gradual doing, a gradual practice?”

So pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto nisīdati pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā, ujuṃ kāyaṃ paṇidhāya, parimukhaṃ satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā. So abhijjhaṃ loke pahāya vigatābhijjhena cetasā viharati, abhijjhāya cittaṃ parisodheti; byāpādapadosaṃ pahāya abyāpannacitto viharati sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampī, byāpādapadosā cittaṃ parisodheti; thinamiddhaṃ <note>thīnamiddhaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note> pahāya vigatathinamiddho viharati ālokasaññī sato sampajāno, thinamiddhā cittaṃ parisodheti; uddhaccakukkuccaṃ pahāya anuddhato viharati ajjhattaṃ vūpasantacitto, uddhaccakukkuccā cittaṃ parisodheti; vicikicchaṃ pahāya tiṇṇavicikiccho viharati akathaṃkathī kusalesu dhammesu, vicikicchāya cittaṃ parisodheti.

75. “It is possible, brāhmaṇa, to describe gradual training, gradual practice, and gradual progress in this Dhamma and Discipline. Just as, brāhmaṇa, when a clever horse-trainer obtains a fine thoroughbred colt, he first makes him get used to wearing the bit, and afterwards trains him further, so when the Tathāgata obtains a person to be tamed he first disciplines him thus: ‘Come, bhikkhu, be virtuous, restrained with the restraint of the Pāṭimokkha, be perfect in conduct and resort, and seeing fear in the slightest fault, train by undertaking the training precepts.’”

“When, brāhmaṇa, the bhikkhu is virtuous…and seeing fear in the slightest fault, trains by undertaking the training precepts, then the Tathāgata disciplines him further: ‘Come, bhikkhu, guard the doors of your sense faculties. On seeing a form with the eye, do not grasp at its signs and features. Since, if you were to leave the eye faculty unguarded, evil unwholesome states of covetousness and grief might invade you, practise the way of its restraint, guard the eye faculty, undertake the restraint of the eye faculty. On hearing a sound with the ear…On smelling an odour with the nose…On tasting a flavour with the tongue…On touching a tangible with the body…On cognizing a mind-object with the mind, do not grasp at its signs and features. Since, if you were to leave the mind faculty unguarded, evil unwholesome states might invade you, practise the way of its restraint, guard the mind faculty, undertake the restraint of the mind faculty.’”

“When, brāhmaṇa, the bhikkhu guards the doors of his sense faculties, then the Tathāgata disciplines him further: ‘Come, bhikkhu, be moderate in eating. Reflecting wisely, you should take food neither for amusement nor for intoxication nor for the sake of physical beauty and attractiveness, but only for the endurance and continuance of this body, for ending discomfort, and for assisting the holy life, considering: “Thus I shall terminate old feelings without arousing new feelings and I shall be healthy and blameless and shall live in comfort.”’”

“When, brāhmaṇa, the bhikkhu is moderate in eating, then the Tathāgata disciplines him further: ‘Come, bhikkhu, be devoted to wakefulness. During the day, while walking back and forth and sitting, purify your mind of obstructive states. In the first watch of the night, while walking back and forth and sitting, purify your mind of obstructive states. In the middle watch of the night you should lie down on the right side in fhe lion’s pose with one foot overlapping the other, mindful and fully aware, focusing on the thought of rising [at the proper time]. In the last watch of the night, on awakening, while walking back and forth and sitting, purify your mind of obstructive states.’”

“When, brāhmaṇa, the bhikkhu possesses mindfulness and full awareness, then the Tathāgata disciplines him further: ‘Come, bhikkhu, be one who acts with full awareness when going forward and back, when looking ahead and looking around, when drawing in and stretching out [his limbs], when wearing his outer robe, bowl, and robe, when eating, drinking, consuming, and tasting, when passing excrement and urine, when walking, standing, sitting, sleeping, waking, speaking, and remaining silent.’”

“When, brāhmaṇa, the bhikkhu possesses mindfulness and full awareness, then the Tathāgata disciplines him further: ‘Come, bhikkhu, resort to a secluded resting place: the forest, the root of a tree, a mountain, a ravine, a hillside cave, a charnel ground, a jungle thicket, an open space, a heap of straw.’ He resorts to a secluded lodging: the forest, the root of a tree, a mountain, a ravine, a hillside cave, a charnel ground, a jungle highland, the open air, a heap of straw. After his meal, on returning from his alms round, he sits down, folding his legs crosswise, setting his body erect and establishing mindfulness before him. Having abandoned longing for the world, he dwells with a mind free from longing; he purifies his mind from longing. Having abandoned ill will and hatred, he dwells with a mind free from ill.will, compassionate toward all living beings; he purifies his mind from ill will and hatred. Having abandoned dullness and drowsiness, he dwells free from dullness and drowsiness, percipient of light, mindful and clearly comprehending; he purifies his mind from dullness and drowsiness. Having abandoned restlessness and remorse, he dwells undisturbed, his mind inwardly composed; he purifies his mind from restlessness and remorse. Having abandoned doubt, he dwells as one who has crossed over doubt, without uncertainty about skillful dharmas; he purifies his mind from doubt.”

<pb ed="M" n="3.0056"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Ko nu kho, bho gotama, hetu ko paccayo yaṃ tiṭṭhateva nibbānaṃ, tiṭṭhati nibbānagāmī maggo, tiṭṭhati bhavaṃ gotamo samādapetā; atha ca pana bhoto gotamassa sāvakā bhotā gotamena evaṃ ovadīyamānā evaṃ anusāsīyamānā appekacce accantaṃ niṭṭhaṃ nibbānaṃ ārādhenti, ekacce nārādhentī’’ti?

76. “Having thus abandoned these five hindrances ( pañca nīvaraṇe pahāya), imperfections of the mind that weaken wisdom, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, he enters upon and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought (savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ), with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion (vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ).

With the stilling of applied and sustained thought (vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā), he enters upon and abides in the second jhāna, which has self-confidence and singleness of mind.

With the fading away as well of rapture (pītiyā ca virāgā), he enters upon and abides in the third jhāna.

With the abandoning of pleasure and pain (sukhassa ca pahānā), he enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna.

“Those monks, O Brahmin, who are still in training (sekkhā), have not attained their heart's desire (apattamānasā), and live aspiring to the unsurpassed security from bondage (anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ patthayamānā), to them this is my kind of instruction.

But these things conduce both to a pleasant abiding here and now and to mindfulness and full awareness for those bhikkhus who are arahants with taints destroyed (khīṇāsavā), who have lived the holy life (vusitavanto), done what had to be done (katakaraṇīyā), laid down the burden (ohitabhārā), reached the true goal (anuppattasadatthā), destroyed the fetters of being (parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanā), and are completely liberated through final knowledge (sammadaññā vimuttā)."

When this was said, the Brahmin Gaṇakamoggallāna said to the Blessed One: “Master Gotama, when your disciples are thus advised and instructed by you, do they all attain Nibbāna, the ultimate goal, or do some not attain it?”

“When, Brahmin, they are thus advised and instructed by me, some of my disciples attain Nibbāna, the ultimate goal, and some do not attain it.”

“Master Gotama, since Nibbāna exists, and the path leading to Nibbāna exists, and Master Gotama is present as the guide, what is the cause and reason why, when Master Gotama’s disciples are thus advised and instructed by him, some of them attain Nibbāna, the ultimate goal, and some do not attain it?”

77. “As to that, Brahmin, I will ask you a question in return. Answer as you see fit. What do you think, Brahmin? Are you skilled in the road to Rājagaha (rājagaha)? " "Yes, sir, I am skilled in the road to Rājagaha."

“What do you think, Brahmin? Suppose a man came who wanted to go to Rājagaha (rājagaha). And he approached you and said: ‘Venerable sir, I want to go to Rājagaha (rājagaha). Show me the road to Rājagaha (rājagaha).’ Then you would tell him: ‘Now, good man (purisa), this road goes to Rājagaha (rājagaha). Follow it for a while, and you will see a certain village, follow it for a while, and you will see a certain town; follow it for a while, and you will see Rājagaha (rājagaha) with its lovely parks (ārāmarāmaṇeyyakaṃ), groves (vanarāmaṇeyyakaṃ), meadows (bhūmirāmaṇeyyakaṃ), and ponds (pokkharaṇīrāmaṇeyyakaṃ).’ Then, having been thus advised and instructed by you, he would take a wrong road and would go to the west.

Then a second man came who wanted to go to Rājagaha (rājagaha). And he approached you and said: ‘Venerable sir, I want to go to Rājagaha (rājagaha). Show me the road to Rājagaha (rājagaha).’ Then you would tell him: ‘Now, good man (purisa), this road goes to Rājagaha (rājagaha). Follow it for a while, and you will see a certain village; follow it for a while, and you will see a certain town; follow it for a while, and you will see Rājagaha (rājagaha) with its lovely parks (ārāmarāmaṇeyyakaṃ), groves (vanarāmaṇeyyakaṃ), meadows (bhūmirāmaṇeyyakaṃ), and ponds (pokkharaṇīrāmaṇeyyakaṃ).’ Then, having been thus advised and instructed by you, he would arrive safely in Rājagaha (rājagaha). Now, Brahmin, what is the cause (hetu) and reason (paccayo) why, since Rājagaha (rājagaha) exists and the path leading to Rājagaha (rājagaha) exists and you are present as the guide (samādapetā), when those men have been thus advised and instructed by you, one man takes a wrong road and goes to the west and one arrives safely in Rājagaha (rājagaha)?” “What can I do about that (ettha), sir Gotama? I am only a pointer of the way (maggakkhāyī) sir (bho) Gotama (gotama).”

“So too, Brahmin, Nirvāṇa (nibbānaṃ) exists and the path leading to Nirvāṇa (nibbānaṃ) exists and I am present as the guide (samādapetā); yet when my disciples (sāvakā) have been thus advised and instructed by me, some of them attain Nirvāṇa (nibbānaṃ), the ultimate goal (niṭṭhaṃ), and some do not attain it. What can I do about that (ettha), Brahmin? I am only a pointer of the way (maggakkhāyī), Brahmin, the Thus-Gone One (tathāgato).”
78
<p n="78" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">78</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ vutte, gaṇakamoggallāno brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘yeme, bho gotama, puggalā assaddhā jīvikatthā na saddhā agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajitā saṭhā māyāvino ketabino <note>keṭubhino (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note> uddhatā unnaḷā capalā mukharā vikiṇṇavācā indriyesu aguttadvārā bhojane amattaññuno jāgariyaṃ ananuyuttā sāmaññe anapekkhavanto sikkhāya na tibbagāravā bāhulikā <note>bāhullikā (syā. kaṃ.)</note> sāthalikā okkamane pubbaṅgamā paviveke nikkhittadhurā kusītā hīnavīriyā muṭṭhassatino asampajānā asamāhitā vibbhantacittā <pb ed="T" n="3.0088"/> duppaññā eḷamūgā, na tehi bhavaṃ gotamo saddhiṃ saṃvasati’’.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Ye pana te kulaputtā saddhā agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajitā asaṭhā amāyāvino aketabino anuddhatā anunnaḷā acapalā amukharā avikiṇṇavācā indriyesu guttadvārā bhojane mattaññuno jāgariyaṃ anuyuttā sāmaññe apekkhavanto sikkhāya tibbagāravā nabāhulikā nasāthalikā okkamane nikkhittadhurā paviveke pubbaṅgamā āraddhavīriyā pahitattā upaṭṭhitassatino sampajānā samāhitā ekaggacittā paññavanto aneḷamūgā, tehi bhavaṃ gotamo saddhiṃ saṃvasati.

<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Seyyathāpi <pb ed="V" n="3.0055"/>, bho gotama, ye keci mūlagandhā, kālānusāri tesaṃ aggamakkhāyati; ye keci sāragandhā, lohitacandanaṃ tesaṃ aggamakkhāyati; ye keci pupphagandhā, vassikaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0007"/> tesaṃ aggamakkhāyati; evameva bhoto gotamassa ovādo paramajjadhammesu.

<pb ed="V" n="3.0055"/> <pb ed="P" n="3.0007"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Abhikkantaṃ <pb ed="M" n="3.0058"/>, bho gotama, abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama! Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya – ‘cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantī’ti; evamevaṃ bhotā gotamena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Esāhaṃ bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsakaṃ maṃ bhavaṃ gotamo dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gata’’nti.

<pb ed="M" n="3.0058"/> <p rend="centre">Gaṇakamoggallānasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ sattamaṃ.

When this was said, the brahmin Gaṇaka Moggallāna said to the Blessed One: “There are, venerable Gotama, individuals who are faithless, who have gone forth from the house life into homelessness not out of faith but for the sake of their livelihood, who are deceitful, fraudulent, treacherous, haughty, vain, fickle, garrulous, loose-tongued, unguarded in their senses, immoderate in eating, not devoted to wakefulness, unconcerned with the ascetic life, not greatly respectful of the training, luxurious, careless, leaders in backsliding, neglectful of seclusion, lazy, lacking in energy, of confused mindfulness, uncomprehending, unconcentrated, with deranged minds, unwise, and dull-witted. The venerable Gotama does not associate with them.”

“But those clansmen who have gone forth from the house life into homelessness out of faith, who are not deceitful, fraudulent, or treacherous, not haughty, vain, or fickle, not garrulous or loose-tongued, who are guarded in their senses, moderate in eating, devoted to wakefulness, concerned with the ascetic life, greatly respectful of the training, not luxurious or careless, who are keen to avoid backsliding, leaders in seclusion, energetic, resolute, established in mindfulness, fully aware, concentrated, of unified mind, wise, and not dull-witted, the venerable Gotama associates with them."

“Just as, venerable Gotama, whatever root perfumes there are, black orris root (kālānusāri) is said to be the best of them; whatever heartwood perfumes there are, red sandalwood (lohitacandana) is said to be the best of them; whatever flower perfumes there are, jasmine (vassikaṃ) is said to be the best of them; just so, venerable Gotama, the advice of the honorable Gotama is the best in the highest teachings today.”

“Magnificent, venerable Gotama! Magnificent, venerable Gotama! It is as if, venerable Gotama, one were to set upright what had been overthrown, or reveal what had been hidden, or show the way to one who was lost, or hold up a lamp in the dark, so that those with eyes might see forms. Even so, the Dhamma has been made clear by the venerable Gotama in many ways. I go to the revered Gotama for refuge, and to the dharma, and to the bhikkhu-saṃgha. May the revered Gotama accept me as a lay follower (upāsaka) who has gone for refuge from this day forward, as long as life lasts.”

The Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta, the seventh, is finished.
