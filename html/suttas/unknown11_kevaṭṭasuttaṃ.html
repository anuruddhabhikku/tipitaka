Paragraph 481
Mūla (Source Text)
481. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā nāḷandāyaṃ viharati pāvārikambavane. Atha kho kevaṭṭo gahapatiputto yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho kevaṭṭo gahapatiputto bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ayaṃ, bhante, nāḷandā iddhā ceva phītā ca bahujanā ākiṇṇamanussā bhagavati abhippasannā. Sādhu, bhante, bhagavā ekaṃ bhikkhuṃ samādisatu, yo uttarimanussadhammā, iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ karissati; evāyaṃ nāḷandā bhiyyoso mattāya bhagavati abhippasīdissatī’’ti. Evaṃ vutte, bhagavā kevaṭṭaṃ gahapatiputtaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘na kho ahaṃ, kevaṭṭa, bhikkhūnaṃ evaṃ dhammaṃ desemi – etha tumhe, bhikkhave, gihīnaṃ odātavasanānaṃ uttarimanussadhammā iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ karothā’’ti.
English Translation
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was staying at Nāḷandā in Pāvārika’s mango grove.

Then Kevaṭṭa, the householder's son, approached the Blessed One (bhagavā); having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ) and sat down to one side.

Seated to one side, Kevaṭṭa, the householder's son, said this to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ): “This Nāḷandā, venerable sir (bhante), is prosperous (iddhā), thriving (phītā), full of people (bahujanā), crowded with humans (ākiṇṇamanussā), and greatly devoted to the Blessed One (bhagavati abhippasannā).

It would be good, venerable sir (bhante), if the Blessed One (bhagavā) were to instruct a monk (bhikkhuṃ) to perform superhuman feats (uttarimanussadhammā) and miracles (iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ); in this way, this Nāḷandā would become even more greatly devoted to the Blessed One (bhagavati abhippasīdissatī).”

When this was said, the Blessed One (bhagavā) said this to Kevaṭṭa, the householder's son: “I do not, Kevaṭṭa, teach the Dhamma (dhammaṃ) to the monks (bhikkhūnaṃ) in this way, saying: ‘Come, monks (bhikkhave), perform superhuman feats (uttarimanussadhammā) and miracles (iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ) for the white-clothed lay followers (gihīnaṃ odātavasanānaṃ.’”
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
481.Evaṃme sutaṃ…pe… nāḷandāyanti kevaṭṭasuttaṃ. Tatrāyaṃ apubbapadavaṇṇanā. Pāvārikambavaneti pāvārikassa ambavane. Kevaṭṭoti idaṃ tassa gahapatiputtassa nāmaṃ. So kira cattālīsakoṭidhano gahapatimahāsālo ativiya saddho pasanno ahosi. So saddhādhikattāyeva ‘‘sace eko bhikkhu aḍḍhamāsantarena vā māsantarena vā saṃvaccharena vā ākāse uppatitvā vividhāni pāṭihāriyāni dasseyya, sabbo jano ativiya pasīdeyya. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ bhagavantaṃ yācitvā pāṭihāriyakaraṇatthāya ekaṃ bhikkhuṃ anujānāpeyya’’nti cintetvā bhagavantaṃ upasaṅkamitvā evamāha. Tattha iddhāti samiddhā phītāti nānābhaṇḍaussannatāya vuddhippattā. Ākiṇṇamanussāti aṃsakūṭena aṃsakūṭaṃ paharitvā viya vicarantehi manussehi ākiṇṇā. Samādisatūti āṇāpetu ṭhānantare ṭhapetu. Uttarimanussadhammāti uttarimanussānaṃ dhammato, dasakusalasaṅkhātato vā manussadhammato uttari. Bhiyyosomattāyāti pakatiyāpi pajjalitapadīpo telasnehaṃ labhitvā viya atirekappamāṇena abhippasīdissati. Na kho ahanti bhagavā rājagahaseṭṭhivatthusmiṃ sikkhāpadaṃ paññapesi, tasmā ‘‘na kho aha’’ntiādimāha.
English Commentary
481. Thus have I heard…pe… the Kevatta Sutta in Nāḷandā.

Here is the explanation of previously unencountered words.

"Pāvārikambavana" means in the mango grove of Pāvārika.

"Kevaṭṭa" is the name of that householder's son (gahapatiputta).

He was a householder Mahāsāla, a man with forty crores of wealth, very faithful and pleased.

He, being exceedingly full of faith, [thought:] "If a certain *bhikkhu* were to rise into the sky and display various miracles after either half a month, a month, or a year, all the people would be exceedingly pleased."

"What if I were to request the Blessed One and ask him to permit a *bhikkhu* for the sake of performing miracles?" Thinking thus, he approached the Blessed One and said this:

There, "iddhā" means prosperous, "phītā" means attained to growth by the abundance of various goods.

"Ākiṇṇamanussā" means crowded with people wandering around as if striking shoulder to shoulder.

"Samādisatu" means may he command, may he establish [him] in another place.

"Uttarimanussadhamma" means superior to the *dharma* of ordinary humans, or superior to the *manussadhamma* consisting of the ten wholesome actions.

"Bhiyyosomattāya" means even by nature, just like a lit lamp, having obtained oil, it will shine forth with exceeding measure.

"Na kho aha" – the Blessed One promulgated the training rule in connection with the incident of the Rājagaha seṭṭhi; therefore, he said, "na kho aha" and so on.

English Translation
482.

For a second time, Kevaddha, the householder’s son, said this to the Blessed One: “I am not importuning the Blessed One, venerable sir; but I say this: ‘This Nāḷandā, venerable sir, is flourishing, prosperous, full of people, and crowded with humans who are devoted to the Blessed One.

It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would direct a certain monk to perform a superhuman miracle (uttarimanussadhamma iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ). In this way, this Nāḷandā would be even more devoted to the Blessed One.’”

For a second time, the Blessed One said this to Kevaddha, the householder’s son: “I do not teach the monks in this way, Kevaddha, saying, ‘Come, monks, perform superhuman miracles (uttarimanussadhamma iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ) for the white-clothed laypeople!’”

For a third time, Kevaddha, the householder’s son, said this to the Blessed One: “I am not importuning the Blessed One, venerable sir; but I say this: ‘This Nāḷandā, venerable sir, is flourishing, prosperous, full of people, and crowded with humans who are devoted to the Blessed One.

It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would direct a certain monk to perform a superhuman miracle (uttarimanussadhamma iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ).

In this way, this Nāḷandā would be even more devoted to the Blessed One.”

Pali Commentary
482.Na dhaṃsemīti na guṇavināsanena dhaṃsemi, sīlabhedaṃ pāpetvā anupubbena uccaṭṭhānato otārento nīcaṭṭhāne na ṭhapemi, atha kho ahaṃ buddhasāsanassa vuddhiṃ paccāsīsanto kathemīti dasseti. Tatiyampi khoti yāvatatiyaṃ buddhānaṃ kathaṃ paṭibāhitvā kathetuṃ visahanto nāma natthi. Ayaṃ pana bhagavatā saddhiṃ vissāsiko vissāsaṃ vaḍḍhetvā vallabho hutvā atthakāmosmīti tikkhattuṃ kathesi.
English Commentary
482. "Na dhaṃsemi": I do not corrupt by destroying virtues. I do not cause a breach of morality, gradually bring down from a high position, and establish in a low position. But rather, I speak expecting the growth of the Buddha's dispensation, this is what it shows.

"Tatiyampi kho": Even up to the third time, there is no one who dares to speak having rejected the words of the Buddhas.

However, this one, being intimate with the Blessed One, having increased intimacy, having become a favorite, and being desirous of benefit, spoke three times.
Paragraph 483
Mūla (Source Text)
483. ‘‘Tīṇi kho imāni, kevaṭṭa, pāṭihāriyāni mayā sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā paveditāni. Katamāni tīṇi? Iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ, ādesanāpāṭihāriyaṃ , anusāsanīpāṭihāriyaṃ.
English Translation
The Lord said: ‘Kevaṭṭa, there are three kinds of marvel that I have declared, having realised them by my own insight. What are the three? The marvel of psychic power (*ṛddhi-prātihārya*), the marvel of telepathy (*ādeśanā-prātihārya*), the marvel of instruction (*anuśāsanī-prātihārya*).
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
483-484. Atha bhagavā ayaṃ upāsako mayi paṭibāhantepi punappunaṃ yācatiyeva. ‘‘Handassa pāṭihāriyakaraṇe ādīnavaṃ dassemī’’ti cintetvā ‘‘tīṇi kho’’tiādimāha. Tattha amāhaṃ bhikkhunti amuṃ ahaṃ bhikkhuṃ. Gandhārīti gandhārena nāma isinā katā, gandhāraraṭṭhe vā uppannā vijjā. Tattha kira bahū isayo vasiṃsu, tesu ekena katā vijjāti adhippāyo. Aṭṭīyāmīti aṭṭo pīḷito viya homi. Harāyāmīti lajjāmi. Jigucchāmīti gūthaṃ disvā viya jigucchaṃ uppādemi.
English Commentary
483-484. Then, although this lay follower (*upāsaka*) was hindering me, he kept requesting again and again.

"I will show the disadvantage in the performance of a marvel," thinking thus, he said, "Three, indeed," etc., as the beginning.

There, I said, "I am a *bhikkhu*," to that *bhikkhu*.

*Gandhārī* means [that it] was made by a seer (*iṣi*) named *Gandhāra*, or a science (*vidyā*) that arose in the *Gandhāra* country.

There, it is said that many seers (*iṣi*) lived; the meaning is that the science (*vidyā*) was made by one of them.

"I am troubled" means I am as if troubled, oppressed.

"I am ashamed" means I am ashamed (*lajjāmi*).

"I feel disgust" means, as if having seen excrement (*gūtha*), I produce disgust.

Mūla (Source Text)
484. ‘‘Katamañca, kevaṭṭa, iddhipāṭihāriyaṃ? Idha, kevaṭṭa, bhikkhu anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccanubhoti. Ekopi hutvā bahudhā hoti, bahudhāpi hutvā eko hoti; āvibhāvaṃ tirobhāvaṃ tirokuṭṭaṃ tiropākāraṃ tiropabbataṃ asajjamāno gacchati seyyathāpi ākāse; pathaviyāpi ummujjanimujjaṃ karoti seyyathāpi udake; udakepi abhijjamāne gacchati seyyathāpi pathaviyaṃ; ākāsepi pallaṅkena kamati seyyathāpi pakkhī sakuṇo; imepi candimasūriye evaṃ mahiddhike evaṃ mahānubhāve pāṇinā parāmasati parimajjati; yāva brahmalokāpi kāyena vasaṃ vatteti. ‘‘Tamenaṃ aññataro saddho pasanno passati taṃ bhikkhuṃ anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccanubhontaṃ – ekopi hutvā bahudhā hontaṃ, bahudhāpi hutvā eko hontaṃ; āvibhāvaṃ tirobhāvaṃ; tirokuṭṭaṃ tiropākāraṃ tiropabbataṃ asajjamānaṃ gacchantaṃ seyyathāpi ākāse; pathaviyāpi ummujjanimujjaṃ karontaṃ seyyathāpi udake; udakepi abhijjamāne gacchantaṃ seyyathāpi pathaviyaṃ; ākāsepi pallaṅkena kamantaṃ seyyathāpi pakkhī sakuṇo; imepi candimasūriye evaṃ mahiddhike evaṃ mahānubhāve pāṇinā parāmasantaṃ parimajjantaṃ yāva brahmalokāpi kāyena vasaṃ vattentaṃ. ‘‘Tamenaṃ so saddho pasanno aññatarassa assaddhassa appasannassa āroceti – ‘acchariyaṃ vata, bho, abbhutaṃ vata, bho, samaṇassa mahiddhikatā mahānubhāvatā. Amāhaṃ bhikkhuṃ addasaṃ anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccanubhontaṃ – ekopi hutvā bahudhā hontaṃ, bahudhāpi hutvā eko hontaṃ…pe… yāva brahmalokāpi kāyena vasaṃ vattenta’nti. ‘‘Tamenaṃ so assaddho appasanno taṃ saddhaṃ pasannaṃ evaṃ vadeyya – ‘atthi kho, bho, gandhārī nāma vijjā. Tāya so bhikkhu anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccanubhoti – ekopi hutvā bahudhā hoti, bahudhāpi hutvā eko hoti…pe… yāva brahmalokāpi kāyena vasaṃ vattetī’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kevaṭṭa, api nu so assaddho appasanno taṃ saddhaṃ pasannaṃ evaṃ vadeyyā’’ti? ‘‘Vadeyya, bhante’’ti. ‘‘Imaṃ kho ahaṃ, kevaṭṭa, iddhipāṭihāriye ādīnavaṃ sampassamāno iddhipāṭihāriyena aṭṭīyāmi harāyāmi jigucchāmi’’.
English Translation
484.
“And what, Kevatta, is the miracle of psychic power ( *iddhipāṭihāriya*)? Here, Kevatta, a monk experiences various kinds of psychic power (*anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ*).

Being one, he becomes many; being many, he becomes one. He appears and vanishes. He goes unhindered through a wall, through an enclosure, through a mountain, as though through space. He dives in and out of the earth as though it were water. He walks on water without sinking as though it were earth. Seated cross-legged, he travels in space like a bird. With his hand he touches and strokes the moon and sun, so powerful and mighty. He exercises mastery with the body as far as the Brahma world.

"Then a faithful believer sees that monk experiencing various kinds of psychic power—being one, becoming many; being many, becoming one; appearing and vanishing; going unhindered through a wall, through an enclosure, through a mountain as though through space; diving in and out of the earth as though it were water; walking on water without sinking as though it were earth; seated cross-legged, traveling in space like a bird; touching and stroking the moon and sun, so powerful and mighty, with his hand; exercising mastery with the body as far as the Brahma world.

"Then that faithful believer tells it to another who is skeptical and unbelieving, saying, 'It's amazing, sir! It's marvelous, sir! The power and might of that ascetic!

I saw a monk experiencing various kinds of psychic power—being one, becoming many; being many, becoming one…exercising mastery with the body as far as the Brahma world.’

"Then that skeptical unbeliever might say this to that faithful believer: 'Sir, there is a Gandhari charm ( *gandhārī nāma vijjā*).

It is by that the monk experiences various kinds of psychic power: being one, he becomes many; being many, he becomes one…exercising mastery with the body as far as the Brahma world.’

“What do you think, Kevatta? Might not that skeptic and unbeliever say that to that faithful believer?” “He might say so, sir.”

“That is why, Kevatta, seeing the danger in the miracle of psychic power (*iddhipāṭihāriya*), I am disgusted, ashamed, and repelled by the miracle of psychic power (*iddhipāṭihāriya*)."

Mūla (Source Text)
485. ‘‘Katamañca, kevaṭṭa, ādesanāpāṭihāriyaṃ? Idha, kevaṭṭa, bhikkhu parasattānaṃ parapuggalānaṃ cittampi ādisati, cetasikampi ādisati, vitakkitampi ādisati, vicāritampi ādisati – ‘evampi te mano, itthampi te mano, itipi te citta’nti. ‘‘Tamenaṃ aññataro saddho pasanno passati taṃ bhikkhuṃ parasattānaṃ parapuggalānaṃ cittampi ādisantaṃ, cetasikampi ādisantaṃ, vitakkitampi ādisantaṃ, vicāritampi ādisantaṃ – ‘evampi te mano, itthampi te mano, itipi te citta’nti. Tamenaṃ so saddho pasanno aññatarassa assaddhassa appasannassa āroceti – ‘acchariyaṃ vata, bho, abbhutaṃ vata, bho, samaṇassa mahiddhikatā mahānubhāvatā. Amāhaṃ bhikkhuṃ addasaṃ parasattānaṃ parapuggalānaṃ cittampi ādisantaṃ, cetasikampi ādisantaṃ, vitakkitampi ādisantaṃ, vicāritampi ādisantaṃ – ‘‘evampi te mano, itthampi te mano, itipi te citta’’’nti. ‘‘Tamenaṃ so assaddho appasanno taṃ saddhaṃ pasannaṃ evaṃ vadeyya – ‘atthi kho, bho, maṇikā nāma vijjā; tāya so bhikkhu parasattānaṃ parapuggalānaṃ cittampi ādisati, cetasikampi ādisati, vitakkitampi ādisati, vicāritampi ādisati – ‘evampi te mano, itthampi te mano, itipi te citta’’’nti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññasi, kevaṭṭa, api nu so assaddho appasanno taṃ saddhaṃ pasannaṃ evaṃ vadeyyā’’ti ? ‘‘Vadeyya, bhante’’ti. ‘‘Imaṃ kho ahaṃ, kevaṭṭa, ādesanāpāṭihāriye ādīnavaṃ sampassamāno ādesanāpāṭihāriyena aṭṭīyāmi harāyāmi jigucchāmi’’.
English Translation
What is the miracle of mind-reading, Kevatta? Here, Kevatta, a monk reads the minds of other beings, of other people, reads their mental states (*cetasika*), their thoughts (*vitakkita*) and ponderings (*vicārita*), and says: “That is how your mind (*manas*) is, that is how it inclines, that is in your heart (*citta*).”

Then another believer, serene in faith, sees that monk reading the minds (*citta*) of other beings, of other people, reading their mental states (*cetasika*), reading their thoughts (*vitakkita*), pondering their ideas (*vicārita*), and saying: “That is how your mind (*manas*) is, that is how it inclines, that is in your heart (*citta*).”

He tells this to someone else who is skeptical and unbelieving, saying: “It is wonderful, sir, it is marvellous, the great power (*mahiddhikatā*) and skill (*mahānubhāvatā*) of that ascetic!”

I saw a monk reading the minds (*citta*) of other beings, of other people, reading their mental states (*cetasika*), reading their thoughts (*vitakkita*), pondering their ideas (*vicārita*), and saying: “That is how your mind (*manas*) is, that is how it inclines, that is in your heart (*citta*).”

And that man, being skeptical and unbelieving, might say to that believer, serene in faith: “Sir, there is something called the *Maṇika* charm. It is by means of this that that monk can read the minds (*citta*) of others, can read their mental states (*cetasika*), can read their thoughts (*vitakkita*), can read their ponderings (*vicārita*)—’That is how your mind (*manas*) is, that is how your mind inclines, that is what is in your heart (*citta*)’."

What do you think, Kevatta, would not a skeptic say that to a believer?” “He would say so, Bhante.”

And that is why, Kevatta, seeing the danger (*ādīnava*) of such mind-reading miracles (*ādesanāpāṭihāriya*), I dislike, reject and despise mind-reading miracles (*ādesanāpāṭihāriya*).
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
485.Parasattānanti aññesaṃ sattānaṃ. Dutiyaṃ tasseva vevacanaṃ. Ādisatīti katheti. Cetasikanti somanassadomanassaṃ adhippetaṃ. Evampi te manoti evaṃ tava mano somanassito vā domanassito vā kāmavitakkādisampayutto vā. Dutiyaṃ tasseva vevacanaṃ. Itipi te cittanti iti tava cittaṃ, idañcidañca atthaṃ cintayamānaṃ pavattatīti attho. Maṇikā nāma vijjāti cintāmaṇīti evaṃ laddhanāmā loke ekā vijjā atthi. Tāya paresaṃ cittaṃ jānātīti dīpeti.
English Commentary
485. *Parasattānanti* means of other beings (*aññesaṃ sattānaṃ*).

The second is a synonym of that.

*Ādisatīti* means he speaks (*katheti*).

*Cetasikanti* refers to pleasure and displeasure (*somanassadomanassaṃ adhippetaṃ*).

*Evampi te manoti* means thus your mind (*evaṃ tava mano*) is associated with pleasure (*somanassito*) or displeasure (*domanassito*) or sensual thoughts (*kāmavitakkādisampayutto*).

The second is a synonym of that.

*Itipi te cittanti* means thus your mind (*iti tava cittaṃ*), thinking about this and that matter (*idañcidañca atthaṃ cintayamānaṃ*), proceeds, this is the meaning (*pavattatīti attho*).

There is a knowledge (*ekā vijjā atthi*) in the world (*loke*) called *Maṇikā*, namely *Cintāmaṇī*, thus it has obtained its name (*evaṃ laddhanāmā*).

By that, he illuminates that he knows the mind of others (*tāya paresaṃ cittaṃ jānātīti dīpeti*).
Paragraph 486

Āciṇṇaṃ kiretaṃ buddhānaṃ, pucchāmūḷhassa janassa pucchāya dosaṃ dassetvā pucchaṃ sikkhāpetvā pucchāvissajjanaṃ. Kasmā? Pucchituṃ ajānitvā paripucchanto duviññāpayo hoti. Pañhaṃ sikkhāpento pana ‘‘kattha āpo cā’’tiādimāha.
English Commentary
497. They augur from a crow or a kulala.

"No, monk, that question should not be asked in that way." Because the Blessed One said, "This question should be asked with a basis," but this monk, even taking what is without basis, asks without a basis, therefore he forbids it.

It is customary for Buddhas, having shown the fault in questioning to a person confused about questioning, and having taught questioning, (to teach) answering questions.

Why? Because without knowing how to ask a question, asking repeatedly leads to unintelligibility.

When teaching a question, however, he says, "Where are the waters, and so on?"

There, because it does not plunge, it does not stand firm. What having come, do these four great elements (catvāro mahābhūtā) become unstable? This is the meaning.

"Long and short" is said as form (rūpa) that is clung to, in terms of shape.

499. ‘‘Tatra veyyākaraṇaṃ bhavati –
English Translation
499. And the answer is:
Aṭṭhakathā (Commentary)
Pali Commentary
499. Tattha viññātabbanti viññāṇaṃ nibbānassetaṃ nāmaṃ, tadetaṃ nidassanābhāvato anidassanaṃ. Uppādanto vā vayanto vā ṭhitassa aññathattanto vā etassa natthīti anantaṃ. Pabhanti panetaṃ kira titthassa nāmaṃ, tañhi papanti etthāti papaṃ, pakārassa pana bhakāro kato. Sabbato pabhamassāti sabbatopabhaṃ. Nibbānassa kira yathā mahāsamuddassa yato yato otaritukāmā honti, taṃ tadeva titthaṃ, atitthaṃ nāma natthi. Evameva aṭṭhatiṃsāya kammaṭṭhānesu yena yena mukhena nibbānaṃ otaritukāmā honti, taṃ tadeva titthaṃ, nibbānassa atitthaṃ nāma natthi. Tena vuttaṃ ‘‘sabbatopabha’’nti. Ettha āpo cāti ettha nibbāne idaṃ nibbānaṃ āgamma sabbametaṃ āpotiādinā nayena vuttaṃ upādinnaka dhammajātaṃ nirujjhati, appavattaṃ hotīti. Idānissa nirujjhanūpāyaṃ dassento ‘‘viññāṇassa nirodhena etthetaṃ uparujjhatī’’ti āha. Tattha viññāṇanti carimakaviññāṇampi abhisaṅkhāraviññāṇampi , carimakaviññāṇassāpi hi nirodhena etthetaṃ uparujjhati. Vijjhātadīpasikhā viya apaṇṇattikabhāvaṃ yāti. Abhisaṅkhāraviññāṇassāpi anuppādanirodhena anuppādavasena uparujjhati. Yathāha ‘‘sotāpattimaggañāṇena abhisaṅkhāraviññāṇassa nirodhena ṭhapetvā sattabhave anamatagge saṃsāre ye uppajjeyyuṃ nāmañca rūpañca etthete nirujjhantī’’ti sabbaṃ cūḷaniddese vuttanayeneva veditabbaṃ. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti.
English Commentary
499.
There, that which may be known is consciousness ( *viññāṇaṃ*), this is a name for nirvāṇa; but this is unmanifest due to the absence of manifestation.

That which is arising, ceasing, or the alteration of that which is abiding, this does not exist for it; therefore, it is endless (*anantaṃ*).

Indeed, they say that this shines as a name for a sacred bathing place (*tīrthassa nāmaṃ*); because one goes down into it, therefore it is *papaṃ*; moreover, the letter "pa" becomes the letter "bha."

Because it shines in every way, therefore it is all-shining (*sabbatopabhaṃ*).

It is said that just as for nirvāṇa, from whichever place one wants to descend into the great ocean (*mahāsamuddassa*), that is the sacred bathing place (*tīrthaṃ*); there is no non-bathing place (*atitthaṃ*).

Just so, in the thirty-eight objects of meditation (*aṭṭhatiṃsāya kammaṭṭhānesu*), by whichever means one wants to descend into nirvāṇa, that is the sacred bathing place (*tīrthaṃ*); there is no non-bathing place for nirvāṇa (*nibbānassa atitthaṃ*).

Therefore, it was said, "all-shining" (*sabbatopabhaṃ*).

Here, "water" and "ca," here in nirvāṇa, having come to this nirvāṇa, all this aggregate of phenomena (*dhammajātaṃ*) that is spoken of by way of "water," etc., ceases; it does not continue, so it is said.

Now, showing the means of cessation, he said, "By the cessation (*nirodhena*) of consciousness (*viññāṇassa*), this ceases here."

There, consciousness (*viññāṇanti*) means both the final consciousness (*carimakaviññāṇampi*) and the consciousness associated with karmic formations (*abhisaṅkhāraviññāṇampi*); indeed, by the cessation (*nirodhena*) of even the final consciousness (*carimakaviññāṇassāpi*), this ceases here.

It goes to a state of non-appearance, like the flame of an extinguished lamp (*vijjhātadīpasikhā viya apaṇṇattikabhāvaṃ yāti*).

Even the consciousness associated with karmic formations (*abhisaṅkhāraviññāṇassāpi*) ceases by the cessation of non-arising, by virtue of non-arising.

As he said, "By the knowledge of the path of stream-entry (*sotāpattimaggañāṇena*), by the cessation (*nirodhena*) of the consciousness associated with karmic formations (*abhisaṅkhāraviññāṇassa*), having established [that], in the seven existences (*sattabhave*), in the cycle of existence (*saṃsāre*) whose beginning is not known (*anamatagge*), those name and form (*nāmañca rūpañca*) that would arise, these cease here," all this should be understood in the same way as it is stated in the Cūḷaniddesa.

The rest is clear everywhere.
