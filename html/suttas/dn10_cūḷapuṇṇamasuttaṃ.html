Kā ca, bhikkhave, asappurisānaṃ gati? Nirayo vā tiracchānayoni vā.

91. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvatthī in the Eastern Park in Migāramātā’s mansion.

Then, on the fifteenth day of the lunar cycle, on the full-moon night, the Blessed One was seated in the open air surrounded by the Community of monks.

Then, surveying the silent Sangha of bhikkhus, the Blessed One addressed them thus: “Bhikkhus, would an untrue man know of an untrue man: ‘This person is an untrue man’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“Good, bhikkhus. It is impossible, bhikkhus, it cannot be, that an untrue man should know of an untrue man: ‘This person is an untrue man.’

Would an untrue man know of a true man: ‘This person is a true man’?” “No, venerable sir.”

“Good, bhikkhus. It is impossible, bhikkhus, it cannot be, that an untrue man should know of a true man: ‘This person is a true man.’

Bhikkhus, an asappurisa (untrue man) is possessed of bad qualities (asaddhammasamannāgato), he associates as an untrue man (asappurisabhatti), he wills as an untrue man, he counsels as an untrue man, he speaks as an untrue man, he acts as an untrue man, and he holds views as an untrue man; and he gives gifts as an untrue man.”

“And how, bhikkhus, is an untrue man possessed of bad qualities? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man has no faith (assaddho), no shame (ahiriko), or moral prudence (anottappī); he is unlearned (appassuto), lazy (kusīto), forgetful (muṭṭhassati), and unwise (duppañño).

That is how an untrue man is possessed of bad qualities.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man associate as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, those śrāmaṇas and brāhmaṇas who are faithless (assaddhā), shameless (ahirikā), without moral prudence (anottappino), unlearned (appassutā), lazy (kusītā), forgetful (muṭṭhassatino), and unwise (duppaññā) are his friends and companions.

That is how an untrue man associates as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man will as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man wills for his own affliction (attabyābādhāya), for the affliction of others (parabyābādhāya), and for the affliction of both (ubhayabyābādhāya).

That is how an untrue man wills as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man counsel as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man counsels for his own affliction (attabyābādhāya), for the affliction of others (parabyābādhāya), and for the affliction of both (ubhayabyābādhāya).

That is how an untrue man counsels as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man speak as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man is a liar (musāvādī), a slanderer (pisuṇavāco), one who speaks harshly (pharusavāco), and a gossip (samphappalāpī).

That is how an untrue man speaks as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man act as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man kills living beings (pāṇātipātī), takes what is not given (adinnādāyī), and engages in sexual misconduct (kāmesumicchācārī).

That is how an untrue man acts as an untrue man.

“And how, bhikkhus, does an untrue man hold views as an untrue man? Here, bhikkhus, an untrue man holds such a view as this: ‘There is nothing given (natthi dinnaṃ), nothing offered (natthi yiṭṭhaṃ), nothing sacrificed (natthi hutaṃ); there is no fruit or result of good and bad actions (sukatadukkaṭānaṃ);
)
There is no fruit or result of actions (karmāṇaṃ phalaṃ vipāko); there is no this world; there is no other world; there is no mother; there is no father; there are no beings who are reborn spontaneously (sattā opapātikā); there are no ascetics or Brahmins in the world who have attained the right path (samaṇabrāhmaṇā sammaggatā) and practice rightly, and who by themselves, having realized direct knowledge (abhiññā), proclaim this world and the other world.’

That is how, monks, an untrue man holds untrue views (asappurisadiṭṭhi).



‘‘And how, monks, does an untrue man give a gift as an untrue man? Here, monks, an untrue man gives a gift carelessly (asakkaccaṃ dānaṃ deti), gives it not with his own hand (asahatthā dānaṃ deti), gives it without showing respect (acittīkatvā dānaṃ deti), gives what is to be discarded (apaviṭṭhaṃ dānaṃ deti), and gives it with the view that nothing will come of it (anāgamanadiṭṭhiko dānaṃ deti).

That is how, monks, an untrue man gives a gift as an untrue man.



‘‘So, monks, an untrue man is thus endowed with disbelief in the Dhamma (assaddhammasamannāgato), thus with unfaithfulness to untrue men (asappurisabhatti), thus thinking the thoughts of an untrue man (asappurisacintī), thus holding the views of an untrue man (asappurisamantī), thus speaking the words of an untrue man (asappurisavāco), thus acting with the actions of an untrue man (asappurisakammanto), thus with untrue views (asappurisadiṭṭhi); thus, having given a gift as an untrue man (asappurisadānaṃ datvā), on the dissolution of the body, after death, that person goes to the destination of untrue men (asappurisānaṃ gati).

And what, monks, is the destination of untrue men (asappurisānaṃ gati)? Hell (nirayo) or the animal realm (tiracchānayoni).

Jāneyya pana, bhikkhave, sappuriso asappurisaṃ – ‘asappuriso ayaṃ bhava’’’nti? ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Sādhu, bhikkhave; etampi kho, bhikkhave, ṭhānaṃ vijjati yaṃ sappuriso asappurisaṃ jāneyya – ‘asappuriso ayaṃ bhava’nti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, sappuriso sappurisabhatti <pb ed="T" n="3.0113"/> hoti.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0113"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, sappuriso sappurisacintī hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, sappuriso nevattabyābādhāya ceteti, na parabyābādhāya ceteti, na ubhayabyābādhāya ceteti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, sappuriso sappurisacintī hoti.

<pb ed="P" n="3.0024"/> <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Kathañca <pb ed="M" n="3.0074"/>, bhikkhave, sappuriso sappurisadiṭṭhi hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, sappuriso evaṃdiṭṭhi hoti – ‘atthi dinnaṃ, atthi yiṭṭhaṃ, atthi hutaṃ, atthi sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko, atthi ayaṃ loko <pb ed="T" n="3.0114"/>, atthi paro loko, atthi mātā, atthi pitā, atthi sattā opapātikā, atthi loke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sammaggatā sammāpaṭipannā ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī’ti. <p rend="bodytext">Tassuddānaṃ –

<p rend="gatha1">Devadahaṃ pañcattayaṃ, kinti-sāma-sunakkhattaṃ;

“Monks, would a true man know a true man, thinking, ‘This person is a true man’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Good, monks. It is possible, monks, that a true man would know a true man, thinking, ‘This person is a true man.’ But monks, would a true man know an untrue man, thinking, ‘This person is an untrue man’?”

“Yes, venerable sir.”

“Good, monks. It is also possible, monks, that a true man would know an untrue man, thinking, ‘This person is an untrue man.’ A true man, monks, is possessed of good qualities (saddhammasamannāgato), associates as a true man (sappurisabhatti), wills as a true man (sappurisacintī), counsels as a true man (sappurisamantī), speaks as a true man (sappurisavāco), acts as a true man (sappurisakammanto), holds views as a true man (sappurisadiṭṭhi); and gives gifts as a true man (sappurisadānaṃ deti).”

“And how, monks, is a true man possessed of good qualities (saddhammasamannāgato)? Here, monks, a true man has faith (saddho), shame (hirimā), and fear of wrongdoing (ottappī); he is learned (bahussuto), energetic (āraddhavīriyo), mindful (upaṭṭhitassati), and wise (paññavā). That is how, monks, a true man is possessed of good qualities.”

“And how, monks, does a true man associate as a true man (sappurisabhatti)? Here, monks, those recluses and brāhmaṇas who are faithful (saddhā), have shame (hirimanto), and fear of wrongdoing (ottappino); who are learned (bahussutā), energetic (āraddhavīriyā), mindful (upaṭṭhitassatino), and wise (paññavanto)—these are his friends, these are his companions. That is how, monks, a true man associates as a true man.”

“And how, monks, does a true man will as a true man (sappurisacintī)? Here, monks, a true man does not will for his own affliction (nevattabyābādhāya ceteti), for the affliction of others (na parabyābādhāya ceteti), or for the affliction of both (na ubhayabyābādhāya ceteti). That is how, monks, a true man wills as a true man.”

“And how, monks, does a true man counsel as a true man (sappurisamantī)? Here, monks, a true man does not counsel for his own affliction (nevattabyābādhāya manteti), for the affliction of others (na parabyābādhāya manteti), or for the affliction of both (na ubhayabyābādhāya manteti). That is how, monks, a true man counsels as a true man.”

“And how, monks, does a true man speak as a true man (sappurisavāco)? Here, monks, a true man abstains from false speech (musāvādā paṭivirato hoti), from malicious speech (pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti), from harsh speech (pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti), and from gossip (samphappalāpā paṭivirato hoti). That is how, monks, a true man speaks as a true man.”

“And how, monks, does a true man act as a true man (sappurisakammanto)? Here, monks, a true man abstains from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti), abstains from taking what is not given (adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti), and abstains from misconduct in sensual pleasures (kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato hoti). That is how, monks, a true man acts as a true man.”

“And how, monks, does a true man hold views as a true man (sappurisadiṭṭhi)? Here, monks, a true man holds such a view as this: ‘There is what is given (atthi dinnaṃ), what is offered (atthi yiṭṭhaṃ), and what is sacrificed (atthi hutaṃ); there is fruit and result of good and bad actions (sukatadukkaṭānaṃ kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko); there is this world (atthi ayaṃ loko) and the other world (atthi paro loko); there is mother (atthi mātā) and father (atthi pitā); there are beings who are reborn spontaneously (atthi sattā opapātikā); there are good and virtuous recluses and brāhmaṇas in the world (atthi loke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sammaggatā sammāpaṭipannā) who have realised for themselves by direct knowledge and declare this world and the other world (ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī).’ That is how, monks, a true man holds views as a true man.”

“And how, monks, does a true man give gifts as a true man (sappurisadānaṃ deti)? Here, monks, a true man gives a gift carefully (sakkaccaṃ dānaṃ deti), gives it with his own hand (sahatthā dānaṃ deti), gives it showing respect (cittīkatvā dānaṃ deti), gives a valuable gift (anapaviṭṭhaṃ dānaṃ deti), and gives it with the view that something will come of it (āgamanadiṭṭhiko dānaṃ deti). That is how, monks, a true man gives gifts as a true man.”

“That true man, monks—thus possessed of good qualities, who thus associates as a true man, wills as a true man, counsels as a true man, speaks as a true man, acts as a true man, holds views as a true man, and gives gifts as a true man—on the dissolution of the body, after death, reappears in the destination of true men. And what, monks, is the destination of true men? Greatness among the gods (devamahattatā) or greatness among humans (manussamahattatā).”

That is what the Blessed One said. Elated, those monks delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.
The Cūḷapuṇṇama Sutta, the tenth, is finished.

The Devadaha Section, the first, is finished.

Its summary:

Devadaha, five triads, Kinti, Sāma, Sunakkhatta;

Sappāya, Gaṇa, Gopaka, Mahāpuṇṇa, and Cūḷapuṇṇa.
