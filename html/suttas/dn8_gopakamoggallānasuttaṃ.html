<p n="79" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">79</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0089"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0056"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ āyasmā ānando rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe aciraparinibbute bhagavati. Tena kho pana samayena rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto rājagahaṃ paṭisaṅkhārāpeti rañño pajjotassa āsaṅkamāno. Atha kho āyasmā ānando pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya rājagahaṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi. Atha kho āyasmato ānandassa etadahosi – ‘‘atippago kho tāva rājagahe piṇḍāya carituṃ. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ yena gopakamoggallānassa brāhmaṇassa kammanto, yena gopakamoggallāno brāhmaṇo tenupasaṅkameyya’’nti.

<p rend="bodytext">Atha kho āyasmā ānando yena gopakamoggallānassa brāhmaṇassa kammanto, yena gopakamoggallāno brāhmaṇo tenupasaṅkami. Addasā kho gopakamoggallāno brāhmaṇo āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ dūratova āgacchantaṃ. Disvāna āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘etu kho bhavaṃ ānando. Svāgataṃ bhoto ānandassa. Cirassaṃ kho bhavaṃ ānando imaṃ pariyāyamakāsi yadidaṃ idhāgamanāya. Nisīdatu bhavaṃ ānando, idamāsanaṃ paññatta’’nti. Nisīdi kho āyasmā ānando paññatte āsane. Gopakamoggallānopi <pb ed="P" n="3.0008"/> kho brāhmaṇo aññataraṃ nīcaṃ āsanaṃ gahetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0090"/> nisinno kho gopakamoggallāno brāhmaṇo āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘atthi nu kho, bho ānanda, ekabhikkhupi tehi <pb ed="M" n="3.0059"/> dhammehi sabbenasabbaṃ sabbathāsabbaṃ samannāgato yehi dhammehi samannāgato so bhavaṃ gotamo ahosi arahaṃ sammāsambuddho’’ti? ‘‘Natthi kho, brāhmaṇa, ekabhikkhupi tehi dhammehi sabbenasabbaṃ sabbathāsabbaṃ samannāgato yehi dhammehi samannāgato so bhagavā ahosi arahaṃ sammāsambuddho. So hi, brāhmaṇa, bhagavā anuppannassa maggassa uppādetā, asañjātassa maggassa sañjanetā, anakkhātassa maggassa akkhātā, maggaññū, maggavidū, maggakovido; maggānugā ca pana etarahi sāvakā viharanti pacchā samannāgatā’’ti. Ayañca hidaṃ āyasmato ānandassa gopakamoggallānena brāhmaṇena saddhiṃ antarākathā vippakatā ahosi.

<pb ed="P" n="3.0008"/> <pb ed="T" n="3.0090"/> <pb ed="M" n="3.0059"/> <p rend="bodytext">Atha <pb ed="V" n="3.0057"/> kho vassakāro brāhmaṇo magadhamahāmatto rājagahe kammante anusaññāyamāno yena gopakamoggallānassa brāhmaṇassa kammanto, yenāyasmā ānando tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmatā ānandena saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho vassakāro brāhmaṇo magadhamahāmatto āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kāyanuttha, bho ānanda, etarahi kathāya sannisinnā, kā ca pana vo antarākathā vippakatā’’ti? ‘‘Idha maṃ, brāhmaṇa, gopakamoggallāno <pb ed="T" n="3.0091"/> brāhmaṇo evamāha – ‘atthi nu kho, bho ānanda, ekabhikkhupi tehi dhammehi sabbenasabbaṃ sabbathāsabbaṃ samannāgato yehi dhammehi samannāgato so bhavaṃ gotamo ahosi arahaṃ sammāsambuddho’ti. Evaṃ vutte ahaṃ, brāhmaṇa, gopakamoggallānaṃ brāhmaṇaṃ etadavocaṃ – ‘natthi kho, brāhmaṇa, ekabhikkhupi tehi dhammehi sabbenasabbaṃ sabbathāsabbaṃ samannāgato yehi dhammehi samannāgato so bhagavā ahosi arahaṃ sammāsambuddho. So hi, brāhmaṇa, bhagavā anuppannassa maggassa uppādetā, asañjātassa <pb ed="P" n="3.0009"/> maggassa sañjanetā, anakkhātassa maggassa akkhātā, maggaññū, maggavidū, maggakovido; maggānugā ca pana etarahi sāvakā viharanti pacchā samannāgatā’ti. Ayaṃ kho no, brāhmaṇa, gopakamoggallānena brāhmaṇena saddhiṃ antarākathā vippakatā. Atha tvaṃ anuppatto’’ti.

THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the venerable Ānanda was living at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove (Veḷuvana), the Squirrels’ Sanctuary (Kalandakanivāpa), not long after the Blessed One (Bhagavati) had attained final Nibbāna (parinibbute).

Now on that occasion King Ajātasattu Vedehiputta of Magadha, being suspicious of King Pajjota, was having Rājagaha fortified (paṭisaṅkhārāpeti).

Then the venerable Ānanda, having dressed in the morning, taking his bowl and outer robe (pattacīvaramādāya), went into Rājagaha for alms (piṇḍāya pāvisi).

Then the venerable Ānanda thought: “It is still too early to wander for alms in Rājagaha.

Suppose I went to the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna at his workplace (kammanto); suppose I went to the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna.”

Then the venerable Ānanda went to the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna at his workplace; the venerable Ānanda went to the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna.

The brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna saw the venerable Ānanda coming in the distance.

Seeing the venerable Ānanda, he said: “Let Master Ānanda come!

Welcome to Master Ānanda!

It is long since Master Ānanda found an opportunity to come here.

Let Master Ānanda be seated; this seat is ready.”

The venerable Ānanda sat down on the prepared seat (paññatte āsane).

The brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna also took a low seat, sat down at one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdi).

Sitting to one side, the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna said to the venerable Ānanda: “Master Ānanda, is there any single bhikkhu who possesses in each and every way all those qualities that were possessed by Master Gotama, the arahaṃ, the fully enlightened one (sammāsambuddho)?” “No, brahmin, there is no single bhikkhu who possesses in each and every way all those qualities that were possessed by the Blessed One (Bhagavā), the arahaṃ, the fully enlightened one.

For the Blessed One (Bhagavā), brahmin, was the arouser of the unarisen path (anuppannassa maggassa uppādetā), the producer of the unproduced path (asañjātassa maggassa sañjanetā), the declarer of the undeclared path (anakkhatassa maggassa akkhātā), the knower of the path (maggaññū), the finder of the path (maggavidū), the one skilled in the path (maggakovido); but his disciples (sāvakā) now abide following that path and become possessed of it afterwards.”

This was our discussion that was interrupted between the venerable Ānanda and the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna.

Then Vassakāra the brahmin, the chief minister of Magadha, while supervising the work at Rājagaha, went to the venerable Ānanda at the workplace of the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna. He exchanged greetings with the venerable Ānanda.

When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdi).

Sitting to one side, Vassakāra the brahmin, the chief minister of Magadha, said to the venerable Ānanda: “For what discussion are you sitting together here now, Master Ānanda? And what was your discussion that was interrupted?” “Here, brahmin, the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna said this to me: ‘Master Ānanda, is there any single bhikkhu who possesses in each and every way all those qualities that were possessed by Master Gotama, the arahaṃ, the fully enlightened one (sammāsambuddho)?’”

“When he had said this, brahmin, I, the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna, said this: ‘No, brahmin, there is no single bhikkhu who possesses in each and every way all those qualities that were possessed by the Blessed One (Bhagavā), the arahaṃ, the fully enlightened one.

For the Blessed One (Bhagavā), brahmin, was the arouser of the unarisen path (anuppannassa maggassa uppādetā), the producer of the unproduced path (asañjātassa maggassa sañjanetā), the declarer of the undeclared path (anakkhatassa maggassa akkhātā), the knower of the path (maggaññū), the finder of the path (maggavidū), the one skilled in the path (maggakovido); but his disciples (sāvakā) now abide following that path and become possessed of it afterwards.’

This was our discussion that was interrupted with the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna.

Then you arrived.”

“Is there, Master Ānanda, any single bhikkhu who was appointed by Master Gotama thus: ‘He will be your refuge (pratisaraṇa) when I am gone,’ and whom you now have recourse to?”

“There is no single bhikkhu, Brahmin, who was appointed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished (arhat) and fully enlightened (sammāsambuddha), thus: ‘He will be your refuge (pratisaraṇa) when I am gone,’ and whom we now have recourse to.”

“Is there, Ānanda, any single bhikkhu who has been chosen by the saṃgha and appointed by a number of elder bhikkhus thus: ‘He will be our refuge (pratisaraṇa) after the Blessed One has gone,’ and whom you now have recourse to?”

“There is no single bhikkhu, Brahmin, who has been chosen by the saṃgha and appointed by a number of elder bhikkhus thus: ‘He will be our refuge (pratisaraṇa) after the Blessed One has gone,’ and whom we now have recourse to.”

“In such a lack of refuge, Master Ānanda, what is the cause of concord (sāmaggiya)?”

“We are not without refuge (appaṭisaraṇā), Brahmin; we have a refuge (sappaṭisaraṇā), Brahmin; we have the Dharma as our refuge (dhammappaṭisaraṇā).”

“But when you were asked: ‘Is there, Master Ānanda, any single bhikkhu who was appointed by Master Gotama thus: “He will be your refuge (pratisaraṇa) when I am gone,” and whom you now have recourse to?’ you answered: ‘There is no such single bhikkhu who was appointed by the Blessed One, knowing, seeing, accomplished, a sammāsambuddha… whom we now have recourse to.’ When you were asked: ‘Is there, Master Ānanda, any single bhikkhu who has been chosen by the saṃgha and appointed by a number of elder bhikkhus thus: “He will be our refuge (pratisaraṇa) after the Blessed One has gone,” and whom you now have recourse to?’ you answered: ‘There is no such single bhikkhu who has been chosen by the saṃgha and appointed by a number of elder bhikkhus… whom we now have recourse to.’ When you were asked: ‘In such a lack of refuge, Master Ānanda, what is the cause of concord (sāmaggiya)?’ being asked thus, you answered: ‘We are not without refuge (appaṭisaraṇā), Brahmin; we have a refuge (sappaṭisaraṇā), Brahmin; we have the Dharma as our refuge (dhammappaṭisaraṇā)’. In what way then, Master Ānanda, should the meaning of what was said be understood?”

“82. There are, brahmin, ten qualities inspiring confidence that have been declared by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

When these qualities are found in anyone among us, we honour, respect, revere, and venerate him, and live in dependence on him honouring and respecting him.

What are the ten?

Here, brahmin, a bhikkhu is virtuous, he dwells restrained with the restraint of the Pāṭimokkha, he is perfect in conduct and resort, and seeing fear in the slightest faults, he trains himself by undertaking the framing precepts.

He has learned much, remembers what he has learned, and consolidates what he has learned.

Such teachings as are good in the beginning (ādikalyāṇā), good in the middle (majjhekalyāṇā), and good in the end (pariyosānakalyāṇā), with the right meaning (sātthaṃ) and phrasing (sabyañjanaṃ), which affirm a holy life that is utterly perfect (kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ) and pure (parisuddhaṃ), such teachings as these he has learned much of, remembered, consolidated them by word of mouth (vacasā paricitā), looked over them with the mind (manasānupekkhitā), and penetrated (them) well by (right) view (diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā).

He is content with robes (cīvara), alms-food (piṇḍapāta), lodging (senāsana), and medicine (bhesajja), and the requisites (parikkhāra) for the sick.

He obtains at will, without trouble or difficulty, the four jhānas that constitute the higher mind (ābhicetasikānaṃ) and provide a pleasant abiding (sukhavihārānaṃ) here and now (diṭṭhadhamma).

He wields the various kinds of supernormal power (anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ): having been one, he becomes many; having been many, he becomes one; he appears (āvibhāvaṃ) and vanishes (tirobhāvaṃ); he goes unhindered through a wall (tirokuṭṭaṃ), through an enclosure (tiropākāraṃ), through a mountain (tiropabbataṃ) as though through space (ākāse); he dives in and out of the earth (pathaviyāpi ummujjanimujjaṃ karoti) as though it were water (udake); he walks on water without sinking as though it were earth (pathaviyaṃ); seated cross-legged (pallaṅkena), he travels in space like a bird (pakkhī sakuṇo); with his hand he touches and strokes the moon (candimasūriye) and sun, so powerful and mighty; he exercises mastery over the body (kāyena vasaṃ vatteti) as far as the Brahma-world (brahmalokāpi).

84. The Blessed One, brahmin, did not praise every type of jhāna (meditation), nor did he condemn every type of jhāna (meditation).

What kind of jhāna (meditation) did the Blessed One not praise? Here, brahmin, someone abides with his mind obsessed by sensual lust (kāmarāga), a prey to sensual lust (kāmarāga), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen sensual lust (kāmarāga); he meditates, premeditates, out-meditates, and mismeditates, with sensual lust as an obstruction.

He abides with his mind obsessed by ill will (byāpāda), a prey to ill will (byāpāda), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen ill will (byāpāda); he meditates, premeditates, out-meditates, and mismeditates, with ill will (byāpāda) as an obstruction.

He abides with his mind obsessed by sloth and torpor (thinamiddha), a prey to sloth and torpor (thinamiddha), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen sloth and torpor (thinamiddha); he meditates, premeditates, out-meditates, and mismeditates, with sloth and torpor (thinamiddha) as an obstruction.

He abides with his mind obsessed by restlessness and remorse (uddhaccakukkucca), a prey to restlessness and remorse (uddhaccakukkucca), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen restlessness and remorse (uddhaccakukkucca); he meditates, premeditates, out-meditates, and mismeditates, with restlessness and remorse (uddhaccakukkucca) as an obstruction.

He abides with his mind obsessed by doubt (vicikicchā), a prey to doubt (vicikicchā), and he does not understand as it actually is the escape from arisen doubt (vicikicchā); he meditates, premeditates, out-meditates, and mismeditates, with doubt (vicikicchā) as an obstruction.

The Blessed One did not praise that kind of meditation.

What kind of jhāna (meditation) did the Blessed One praise? Here, brahmin, a bhikkhu (monk), quite secluded from sense pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters upon and abides in the first jhāna (meditation), which is accompanied by applied thought (vitakka) and sustained thought (vicāra), born of seclusion, filled with rapture (pīti) and pleasure (sukha).

With the stilling of applied and sustained thought (vitakka and vicāra), he enters upon and abides in the second jhāna (meditation)…the third jhāna (meditation)…he enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna (meditation).

The Blessed One praised that kind of meditation.”

“It seems, Master Ānanda, that Master Gotama censured that kind of jhāna (meditation) that should be censured and praised that kind of jhāna (meditation) that should be praised.

And now, Master Ānanda, we depart. We are busy and have much to do.”

“Now is the time, brahmin, to do as you think fit.”

Then the brahmin Vassakāra, the minister of Magadha, having delighted and rejoiced in the venerable Ānanda’s words, rose from his seat and departed.

Then, soon after he had left, the brahmin Gopaka Moggallāna said to the venerable Ānanda: “Master Ānanda has not yet answered what we asked him.”

“Did we not tell you, brahmin: ‘There is no single bhikkhu (monk), brahmin, who possesses in each and every way all those qualities that were possessed by the Blessed One, accomplished and fully enlightened.

For the Blessed One was the arouser of the unarisen path, the producer of the unproduced path, the declarer of the undeclared path; he was the knower of the path, the finder of the path, the one skilled in the path.

But his disciples now abide following that path and become possessed of it afterwards.’”

The Gopakamoggallāna Sutta, the eighth [sutta], is finished.
