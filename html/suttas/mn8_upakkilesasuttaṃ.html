Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Kosambi in Ghosita’s Park.

Now on that occasion the bhikkhus at Kosambi had taken to quarrelling and brawling and were deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers.

Then another bhikkhu approached the Blessed One; and after going and worshipping the Fortunate One, he stood on one side.

As he was standing there, he said to the Blessed One, “Venerable sir, the bhikkhus here at Kosambi have taken to quarrelling and brawling and are deep in disputes, stabbing each other with verbal daggers.

It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would go to those bhikkhus out of compassion.”

The Lord signified his acceptance by silence.

Then the Blessed One went to those bhikkhus and said to them: “Enough, bhikkhus, let there be no quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, or dispute.”

When this was said, another bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Let the Blessed One, the lord of the Dhamma, remain here! Let the Blessed One, untroubled, remain devoted to dwelling in the bliss of the Dhamma visible in this life! We are the ones who will be responsible for this quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, and dispute.”

For a second time the Blessed One said to the monks, “Enough, monks. Don’t quarrel. Don’t argue. Don’t make strife. Don’t dispute.”

For a second time that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Let the Blessed One, the lord of the Dhamma, remain here! Let the Blessed One, untroubled, remain devoted to dwelling in the bliss of the Dhamma visible in this life! We are the ones who will be responsible for this quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, and dispute.”

For a third time the Blessed One said: “Enough, bhikkhus, let there be no quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, or dispute.”

For a third time that bhikkhu said to the Blessed One: “Let the Blessed One, the lord of the Dhamma, remain here! Let the Blessed One, untroubled, remain devoted to dwelling in the bliss of the Dhamma visible in this life! We are the ones who will be responsible for this quarrelling, brawling, wrangling, and dispute.”

Then, early in the morning, the Blessed One dressed, and taking his bowl and outer robe, entered Kosambi for alms.

After walking for alms in Kosambi, after his meal, the Blessed One, returning from the alms round, having arranged his lodging, and taking his bowl and robe, standing right there, uttered these verses:

“The sound of the crowd is great; don't think that no one is foolish.

When the Sangha is breaking apart, they don't think there's anything worse.”

“Those with distorted minds only feign intelligence; they are eloquent in speech,

but they don't know the guide who has led them to the mouth's extent.”

“He abused me, he struck me, he overpowered me, he robbed me.”

Those who harbor such thoughts do not still their hatred.

“He abused me, he struck me, he overpowered me, he robbed me.”

Those who do not harbor such thoughts still their hatred.

“For hatred is never appeased by hatred in this world;

it is appeased by non-hatred. This is an eternal law.”

“Others do not realize that in this quarrel we are perishing;

those who do realize this settle their quarrels.”

“Though bone-cutters, murderers, horse and wealth plunderers,

marauders of kingdoms also achieve concord;

why should you not?”

“If one finds a prudent companion,

a fellow traveler, righteous and steadfast,

one should live with him joyfully and mindfully, overcoming all dangers.”

“If one does not find a prudent companion,

a fellow traveler, righteous and steadfast,

one should wander alone like a king renouncing his conquered kingdom, or like the elephant Mātaṅga in the forest.”
Better it is to wander alone; there is no companionship with fools. Let one wander alone and do no evil, unconcerned, like an elephant in the elephant forest (mātaṅgaraññeva nāgo).

236. Thus I have heard: the Discourse on Defilements ( upakkilesasuttaṃ).

There, "etadavocā," means neither from intention to divide, nor from lovableness, but it occurred to him: "These monks, having taken my word, will not desist; the Buddhas are compassionate for the welfare [of beings]. Assuredly, the Blessed One will relate a reason; having heard that, these [monks] will desist; then there will be a comfortable dwelling for them."

Therefore, he spoke this statement beginning, "Here, venerable sir."

In "mā bhaṇḍana," having taken the remainder of the pāṭha as "akattha", the meaning should be regarded thus: "mā bhaṇḍanaṃ akattha."

"aññataro" means that a certain monk desired the good of the Blessed One; this was his intention: "These monks are overwhelmed by anger, they do not heed the Teacher's word; may the Blessed One not weary himself admonishing these," therefore, he spoke thus.

"piṇḍāya pāvisī" means he not only entered for alms, but he also resolved, "By whatever person I have not been seen, may he see me."

Why did he resolve? For the taming of those monks.

Indeed, the Blessed One, having returned from the alms round at that time, having spoken the verses beginning, "Separate is the sound, communal is the people" and having gone to Kosambi, to the village of Bālakaloṇakāra, from there to Pācīnavaṁsadāya, from there, having gone to Pāḷileyyaka forest grove, being attended by the elephant Nāga of Pāḷileyyaka, he stayed for three months.

The residents of the city also, "The Teacher has gone to the monastery, let us go for the hearing of the Dharma," with fragrant flowers in their hands, having gone to the monastery, they asked, "Where, venerable sir, is the Teacher?"

"Where will you see the Teacher? The Teacher has come, thinking, 'I will make these monks harmonious', unable to make them harmonious, he departed."

"We are not able to bring the Teacher even by giving a hundred or a thousand, he has come of his own accord unasked, we, relying on these monks, have not obtained the Dharma talk in the Teacher's presence.

These have gone forth dedicating themselves to the Teacher, even in that, they have not become harmonious making harmony, whose else word will they do?

Enough, alms should not be given to these," in the entire city they imposed a penalty.

They, on the following day, having wandered for alms in the entire city, without obtaining even a ladleful of alms, returned to the monastery.

The lay followers also said to them again: "Until you appease the Teacher, the penalty will be the same for you."

They, "We will appease the Teacher," when the Blessed One had arrived in Sāvatthi, there they went.

The Teacher taught them the eighteen grounds for division. This is the traditional story outside the Pāḷi.

But he does not have [that] from the elders. Therefore, thinking that it is not appropriate to ask about that, he asks about preliminary illumination.

"We are conscious of illumination," means, we are conscious of preliminary illumination.

"And the sight of forms," means, with the divine eye, we are conscious of the sight of forms.

"And we do not penetrate that condition," means, we do not know that cause, by which the illumination and the sight of forms disappear for us.

"And that condition, Anuruddha, should be penetrated by you," means, that cause should be known by you.

"I too, indeed," means, Anuruddha, why do you not harass [yourselves]? I, too, with these eleven defilements, having harassed in the past, to show this teaching, he began.

"Doubt, indeed, to me," etc., to the great being, having increased the light, having seen various forms with the divine eye, "What is this?" doubt arose.

"Cessation of concentration," means, cessation of preliminary concentration.

"Illumination," means, preliminary illumination also disappeared, with the divine eye also, he did not see form.

"Non-attention," means, while seeing forms, doubt arises, now I will not attend to anything, thus non-attention arose.

That condition, Anuruddha, should be penetrated by you. I too, indeed.

Doubt, indeed, to me. Cessation of concentration. Illumination. Non-attention.

"Sloth and torpor," means, to one not attending to anything, sloth and torpor arose.

Sloth and torpor.

"Terrified state," means, having increased the light towards the Himalayas, he saw demons, ogres, pythons, etc., then to him, a terrified state arose.

Terrified state.

"Excitement," means, "Fear seen by me does not exist by nature when looking around. What is fear in the unseen?" to one thinking, a state of excitedness arose.

"At once," means, with a single application, he could see even five treasure pots.

Excitement. At once.

"Disgust," means, by me, energy was strongly exerted, therefore, excitement arose to me, thus he made energy weak, then bodily agitation, bodily disgust, bodily sloth arose.

Disgust.

"Excessive energy," means, while making my energy weak, disgust arose, thus again, while taking up energy, excessive energy arose.

"He might die," means, he might decease.

Excessive energy. He might die.

"Excessively sluggish energy," means, while taking up my energy, thus it happened, thus again, while making energy weak, excessively sluggish energy arose.

Excessively sluggish energy.

"Longing," means, having increased the light towards the world of the gods, while seeing the assembly of gods, craving arose.

Longing.

"Various perceptions," means, to me, while attending to a single kind of form, longing arose, a various kind of form, "I will attend in mind," thus, at one time, towards the world of the gods, at one time, towards the world of humans, having increased, while attending in mind to various forms, various perceptions arose.

Various perceptions.

"Excessive absorption," means, to me, while attending in mind to various kinds of forms, various perceptions arose, desirable or undesirable, or I will attend in mind only to a single kind, thus, while attending in mind, excessive absorption of forms arose.
