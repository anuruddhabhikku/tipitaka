122. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho āyasmato mālukyaputtassa māluṅkyaputtassa (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) rahogatassa paṭisallīnassa evaṃ cetaso parivitakko udapādi – ‘‘yānimāni diṭṭhigatāni bhagavatā abyākatāni ṭhapitāni paṭikkhittāni – ‘sassato loko’tipi, ‘asassato loko’tipi, ‘antavā loko’tipi, ‘anantavā loko’tipi, ‘taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’ntipi, ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’ntipi, ‘hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’tipi, ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’tipi, ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’tipi, ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’tipi – tāni me bhagavā na byākaroti. Yāni me bhagavā na byākaroti taṃ me na ruccati, taṃ me nakkhamati. Sohaṃ bhagavantaṃ upasaṅkamitvā etamatthaṃ pucchissāmi. Sace me bhagavā byākarissati – ‘sassato loko’ti vā ‘asassato loko’ti vā…pe… ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā – evāhaṃ bhagavati brahmacariyaṃ carissāmi; no ce me bhagavā byākarissati – ‘sassato loko’ti vā ‘asassato loko’ti vā…pe… ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā – evāhaṃ sikkhaṃ paccakkhāya hīnāyāvattissāmī’’ti.
122. Thus have I heard:

On one occasion the Blessed One was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Then, while the venerable Mālukyaputta was alone in meditation, the following thought arose in his mind: “These speculative views have been undeclared by the Blessed One, set aside and rejected by him, namely: ‘the world is eternal’ (śāśvato lokaḥ) and ‘the world is not eternal’ (aśāśvato lokaḥ); ‘the world is finite’ (antavā lokaḥ) and ‘the world is infinite’ (anantavā lokaḥ); ‘the soul is the same as the body’ and ‘the soul is one thing and the body another’; and ‘after death a Tathāgata exists’ (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā) and ‘after death a Tathāgata does not exist’ (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā) and ‘after death a Tathāgata both exists and does not exist’ and ‘after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ – these the Blessed One does not declare to me.

The Blessed One does not declare these to me, and I do not approve of and accept the fact that he does not declare these to me,

so I shall go to the Blessed One and ask him the meaning of this.

If he declares to me either ‘the world is eternal’ (śāśvato lokaḥ) or ‘the world is not eternal’ (aśāśvato lokaḥ)…or ‘after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist,’ then I will lead the holy life under him; if he does not declare these to me, then I will abandon the training and return to the low life.”
122.Evaṃme sutanti mālukyasuttaṃ. Tattha mālukyaputtassāti evaṃnāmakassa therassa. Ṭhapitāni paṭikkhittānīti diṭṭhigatāni nāma na byākātabbānīti evaṃ ṭhapitāni ceva paṭikkhittāni ca. Tathāgatoti satto. Taṃ me na ruccatīti taṃ abyākaraṇaṃ mayhaṃ na ruccati. Sikkhaṃpaccakkhāyāti sikkhaṃ paṭikkhipitvā.
122. Thus, "I have heard" is the Mālukya Sūtra.

There, "of Mālukyaputta" refers to a thera of such a name.

"Established and rejected" means that views are not to be explained as things established and rejected in this way.

"Tathāgata" means a being.

"That does not please me" means that non-explanation does not please me.

"Training," having rejected the training, means having rejected it.

123. Atha kho āyasmā mālukyaputto sāyanhasamayaṃ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā mālukyaputto bhagavantaṃ etadavoca –
Then, when it was evening, the venerable Mālunkyaputta rose from meditation and went to the Blessed One. After paying homage to the Blessed One he sat down at one side. As he was sitting there, the Venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One,

124. ‘‘Idha mayhaṃ, bhante, rahogatassa paṭisallīnassa evaṃ cetaso parivitakko udapādi – yānimāni diṭṭhigatāni bhagavatā abyākatāni ṭhapitāni paṭikkhittāni – ‘sassato loko’tipi, ‘asassato loko’tipi…pe… ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’tipi – tāni me bhagavā na byākaroti. Yāni me bhagavā na byākaroti taṃ me na ruccati, taṃ me nakkhamati. Sohaṃ bhagavantaṃ upasaṅkamitvā etamatthaṃ pucchissāmi. Sace me bhagavā byākarissati – ‘sassato loko’ti vā, ‘asassato loko’ti vā…pe… ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā – evāhaṃ bhagavati, brahmacariyaṃ carissāmi. No ce me bhagavā byākarissati – ‘sassato loko’ti vā, ‘asassato loko’ti vā…pe… ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā – evāhaṃ sikkhaṃ paccakkhāya hīnāyāvattissāmīti. Sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘sassato loko’ti, ‘sassato loko’ti me bhagavā byākarotu; sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘asassato loko’ti, ‘asassato loko’ti me bhagavā byākarotu. No ce bhagavā jānāti – ‘sassato loko’ti vā, ‘asassato loko’ti vā, ajānato kho pana apassato etadeva ujukaṃ hoti yadidaṃ – ‘na jānāmi, na passāmī’ti. Sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘antavā loko’ti, ‘anantavā loko’ti me bhagavā byākarotu; sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘anantavā loko’ti, ‘anantavā loko’ti me bhagavā byākarotu. No ce bhagavā jānāti – ‘antavā loko’ti vā, ‘anantavā loko’ti vā, ajānato kho pana apassato etadeva ujukaṃ hoti yadidaṃ – ‘na jānāmi, na passāmī’ti. Sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti, ‘taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti me bhagavā byākarotu; sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti, ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti me bhagavā byākarotu. No ce bhagavā jānāti – ‘taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti vā, ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti vā, ajānato kho pana apassato etadeva ujukaṃ hoti yadidaṃ – ‘na jānāmi, na passāmī’ti. Sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, ‘hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti me bhagavā byākarotu; sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti me bhagavā byākarotu. No ce bhagavā jānāti – ‘hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ajānato kho pana apassato etadeva ujukaṃ hoti yadidaṃ – ‘na jānāmi na passāmī’ti. Sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti me bhagavā byākarotu; sace bhagavā jānāti – ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti me bhagavā byākarotu. No ce bhagavā jānāti – ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ajānato kho pana apassato etadeva ujukaṃ hoti yadidaṃ – ‘na jānāmi, na passāmī’’’ti.
124. Here, venerable sir (bhante), while I was alone in meditation (rahogatassa paṭisallīnassa), the following thought arose in my mind (cetaso parivitakko udapādi): “These speculative views (diṭṭhigatāni) have been undeclared (abyākatāni) by the Blessed One (bhagavatā), set aside (ṭhapitāni) and rejected (paṭikkhittāni), namely: ‘the world is eternal’ (sassato loko) and ‘the world is not eternal’ (asassato loko)...pe... ‘after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)”—the Blessed One (bhagavā) does not declare these to me (tāni me bhagavā na byākaroti).

The Blessed One (bhagavā) does not declare these to me (yāni me bhagavā na byākaroti), and I do not approve of it (taṃ me na ruccati), I do not accept it (taṃ me nakkhamati).

So (sohaṃ), I shall go to the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ upasaṅkamitvā) and ask him the meaning of this (etamatthaṃ pucchissāmi).

If (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) declares to me (byākarissati): ‘the world is eternal’ (sassato loko) or ‘the world is not eternal’ (asassato loko)...pe... ‘after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)—then (eva) I will lead the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ carissāmi) under the Blessed One (bhagavati).

If (no ce) the Blessed One (bhagavā) does not declare to me (byākarissati): ‘the world is eternal’ (sassato loko) or ‘the world is not eternal’ (asassato loko)...pe... ‘after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)—then (eva) I will abandon the training (sikkhaṃ paccakkhāya) and return to the lower life (hīnāyāvattissāmīti).

If (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘the world is eternal’ (sassato loko), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘the world is eternal’ (sassato loko); if (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘the world is not eternal’ (asassato loko), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘the world is not eternal’ (asassato loko).

If (no ce) the Blessed One (bhagavā) does not know (jānāti): ‘the world is eternal’ (sassato loko) or ‘the world is not eternal’ (asassato loko), then it is straightforward (etadeva ujukaṃ hoti) for one who does not know (ajānato) and does not see (apassato) to say (yadidaṃ): ‘I do not know (na jānāmi), I do not see (na passāmī)’.

If (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘the world is finite’ (antavā loko) or ‘the world is infinite’ (anantavā loko), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘the world is finite’ (antavā loko) or ‘the world is infinite’ (anantavā loko).

If (no ce) the Blessed One (bhagavā) does not know (jānāti): ‘the world is finite’ (antavā loko) or ‘the world is infinite’ (anantavā loko), then it is straightforward (etadeva ujukaṃ hoti) for one who does not know (ajānato) and does not see (apassato) to say (yadidaṃ): ‘I do not know (na jānāmi), I do not see (na passāmī)’.

If (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘the soul is the same as the body’ (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīraṃ), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘the soul is the same as the body’ (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīraṃ); if (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘the soul is one thing and the body another’ (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīraṃ), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘the soul is one thing and the body another’ (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīraṃ).

If (no ce) the Blessed One (bhagavā) does not know (jānāti): ‘the soul is the same as the body’ (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīraṃ) or ‘the soul is one thing and the body another’ (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīraṃ), then it is straightforward (etadeva ujukaṃ hoti) for one who does not know (ajānato) and does not see (apassato) to say (yadidaṃ): ‘I do not know (na jānāmi), I do not see (na passāmī)’.

If (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘after death a Tathāgata exists’ (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘after death a Tathāgata exists’ (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā); if (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘after death a Tathāgata does not exist’ (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘after death a Tathāgata does not exist’ (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā).

If (no ce) the Blessed One (bhagavā) does not know (jānāti): ‘after death a Tathāgata exists’ (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā) or ‘after death a Tathāgata does not exist’ (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), then it is straightforward (etadeva ujukaṃ hoti) for one who does not know (ajānato) and does not see (apassato) to say (yadidaṃ): ‘I do not know (na jānāmi), I do not see (na passāmī)’.

If (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘after death a Tathāgata both exists and does not exist’ (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘after death a Tathāgata both exists and does not exist’ (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā); if (sace) the Blessed One (bhagavā) knows (jānāti): ‘after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), let the Blessed One (bhagavā) declare to me (byākarotu): ‘after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā).

If (no ce) the Blessed One (bhagavā) does not know (jānāti): ‘after death a Tathāgata both exists and does not exist’ (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā) or ‘after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), then it is straightforward (etadeva ujukaṃ hoti) for one who does not know (ajānato) and does not see (apassato) to say (yadidaṃ): ‘I do not know (na jānāmi), I do not see (na passāmī)’.

125. ‘‘Kiṃ nu kiṃ nu kho (syā. kaṃ. ka.) tāhaṃ, mālukyaputta, evaṃ avacaṃ – ‘ehi tvaṃ, mālukyaputta, mayi brahmacariyaṃ cara, ahaṃ te byākarissāmi – ‘sassato loko’ti vā, ‘asassato loko’ti vā, ‘antavā loko’ti vā, ‘anantavā loko’ti vā, ‘taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti vā, ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti vā, ‘hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Tvaṃ vā pana maṃ evaṃ avaca – ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavati brahmacariyaṃ carissāmi , bhagavā me byākarissati – ‘sassato loko’ti vā, ‘asassato loko’ti vā, ‘antavā loko’ti vā, ‘anantavā loko’ti vā, ‘taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti vā, ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti vā, ‘hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā’’ti? ‘‘No hetaṃ, bhante’’. ‘‘Iti kira, mālukyaputta, nevāhaṃ taṃ vadāmi – ehi tvaṃ, mālukyaputta, mayi brahmacariyaṃ cara, ahaṃ te byākarissāmi – ‘sassato loko’ti vā, ‘asassato loko’ti vā…pe… ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇāti vā’ti; napi kira maṃ tvaṃ vadesi – ahaṃ, bhante, bhagavati brahmacariyaṃ carissāmi, bhagavā me byākarissati – ‘sassato loko’ti vā ‘asassato loko’ti vā…pe… ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā’’ti. Evaṃ sante, moghapurisa, ko santo kaṃ paccācikkhasi?
“125. How then, Mālunkyaputta, did I ever say to you: ‘Come, Mālunkyaputta, lead the holy life under me, and I will declare to you: ‘The world is eternal’ (śāśvato loko’ti vā), or ‘The world is not eternal,’ (aśāśvato loko’ti vā) or ‘The world is finite,’ (antavā loko’ti vā) or ‘The world is infinite,’ (anantavā loko’ti vā) or ‘The soul is the same as the body,’ (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ śarīraṃ) or ‘The soul is one thing and the body another,’ (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ śarīraṃ) or ‘After death a Tathāgata exists,’ (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā) or ‘After death a Tathāgata does not exist,’ (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā) or ‘After death a Tathāgata both exists and does not exist,’ (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā) or ‘After death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā)?”

“No, Bhante.”

“Or did you ever tell me: ‘I will lead the holy life under the Blessed One, and the Blessed One will declare to me: ‘The world is eternal’ (śāśvato loko’ti vā), or ‘The world is not eternal,’ (aśāśvato loko’ti vā) or ‘The world is finite,’ (antavā loko’ti vā) or ‘The world is infinite,’ (anantavā loko’ti vā) or ‘The soul is the same as the body,’ (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ śarīraṃ) or ‘The soul is one thing and the body another,’ (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ śarīraṃ) or ‘After death a Tathāgata exists,’ (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā) or ‘After death a Tathāgata does not exist,’ (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā) or ‘After death a Tathāgata both exists and does not exist,’ (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā) or ‘After death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā)?”

“No, Bhante.”

“So it seems, Mālunkyaputta, that I do not say that you should come, Mālunkyaputta, lead the holy life under me, and I will declare to you: ‘The world is eternal’ (śāśvato loko’ti vā), or ‘The world is not eternal’ (aśāśvato loko’ti vā)...or...'After death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā). Nor do you say to me: ‘I, Bhante, will lead the holy life under the Blessed One, and the Blessed One will declare to me: ‘The world is eternal’ (śāśvato loko’ti vā), or ‘The world is not eternal’ (aśāśvato loko’ti vā)...or... ‘After death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā).”

Such being the case, you foolish man (moghapurisa), who are you, and what are you giving up (kaṃ paccācikkhasi)?
125.Ko santo kaṃ paccācikkhasīti yācako vā hi yācitakaṃ paccācikkheyya, yācitako vā yācakaṃ. Tvaṃ neva yācako na yācitako, so dāni tvaṃ ko santo kaṃ paccācikkhasīti attho.
Who, being, refuses whom? If a beggar refuses a borrower, or a borrower a beggar.
You are neither a beggar nor a borrower; so now, who are you, being, [that] you refuse whom? This is the meaning.

126. ‘‘Yo kho, mālukyaputta, evaṃ vadeyya – ‘na tāvāhaṃ bhagavati brahmacariyaṃ carissāmi yāva me bhagavā na byākarissati – ‘‘sassato loko’’ti vā, ‘‘asassato loko’’ti vā…pe… ‘‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’’ti vāti, abyākatameva taṃ, mālukyaputta, tathāgatena assa, atha so puggalo kālaṃ kareyya. Seyyathāpi, mālukyaputta, puriso sallena viddho assa savisena gāḷhapalepanena. Tassa mittāmaccā ñātisālohitā bhisakkaṃ sallakattaṃ upaṭṭhapeyyuṃ. Evameva kho, mālukyaputta, yo evaṃ vadeyya – ‘na tāvāhaṃ bhagavati brahmacariyaṃ carissāmi yāva me bhagavā na byākarissati – ‘‘sassato loko’’ti vā ‘‘asassato loko’’ti vā…pe… ‘‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’’ti vāti – abyākatameva taṃ, mālukyaputta, tathāgatena assa, atha so puggalo kālaṅkareyya.

"If someone should say thus, Mālukyaputta: 'I will not lead the holy life under the Blessed One until the Blessed One declares to me: "The world is eternal" or "The world is not eternal"... or "after death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist,"' that would still remain undeclared by the Tathāgata, Mālukyaputta, and meanwhile that person would die.

Suppose, Mālukyaputta, a man were wounded by an arrow thickly smeared with poison, and his friends and companions, his kinsmen and relatives, brought a surgeon to treat him.

The man would say: 'I will not let the surgeon pull out this arrow until I know whether the man who wounded me was a noble (kṣatriya) or a brāhmin (brāhmaṇa) or a merchant (vaiśya) or a worker (śūdra).' And he would say: 'I will not let the surgeon pull out this arrow until I know the name and clan of the man who wounded me,' or 'until I know whether the man who wounded me was tall or short or of middle height,' or 'until I know whether the man who wounded me was dark or brown or golden-skinned,' or 'until I know whether the man who wounded me lives in such and such a village or town or city.' And he would say: 'I will not let the surgeon pull out this arrow until I know the bow with which I was wounded, whether it was a cāpa or a kodaṇḍa.' And he would say: 'I will not let the surgeon pull out this arrow until I know the bowstring with which I was wounded, whether of akka or of saṇha, whether of nhāru or of maruva or of khīrapaṇṇin.' And he would say: 'I will not let the surgeon pull out this arrow until I know the arrow with which I was wounded, whether it was gaccha or ropima.' And he would say: 'I will not let the surgeon pull out this arrow until I know with whose feathers it was winged, whether of vulture (gijjha) or of heron (kaṅka) or of osprey (kulala) or of peacock (mora) or of sithilahanu.' And he would say: 'I will not let the surgeon pull out this arrow until I know with whose sinews it was bound, whether of cow (gava) or of buffalo (mahiṃsa) or of bherava or of semhāra.' And he would say: 'I will not let the surgeon pull out this arrow until I know the arrow with which I was wounded, whether it was a salla or a khurappa or a vekaṇḍa or a nārāca or a vacchadanta or a karavīrapatta.' That would still remain unknown to that man, Mālukyaputta, and meanwhile that man would die.

So too, Mālukyaputta, if anyone should say thus: ‘I will not lead the holy life under the Blessed One until the Blessed One declares to me: "The world is eternal" or "The world is not eternal"... or "a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist after death,"' that would still remain undeclared by the Tathāgata, Mālukyaputta, and meanwhile that person would die."

127. ‘‘‘Sassato loko’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti , evaṃ ‘no asassato loko’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evampi ‘no sassato loko’ti vā, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati, ‘asassato loko’ti vā diṭṭhiyā sati attheva jāti, atthi jarā, atthi maraṇaṃ, santi sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā; yesāhaṃ diṭṭheva dhamme nighātaṃ paññapemi . ‘Antavā loko’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evaṃ ‘no anantavā loko’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evampi ‘no antavā loko’ti vā, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati, ‘anantavā loko’ti vā diṭṭhiyā sati attheva jāti, atthi jarā, atthi maraṇaṃ, santi sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā; yesāhaṃ diṭṭheva dhamme nighātaṃ paññapemi. ‘Taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evaṃ ‘no aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evampi ‘no taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti vā, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati, ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti vā diṭṭhiyā sati attheva jāti…pe… nighātaṃ paññapemi. ‘Hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evaṃ ‘no na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evampi ‘no hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati, ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā diṭṭhiyā sati attheva jāti…pe… yesāhaṃ diṭṭheva dhamme nighātaṃ paññapemi. ‘Hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evaṃ ‘no neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati brahmacariyavāso abhavissāti, evampi ‘no hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti, mālukyaputta, diṭṭhiyā sati, ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti vā diṭṭhiyā sati attheva jāti…pe… yesāhaṃ diṭṭheva dhamme nighātaṃ paññapemi.
127. “Mālukyaputta, if there is the view ‘the world is eternal’ (śāśvataḥ lokaḥ), the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived; and if there is the view ‘the world is not eternal’ (aśāśvataḥ lokaḥ), the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived. Whether there is the view ‘the world is eternal’ or the view ‘the world is not eternal,’ there is birth (jāti), there is ageing (jarā), there is death (maraṇaṃ), there are sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair, the destruction of which I prescribe here and now.

‘The world is finite’ (antavā lokaḥ), Mālukyaputta, if there is the view, the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived; and if there is the view ‘the world is not finite’ (anantavā lokaḥ), Mālukyaputta, if there is the view, the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived. Whether there is the view ‘the world is finite’ or the view ‘the world is not finite,’ there is birth (jāti), there is ageing (jarā), there is death (maraṇaṃ), there are sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair, the destruction of which I prescribe here and now.

'The soul is the same as the body' (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ śarīra), Mālukyaputta, if there is the view, the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived; and if there is the view 'the soul is one thing and the body another' (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ śarīra), Mālukyaputta, if there is the view, the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived. Whether there is the view 'the soul is the same as the body' or the view 'the soul is one thing and the body another,' there is birth (jāti)…pe… the destruction of which I prescribe here and now.

‘After death a tathāgata exists’ (tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), Mālukyaputta, if there is the view, the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived; and if there is the view ‘after death a tathāgata does not exist’ (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), Mālukyaputta, if there is the view, the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived. Whether there is the view ‘after death a tathāgata exists’ or the view ‘after death a tathāgata does not exist,’ there is birth (jāti)…pe… the destruction of which I prescribe here and now.

If there is the view ‘after death a tathāgata both exists and does not exist’ (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), Mālukyaputta, if there is the view, the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived; and if there is the view ‘after death a tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist’ (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā), Mālukyaputta, if there is the view, the holy life (brahmacariyavāsa) cannot be lived. Whether there is the view ‘after death a tathāgata both exists and does not exist’ or the view ‘after death a tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist,’ there is birth (jāti)…pe… the destruction of which I prescribe here and now.

127. attheva jātī means that with this view, there is no living of the holy life, but there is indeed birth.

Similarly, it indicates aging, death, and so on.

yesāha means "of whom am I?"

nighāta means injury and destruction ( upaghātaṃ vināsaṃ).

The meaning is: "My disciples, having become disgusted with these things, will attain nirvāṇa right here ( nibbānaṃ pāpuṇanti)."

128. ‘‘Tasmātiha, mālukyaputta, abyākatañca me abyākatato dhāretha; byākatañca me byākatato dhāretha. Kiñca, mālukyaputta, mayā abyākataṃ? ‘Sassato loko’ti mālukyaputta, mayā abyākataṃ; ‘asassato loko’ti – mayā abyākataṃ; ‘antavā loko’ti – mayā abyākataṃ; ‘anantavā loko’ti – mayā abyākataṃ; ‘taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ sarīra’nti – mayā abyākataṃ; ‘aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ sarīra’nti – mayā abyākataṃ; ‘hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti – mayā abyākataṃ; ‘na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti – mayā abyākataṃ; ‘hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti – mayā abyākataṃ; ‘neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ti – mayā abyākataṃ. Kasmā cetaṃ, mālukyaputta, mayā abyākataṃ? Na hetaṃ, mālukyaputta, atthasaṃhitaṃ na ādibrahmacariyakaṃ na netaṃ (sī.) nibbidāya na virāgāya na nirodhāya na upasamāya na abhiññāya na sambodhāya na nibbānāya saṃvattati. Tasmā taṃ mayā abyākataṃ. Kiñca, mālukyaputta, mayā byākataṃ? ‘Idaṃ dukkha’nti, mālukyaputta, mayā byākataṃ; ‘ayaṃ dukkhasamudayo’ti – mayā byākataṃ; ‘ayaṃ dukkhanirodho’ti – mayā byākataṃ; ‘ayaṃ dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā’ti – mayā byākataṃ. Kasmā cetaṃ, mālukyaputta, mayā byākataṃ? Etañhi, mālukyaputta, atthasaṃhitaṃ etaṃ ādibrahmacariyakaṃ nibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattati. Tasmā taṃ mayā byākataṃ. Tasmātiha, mālukyaputta , abyākatañca me abyākatato dhāretha; byākatañca me byākatato dhārethā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā mālukyaputto bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti. Cūḷamālukyasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ tatiyaṃ.
“Therefore, Mālunkyaputta, remember what I have left undeclared as undeclared, and remember what I have declared as declared.

Furthermore, Mālunkyaputta, what have I left undeclared? ‘The world is eternal (śāśvato lokaḥ)’— I have left undeclared. ‘The world is not eternal (aśāśvato lokaḥ)’— I have left undeclared. ‘The world is finite (antavā loko)’— I have left undeclared. ‘The world is infinite (anantavā loko)’— I have left undeclared. ‘The soul is the same as the body (taṃ jīvaṃ taṃ śarīraṃ)’— I have left undeclared. ‘The soul is one thing and the body another (aññaṃ jīvaṃ aññaṃ śarīraṃ)’— I have left undeclared. ‘After death a Tathāgata exists (hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)’— I have left undeclared. ‘After death a Tathāgata does not exist (na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)’— I have left undeclared. ‘After death a Tathāgata both exists and does not exist (hoti ca na ca hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)’— I have left undeclared. ‘After death a Tathāgata neither exists nor does not exist (neva hoti na na hoti tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā)’— I have left undeclared.

Why have I left that undeclared, Mālunkyaputta? Because it is unbeneficial, Mālunkyaputta, it does not belong to the fundamentals of the holy life, it does not lead to disenchantment (nirbidā), to dispassion (virāga), to cessation (nirodha), to peace (upaśama), to direct knowledge (abhijñā), to enlightenment (saṃbodha), to Nirvāṇa (nirbāṇa). That is why I have left it undeclared.

Furthermore, Mālunkyaputta, what have I declared? ‘This is suffering (idaṃ duḥkhaṃ)’— I have declared. ‘This is the origin of suffering (ayaṃ duḥkhasamudayo)’— I have declared. ‘This is the cessation of suffering (ayaṃ duḥkhanirodho)’— I have declared. ‘This is the path leading to the cessation of suffering (ayaṃ duḥkhanirodhagāminī pratipadā)’— I have declared.

Why have I declared that, Mālunkyaputta? Because it is beneficial, Mālunkyaputta, it belongs to the fundamentals of the holy life, it leads to disenchantment (nirbidā), to dispassion (virāga), to cessation (nirodha), to peace (upaśama), to direct knowledge (abhijñā), to enlightenment (saṃbodha), to Nirvāṇa (nirbāṇa). That is why I have declared it.

Therefore, Mālunkyaputta, remember what I have left undeclared as undeclared, and remember what I have declared as declared.”

This is what the Blessed One said. The venerable Mālunkyaputta was satisfied and delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

The Cūḷamālunkya-sūtra, the third [sūtra], is finished.

Na ādibrahmacariyakanti brahmacariyassa ādimattampi pubbabhāgasīlamattampi na hoti. Na nibbidāyātiādīsu vaṭṭe nibbindanatthāya vā virajjhanatthāya vā vaṭṭanirodhāya vā rāgādivūpasamanatthāya vā abhiññeyye dhamme abhijānanatthāya vā catumaggasaṅkhātasambodhatthāya vā asaṅkhatanibbānasacchikiriyatthāya vā na hoti. Etaṃ hīti etaṃ catusaccabyākaraṇaṃ. Ādibrahmacariyakanti brahmacariyassa ādibhūtaṃ pubbapadaṭṭhānaṃ. Sesaṃ vuttapaṭivipakkhanayena veditabbaṃ. Imampi desanaṃ bhagavā neyyapuggalavasena niṭṭhāpesīti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Cūḷamālukyasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
128. Therefore, that means: because this is unrevealed, but the Four Noble Truths are revealed by me, therefore it means that.

"Not indeed, Mālukyaputta, is this connected with the goal," meaning this is either a matter of (wrong) view, or this is a grammar, or it is based on a cause, it is not.

"Not the beginning of the holy life," meaning there is not even the slightest beginning of the holy life, nor even just a preliminary virtue.

"Not for disenchantment," etc., meaning in the cycle [of rebirth], it is not for the purpose of disenchantment, or for the purpose of detachment, or for the cessation of the cycle, or for the appeasement of passions and so forth, or for thoroughly knowing knowable things, or for the sake of the enlightenment consisting of the four paths, or for the sake of realizing the unconditioned nirvāṇa.

"This indeed," means this is the explanation of the Four Noble Truths.

"The beginning of the holy life," means it is the first element, the preliminary foundation of the holy life.

The remainder should be understood by the method of the opposite of what has been said.

The Blessed One brought even this teaching to a conclusion in accordance with the disposition of one who needs guidance.

The commentary to the Cūḷamālukya Sūtra in the Papañcasūdanī, commentary to the Majjhimanikāya, is finished.
