<p n="176" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">176</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0226"/><pb ed="M" n="3.0147"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0148"/><pb ed="P" n="3.0104"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde. Atha kho āyasmā ānando sāyanhasamayaṃ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘ekamidaṃ, bhante, samayaṃ bhagavā sakkesu viharati nagarakaṃ nāma sakyānaṃ nigamo. Tattha me, bhante, bhagavato sammukhā sutaṃ, sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ – ‘suññatāvihārenāhaṃ, ānanda, etarahi bahulaṃ viharāmī’ti. Kacci metaṃ, bhante, sussutaṃ suggahitaṃ sumanasikataṃ sūpadhārita’’nti? ‘‘Taggha te etaṃ, ānanda, sussutaṃ suggahitaṃ sumanasikataṃ sūpadhāritaṃ. Pubbepāhaṃ <note>pubbecāhaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note>, ānanda, etarahipi <note>etarahi ca (sabbattha)</note> suññatāvihārena bahulaṃ viharāmi. Seyyathāpi, ānanda, ayaṃ migāramātupāsādo suñño hatthigavassavaḷavena, suñño jātarūparajatena, suñño itthipurisasannipātena atthi cevidaṃ asuññataṃ yadidaṃ – bhikkhusaṅghaṃ paṭicca ekattaṃ; evameva kho, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā gāmasaññaṃ, amanasikaritvā manussasaññaṃ, araññasaññaṃ paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0227"/>. Tassa araññasaññāya cittaṃ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati. So evaṃ pajānāti – ‘ye assu darathā gāmasaññaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā manussasaññaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṃ darathamattā yadidaṃ – araññasaññaṃ paṭicca ekatta’nti. So ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ gāmasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ manussasaññāyā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṃ asuññataṃ yadidaṃ – araññasaññaṃ paṭicca ekatta’nti. Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṃ suññaṃ samanupassati, yaṃ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0105"/> hoti taṃ ‘santamidaṃ <pb ed="V" n="3.0149"/> atthī’’’ti pajānāti. Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā parisuddhā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.

Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvatthī in the Eastern Park in Migāramātā’s mansion.

Then Ven. Ānanda, emerging from his seclusion in the late afternoon, went to the Blessed One and, on arrival, having bowed down to him, sat to one side.

Seated to one side, Ven. Ānanda said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir, on one occasion the Blessed One was living in the Sakyan country at a town of the Sakyans named Nagaraka.

There, venerable sir, I heard and learned this from the Blessed One’s own lips: ‘Now, Ananda, I often abide in voidness (śūnyatāvihāra).’

Bhante, did I hear that correctly from the Blessed One, grasp it correctly, attend to it correctly, remember it correctly?"

"Indeed, Ananda, you heard that correctly, grasped it correctly, attended to it correctly, remembered it correctly.

Before, Ananda, and now, I often abide in voidness.

Ananda, just as this Palace of Migara’s Mother is void of elephants, cattle, horses, and mares, void of gold and silver, void of the assembly of men and women, and there is present only this non-voidness, namely, the singleness dependent on the Sangha of bhikkhus; so too, a bhikkhu - not attending to the perception of village, not attending to the perception of people -attends to the singleness dependent on the perception of forest.

His mind enters into that perception of forest and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.

He understands thus: ‘Whatever disturbances there might be dependent on the perception of village, those are not present here; whatever disturbances there might be dependent on the perception of people, those are not present here. There is present only this amount of disturbance, namely, the singleness dependent on the perception of forest.’

He understands: ‘This field of perception is void of the perception of village; this field of perception is void of the perception of people. There is present only this non-voidness, namely, the singleness dependent on the perception of forest.’

Thus he regards it as void of what is not there, but as to what remains there he understands that which is present thus: ‘This is present.’

Thus, Ananda, this is his genuine, undistorted, pure descent into voidness (śūnyatāvakranti).

“Again, Ānanda, a bhikkhu—not attending to the perception of people (manussasaññaṃ), not attending to the perception of forest (araññasaññaṃ)—attends to the singleness dependent on the perception of earth (pathavīsaññaṃ).

His mind enters into that perception of earth (pathavīsaññāya) and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.

Just as a bull’s hide becomes free from folds when fully stretched with a hundred pegs; so too, Ānanda, a bhikkhu—not attending to any of the ridges and hollows of this earth, to the rivers and ravines, the tracts of stumps and thorns, the mountains and uneven places—attends to the singleness dependent on the perception of earth (pathavīsaññaṃ).

His mind enters into that perception of earth (pathavīsaññāya) and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.

He understands thus: ‘Whatever disturbances there might be dependent on the perception of people (manussasaññaṃ), those are not present here; whatever disturbances there might be dependent on the perception of forest (araññasaññaṃ), those are not present here. There is present only this amount of disturbance, namely, the singleness dependent on the perception of earth (pathavīsaññaṃ).’

He understands: ‘This field of perception is void of the perception of people (manussasaññāyā); this field of perception is void of the perception of forest (araññasaññāyā). There is present only this non-voidness, namely, the singleness dependent on the perception of earth (pathavīsaññaṃ).’

Thus he regards it as void of what is not there, but as to what remains there he understands that which is present thus: ‘This is present.’

Thus, Ānanda, this is his genuine, undistorted, pure descent into voidness (śūnyatā).

His mind enters into that signless concentration of mind (animitte cetosamādhimhi) and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.

He understands thus: ‘Whatever disturbances (darathā) there might be dependent on the perception of the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana-saññāṃ), those are not present here; whatever disturbances (darathā) there might be dependent on the perception of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana-saññaṃ), those are not present here. There is present only this amount of disturbance (darathamattā), namely, that connected with the six bases (saḷāyatanikaṃ) that are dependent on this body (imameva kāyaṃ) and conditioned by life (jīvitapaccayā)’.

He understands: ‘This field of perception (saññāgataṃ) is void (suññamidaṃ) of the perception of the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatanasaññāyā); this field of perception (saññāgataṃ) is void (suññamidaṃ) of the perception of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññāyā). There is present only this non-voidness (asuññataṃ), namely, that connected with the six bases (saḷāyatanikaṃ) that are dependent on this body (imameva kāyaṃ) and conditioned by life (jīvitapaccayā)’.

Thus he regards it as void (suññaṃ) of what is not there (tattha na hoti), but as to what remains there (tattha avasiṭṭhaṃ hoti) he understands that which is present thus: ‘This is present (santamidaṃ atthī)’.

Thus, Ānanda, this is his genuine (yathābhuccā), undistorted (avipallatthā), pure (parisuddhā) descent into voidness (suññatāvakkanti).
183
<p n="183" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">183</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, ānanda, bhikkhu amanasikaritvā ākiñcaññāyatanasaññaṃ, amanasikaritvā nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññaṃ, animittaṃ cetosamādhiṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0232"/> paṭicca manasi karoti ekattaṃ. Tassa animitte cetosamādhimhi cittaṃ pakkhandati pasīdati santiṭṭhati adhimuccati. So evaṃ pajānāti – ‘ayampi kho animitto cetosamādhi abhisaṅkhato abhisañcetayito’. ‘Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. Tassa evaṃ jānato evaṃ passato kāmāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccati, bhavāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccati, avijjāsavāpi cittaṃ vimuccati. Vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāti. So evaṃ pajānāti – ‘ye assu darathā <pb ed="V" n="3.0152"/> kāmāsavaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā bhavāsavaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, ye assu darathā avijjāsavaṃ paṭicca tedha na santi, atthi cevāyaṃ darathamattā yadidaṃ – imameva kāyaṃ paṭicca saḷāyatanikaṃ <pb ed="M" n="3.0151"/> jīvitapaccayā’ti. So ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ kāmāsavenā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ bhavāsavenā’ti pajānāti, ‘suññamidaṃ saññāgataṃ avijjāsavenā’ti pajānāti, ‘atthi cevidaṃ asuññataṃ yadidaṃ – imameva kāyaṃ paṭicca saḷāyatanikaṃ jīvitapaccayā’ti. Iti yañhi kho tattha na hoti tena taṃ suññaṃ samanupassati, yaṃ pana tattha avasiṭṭhaṃ hoti taṃ ‘santamidaṃ atthī’ti pajānāti. Evampissa esā, ānanda, yathābhuccā avipallatthā <pb ed="P" n="3.0109"/> parisuddhā paramānuttarā suññatāvakkanti bhavati.

“Again, Ānanda, a monk, not attending to the perception of the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana-saññā), not attending to the perception of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana-saññā), attends to singleness dependent on the signless concentration of mind (animittaṃ cetosamādhiṃ).

His mind enters into that signless concentration of mind (animitte cetosamādhimhi) and acquires confidence, steadiness, and decision.

He understands thus: ‘This signless concentration of mind (animitto cetosamādhi) is conditioned and volitionally produced.’

‘But whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent, subject to cessation (nirodhadhamma)’ he understands.

When he knows and sees thus, his mind is liberated from the taint of sensual desire (kāmāsava), from the taint of existence (bhavāsava), and from taint of ignorance (avijjāsava).

When it is liberated, there comes the knowledge: ‘It’s liberated.’ (vimuttasmiṃ vimuttamiti ñāṇaṃ hoti).

‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the spiritual life (brahmacariyaṃ) has been lived, what had to be done (karaṇīyaṃ) has been done, there is no more coming back to any state of being (itthattāya)’ he understands.

He understands thus: ‘Whatever disturbances (darathā) there might be dependent on the taint of sensual desire (kāmāsavaṃ), those are not present here; whatever disturbances (darathā) there might be dependent on the taint of being (bhavāsavaṃ), those are not present here; whatever disturbances (darathā) there might be dependent on the taint of ignorance (avijjāsavaṃ), those are not present here. There is present only this amount of disturbance, namely, that connected with the six bases (saḷāyatanikaṃ) that are dependent on this body (kāyaṃ) and conditioned by life (jīvitapaccayā)’.

He understands: ‘This field of perception is void of the taint of sensual desire (kāmāsavena); this field of perception is void of the taint of being (bhavāsavena); this field of perception is void of the taint of ignorance (avijjāsavena). There is present only this non-voidness, namely, that connected with the six bases (saḷāyatanikaṃ) that are dependent on this body (kāyaṃ) and conditioned by life (jīvitapaccayā)’.

Thus he regards it as void of what is not there, but as to what remains there he understands that which is present thus: ‘This is present.’

Thus, Ānanda, this is his purified, supreme, unsurpassed, undeceiving turning towards emptiness (suññatāvakkanti).

<p n="184" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">184</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Yepi hi keci, ānanda, atītamaddhānaṃ samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā <pb ed="T" n="3.0233"/> vā parisuddhaṃ paramānuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja vihariṃsu, sabbe te imaṃyeva parisuddhaṃ paramānuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja vihariṃsu. Yepi <note>ye (sī. pī.)</note> hi keci, ānanda, anāgatamaddhānaṃ samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā parisuddhaṃ paramānuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja viharissanti, sabbe te imaṃyeva parisuddhaṃ paramānuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja viharissanti. Yepi <note>ye (sī. pī.)</note> hi keci, ānanda, etarahi samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā parisuddhaṃ paramānuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja viharanti, sabbe te imaṃyeva parisuddhaṃ paramānuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja viharanti. Tasmātiha, ānanda, ‘parisuddhaṃ paramānuttaraṃ suññataṃ upasampajja viharissāmā’ti <note>viharissāmīti (pī. ka.)</note> – evañhi vo <note>te (ka.)</note>, ānanda, sikkhitabba’’nti.

<p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamano āyasmā ānando bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti.

<p rend="centre">Cūḷasuññatasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.

184.
“Ānanda, whatever recluses (śramaṇa) and priests (brāhmaṇa) in the past entered upon and abided in pure (parisuddhaṃ), supreme (paramānuttaraṃ), unsurpassed voidness (śūnyatā), all entered upon and abided in this same pure, supreme, unsurpassed voidness.

Ānanda, whatever recluses and brāhmaṇa priests in the future will enter upon and abide in pure (parisuddhaṃ), supreme (paramānuttaraṃ), unsurpassed voidness (śūnyatā), all will enter upon and abide in this same pure, supreme, unsurpassed voidness.

Ānanda, whatever recluses and brāhmaṇa priests in the present enter upon and abide in pure (parisuddhaṃ), supreme (paramānuttaraṃ), unsurpassed voidness (śūnyatā), all enter upon and abide in this same pure, supreme, unsurpassed voidness.

Therefore, Ānanda, ‘We will enter upon and abide in pure (parisuddhaṃ), supreme (paramānuttaraṃ), unsurpassed voidness (śūnyatā)’ – thus, Ānanda, should you train yourselves.”

The Blessed One (bhagavān) spoke thus. The venerable Ānanda, being pleased, rejoiced in the Exalted One’s words (bhāsitaṃ abhinandīti).

The Cūḷasuññatasuttaṃ is finished, the first.
