199. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sakkesu viharati kapilavatthusmiṃ nigrodhārāme. Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya kapilavatthuṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi. Kapilavatthusmiṃ piṇḍāya caritvā pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto yena mahāvanaṃ tenupasaṅkami divāvihārāya. Mahāvanaṃ ajjhogāhetvā beluvalaṭṭhikāya mūle divāvihāraṃ nisīdi. Daṇḍapāṇipi kho sakko jaṅghāvihāraṃ jaṅghavihāraṃ (ka.) anucaṅkamamāno anuvicaramāno yena mahāvanaṃ tenupasaṅkami. Mahāvanaṃ ajjhogāhetvā yena beluvalaṭṭhikā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṃ sammodi. Sammodanīyaṃ kathaṃ sāraṇīyaṃ vītisāretvā daṇḍamolubbha ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi. Ekamantaṃ ṭhito kho daṇḍapāṇi sakko bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kiṃvādī samaṇo kimakkhāyī’’ti? ‘‘Yathāvādī kho, āvuso, sadevake loke samārake sabrahmake sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya na kenaci loke viggayha tiṭṭhati, yathā ca pana kāmehi visaṃyuttaṃ viharantaṃ taṃ brāhmaṇaṃ akathaṃkathiṃ chinnakukkuccaṃ bhavābhave vītataṇhaṃ saññā nānusenti – evaṃvādī kho ahaṃ, āvuso, evamakkhāyī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ vutte daṇḍapāṇi sakko sīsaṃ okampetvā , jivhaṃ nillāḷetvā, tivisākhaṃ nalāṭikaṃ nalāṭe vuṭṭhāpetvā daṇḍamolubbha pakkāmi.
Thus I have heard.

On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the Sakyan country at Kapilavatthu in Nigrodha’s Park. Then, when it was morning, the Blessed One dressed, and taking his bowl and outer robe, went into Kapilavatthu for alms. When he had wandered for alms in Kapilavatthu and had returned from his almsround, after his meal he went to the Great Wood for the day’s abiding. And entering the Great Wood, sat down at the root of a beluva sapling for the day’s abiding.

Dandapani the Sakyan, while walking and wandering for exercise (jaṅghāvihāraṃ), also went to the Great Wood. and when he had entered the Great Wood, he went to the beluva sapling where the Blessed One was and exchanged greetings with him. When this courteous and amiable talk was finished, he stood at one side leaning on his stick (daṇḍamolubbha ekamantaṃ aṭṭhāsi).

Standing to one side, Dandapani the Sakyan said to the Blessed One: “What doctrine does this śramaṇa profess? What does he proclaim?”

“Friend, I assert and proclaim such a teaching that one does not quarrel with anyone in the world with its gods (sadevake loke), its Maras (samārake), and its Brahmas (sabrahmake), in this generation with its recluses and brāhmaṇas (sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya), its princes and its people; such a teaching that perceptions no more underlie that brāhmaṇa who abides detached from sensual pleasures (kāmehi visaṃyuttaṃ), without perplexity (akathaṃkathiṃ), shorn of worry (chinnakukkuccaṃ), free from craving for any kind of being (bhavābhave vītataṇhaṃ).”

When this was said, Dandapani the Sakyan shook his head, wagged his tongue, and raised his eyebrows until his forehead was puckered in three lines. Then he departed, leaning on his stick.

Thus, "me suta " means the madhupiṇḍikasutta.

There, "mahāvana" means a jātivana that is connected to the Himalayas, not like the vesāliyaṃ ropitāropitamissakaṃ.

"divāvihārāyā" means for the sake of rest by day.

"beluvalaṭṭhikāyā" means of a young beluvarukkha.

"daṇḍapāṇī" means not one who has a stick in [his] hand due to the weakness of old age.

Indeed, this youth, standing at an early age, wanders around holding a golden staff because of [his] interest in sticks, therefore, he is called "daṇḍapāṇī."

"jaṅghāvihāra" means jaṅghācāraṃ for the sake of dispelling the weariness of the shanks.

"anucaṅkamamāno anuvicaramāno" means wandering here and there for the sake of seeing gardens, seeing forests, seeing mountains, and so on.

It seems that this deity sometimes leaves by chance and wanders in this way.

"daṇḍamolubbhā" means having leaned on a stick, like a cowherd boy, having placed the stick in front, having established two hands on the top of the stick, having pressed the back of the hand with the chin, he stood on one side.

Then, when it was evening, the Blessed One arose from seclusion and went to Nigrodha's Park; having arrived, he sat down on a seat that was prepared.

Seated, the Blessed One addressed the monks, "Here, monks, in the morning, having dressed, taking my bowl and robe, I entered Kapilavatthu for alms. Having wandered for alms in Kapilavatthu, and having returned from the alms round, after my meal, I went to the Great Wood for the day's abiding. Having entered the Great Wood, he went to the beluva sapling where I was; having arrived, he exchanged greetings with me. "When this was said, monks, Daṇḍapāṇi the Śākya shook his head, wagged his tongue, raised his triple-lined brow on his forehead, and departed, leaning on his stick."

200. "What is he saying?" means, "What is his view?"

"What does he declare?" means, "What does he say?"

This king, without venerating the Blessed One, only makes a slight greeting and asks a question.

And he does not ask out of a desire to know, but he asks disrespectfully.

Why? Because he is a partisan of Devadatta, it is said.

Devadatta breaks with the Tathāgata when he comes into his presence.

He says thus: "The samaṇa Gotama is an enemy to our family, he does not desire the growth of our family.

My sister was enjoying the wealth of a wheel-turning monarch, but having abandoned that, she left home, saying, 'I will be destroyed,' and became a renunciate.

My nephew, knowing [he was] the seed of a wheel-turning monarch, being displeased with the prosperity of our family, made him renounce, saying, 'He will be destroyed,' even in his early youth.

But I, unable to live without him, followed him into homelessness.

Even though I have gone forth, from the day I went forth, he does not look at me with upright eyes.

Even while speaking in the midst of an assembly, he speaks saying, 'Devadatta is destined for the lower realms, striking with a great axe', and so forth."

Thus, this king, too, is split off by Devadatta; therefore, he acted thus.

Then, the Blessed One [knew that] just as this king is not able to speak when asked a question by me, and just as he does not understand the meaning of what is spoken, [so] I will speak about him appropriately, saying, "He is one who speaks the truth," and so on.

He is one who speaks the truth: There, "He does not stand disputing with anyone in the world" means he does not engage in quarrelsome talk or dispute with anyone in the world.

For the Tathāgata does not dispute with the world; but the world disputes with the Tathāgata. When it is said, "Impermanent," he speaks, saying, "Permanent." Suffering, not-self, impure, when it is said, he speaks, saying, "Pure," he disputes.

Therefore, he said, "Monks, I do not dispute with the world, but the world disputes with me. Thus, monks, one who speaks the Dharma does not dispute with anyone in the world, but it is the one who speaks non-Dharma who disputes" (Saṃ. Ni. 3.94).

"As" means by whatever reason.

"Desiring sense pleasures" means desiring both the objects of sense pleasure and the defilements of sense pleasure. (vatthukāmehipi kilesakāmehipi)

"That brāhmaṇa" means that brāhmaṇa whose āsavas are destroyed ( khīṇāsava).

"Akathaṃkathi" means without doubt ( nibbicikicchaṃ).

"Chinnakukkucca" means because remorseful worry and worry about hands and feet are cut off, [he is one] whose worry is cut off (vippaṭisārakukkuccassa ceva hatthapādakukkuccassa ca chinnattā chinnakukkuccaṃ).

"Bhavābhave" means again and again in existence, or in inferior or superior existence, for a superior existence that has attained growth is called non-existence.

"Saññā" means defiled perception (kilesasaññā).

Or, here, it is said by the name of defilements themselves. Therefore, for whatever reason the defilements of perception do not follow that one who dwells detached from sense pleasures, who is a khīṇāsavabrāhmaṇa who refrains from leading others astray in the world, that reason I will speak, this is the meaning here.

Thus, the Blessed One reveals his state of being one whose āsavas are destroyed (khīṇāsava).

"Nillāḷetvā" means having brought out, having played with (nīharitvā kīḷāpetvā).

"Tivisākha" means three-branched (tisākhaṃ).

"Nalāṭika" means showing three lines on the forehead in the form of broken wrinkles, the meaning is having established the broken wrinkles.

"Daṇḍamolubbhā" means having oppressed the stick (daṇḍaṃ uppīḷetvā).
The reading is also "daṇḍamālubbhā," meaning having taken [it], he departed.

He does not stand quarreling with anyone in the world. Just as with desires, that brāhmaṇa, who is without questions, who has cut off worry, in being and non-being, having discarded perception, three-pronged, with a forehead plate, daṇḍamolubbhā.

201. Evaṃ vutte aññataro bhikkhu bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kiṃvādī pana, bhante, bhagavā sadevake loke samārake sabrahmake sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya na kenaci loke viggayha tiṭṭhati? Kathañca pana, bhante, bhagavantaṃ kāmehi visaṃyuttaṃ viharantaṃ taṃ brāhmaṇaṃ akathaṃkathiṃ chinnakukkuccaṃ bhavābhave vītataṇhaṃ saññā nānusentī’’ti? ‘‘Yatonidānaṃ, bhikkhu, purisaṃ papañcasaññāsaṅkhā samudācaranti. Ettha ce natthi abhinanditabbaṃ abhivaditabbaṃ ajjhositabbaṃ. Esevanto rāgānusayānaṃ, esevanto paṭighānusayānaṃ, esevanto diṭṭhānusayānaṃ , esevanto vicikicchānusayānaṃ, esevanto mānānusayānaṃ, esevanto bhavarāgānusayānaṃ, esevanto avijjānusayānaṃ, esevanto daṇḍādāna-satthādāna-kalaha-viggaha-vivāda-tuvaṃtuvaṃ-pesuñña-musāvādānaṃ. Etthete pāpakā akusalā dhammā aparisesā nirujjhantī’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Idaṃ vatvāna sugato uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṃ pāvisi.
Thus it was said, a certain bhikkhu said this to the Blessed One: “But, venerable sir, what is the Blessed One’s doctrine that he abides in the world together with the devas, with Māra, with Brahmā, with the generation together with ascetics and brāhmaṇas, with devas and humans, without contending with anyone in the world? And how do perceptions not underlie that brāhmaṇa who abides detached from sensual pleasures, is without doubt, has cut off worry, and is without craving for existence and non-existence?”

“Bhikkhu, expansive perceptions and notions harass a person because of whatever cause.

If nothing is found there to delight in, welcome, and hold to, this is the end of the underlying tendency to craving (rāgānusaya), this is the end of the underlying tendency to aversion (paṭighānusaya), this is the end of the underlying tendency to views (dīṭṭhānusaya), this is the end of the underlying tendency to doubt (vicikicchānusaya), this is the end of the underlying tendency to conceit (mānānusaya), this is the end of the underlying tendency to craving for existence (bhavarāgānusaya), this is the end of the underlying tendency to ignorance (avijjānusaya); this is the end of resorting to rods and weapons, of quarrels (kalaha), brawls (viggaha), disputes (vivāda), recrimination, malice (pesuñña), and false speech (musāvāda).

Here these evil unwholesome states (akusala dharma) cease without remainder.”

That is what the Blessed One said.

Having said this, the Well-Farer rose from his seat and entered his dwelling (vihāra).

"Intelligent" (paṇḍito) means endowed with intelligence (paṇḍiccena samannāgato).
Or, by four causes (catūhi vā kāraṇehi), intelligent (paṇḍito) is skilled in the elements (dhātukusalo), skilled in the sense-fields (āyatanakusalo), skilled in the modes of conditionality (paccayākārakusalo), skilled in cause and non-cause (kāraṇākāraṇakusaloti).
"Great wisdom" (mahāpañño) means endowed with great wisdom (mahāpaññāya samannāgato), which is great in meaning (mahante atthe), great in doctrines (mahante dhamme), great in expressions (mahantā niruttiyo), and great in insights (mahantāni paṭibhānāni) for the sake of skillful understanding (pariggahaṇasamatthāya).
"As explained by Mahākaccāna" (yathā taṃ mahākaccānenā) means as explained by Mahākaccāna (yathā mahākaccānena byākataṃ), referring to that (taṃ sandhāya), "that" (taṃ) is said (nti vuttaṃ).
As explained by Mahākaccāna (yathā mahākaccānena byākataṃ), the meaning is, "I, too, would explain it in just this way" (ahampi taṃ evamevaṃ byākareyyenti attho).

"Honeycomb" (madhupiṇḍika) means a large cake of molasses (mahantaṃ guḷapūvaṃ), or a ball of bound barley-meal (baddhasattuguḷakaṃ vā).
"Not to be sprinkled" (asecanaka) means not requiring sprinkling (asecitabbakaṃ).
In the case of ghee, treacle, honey, sugar, etc. (sappiphāṇitamadhusakkarādīsu), one should not say, "This is little, that is much" (idaṃ nāmettha mandaṃ idaṃ bahukanti), but [it should be] of balanced flavor (samayojitarasaṃ).
"Of the mind" (cetaso) means of the nature of thought (cintakajātiko).
"Of substance by birth" (dabbajātiko) means having the nature of intelligence (paṇḍitasabhāvo).
"What is the name of this?" (ko nāmo aya) means, "This is the Elder Atibhaddaka, this is the discourse on the honeycomb [Madhu Piṇḍika Sutta] (ayaṃ dhammapariyāyo); I will have him take the name through the all-knowing wisdom of the Ten-Powered One" (dasabalassa sabbaññutaññāṇenevassa nāmaṃ gaṇhāpessāmīti), thinking thus, he said (cintetvā āha).
"Therefore" (tasmā) means since (yasmā) the honeycomb [Madhu Piṇḍika] is like honey, sweet (madhupiṇḍiko viya madhuro), therefore (tasmā), "Bear it in mind as the discourse on the honeycomb [Madhu Piṇḍika Pariyāya]" (madhupiṇḍikapariyāyotveva naṃ dhārehīti vadati).
The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānatthamevāti).

Honeycomb, Not to be sprinkled, Of the mind, Of substance by birth, What is the name of this?, Therefore.
The Madhupiṇḍika Sutta Commentary in the Papañcasūdanī, the commentary to the Majjhima Nikāya, is completed.
