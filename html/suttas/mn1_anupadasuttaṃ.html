Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāavatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks: “Monks!”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied.

The Blessed One said this:

“Monks, Sāriputta is wise (paṇḍito); monks, Sāriputta has great wisdom (mahāpañño); monks, Sāriputta has wide wisdom (puthupañño); monks, Sāriputta has joyous wisdom (hāsa pañño); monks, Sāriputta has quick wisdom (javanapañño); monks, Sāriputta has keen wisdom (tikkhapañño); monks, Sāriputta has penetrative wisdom (nibbedhikapañño); monks, Sāriputta for half a month had insight into states one by one as they occurred (anupadadhammavipassanāṃ vipassati).

93. Thus.

I have heard that the anupadasuttaṃ (the connected or sequential discourse).

There, "thus he spoke," means that he spoke about the qualities (guṇa) of the Dharma-army-chief, the venerable Śāriputta, in the manner beginning with "wise."

Why? Because among the remaining elders, the venerable Mahāmoggallāna was known for his quality of having magical power (iddhi), Mahākassapa for his asceticism, Anuruddha for his divine eye, Upāli for his mastery of the Vinaya, Revata for his delight in meditative absorption (jhāna), and Ānanda for his great learning.

Thus, those qualities were evident in those respective elders, but not in Śāriputta.

Why? Because the qualities of the wise are not easily known if they are not spoken of.

Thus, the Blessed One, thinking, "I will speak of the qualities of Śāriputta," approached the assembly of those who shared similar virtues.

It is not fitting to speak of virtues in the presence of those who do not share them, for when virtues are spoken of, they speak only of faults.

On this day, however, the assembly of the elder's virtuous companions had gathered, and seeing their gathering, the Teacher began this discourse, speaking of virtues.

There, "wise" means wise due to skill in the elements (dhātu), skill in the sense-fields (āyatana), skill in dependent origination (paṭiccasamuppāda), and skill in what is possible and impossible; thus, he is wise due to these four causes.

In "[he is] greatly wise" and so forth, the meaning is that he is endowed with great wisdom and so forth.

Wise, greatly wise.

Here, there is a distinction among the types of great wisdom and so forth—what is "great wisdom" (mahāpaññā)? Mahāpaññā is because he comprehends great aggregates of moral conduct (śīlakkhandha), great aggregates of concentration (samādhi), great aggregates of wisdom (paññā), great aggregates of liberation (vimutti), and great aggregates of the vision of the knowledge of liberation (vimuttiñāṇadassanakkhandha); thus, it is mahāpaññā. [It is also mahāpaññā because he comprehends] great possibilities and impossibilities, great meditative attainments (vihārasamāpatti), great noble truths (ariyasacca), great mindfulness practices (satipaṭṭhāna), right efforts (sammappadhāna), bases of power (iddhipāda), faculties (indriya), strengths (bala), factors of enlightenment (bojjhaṅga), the great noble path (ariyamagga), great fruits of asceticism (sāmaññaphala), great superknowledges (abhiññā), and comprehends the ultimate meaning, nirvāṇa; thus, it is mahāpaññā.

Mahāpaññā.

What is "extensive wisdom" (puthupaññā)? Puthupaññā is because knowledge functions extensively in various aggregates; thus, it is puthupaññā. [It is also puthupaññā because knowledge functions extensively] in various elements, in various sense-fields, in various meanings, in various instances of dependent origination, in various apprehended voids, in various meanings, in the dharmas, in etymologies (nirutti), in eloquence (paṭibhāna), in various aggregates of moral conduct (sīlakkhandha), in various aggregates of concentration, wisdom, liberation, and the vision of the knowledge of liberation, in various possibilities and impossibilities, in various meditative attainments, in various noble truths, in various mindfulness practices, right efforts, bases of power, faculties, strengths, factors of enlightenment, in various noble paths, fruits of asceticism, superknowledges, and having transcended what is common to various ordinary people, knowledge functions in the ultimate meaning, nirvāṇa; thus, it is puthupaññā.

Puthupaññā.

What is "joyful wisdom" (hāsapaññā)? Here, a certain person, abounding in joy (hāsa), abounding in vedas, abounding in satisfaction (tuṭṭhi), abounding in delight (pāmojja), perfects moral conduct, perfects restraint of the senses, [knows] moderation in eating, devotion to wakefulness, perfects the aggregate of moral conduct, the aggregate of concentration, the aggregate of wisdom, the aggregate of liberation, and the aggregate of the vision of the knowledge of liberation; thus, it is hāsapaññā.

Abounding in joy, abounding in delight, he penetrates what is possible and impossible; abounding in joy, he perfects the meditative attainments; thus, it is hāsapaññā; abounding in joy, he penetrates the noble truths.

[He cultivates] mindfulness practices, right efforts, bases of power, faculties, strengths, factors of enlightenment, and cultivates the noble path; thus, it is hāsapaññā; abounding in joy, he realizes the fruits of asceticism, penetrates the superknowledges; thus, it is hāsapaññā; abounding in joy, abounding in the vedas, satisfaction, and delight, he realizes the ultimate meaning, nirvāṇa; thus, it is hāsapaññā.

Hāsapaññā.

What is "swift wisdom" (javanapaññā)? Whatever form there is—past, future, or present… whatever is far or near—all form swiftly passes away due to impermanence; thus, it is javanapaññā.

Swiftly due to suffering… swiftly due to non-self; thus, it is javanapaññā.

Whatever feeling there is… whatever consciousness there is—past, future, or present… all consciousness swiftly passes away due to impermanence, suffering, and non-self; thus, it is javanapaññā.

Eye… old age and death—past, future, or present—swiftly pass away due to impermanence and suffering; thus, it is javanapaññā.

Having weighed, having examined, having analyzed, having made manifest, form—past, future, or present—is impermanent in the sense of destruction, suffering in the sense of terror, and non-self in the sense of lacking essence, and having made it disappear, he swiftly proceeds to nirvāṇa where form ceases; thus, it is javanapaññā.

Feeling, perception, volitions, consciousness, eye… old age and death—past, future, or present—are impermanent in the sense of destruction… and having made it disappear, he swiftly proceeds to nirvāṇa where old age and death cease; thus, it is javanapaññā.
Materiality, whether past, future or present… and so on… consciousness.

Eye… and so on… aging and death, whether past, future or present, are impermanent, conditioned, dependently arisen, subject to destruction, subject to decay, subject to fading away, subject to cessation.’ Having weighed, considered, analyzed, and made distinct, quick insight swiftly inclines towards the cessation of aging and death, towards nirvāṇa; thus, it is swift insight (javanapaññā).

94. Paṭhame jhāne means the dharmas in the first jhāna that are internal to meditative attainment (antosamāpatti).

Tyāssā means "they are his."

Anupadavavatthitā hontī means they are arranged successively (anupaṭipāṭiyā), determined (vavatthitā), distinct (paricchinnā), known (ñātā), and recognized (viditā).

How so? A thera, while contemplating these dharmas, knows that initial application of mind (vitakka) is characterized by directing the mind (abhiniropanalakkhaṇa).

Similarly, sustained application of mind (vicāra) is characterized by repeated engagement (anumajjanalakkhaṇa), joy (pīti) is characterized by suffusion (pharaṇalakkhaṇā), happiness (sukhaṃ) is characterized by satisfaction (sātalakkhaṇaṃ), one-pointedness of mind (cittekaggatā) is characterized by non-distraction (avikkhepalakkhaṇā), contact (phasso) is characterized by touching (phusanalakkhaṇa), feeling (vedanā) is characterized by experiencing (vedayitalakkhaṇā), perception (saññā) is characterized by cognizing (sañjānanalakkhaṇā), volition (cetanā) is characterized by intending (cetayitalakkhaṇā), consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) is characterized by discerning (vijānanalakkhaṇaṃ), intention (chando) is characterized by wanting to do (kattukamyatālakkhaṇo), resolve (adhimokkho) is characterized by decision (adhimokkhalakkhaṇo), effort (vīriyaṃ) is characterized by exertion (paggāhalakkhaṇaṃ), mindfulness (sati) is characterized by establishing (upaṭṭhānalakkhaṇā), equanimity (upekkhā) is characterized by balance (majjhattalakkhaṇā), and attention (manasikāro) is characterized by directing towards conformity (anunayamanasikāralakkhaṇo).

Knowing thus, he determines (vavatthapeti) initial application of mind (vitakkaṃ) in terms of its nature (sabhāvato) through the meaning of directing the mind (abhiniropanaṭṭhena)… and he determines (vavatthapeti) attention (manasikāraṃ) in terms of the nature of its being (sabhāvabhāvato) through the meaning of directing towards conformity (anunayamanasikāraṇaṭṭhena).

Therefore, it was said, "their dharmas are arranged successively (anupadavavatthitā hontī)."

Viditā uppajjantī means, while arising, they arise as recognized and manifest (pākaṭāva hutvā uppajjanti).

Viditā upaṭṭhahantī means, while persisting, they persist as recognized and manifest (pākaṭāva hutvā tiṭṭhanti).

Viditā abbhatthaṃ gacchantī means, while ceasing, they cease as recognized and manifest (pākaṭāva hutvā nirujjhanti).

Here, however, both that knowledge-ness (taṃñāṇatā) and knowledge-abundance (ñāṇabahutā) must be removed (mocetabbā).

Just as it is not possible to touch that fingertip with that very fingertip (aṅgulaggena taṃ aṅgulaggaṃ na sakkā phusituṃ), just so it is not possible to know (jānitunti) the arising (uppādo), persistence (ṭhiti), or dissolution (bhaṅgo) of that mind with that very mind (teneva cittena tassa cittassa).

Thus, first knowledge-ness (taṃñāṇatā) must be removed (mocetabbā).

If, however, two minds should arise at once (dve cittāni ekato uppajjeyyuṃ), it would be possible to know the arising (uppādo), persistence (ṭhiti), or dissolution (bhaṅgo) of one with one mind (ekena cittena ekassa).

But indeed (pana) there are no dharmas that arise at once (ekato uppajjanakāni nāma natthi), be they two contacts (dve phassā), or feelings (vedanā), or perceptions (saññā), or volitions (cetanā), or minds (cittāni); only one at a time arises (ekekameva uppajjati).

Thus, knowledge-abundance (ñāṇabahutā) must be removed (mocetabbā).

Thus being the case (evaṃ sante), how so? For the great thera, sixteen dharmas in internal meditative attainment (antosamāpattiyaṃ) are recognized and manifest (viditā pākaṭā hontīti).

Because of being apprehended in terms of the object-basis (vatthārammaṇānaṃ pariggahitatāya).

For the thera has indeed apprehended both the basis and the object (vatthu ceva ārammaṇañca pariggahitaṃ); therefore, for him, while considering the arising (uppādaṃ āvajjantassa) of those dharmas, the arising is manifest (uppādo pākaṭo hoti), while considering the persistence (ṭhānaṃ āvajjantassa), the persistence is manifest (ṭhānaṃ pākaṭaṃ hoti), and while considering the dissolution (bhedaṃ āvajjantassa), the dissolution is manifest (bhedo pākaṭo hoti).

Therefore, it was said, "They arise recognized (viditā uppajjanti), they persist recognized (viditā upaṭṭhahanti), they cease recognized (viditā abbhatthaṃ gacchantī)."

Ahutvā sambhontī means, with this, he sees arising (udayaṃ passati).

Hutvā paṭiventī means, with this, he sees passing away (vayaṃ passati).

Recognized they arise, recognized they persist, recognized they cease, not having been, they come to be, having been, they pass away.

There, however, are two limits: the limit of defilements (kilesamariyādā) and the limit of objects (ārammaṇamariyādā ca).

If, indeed, having aroused the sixteen dharmas occurring in internal meditative attainment (antosamāpattiyaṃ pavatte soḷasa dhamme ārambha), craving and so forth (rāgādayo) should arise (uppajjeyyuṃ), the limit of defilements (kilesamariyādā) would have been made by him (tena katā bhaveyya); but since even one of them has not arisen in them (tesu panassa ekopi na uppanno), there is no limit of defilements (kilesamariyādā natthi).

Again, O monks, Sāriputta, by completely transcending the base of nothingness (ākiñcanyāyatana), enters and abides in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ).
He emerges mindful from that attainment (samāpatti).
Having emerged mindfully from that attainment, he reviews those past states (dhamma) which have ceased, been suppressed, and changed, thinking, ‘So indeed, these states, not having been, come into being; having been, they vanish.’
Regarding those states (dhamma), he abides unattracted, unrepelled, independent, detached, liberated, dissociated, with a mind rid of barriers (vimariyādīkatena cetasā viharati).
He understands: ‘There is an escape beyond (uttari nissaraṇa).’
And with the cultivation of that attainment, he confirms that there is (atthitvevassa hoti).
95.Sato vuṭṭhahatīti satiyā samannāgato ñāṇena sampajāno hutvā vuṭṭhāti. Te dhamme samanupassatīti yasmā nevasaññānāsaññāyatane buddhānaṃyeva anupadadhammavipassanā hoti, na sāvakānaṃ, tasmā ettha kalāpavipassanaṃ dassento evamāha. Paññāyacassa disvā āsavā parikkhīṇā hontīti maggapaññāya cattāri saccāni disvā cattāro āsavā khīṇā honti. Sāriputtattherassa samathavipassanaṃ yuganaddhaṃ āharitvā arahattaṃ pattavāropi atthi, nirodhasamāpattisamāpannavāropi. Arahattaṃ pattavāro idha gahito, nirodhaṃ pana ciṇṇavasitāya aparāparaṃ samāpajjissatīti vadanti. Paññāya cassa disvā āsavā parikkhīṇā hontī Tatthassa yasmiṃ kāle nirodhasamāpatti sīsaṃ hoti, nirodhassa vāro āgacchati, phalasamāpatti gūḷhā hoti. Yasmiṃ kāle phalasamāpatti sīsaṃ hoti, phalasamāpattiyā vāro āgacchati, nirodhasamāpatti gūḷhā hoti. Jambudīpavāsino therā pana vadanti ‘‘sāriputtatthero samathavipassanaṃ yuganaddhaṃ āharitvā anāgāmiphalaṃ sacchikatvā nirodhaṃ samāpajji, nirodhā vuṭṭhāya arahattaṃ patto’’ti. Te dhammeti antosamāpattiyaṃ pavatte tisamuṭṭhānikarūpadhamme, heṭṭhā nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasamāpattiyaṃ pavattadhamme vā. Tepi hi imasmiṃ vāre vipassitabbadhammāva, tasmā te vā vipassatīti dassetuṃ idaṃ vuttanti veditabbaṃ. Te dhamme
95. "One who rises mindfully (sato vuṭṭhahatī)" means one rises possessing mindfulness (smṛti), fully knowing with wisdom (jñāna), having become conscious.

"He contemplates those phenomena (te dhamme samanupassatī)" Since in the realm of neither perception nor non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana), the uninterrupted contemplation of phenomena (anupadadhammavipassanā) is only for Buddhas, not for disciples (śrāvakas), therefore, here, showing the contemplation of aggregates (kalāpavipassanā), he speaks thus.

By wisdom (prajñā).

Having seen, his outflows are eliminated (cassa disvā āsavā parikkhīṇā hontī)" means by the wisdom of the path (maggapaññā), having seen the four truths (catvāri satyāni), the four outflows (catvāro āsavā) are eliminated (khīṇā).

Sāriputta, having brought together serenity and insight (śamathavipassanā) in a paired manner, there is also the attainment of arhatship (arahattaṃ pattavāropi), and the attainment of cessation (nirodhasamāpattisamāpannavāropi).

Here, the attainment of arhatship (arahattaṃ pattavāro) is taken, but they say that one will attain cessation (nirodhaṃ) again and again by the power of habituation (ciṇṇavasitāya).

By wisdom (prajñā).

Having seen, his outflows are eliminated (cassa disvā āsavā parikkhīṇā hontī).

There, at what time the cessation attainment (nirodhasamāpatti) is at its peak, the moment of cessation (nirodhassa vāro) arrives, the fruition attainment (phalasamāpatti) is hidden (gūḷhā).

At what time the fruition attainment (phalasamāpatti) is at its peak, the moment of fruition attainment (phalasamāpattiyā vāro) arrives, the cessation attainment (nirodhasamāpatti) is hidden (gūḷhā).

The elders (therā) dwelling in Jambudvīpa (jambudīpavāsino) say, "Sāriputta, having brought together serenity and insight (śamathavipassanā) in a paired manner, having realized the fruit of non-returning (anāgāmiphalaṃ sacchikatvā), attained cessation (nirodhaṃ samāpajji), having arisen from cessation (nirodhā vuṭṭhāya), attained arhatship (arahattaṃ patto)."

"Those phenomena (te dhamme)" means the compounded form-phenomena (tisamuṭṭhānikarūpadhamme) that occur in the inner attainment (antosamāpattiyaṃ pavatte), or the phenomena that occur in the lower realm of neither perception nor non-perception attainment (nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasamāpattiyaṃ pavattadhamme).

Indeed, even those phenomena are to be contemplated (vipassitabbadhammāva) at this time, therefore, to show that he contemplates them (te vā vipassatīti), this was said (idaṃ vuttanti), it should be understood (veditabbaṃ).

Those phenomena (te dhamme).

96. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, sāriputto sabbaso nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ samatikkamma saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ upasampajja viharati. Paññāya cassa disvā āsavā parikkhīṇā honti. So tāya samāpattiyā sato vuṭṭhahati. So tāya samāpattiyā sato vuṭṭhahitvā ye dhammā atītā niruddhā vipariṇatā te dhamme samanupassati – ‘evaṃ kirame dhammā ahutvā sambhonti, hutvā paṭiventī’ti. So tesu dhammesu anupāyo anapāyo anissito appaṭibaddho vippamutto visaṃyutto vimariyādīkatena cetasā viharati. So ‘natthi uttari nissaraṇa’nti pajānāti. Tabbahulīkārā natthitvevassa hoti.
“Again, monks, Sāriputta, by entirely transcending the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, enters and remains in the cessation of perception and feeling (saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ upasampajja viharati).

And his taints (āsavā) are destroyed by seeing with wisdom (paññāya cassa disvā āsavā parikkhīṇā honti).

He emerges mindful from that attainment (samāpattiyā) (so tāya samāpattiyā sato vuṭṭhahati).

Having done so, he contemplates the past states (dhammā), which have ceased (niruddhā) and changed (vipariṇatā), thus: ‘So indeed, these states (dhammā), not having been, come into being; having been, they vanish.’

Regarding those states (dhammesu), he abides unattracted (anupāyo), unrepelled (anapāyo), independent (anissito), detached (appaṭibaddho), free (vippamutto), dissociated (visaṃyutto), with a mind (cetasā) rid of barriers.

He understands: ‘There is an escape (nissaraṇa) beyond.’

Through repeated practice, it is simply non-existent for him.

97. ‘‘Yaṃ kho taṃ, bhikkhave, sammā vadamāno vadeyya – ‘vasippatto pāramippatto ariyasmiṃ sīlasmiṃ, vasippatto pāramippatto ariyasmiṃ samādhismiṃ, vasippatto pāramippatto ariyāya paññāya , vasippatto pāramippatto ariyāya vimuttiyā’ti, sāriputtameva taṃ sammā vadamāno vadeyya – ‘vasippatto pāramippatto ariyasmiṃ sīlasmiṃ, vasippatto pāramippatto ariyasmiṃ samādhismiṃ, vasippatto pāramippatto ariyāya paññāya, vasippatto pāramippatto ariyāya vimuttiyā’ti. Yaṃ kho taṃ, bhikkhave , sammā vadamāno vadeyya – ‘bhagavato putto oraso mukhato jāto dhammajo dhammanimmito dhammadāyādo no āmisadāyādo’ti, sāriputtameva taṃ sammā vadamāno vadeyya – ‘bhagavato putto oraso mukhato jāto dhammajo dhammanimmito dhammadāyādo no āmisadāyādo’ti. Sāriputto, bhikkhave, tathāgatena anuttaraṃ dhammacakkaṃ pavattitaṃ sammadeva anuppavattetī’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Anupadasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.
“Bhikkhus, rightly speaking, were it to be said of anyone: ‘He has attained mastery and perfection in noble virtue, attained mastery and perfection in noble concentration, attained mastery and perfection in noble wisdom, attained mastery and perfection in noble deliverance,’ it is of Śāriputra indeed that rightly speaking this should be said: ‘He has attained mastery and perfection in noble virtue, attained mastery and perfection in noble concentration, attained mastery and perfection in noble wisdom, attained mastery and perfection in noble deliverance.’

“Bhikkhus, rightly speaking, were it to be said of anyone: ‘He is the son of the Blessed One, born of his breast, born of his mouth, born of the Dharma, created by the Dharma, an heir in the Dharma, not an heir in material things,’ it is of Śāriputra indeed that rightly speaking this should be said: ‘He is the son of the Blessed One, born of his breast, born of his mouth, born of the Dharma, created by the Dharma, an heir in the Dharma, not an heir in material things.’ Śāriputra properly continues to keep in motion the unsurpassed Wheel of the Dharma (anuttaraṃ dhammacakkaṃ) set in motion by the Tathāgata.”

This the Blessed One said. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Anupada Sutta, the first, is finished.
97.Vasippatoti ciṇṇavasitaṃ patto. Pāramippattoti nipphattiṃ patto. Orasotiādīsu thero bhagavato ure nibbattasaddaṃ sutvā jātoti oraso, mukhena pabhāvitaṃ saddaṃ sutvā jātoti mukhato jāto, dhammena pana jātattā nimmitattā dhammajo dhammanimmito, dhammadāyassa ādiyanato dhammadāyādo, āmisadāyassa anādiyanato no āmisadāyādoti veditabbo. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Anupadasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
97. "Having mastery (vasippatto)" means having attained mastery in conduct.

"Having attained the perfection (pāramippatto)" means having attained completion.

Among "the own son (oraso)" etc., the Elder, having heard the sound arising from the breast (ure) of the Blessed One, was born, therefore (he is called) "the own son (oraso)"; having heard the sound brought forth from the mouth, he was born, therefore (he is called) "born from the mouth (mukhato jāto)"; but because of being born or created by the Dharma, (he is called) "born of the Dharma (dhammajo)," "created by the Dharma (dhammanimmito)"; because of taking the inheritance of the Dharma, (he is called) "heir to the Dharma (dhammadāyādo)"; because of not taking the inheritance of material things, (he is called) "not heir to material things (no āmisadāyādo)"—this should be understood.

The rest is clear throughout.

The Commentary on the Anupada Sutta, from the Papañcasūdanī, the Commentary to the Middle Length Discourses, is finished.
