Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Savatthi in the Jetavana in Anathapindika’s monastery.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: “Monks.”

“Venerable sir,” those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“Here, monks, a monk declares: ‘Destroyed is birth, the holy life has been lived, what had to be done has been done, there is no more for this state of being.’

Monks, the speech of such a monk is neither to be approved nor disapproved.

Without approving or disapproving, the question should be asked: ‘Friend, there are four kinds of expression rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, the Accomplished One, the Fully Enlightened One.

What are the four? Telling the seen as it is seen; telling the heard as it is heard; telling the sensed as it is sensed; telling the cognized as it is cognized – these, friend, are the four kinds of expression rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, the Accomplished One, the Fully Enlightened One.

How, when knowing, how, when seeing, is the mind of the venerable one released from the taints through non-clinging regarding these four kinds of expression?’ When a monk is one with taints destroyed, who has lived the holy life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, reached the true goal, destroyed the fetters of being, and is completely liberated through final knowledge, this is the nature of his answer: ‘Regarding the seen, friend, I abide unattracted, unrepelled, independent, detached, free, dissociated, with a mind rid of barriers.

Regarding the heard… Regarding the sensed… Regarding the cognized I abide unattracted, unrepelled, independent, detached, free, dissociated, with a mind rid of barriers.

It is by knowing thus, seeing thus, regarding these four kinds of expression, that through not clinging my mind is liberated from the taints.’

Saying ‘good,’ monks, one may delight and rejoice in that monk’s words.

Having delighted and rejoiced in the words, saying ‘good,’ a further question should be asked.

99. “These five aggregates affected by clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhā), friends, were rightly expounded by the Blessed One (bhagavatā)—the one who knows (jānatā), the one who sees (passatā), the Worthy One (arhatā), the rightly self-awakened (sammāsambuddhena).

Which five? They are: the material form aggregate affected by clinging (rūpupādānakkhandho), the feeling aggregate affected by clinging (vedanupādānakkhandho), the perception aggregate affected by clinging (saññupādānakkhandho), the formations aggregate affected by clinging (saṅkhārupādānakkhandho), and the consciousness aggregate affected by clinging (viññāṇupādānakkhandho). These, friends, are the five aggregates affected by clinging, rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

How does the venerable one know (jānato), how does he see (passato), regarding these five aggregates affected by clinging (upādānakkhandhesu), so that through not clinging (anupādāya) his mind (cittaṃ) is liberated (vimutta) from the taints (āsavehi)?’

“Bhikkhus, when a bhikkhu is one with taints destroyed (khīṇāsavassa)… and is completely liberated through final knowledge (sammadaññāvimuttassa), this is the nature of his answer: ‘Friends, having known material form (rūpaṃ) to be feeble (abalaṃ), fading away (virāgunaṃ), and comfortless (anassāsikanti), with the destruction (khayā), fading away (virāgā), cessation (nirodhā), giving up (cāgā), and relinquishing (paṭinissaggā) of attraction and clinging (upāyūpādānā) regarding material form, of mental standpoints (cetaso), adherences (adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā), and underlying tendencies regarding material form, I have understood that my mind is liberated (vimuttaṃ me cittanti pajānāmi).

‘Friends, having known feeling (vedanaṃ)…Having known perception (saññaṃ)… Having known formations (saṅkhāre)…Having known consciousness (viññāṇaṃ) to be feeble, fading away, and comfortless, with the destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing of attraction and clinging regarding consciousness, of mental standpoints, adherences, and underlying tendencies regarding consciousness, I have understood that my mind is liberated.

‘It is by knowing thus (evaṃ), seeing thus (evaṃ), regarding these five aggregates affected by clinging, that through not clinging my mind is liberated from the taints.’

“Saying ‘good (sādhū),’ bhikkhus, one may delight (abhinanditabbaṃ) and rejoice (anumoditabbaṃ) in that bhikkhu’s words. Having delighted and rejoiced in that bhikkhu’s words, a further question should be asked (uttariṃ pañho pucchitabbo).”

100. “‘Friend, there are these six dhātus rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

What are the six? They are the earth element (pṛthivīdhātu), the water element (āpodhātu), the fire element (tejodhātu), the air element (vāyodhātu), the space element (ākāśadhātu), and the consciousness element (vijñāṇadhātu). These, friend, are the six dhātus rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

‘How, friend, by knowing, how by seeing, is the mind liberated from taints through not clinging to these six elements?’

“Bhikkhus, when a bhikkhu is one with taints destroyed… and is completely liberated through final knowledge, this is the nature of his answer: ‘“Friends, I have treated the earth element (pṛthivīdhātu) as not self, with no self based on the earth element (pṛthivīdhātu).

And with the destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing of attraction and clinging based on the earth element (pṛthivīdhātu), of mental standpoints, adherences, and underlying tendencies based on the earth element (pṛthivīdhātu), I have understood that my mind is liberated.

‘“Friends, I have treated the water element (āpodhātu)…

the fire element (tejodhātu)… the air element (vāyodhātu)… the space element (ākāśadhātu)… the consciousness element (vijñāṇadhātu) as not self, with no self based on the consciousness element (vijñāṇadhātu).

And with the destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing of attraction and clinging based on the consciousness element (vijñāṇadhātu), of mental standpoints, adherences, and underlying tendencies based on the consciousness element (vijñāṇadhātu), I have understood that my mind is liberated.

‘It is by knowing thus, seeing thus, regarding these six elements (dhātus), that through not clinging my mind is liberated from the taints.’

“Bhikkhus, the speech of that bhikkhu should be rejoiced at and approved. Having rejoiced at and approved his speech, a further question should be asked.

101. “But, friend, there are these six internal and external bases (ajjhattikabāhirāni āyatanāni) rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

What are the six? They are the eye (cakkhu) and forms (rūpa), the ear (sota) and sounds (sadda), the nose (ghāna) and odours (gandha), the tongue (jivhā) and flavours (rasa), the body (kāya) and tangibles (phoṭṭhabba), the mind (manas) and mind-objects (dhamma)—these, friend, are the six internal and external bases rightly proclaimed by the Blessed One who knows and sees, accomplished and fully enlightened.

“How, friend, by knowing, how, by seeing, regarding these six internal and external bases, is one's mind liberated from taints through non-clinging?” “When a bhikkhu is one with taints destroyed… and is completely liberated through final knowledge, this is the nature of his answer: ‘With the destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing of desire (chanda), lust (rāga), delight (nandī), craving (taṇhā), attraction (upāyūpādāna), and clinging, and of mental standpoints (adhiṭṭhānābhinivesānusayā) regarding the eye, forms, eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññāṇa), and things cognizable by the mind through eye-consciousness, I have understood that my mind is liberated.

“With the destruction, fading away, cessation, giving up, and relinquishing of desire, lust, delight, craving, attraction, and clinging, and of mental standpoints regarding the ear, sounds, ear-consciousness…regarding the nose, odours, nose-consciousness…regarding the tongue, flavours, tongue-consciousness…regarding the body, tangibles, body-consciousness…regarding the mind, mind-objects, mind-consciousness, and things cognizable by the mind through mind-consciousness, I have understood that my mind is liberated.’

“It is by knowing thus, seeing thus, regarding these six internal and external bases, that through not clinging my mind is liberated from the taints.”

“Saying ‘good,’ bhikkhus, one may delight and rejoice in that bhikkhu’s words.

“Having delighted and rejoiced in that one’s words, a further question should be asked.

So yena yeneva pakkamiṃ samādāyeva pakkamiṃ. So iminā ariyena sīlakkhandhena samannāgato ajjhattaṃ anavajjasukhaṃ paṭisaṃvedesiṃ.

102. “But, friend, how does the venerable one know, how does he see, so that in regard to this body with its consciousness and all external signs, I-making, mine-making, and the underlying tendency to conceit have been eradicated in him?”
“Bhikkhus, when a bhikkhu is one with taints destroyed, who has lived the holy life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, reached his own goal, utterly destroyed the fetters of existence, one completely liberated through final knowledge, this is the nature of his answer: ‘“Friends, formerly when I lived the home life I was ignorant.
Then the Tathāgata or his disciple taught me the Dharma.
On hearing the Dhamma I acquired faith in the Tathagata.
Possessing that faith, I considered thus: “Household life is crowded and dusty; life gone forth is wide open.
It is not easy while living in a home to lead the holy life utterly perfect and pure as a polished shell.
What if I were to shave off my hair and beard, put on the yellow robe, and went forth from the home life into homelessness?’”
“On a later occasion, abandoning a small or a large fortune, abandoning a small or a large circle of relations, I shaved off my hair and beard, put on the yellow robe, and went forth from the home life into homelessness.
Having thus gone forth and possessing the bhikkhus’ training and way of life…, abandoning the killing of living beings, he abstains from killing living beings; with rod and weapon laid aside, gentle and kindly, he abides compassionate to all living beings.
Having abandoned untrue speech, [he] indeed turns away from speaking untruth: [he] is a truth-speaker; [he] delights in truth-speaking… to the world.
Abandoning malicious speech, he abstains from malicious speech; he does not repeat elsewhere what he has heard here in order to divide those people from these, nor does he repeat to these people what he has heard elsewhere in order to divide these people from those; thus he is one who reunites those who are divided, a promoter of friendships, who enjoys concord, rejoices in concord, delights in concord, a speaker of words that promote concord.
Abandoning harsh speech, he abstains from harsh speech; he speaks such words as are gentle, pleasing to the ear, and loveable, as go to the heart, are courteous, desired by many and agreeable to many.
Having abandoned idle chatter, he abstains from idle chatter. He speaks at the right time, speaks what is factual and beneficial, speaks on the Dhamma and the Discipline. His words are worth treasuring; they are timely, backed by reasons, measured, and connected with the good.

“He abstains from damaging seed and plant life, he partakes of one meal a day, abstains from night meals, and desists from untimely eating.
He abstains from dancing, singing, instrumental music, and from witnessing unsuitable shows.
He abstains from wearing garlands, embellishing himself with scents, and beautifying himself with unguents.
He abstains from high and luxurious beds and seats.
He abstains from receiving gold and silver, he abstains from receiving uncooked grain, he abstains from receiving raw meat; he abstains from receiving women and girls, he abstains from receiving male and female slaves, he abstains from receiving goats and sheep, he abstains from receiving fowl and pigs, he abstains from receiving elephants, cattle, horses, and mares, he abstains from receiving fields and lands.
He abstains from going on errands and messages; he abstains from buying and selling; he abstains from false weights, counterfeit metals, and false measures; he abstains from cheating, deception, trickery, and crooked dealings; he abstains from injuring, murdering, imprisoning, plundering, robbing, and using violence.

“He was content with robes to protect his body and with alms-food to appease his hunger.
Wherever he went, he set out taking only those.
Just as a bird, wherever it flies, flies with its wings as its only burden, so too, I, friend, was content with robes to protect my body and with alms-food to maintain my stomach.

And wherever he went, he set out, taking only these with him.

Possessing this noble aggregate of moral discipline ( ārya sīlakkhandha), he experienced within himself a blameless happiness (anavajjasukhaṃ).

Note: Wherever and (ka.)

103. "Having seen a form with the eye, I was not one who grasps at features or details. Since evil, unwholesome states, namely covetousness and displeasure, would flow into him who dwells unrestrained in the eye faculty, I undertook restraint for the sake of preventing that; I guarded the eye faculty, I undertook restraint in the eye faculty.

Having heard a sound with the ear…pe… Having smelled an odor with the nose…pe… Having tasted a taste with the tongue…pe… Having felt a tactile object with the body…pe… Having cognized a mental phenomenon with the mind, I was not one who grasps at features or details. Since evil, unwholesome states, namely covetousness and displeasure, would flow into him who dwells unrestrained in the mind faculty, I undertook restraint for the sake of preventing that; I guarded the mind faculty, I undertook restraint in the mind faculty.

Endowed with this noble restraint of the faculties (indriyasaṃvara), I experienced within myself a bliss that is unsullied.

"In going forward and in going back, I was one who acts with full awareness (sampajānakārī); in looking straight on and in looking around, I was one who acts with full awareness; in drawing in and in stretching out, I was one who acts with full awareness; in wearing the double robe, the bowl, and the (other) robe, I was one who acts with full awareness; in eating, drinking, chewing, and savoring, I was one who acts with full awareness; in evacuating the bowels and bladder, I was one who acts with full awareness; in going, standing, sitting, sleeping, waking, speaking, and being silent, I was one who acts with full awareness.

"Endowed with this noble aggregate of moral discipline (śīlakkhandha), (endowed with this noble contentment,) endowed with this noble restraint over the sense faculties (indriyasaṃvara), endowed with this noble mindfulness and clear comprehension (satisampajañña), I frequented a secluded dwelling—a forest (arañña), the foot of a tree (rukkhamūla), a mountain (pabbata), a glen (kandara), a hillside cave (giriguha), a cremation ground (susāna), a jungle grove (vanapattha), the open air (abbhokāsa), a heap of straw (palālapuñja). After the meal, returning from the alms round, I sat down, folding my legs crosswise, setting my body erect, establishing mindfulness before me.

"Having abandoned covetousness (abhijjhā) for the world, I dwelt with a mind free from covetousness; I purified my mind from covetousness (abhijjhā). Having abandoned ill will and hatred (byāpādapadosa), I dwelt with a mind free from ill will, compassionate for the welfare of all living beings; I purified my mind from ill will and hatred. Having abandoned sloth and torpor (thinamiddha), I dwelt free from sloth and torpor, percipient of light (ālokasaññī), mindful (sato) and fully aware (sampajāno); I purified my mind from sloth and torpor. Having abandoned restlessness and remorse (uddhaccakukkucca), I dwelt unagitated within myself, with a peaceful mind (vūpasantacitto); I purified my mind from restlessness and remorse. Having abandoned doubt (vicikicchā), I dwelt having gone beyond doubt, without uncertainty as to what things are wholesome (kusalesu dhammesu); I purified my mind from doubt.

104. "Having abandoned these five hindrances (pañca nīvaraṇe pahāya), the defilements of the mind (cetaso upakkilese), which weaken wisdom (paññāya dubbalīkaraṇe), quite secluded from sense pleasures (kāmehi vivicca), secluded from unwholesome states (akusalehi dhammehi), with initial thought (savitakkaṃ) and sustained thought (savicāraṃ), [and having attained] the first jhāna (paṭhamaṃ dhyānaṃ), which is born of seclusion, [and is filled with] joy (pīti) and pleasure (sukhaṃ), I dwelled (vihāsiṃ).

Through the subsiding of initial thought (vitakka) and sustained thought (vicārānaṃ vūpasamā), with inner tranquility (ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ) and unification of mind (cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ), without initial thought (avitakkaṃ) and without sustained thought (avicāraṃ), [and having attained] the second jhāna (dutiyaṃ dhyānaṃ) which is born of concentration (samādhijaṃ), [and is filled with] joy (pīti) and pleasure (sukhaṃ), ...pe... the third jhāna (tatiyaṃ dhyānaṃ)… the fourth jhāna (catutthaṃ dhyānaṃ), I dwelled (vihāsiṃ).

"When my mind was thus concentrated (evaṃ samāhite citte), purified (parisuddhe), clarified (pariyodāte), without blemish (anaṅgaṇe), rid of defilements (vigatūpakkilese), malleable (mudubhūte), workable (kammaniye), steady (ṭhite), and had attained imperturbability (āneñjappatte), I directed (abhininnāmesiṃ) my mind (cittaṃ) to the knowledge of the destruction of the āsavas (āsavānaṃ khayañāṇāya).

I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ) suffering (idaṃ dukkhaṃ) as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ), I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ) the origin of suffering (ayaṃ dukkhasamudayo) as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ), I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ) the cessation of suffering (ayaṃ dukkhanirodho) as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ), I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ) the path leading to the cessation of suffering (ayaṃ dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā) as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ); I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ) these āsavas (ime āsavāti) as they actually are (yathābhūtaṃ), I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ) the origin of the āsavas (ayaṃ āsavasamudayo) as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ), I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ) the cessation of the āsavas (ayaṃ āsavanirodho) as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ), I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ) the path leading to the cessation of the āsavas (ayaṃ āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā) as it actually is (yathābhūtaṃ).

Knowing thus (evaṃ jānato), seeing thus (evaṃ passato), my mind (cittaṃ) was liberated (vimuccittha) from the āsava of sensual desire (kāmāsavāpi), my mind (cittaṃ) was liberated (vimuccittha) from the āsava of becoming (bhavāsavāpi), my mind (cittaṃ) was liberated (vimuccittha) from the āsava of ignorance (avijjāsavāpi): there was knowledge (ñāṇaṃ ahosi) that in the liberated [mind] (vimuttasmiṃ) there is liberation (vimuttamiti).

I understood (abbhaññāsiṃ): Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more for this state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāya).

Thus (evaṃ kho), friends (āvuso), knowing thus (jānato), seeing thus (passato), in this very body (imasmiñca kāye) with its consciousness (saviññāṇake) and all external signs (bahiddhā ca sabbanimittesu), the I-making (ahaṃkāra), the mine-making (mamaṃkāra), and the underlying tendency to conceit (mānānusayā) have been eradicated (samūhatā)."

"Bhikkhus, the speech of a bhikkhu should be welcomed, approved and rejoiced at saying 'Excellent!'"

Having welcomed the well-spoken, having rejoiced, one should say to him, 'It is gain for us, friend, a gain indeed, that we associate with such a companion in the holy life.'"

The Blessed One said this.

The bhikkhus, glad at heart, welcomed the words of the Blessed One.
