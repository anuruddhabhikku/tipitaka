<p rend="bodytext">‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, dve agārā sadvārā <note>sandhidvārā (ka.)</note>, tattha cakkhumā puriso majjhe ṭhito passeyya manusse gehaṃ pavisantepi nikkhamantepi anucaṅkamantepi anuvicarantepi; evameva kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passāmi cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe, sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāmi – ‘ime vata bhonto sattā kāyasucaritena samannāgatā vacīsucaritena samannāgatā manosucaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ anupavādakā <pb ed="M" n="3.0217"/> sammādiṭṭhikā sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā; te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannā. Ime vā pana bhonto sattā kāyasucaritena samannāgatā vacīsucaritena samannāgatā manosucaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0179"/> anupavādakā sammādiṭṭhikā sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā; te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā manussesu upapannā. Ime vā pana bhonto sattā kāyaduccaritena samannāgatā vacīduccaritena samannāgatā manoduccaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ upavādakā micchādiṭṭhikā micchādiṭṭhikammasamādānā; te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā tiracchānayoniṃ upapannā. Ime vā pana bhonto sattā kāyaduccaritena samannāgatā vacīduccaritena samannāgatā manoduccaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ upavādakā micchādiṭṭhikā micchādiṭṭhikammasamādānā; te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapannā’’’ti.

<note>sandhidvārā (ka.)</note> <pb ed="M" n="3.0217"/> <pb ed="P" n="3.0179"/> <pb ed="T" n="3.0335"/>
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Sāvatthi.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: “Bhikkhus.”

“Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied.

The Blessed One said this:

“Bhikkhus, suppose there were two houses with doors, and a man with good sight standing in the middle could see people entering, leaving, walking back and forth, and moving about in the houses. In the same way, with the divine eye ( dibbaṃ cakkhuṃ), which is purified and surpasses the human, I see beings passing away and being reborn, inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, fortunate and unfortunate. I understand how beings pass on according to their actions thus: ‘These worthy beings were endowed with good conduct of body ( kāyasucarita), speech ( vacīsucarita), and mind ( manosucarita), were not revilers of the noble ones ( ariyānaṃ anupavādakā), held right view ( sammādiṭṭhi), and undertook actions governed by right view ( sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā). At the breakup of the body, after death, they have reappeared in a good destination, a heavenly world ( sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannā). Or these worthy beings were endowed with good conduct of body, speech, and mind, were not revilers of the noble ones, held right view, and undertook actions governed by right view. At the breakup of the body, after death, they have reappeared among human beings ( manussesu upapannā). But these worthy beings were endowed with bad conduct of body ( kāyaduccarita), speech ( vacīduccarita), and mind ( manoduccarita), were revilers of the noble ones ( ariyānaṃ upavādakā), held wrong view ( micchādiṭṭhi), and undertook actions governed by wrong view ( micchādiṭṭhikammasamādānā). At the breakup of the body, after death, they have reappeared in the realm of ghosts ( pettivisayaṃ upapannā). Or these worthy beings were endowed with bad conduct of body, speech, and mind, were revilers of the noble ones, held wrong view, and undertook actions governed by wrong view. At the breakup of the body, after death, they have reappeared in the animal world ( tiracchānayoniṃ upapannā). Or these worthy beings were endowed with bad conduct of body, speech, and mind, were revilers of the noble ones, held wrong view, and undertook actions governed by wrong view. At the breakup of the body, after death, they have reappeared in a state of deprivation, in an unhappy destination, in perdition, even in hell ( apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapannā).’”

262. “Then, monks, the hell-wardens, seizing him by the arms, present him to Yama, the king, [saying]: ‘Sire, this man has not treated his mother well (amatteyyo), has not treated his father well (apetteyyo), has not treated ascetics well (asāmañño), has not treated Brahmins well (abrāhmañño), and had no respect for the elders in his family (kule jeṭṭhāpacāyī).

Let the king order his punishment (imassa devo daṇḍaṃ paṇetū’ti).’

Then, monks, Yama, the king, questions him about the first divine messenger (devadūta), interrogating and cross-examining him: ‘Good man (ambho purisa), did you never see the first divine messenger (paṭhamaṃ devadūtaṃ pātubhūta) appearing among humans (manussesu)’? He replies, ‘I did not see, sir (nāddasaṃ, bhante).’

“Then, monks, Yama, the king, says this to him: ‘Good man (ambho purisa), did you never see among humans a tender (daharaṃ) young boy (kumāraṃ), a weak (mandaṃ) infant (uttānaseyyakaṃ), lying (semānaṃ) soiled (palipannaṃ) in his own urine and excrement (sake muttakarīse)’?” He replies: “I saw, sir (addasaṃ, bhante).”

“Then, monks, Yama, the king, says this to him: ‘Good man (ambho purisa), did it never occur to you – an intelligent (viññussa), mindful (sato), mature (mahallakassa) man – “I, too, am subject to the nature of birth (jātidhammo), I am not exempt from birth (jātiṃ anatīto).
I should do good deeds (kalyāṇaṃ karomi) with body (kāyena), speech (vācāya), and mind (manasā)?”’ He replies, ‘I was not able, sir (nāsakkhissaṃ, bhante), I was negligent, sir (pamādassaṃ, bhante).’”

“Then, monks, Yama, the king, says this to him: ‘Good man (ambho purisa), through negligence (pamādavatāya) you did not do good (na kalyāṇamakāsi) with body (kāyena), speech (vācāya), and mind (manasā).
Indeed (taggha), good man (ambho purisa), they will do to you according to your negligence (yathā taṃ pamattaṃ).

That evil deed (pāpakammaṃ) of yours, was not done by your mother (mātarā kataṃ), nor by your father (pitarā kataṃ), nor by your brother (bhātarā kataṃ), nor by your sister (bhaginiyā kataṃ), nor by your friends and companions (mittāmaccehi kataṃ), nor by your relatives (ñātisālohitehi kataṃ), nor by ascetics and Brahmins (samaṇabrāhmaṇehi kataṃ), nor by the gods (devatāhi kataṃ); but this evil deed (pāpakammaṃ) was done by you alone (tayāva etaṃ kataṃ), and you alone (tvaññeva) will experience its result (vipākaṃ paṭisaṃvedissasi).’”

263. “Then, monks, King Yama, having questioned, examined, and addressed him concerning the first divine messenger, questions, examines, and addresses him about the second divine messenger, saying: ‘Good man, did you not see among humans the second divine messenger appearing?’ He replies, ‘I did not see, venerable sir.’”

“Then, monks, King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, didn't you ever see among humans a woman or a man ( ) (eighty, ninety, or a hundred years old by birth) also in the Tikaṅguttara, frail, bent like a roof bracket, crooked, leaning on a stick, shakily going along, ailing, youth and vigor gone, with broken teeth, with gray hair, scanty hair or bald, wrinkled, with blotched limbs?’ He replies, ‘I did see, venerable sir.’”

“Then, monks, King Yama says: ‘Good man, did it never occur to you – an intelligent and mature man – “I too am subject to aging (jarādharma), I am not exempt from aging (jaraṃ anatīto). “Then, monks, King Yama, after questioning, examining, and cross-examining him about the third divine messenger, questions, examines, and cross-examines him about the fourth divine messenger: ‘Good man, did you not see the fourth divine messenger appear in the world?’ He replies, ‘I did not, venerable sir.’”

“Then, monks, King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did you not see in the world kings taking a criminal, a wrongdoer, and having various tortures inflicted on him—having him flogged with whips, having him beaten with canes, having him beaten with half-sticks, even having his hand cut off, even having his foot cut off, even having both hand and foot cut off, even having his ear cut off, having his nose cut off, having both ear and nose cut off, making him into a bilaṅgathālika, making him into a saṅkhamuṇḍika, making him into a rāhumukha, making him into a jotimālika, making him into a hatthapajjotika, making him into an erakavattika, making him into a cīrakavāsika, making him into an eṇeyyaka, making him into a baḷisamaṃsika, making him into a kahāpaṇika, making him into a khārāpatacchika, making him into a palighaparivattika, making him into a palālapīṭhaka, even pouring hot oil over him, even having him eaten by dogs, even impaling him alive on a stake, even cutting off his head with a sword?’ He replies, ‘I did see it, venerable sir.’”

“Then, monks, King Yama says to him: ‘Good man, did it never occur to you—an intelligent and mature man—"Those who do evil actions have such tortures of various kinds inflicted on them here and now; so what to say of in the hereafter!"? Then, monks, the wardens of hell throw him back again into the Great Hell.

“Once, monks, King Yama thought: ‘Those in the world who do evil, unwholesome actions are punished with such diverse tortures.
Oh, that I might attain the human state!
That a Tathāgata, worthy and perfectly enlightened, might appear in the world!
That I might attend upon that Blessed One!
That the Blessed One might teach me the dharma!
And that I might understand the Blessed One’s dharma!’
Bhikkhus, I tell you this not as something I heard from another śramaṇa or brāhmaṇa. I tell you this as something that I have actually known, seen, and discovered by myself.”

271. The Blessed One said this. Having said this, the Well-Gone One, the Teacher, then said something further:

“Admonished by divine messengers, those youths who are negligent,
Those beings who go to a lower existence grieve for a long time.

“But those noble persons here who are mindful, admonished by divine messengers,
Never are they negligent in the Noble Dharma.

“Seeing fear in clinging, in the arising of birth and death,
Without clinging, they are liberated, at the ending of birth and death.

“They have attained safety, they are happy, they are cooled, having seen the Dharma,
They have gone beyond all fear of hostility, they have gone beyond all suffering.”

The Devadūta Sūtra, the tenth, is finished.

The Suññatā Chapter, the third, is finished.

The summary of it:

Emptiness is twofold, filled with wondrous Dharma;
Aciravata, Bhūmijanāma, Anuruddhupakkilesaṃ;
The fool and the wise, and the divine messengers, these are the ten.
