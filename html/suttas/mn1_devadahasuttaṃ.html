1. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sakkesu viharati devadahaṃ nāma sakyānaṃ nigamo. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘santi, bhikkhave, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – ‘yaṃ kiñcāyaṃ purisapuggalo paṭisaṃvedeti sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā, sabbaṃ taṃ pubbekatahetu. Iti purāṇānaṃ kammānaṃ tapasā byantībhāvā, navānaṃ kammānaṃ akaraṇā, āyatiṃ anavassavo; āyatiṃ anavassavā kammakkhayo; kammakkhayā dukkhakkhayo; dukkhakkhayā vedanākkhayo; vedanākkhayā sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī’ti. Evaṃvādino, bhikkhave, nigaṇṭhā. ‘‘Evaṃvādāhaṃ , bhikkhave, nigaṇṭhe upasaṅkamitvā evaṃ vadāmi – ‘saccaṃ kira tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, evaṃvādino evaṃdiṭṭhino – yaṃ kiñcāyaṃ purisapuggalo paṭisaṃvedeti sukhaṃ vā dukkhaṃ vā adukkhamasukhaṃ vā, sabbaṃ taṃ pubbekatahetu. Iti purāṇānaṃ kammānaṃ tapasā byantībhāvā, navānaṃ kammānaṃ akaraṇā, āyatiṃ anavassavo; āyatiṃ anavassavā kammakkhayo; kammakkhayā dukkhakkhayo; dukkhakkhayā vedanākkhayo; vedanākkhayā sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī’ti? Te ca me, bhikkhave, nigaṇṭhā evaṃ puṭṭhā ‘āmā’ti paṭijānanti. ‘‘Tyāhaṃ evaṃ vadāmi – ‘kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – ahuvamheva mayaṃ pubbe, na nāhuvamhā’ti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’. ‘‘‘Kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – akaramheva mayaṃ pubbe pāpakammaṃ, na nākaramhā’ti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’. ‘‘‘Kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – evarūpaṃ vā evarūpaṃ vā pāpakammaṃ akaramhā’ti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’. ‘‘‘Kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – ettakaṃ vā dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ, ettakaṃ vā dukkhaṃ nijjīretabbaṃ, ettakamhi vā dukkhe nijjiṇṇe sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī’ti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’. ‘‘‘Kiṃ pana tumhe, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, jānātha – diṭṭheva dhamme akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānaṃ, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ upasampada’nti? ‘No hidaṃ, āvuso’.
1. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling among the Sakyans where there was a town of the Sakyans named Devadaha. There the Fortunate One addressed the monks (saying): "Monks." "Venerable sir," those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this: "Bhikkhus, there are some recluses and brāhmaṇas who hold such a doctrine and view as this: 'Whatever this person (purisapuggalo) feels, whether pleasure (sukhaṃ) or pain (dukkhaṃ) or neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukhaṃ), all that is caused by what was done in the past (pubbekatahetu). So by annihilating with asceticism past actions (purāṇānaṃ kammānaṃ tapasā byantībhāvā) and by doing no fresh actions (navānaṃ kammānaṃ akaraṇā), there will be no consequence in the future (āyatiṃ anavassavo); with no consequence in the future, there is the destruction of action (kammakkhayo); with the destruction of action, there is the destruction of suffering (dukkhakkhayo); with the destruction of suffering, there is the destruction of feeling (vedanākkhayo); with the destruction of feeling, all suffering will be exhausted (sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī)’ti. So speak the Niganthas (nigaṇṭhā), bhikkhus."

"Bhikkhus, I go to the Niganthas who speak thus and I say: ‘Friend Niganthas, is it true that you hold such a doctrine and view as this: “Whatever this person feels...all suffering will be exhausted”’ti? And when the Niganthas are questioned by me in this way, they acknowledge ‘Yes’."

"Then I say this: ‘But, friends, do you Niganthas, know that we existed in the past, and that it is not the case that we did not exist (ahuvamheva mayaṃ pubbe, na nāhuvamhā)?’ ‘No, friend.’"

"‘But, friends, do you Niganthas, know that we did evil actions in the past, and did not abstain from them (akaramheva mayaṃ pubbe pāpakammaṃ, na nākaramhā)?’ ‘No, friend.’"

"‘But, friends, do you Niganthas, know that we did such and such kind of evil action (evarūpaṃ vā evarūpaṃ vā pāpakammaṃ akaramhā)?’ ‘No, friend.’"

"‘But, friends, do you Niganthas, know that so much suffering has already been exhausted (ettakaṃ vā dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ), or that so much suffering has still to be exhausted (ettakaṃ vā dukkhaṃ nijjīretabbaṃ), or that when so much suffering has been exhausted all suffering will have been exhausted (ettakamhi vā dukkhe nijjiṇṇe sabbaṃ dukkhaṃ nijjiṇṇaṃ bhavissatī)?’ ‘No, friend.’"

"‘But, friends, do you Niganthas, know that in this very life there is abandonment of unwholesome states and the attainment of wholesome states (diṭṭheva dhamme akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānaṃ, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ upasampada)?’ ‘No, friend.’"
1.Evaṃme sutanti devadahasuttaṃ. Tattha devadahaṃ nāmāti devā vuccanti rājāno, tattha ca sakyarājūnaṃ maṅgalapokkharaṇī ahosi pāsādikā ārakkhasampannā, sā devānaṃ dahattā ‘‘devadaha’’nti paññāyittha. Tadupādāya sopi nigamo devadahantveva saṅkhaṃ gato. Bhagavā taṃ nigamaṃ nissāya lumbinivane viharati. Sabbaṃ taṃ pubbekatahetūti pubbe katakammapaccayā. Iminā kammavedanañca kiriyavedanañca paṭikkhipitvā ekaṃ vipākavedanameva sampaṭicchantīti dasseti. Evaṃ vādino, bhikkhave, nigaṇṭhāti iminā pubbe aniyametvā vuttaṃ niyametvā dasseti. Ahuvamhevamayanti idaṃ bhagavā tesaṃ ajānanabhāvaṃ jānantova kevalaṃ kalisāsanaṃ āropetukāmo pucchati. Ye hi ‘‘mayaṃ ahuvamhā’’tipi na jānanti, te kathaṃ kammassa katabhāvaṃ vā akatabhāvaṃ vā jānissanti. Uttaripucchāyapi eseva nayo. Ahuvamheva maya
Thus have I heard.
This is the Devadaha Sutta.
There, devadahaṃ nāma means, devā are called kings, and there was a beautiful and well-protected auspicious lotus pond of the Sakya kings there, and because it belonged to the gods, it was known as "Devadaha."
Because of this, that town also came to be known as Devadaha.
The Blessed One, relying on that town, dwells in the Lumbini Grove.
Sabbaṃ taṃ pubbekatahetū means because of past actions.
By this, he shows that they accept only one result of action, rejecting both karmic feeling and action feeling.
Evaṃ vādino, bhikkhave, nigaṇṭhā by this, having previously spoken without restriction, he shows with restriction.

Ahuvamheva maya this the Blessed One, knowing their state of not knowing, asks only wishing to impose the stain teaching.
For those who do not even know "we were," how will they know whether an action has been done or not done?
The same rule applies to further questions.

2. “So, friends, it seems that you do not know that you existed in the past and that it is not the case that you did not exist; or that you did evil actions (pāpakammaṃ) in the past and did not abstain from them; or that you did such and such evil actions; or that so much suffering (dukkhaṃ) has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering (dukkhaṃ) has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering (dukkhaṃ) has been exhausted all suffering (dukkhaṃ) will have been exhausted; or what the abandoning of unwholesome states (akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ) is and what the cultivation of wholesome states (kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ) is; that being so, it is not fitting for the venerable Niganthas to declare: “Whatever this person feels, whether pleasure (sukhaṃ) or pain (dukkhaṃ) or neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukhaṃ), all that is caused by what was done in the past (pūrbekatahetu).” So by annihilating with asceticism past actions (kammānaṃ) and by doing no fresh actions, there will be no consequence in the future. With no consequence in the future, there is the destruction of action (kammakkhayo). With the destruction of action (kammakkhayo), there is the destruction of suffering (dukkhakkhayo). With the destruction of suffering (dukkhakkhayo), there is the destruction of feeling (vedanākkhayo). With the destruction of feeling (vedanākkhayo), all suffering (dukkhaṃ) will be exhausted.”

2.Evaṃ santeti cūḷadukkhakkhandhe (ma. ni. 1.179-180) mahānigaṇṭhassa vacane sacce santeti attho, idha pana ettakassa ṭhānassa tumhākaṃ ajānanabhāve santeti attho. Na kallanti na yuttaṃ.
"evaṃ sante" means in the Cūḷadukkhakkhandha (in the Great Collection of Discourses, 1. 179-180), "in the words of the great Nigaṇṭha, there is truth," this is the meaning. Here, however, the meaning is, "In your state of not knowing this much, there is..."
"na kalla" means it is not fitting.

3. ‘‘Seyyathāpi, āvuso nigaṇṭhā, puriso sallena viddho assa savisena gāḷhūpalepanena gāḷhapalepanena (ka.); so sallassapi vedhanahetu vedanāhetu (sī. pī. ka.) dukkhā tibbā tippā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) kaṭukā vedanā vediyeyya. 3. “Just as, friend Nigaṇṭhas, suppose a man were wounded by an arrow thickly smeared with poison; and because of this arrow he would feel painful, racking, piercing feelings. His friends and companions, his kinsmen and relatives, would bring a physician (bhisakkaṃ sallakattaṃ) to treat him. The physician, the surgeon (sallakatto), would cut around the opening of the wound with a knife; and because of cutting around the opening of the wound with a knife he would feel painful, racking, piercing feelings. The physician, the surgeon, would probe for the arrow with a probe; and because of probing for the arrow with a probe he would feel painful, racking, piercing feelings. The physician, the surgeon, would pull out the arrow; and because of pulling out the arrow he would feel painful, racking, piercing feelings. The physician, the surgeon, would apply a medicinal cauteriser (agadaṅgāraṃ) to the opening of the wound; and because of applying a medicinal cauteriser to the opening of the wound he would feel painful, racking, piercing feelings. Then on a later occasion, when the wound was healed and covered with skin, the man would be well and happy, independent, master of himself, able to go where he likes. He might think: “Formerly I was pierced by an arrow thickly smeared with poison, and because of this arrow I felt painful, racking, piercing feelings. Then my friends and companions, my kinsmen and relatives, brought a physician, a surgeon, to treat me. The physician, the surgeon, cut around the opening of the wound with a knife; and because of cutting around the opening of the wound with a knife I felt painful, racking, piercing feelings. The physician, the surgeon, probed for the arrow with a probe; and because of probing for the arrow with a probe I felt painful, racking, piercing feelings. The physician, the surgeon, pulled out the arrow; and because of pulling out the arrow I felt painful, racking, piercing feelings. The physician, the surgeon, applied a medicinal cauteriser to the opening of the wound; and because of applying a medicinal cauteriser to the opening of the wound I felt painful, racking, piercing feelings. But now that the wound is healed and covered with skin, I am well and happy, independent, my own master, able to go where I like.”

“So too, friend Nigaṇṭhas, if you knew that you existed in the past and that it is not the case that you did not exist; or that you did evil actions in the past and did not abstain from them; or that you did such and such evil actions; or that so much suffering (dukkhaṃ) has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering (dukkhaṃ) has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering (dukkhaṃ) has been exhausted all suffering (dukkhaṃ) will have been exhausted; or what the abandoning of unwholesome states is and what the cultivation of wholesome states is here and now; that being so, it would be fitting for the venerable Nigaṇṭhas to declare: “Whatever this person feels, whether pleasure (sukhaṃ) or pain (dukkhaṃ) or neither-pain-nor-pleasure (adukkhamasukhaṃ), all that is caused by what was done in the past. So by annihilating with asceticism past actions and by doing no fresh actions, there will be no consequence in the future. With no consequence in the future, there is the destruction of action. With the destruction of action, there is the destruction of suffering (dukkhaṃ). With the destruction of suffering (dukkhaṃ), there is the destruction of feeling (vedanā). With the destruction of feeling (vedanā), all suffering (dukkhaṃ) will be exhausted.”
“But since you, friends, the Nigaṇṭhas, do not know that we existed in the past and that it is not the case that we did not exist; you do not know that we did evil actions ( pāpakamma) in the past and did not abstain from them; you do not know that we did such and such evil actions; you do not know that so much suffering (dukkhaṃ ) has already been exhausted, or that so much suffering has still to be exhausted, or that when so much suffering has been exhausted all suffering will be exhausted; you do not know what the abandoning of unwholesome states (akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānaṃ), is and what the attainment (upasampadaṃ) of wholesome states (kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ) is here and now; therefore, it is not fitting for the venerable Nigaṇṭhas to declare: “Whatever this person feels, whether it is pleasure (sukhaṃ), pain (dukkhaṃ), or neither-pleasure-nor-pain (adukkhamasukhaṃ), all of that is due to past actions.”

7. Diṭṭhadhammavedanīya means yielding its result in this very existence.

Upakkamenā means by effort.

Padhānenā means by energy (vīriyena).

Samparāyavedanīya means yielding its result in the second or third existence.

Sukhavedanīya means wholesome karma (kusalakammaṃ) that yields a desirable object.

The opposite is dukkhavedanīya.

Paripakkavedanīya means experienceable in a fully ripened, accomplished existence; this is another term for diṭṭhadhammavedanīya.

Aparipakkavedanīya means experienceable in an unripened existence; this is another term for samparāyavedanīya.

Even so, this is the distinction here: that which is done in the first stage of life yields its result in the first, middle, or last stage of life; that which is done in the middle stage of life yields its result in the middle or last stage of life; that which is done in the last stage of life yields its result right there; this is called diṭṭhadhammavedanīya.

Moreover, that which yields its result within seven days is called paripakkavedanīya.

That can be either wholesome or unwholesome.

10. Bhikkhus, how is striving fruitful, and effort successful? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not overwhelmed by suffering and does not overwhelm himself with suffering; and he does not give up the pleasure that accords with the Dhamma, yet he is not infatuated with that pleasure.

10. Having shown the fruitlessness of the efforts of the Nigaṇṭhas, now, in order to show the fruitfulness of effort and energy in the teaching that leads out [of suffering], he says, "And how, monks," etc.

There, "anaddhabhūta" means anadhibhūtaṃ. The state of being a human being is said to be "anadhibhūta" by suffering; that is, he does not overcome or dominate it by suffering. But while employing even that in various kinds of difficult practices, he overcomes it by suffering. Those, however, who have gone forth in the Dispensation, whether they are forest-dwellers or those who live at the foot of a tree, do not overcome it by suffering. For in the teaching that leads out [of suffering], energy is called right effort (sammāvāyāmo).

The Elder, however, said: "A person born in a wealthy family, becoming seven years old, being adorned and prepared, sitting on his father's lap, attaining arahantship when the truth is revealed after the meal is finished in the house and the Saṅgha of monks is performing the anumodana; or, when his parents say, 'Go forth, dear one,' and he says, 'Yes, I will go forth,' being bathed and adorned, being taken to the monastery, having learned the five skins, sitting down and attaining arahantship at the very tip of the razor when his hairs are being removed; or again, a newly ordained monk, having eaten the kājabhattaṃ sent by his parents on the following day with his head anointed with manosilā oil, attaining arahantship while sitting in the monastery: this one does not overcome himself with suffering."

This is an excessive honor.

But the one born in a slave's womb, even wearing a silver ring, anointing his body with gorakapiyaṅgu-sized pieces, being brought and [told] "Let him go forth," attaining arahantship at the tip of the razor or on the following day, this one also does not overcome himself, who is not already overcome, with suffering.

"Dhammikaṃ sukhaṃ" means the four-necessity happiness that arises from the Saṅgha or a group. "Anadhimucchito" means not infatuated by craving-infatuation. For one should not be greedy there, [thinking,] "I will not abandon justifiable happiness." For a salākabhattaṃ or vassāvāsikaṃ that arises from the Saṅgha, after determining, "This is for that purpose," while partaking of it among the monks in the midst of the Saṅgha, he grows with sīla, samādhi, vipassanā, magga, and phala, like a lotus in a bowl. "Imassā" [means] of this, the root of the five aggregates of the present. "Dukkhanidānassā" [means] of craving (taṇhā). For that is the cause of the suffering of the five aggregates. "Saṅkhāraṃ padahato" [means] of one who is making effort of application. "Virāgo hotī" means virāga arises through the path. This has been said: "Through the exertion of effort, virāga arises for me from this cause of suffering." "Evaṃ pajānātīti" [means] in this way, the sukha-paṭipadā khippābhiññā has been spoken of. In the second statement, the middleness of that effort of application has been spoken of. "So yassa hi khvāssā" Here, this is the condensed meaning: That person for whom virāga arises from the cause of suffering through the exertion of effort, he exerts effort there, he exerts effort through the path-exertion. But for whomever virāga arises from the cause of suffering through overlooking while developing equanimity, he develops equanimity there, he develops it through the development of the path. "Tassā" [means] of that person.

11. “Suppose, bhikkhus, a man loved a woman with his mind bound to her by intense desire and passion. He might see that woman standing with another man, chatting, joking, and laughing. Would not sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair arise in that man when he sees that woman standing with another man, chatting, joking, and laughing?” “Yes, venerable sir.” “Why is that?” “Because that man loves that woman with his mind bound to her by intense desire and passion. Therefore, sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair would arise in him when he sees that woman standing with another man, chatting, joking, and laughing.” “Then, bhikkhus, the man might think: ‘I love this woman with my mind bound to her by intense desire and passion. Thus sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair arise in me when I see her standing with another man, chatting, joking, and laughing. What if I were to abandon my desire and lust for that woman?’ He would abandon his desire and lust for that woman. On a later occasion he might see that woman standing with another man, chatting, joking, and laughing. What do you think, bhikkhus? Would sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair arise in that man when he sees that woman standing with another man, chatting, joking, and laughing?” “No, venerable sir.” “Why is that?” “Because that man no longer loves that woman. that is why sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair do not arise in him when he sees that woman standing with another man…”

Sā kenaci upāyena tena saddhiṃ santhavaṃ kātuṃ asakkontī naṭanaccakādīhi saddhiṃ vicarati. Tassa purisassa taṃ disvā neva uppajjati domanassaṃ, somanassaṃ pana uppajjati. Evameva kho, bhikkhave Tattha purisassa itthiyā sārattakālo viya imassa bhikkhuno attabhāve ālayo. Ghāsacchādanādīni datvā ghare vasāpanakālo viya attabhāvassa paṭijagganakālo. Tassā aticaraṇakālo viya jaggiyamānasseva attabhāvassa pittapakopādīnaṃ vasena sābādhatā. ‘‘Attano anurūpaṃ sakkāraṃ alabhantī aticaratī’’ti sallakkhetvā sakkāravaḍḍhanaṃ viya ‘‘bhesajjaṃ alabhanto evaṃ hotī’’ti sallakkhetvā bhesajjakaraṇakālo. Sakkāre vaḍḍhitepi puna aticaraṇaṃ viya pittādīsu ekassa bhesajje kariyamāne sesānaṃ pakopavasena puna sābādhatā. Parisamajjhe alaṃvacanīyaṃ katvā gehā nikkaḍḍhanaṃ viya ‘‘idāni te nāhaṃ dāso na kammakaro, anamatagge saṃsāre taṃyeva upaṭṭhahanto vicariṃ, ko me tayā attho, chijja vā bhijja vā’’ti tasmiṃ anapekkhataṃ āpajjitvā vīriyaṃ thiraṃ katvā maggena kilesasamugghātanaṃ. Naṭanaccakādīhi naccamānaṃ vicarantiṃ disvā yathā tassa purisassa domanassaṃ na uppajjati, somanassameva uppajjati, evameva imassa bhikkhuno arahattaṃ pattassa pittapakopādīnaṃ vasena ābādhikaṃ attabhāvaṃ disvā domanassaṃ na uppajjati, ‘‘muccissāmi vata khandhaparihāradukkhato’’ti somanassameva uppajjatīti. Ayaṃ pana upamā ‘‘paṭibaddhacittassa domanassaṃ uppajjati, appaṭibaddhacittassa natthetanti ñatvā itthiyā chandarāgaṃ pajahati, evamayaṃ bhikkhu saṅkhāraṃ vā padahantassa upekkhaṃ vā bhāventassa dukkhanidānaṃ pahīyati, no aññathāti ñatvā tadubhayaṃ sampādento dukkhanidānaṃ pajahatī’’ti etamatthaṃ vibhāvetuṃ āgatāti veditabbā.
11. paṭibaddhacitto: the mind bound by desire and passion (chanda-rāga).
tibbacchando: intense desire.
tibbāpekkho: intense longing.
santiṭṭhanti: they stand together, they remain in one place.
sañjagghanti: laughing with a great laugh.
saṃhasanti: smiling.

Thus, monks, this is a simile: a man, infatuated with a woman, gives her food, clothing, garlands, ornaments, etc., and keeps her in his house. She, unfaithful to him, consorts with another. He thinks, "Surely, I am not showing her the respect she deserves," and increases his attentions. She becomes even more unfaithful. He thinks, "Even though I am showing her respect, she is still unfaithful. Living in my house, she might even cause me harm. I will get rid of her!" So, in the midst of his assembly, he publicly denounces her, saying, "Don't ever enter my house again!" Unable to form a relationship with him by any means, she consorts with actors, dancers, and the like. Seeing her, that man no longer feels displeasure, but rather pleasure.

Thus, monks, there is an analogy: just as a man is infatuated with a woman, so too is a monk attached to his own existence (attabhāva). Just as he gives her food, clothing, etc., and keeps her in his house, so too does he cherish and protect his own existence. Just as she is unfaithful to him, so too does his own existence become afflicted due to imbalances of bile, phlegm, etc. Just as he thinks, "She is unfaithful because I am not showing her the respect she deserves," and increases his attentions, so too does he think, "I am sick because I am not getting the right medicine," and seeks medical treatment. Just as she is still unfaithful even when he increases his attentions, so too does his own existence remain afflicted even when he takes medicine for one ailment, because the other elements are aggravated. Just as he publicly denounces her and throws her out of his house, so too does he, having lost all attachment to his existence, develop detachment, strengthen his resolve, and destroy the defilements through the path, [thinking]: "Now, I am not your slave or servant. For countless lifetimes, I have only been attending to you in this cycle of existence (saṃsāra). What is the use of you to me? Be destroyed or break apart!" Just as that man feels no displeasure when he sees her consorting with actors, dancers, and the like, but only pleasure, so too does this monk who has attained arahantship feel no displeasure when he sees his own existence afflicted due to imbalances of bile, phlegm, etc., but only pleasure, [thinking]: "I will be freed from the suffering of constantly caring for this body!"

This simile, however, is [meant to show that] just as displeasure arises in one whose mind is bound [by desire], and no displeasure arises in one whose mind is not bound [by desire], having understood this, he abandons desire and passion for women. Likewise, this monk, whether he is giving up compounded things (saṅkhāra) or developing equanimity (upekkhā), the root of suffering ceases. Knowing that it is not otherwise, and accomplishing both of these things, he abandons the root of suffering. It should be understood that [this passage] has come to clarify this meaning.

12. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘yathāsukhaṃ kho me viharato akusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti, kusalā dhammā parihāyanti; dukkhāya pana me attānaṃ padahato akusalā dhammā parihāyanti, kusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ dukkhāya attānaṃ padaheyya’nti. So dukkhāya attānaṃ padahati. Tassa dukkhāya attānaṃ padahato akusalā dhammā parihāyanti kusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti. So na aparena samayena dukkhāya attānaṃ padahati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Yassa hi so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu atthāya dukkhāya attānaṃ padaheyya svāssa attho abhinipphanno hoti. Tasmā na aparena samayena dukkhāya attānaṃ padahati. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, usukāro tejanaṃ dvīsu alātesu ātāpeti paritāpeti ujuṃ karoti kammaniyaṃ. Yato kho, bhikkhave, usukārassa tejanaṃ dvīsu alātesu ātāpitaṃ hoti paritāpitaṃ ujuṃ kataṃ ujuṃ kataṃ hoti (sī.) kammaniyaṃ, na so taṃ aparena samayena usukāro tejanaṃ dvīsu alātesu ātāpeti paritāpeti ujuṃ karoti kammaniyaṃ. Taṃ kissa hetu? Yassa hi so, bhikkhave, atthāya usukāro tejanaṃ dvīsu alātesu ātāpeyya paritāpeyya ujuṃ kareyya kammaniyaṃ svāssa attho abhinipphanno hoti. Tasmā na aparena samayena usukāro tejanaṃ dvīsu alātesu ātāpeti paritāpeti ujuṃ karoti kammaniyaṃ. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘yathāsukhaṃ kho me viharato akusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti, kusalā dhammā parihāyanti; dukkhāya pana me attānaṃ padahato akusalā dhammā parihāyanti, kusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ dukkhāya attānaṃ padaheyya’nti. So dukkhāya attānaṃ padahati. Tassa dukkhāya attānaṃ padahato akusalā dhammā parihāyanti, kusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti. So na aparena samayena dukkhāya attānaṃ padahati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Yassa hi so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu atthāya dukkhāya attānaṃ padaheyya svāssa attho abhinipphanno hoti. Tasmā na aparena samayena dukkhāya attānaṃ padahati. Evampi, bhikkhave, saphalo upakkamo hoti, saphalaṃ padhānaṃ.
“Again, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu considers thus: ‘While I live according to my pleasure, unwholesome states (akuśalā dharmā) increase in me and wholesome states (kuśalā dharmā) diminish; but when I exert myself in what is painful (duḥkhāya), unwholesome states (akuśalā dharmā) diminish in me and wholesome states (kuśalā dharmā) increase. What if I exert myself in what is painful (duḥkhāya)?’”

He exerts himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya). When he exerts himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya), unwholesome states (akuśalā dharmā) diminish and wholesome states (kuśalā dharmā) increase. At a later time he does not exert himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya). Why is that? The purpose for which that bhikkhu exerted himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya) has been achieved. That is why at a later time he does not exert himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya).

“Suppose, bhikkhus, an arrowsmith were warming and heating an arrow shaft between two flames, making it straight and workable. When the arrow shaft had been warmed and heated between the two flames and had been made straight and workable, then at a later time he would not again warm and heat the arrow shaft and make it straight and workable. Why is that? The purpose for which that arrowsmith warmed and heated the arrow shaft between the two flames and made it straight and workable has been achieved. That is why at a later time he would not again warm and heat the arrow shaft and make it straight and workable.

“So too, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu considers thus: ‘While I live according to my pleasure, unwholesome states (akuśalā dharmā) increase in me and wholesome states (kuśalā dharmā) diminish; but when I exert myself in what is painful (duḥkhāya), unwholesome states (akuśalā dharmā) diminish in me and wholesome states (kuśalā dharmā) increase. What if I exert myself in what is painful (duḥkhāya)?’”

He exerts himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya). When he exerts himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya), unwholesome states (akuśalā dharmā) diminish and wholesome states (kuśalā dharmā) increase. At a later time he does not exert himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya). Why is that? The purpose for which that bhikkhu exerted himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya) has been achieved. That is why at a later time he does not exert himself in what is painful (duḥkhāya). Thus, bhikkhus, the exertion is fruitful, the striving is fruitful.
12.Yathā sukhaṃ kho me viharatoti yena sukhena viharituṃ icchāmi tena, me viharato. Padahatoti pesentassa. Ettha ca yassa sukhā paṭipadā asappāyā, sukhumacīvarāni dhārentassa pāsādike senāsane vasantassa cittaṃ vikkhipati, dukkhā paṭipadā sappāyā, chinnabhinnāni thūlacīvarāni dhārentassa susānarukkhamūlādīsu vasantassa cittaṃ ekaggaṃ hoti, taṃ sandhāyetaṃ vuttaṃ. Evameva khoti ettha idaṃ opammasaṃsandanaṃ, usukāro viya hi jātijarāmaraṇabhīto yogī daṭṭhabbo, vaṅkakuṭilajimhatejanaṃ viya vaṅkakuṭilajimhacittaṃ, dve alātā viya kāyikacetasikavīriyaṃ, tejanaṃ ujuṃ karontassa kañjikatelaṃ viya saddhā, namanadaṇḍako viya lokuttaramaggo, ussukārassa vaṅkakuṭilajimhatejanaṃ kañjikatelena sinehetvā alātesu tāpetvā namanadaṇḍakena ujukaraṇaṃ viya imassa bhikkhuno vaṅkakuṭilajimhacittaṃ saddhāya sinehetvā kāyikacetasikavīriyena tāpetvā lokuttaramaggena ujukaraṇaṃ, usukārasseva evaṃ ujukatena tejanena sapattaṃ vijjhitvā sampattianubhavanaṃ viya imassa yogino tathā ujukatena cittena kilesagaṇaṃ vijjhitvā pāsādike senāsane nirodhavaratalagatassa phalasamāpattisukhānubhavanaṃ daṭṭhabbaṃ. Idha tathāgato sukhāpaṭipadākhippābhiññabhikkhuno, dukkhāpaṭipadādandhābhiññabhikkhuno ca paṭipattiyo kathitā, itaresaṃ dvinnaṃ na kathitā, tā kathetuṃ imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi. Imāsu vā dvīsu kathitāsu itarāpi kathitāva honti, āgamanīyapaṭipadā pana na kathitā, taṃ kathetuṃ imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi. Sahāgamanīyāpi vā paṭipadā kathitāva, adassitaṃ pana ekaṃ buddhuppādaṃ dassetvā ekassa kulaputtassa nikkhamanadesanaṃ arahattena vinivaṭṭessāmīti dassetuṃ imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Evameva kho Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Devadahasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
12. "Just as happiness for me while dwelling [thus]" means that with which happiness I desire to dwell, with that, for me while dwelling.

"Because of striking" means because of sending.

Here, the paṭipadā (path or practice) that is pleasant ( sukhā) but unsuitable (asappāyā) for someone who wears fine robes (sukhumacīvarāni), dwelling in a delightful monastery (pāsādike senāsane), scatters the mind (cittaṃ vikkhipati). The paṭipadā that is painful (dukkhā) but suitable (sappāyā) for someone who wears torn and coarse robes (chinnabhinnāni thūlacīvarāni), dwelling at the root of a cemetery tree (susānarukkhamūlādīsu), makes the mind one-pointed (cittaṃ ekaggaṃ hoti). Having this in mind, this was said (taṃ sandhāyetaṃ vuttaṃ).

"Just so, indeed" here, this is a connection through simile (opammasaṃsandanaṃ). The yogi (yogī) who is frightened by birth, aging, and death (jātijarāmaraṇabhīto) should be seen (daṭṭhabbo) like an arrow-maker (usukāro). The crooked, bent, and curved shaft (vaṅkakuṭilajimhatejanaṃ) [is] like the crooked, bent, and curved mind (vaṅkakuṭilajimhacittaṃ). The two bellows (dve alātā) are like physical and mental effort (kāyikacetasikavīriyaṃ). Faith (saddhā) is like the kañjika oil for one straightening the shaft (tejanaṃ ujuṃ karontassa kañjikatelaṃ viya). The lokuttara-magga (the path that is transcendent) is like the bending tool (namanadaṇḍako viya). Just as the arrow-maker, having moistened the crooked, bent, and curved shaft with kañjika oil (kāñjikatelena sinehetvā), having heated it in the bellows (alātesu tāpetvā), straightens it with the bending tool (namanadaṇḍakena ujukaraṇaṃ viya), so too, this bhikkhu, having moistened the crooked, bent, and curved mind with faith (saddhāya sinehetvā), having heated it through physical and mental effort (kāyikacetasikavīriyena tāpetvā), straightens it with the lokuttara-magga. Just as the arrow-maker pierces the target (sapattaṃ vijjhitvā) and experiences attainment (sampattianubhavanaṃ) with the shaft straightened in this way (evaṃ ujukatena tejanena), so too this yogi, having pierced the mass of defilements (kilesagaṇaṃ vijjhitvā) with the mind straightened in this way (tathā ujukatena cittena), the experience of the bliss of fruition-attainment (phalasamāpattisukhānubhavanaṃ) of one who has reached the base of the suppression of disturbances (nirodhavaratalagatassa) in a delightful monastery (pāsādike senāsane) should be seen (daṭṭhabbaṃ).

Here, the Tathāgata spoke of the practices (paṭipattiyo kathitā) of the bhikkhu with pleasant practice and swift insight (sukhāpaṭipadākhippābhiññabhikkhuno) and of the bhikkhu with painful practice and slow insight (dukkhāpaṭipadādandhābhiññabhikkhuno ca). The practices of the other two were not spoken of (itaresaṃ dvinnaṃ na kathitā). To speak of them, he began this teaching (tā kathetuṃ imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi).

Or, having spoken of these two, the others too are spoken of, but the practice that leads to coming (āgamanīyapaṭipadā) was not spoken of. To speak of that, he began this teaching (taṃ kathetuṃ imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi).

Or, the practice that leads to coming together (sahāgamanīyāpi vā paṭipadā kathitāva) was spoken of. Having shown (dassetvā) a single arising of a Buddha (ekaṃ buddhuppādaṃ), to show (dassetuṃ) that I will convert (vinivaṭṭessāmi) the teaching of renunciation (nikkhamanadesanaṃ) of a single son of a good family (ekassa kulaputtassa) into arahatta (worthiness) he began this teaching (imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi).

The rest is clear throughout (sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti).

Just so, indeed.

He makes known—having realized it through direct knowledge—this world with its gods (sadevakaṃ), its Māras (samārakaṃ), & Brahmās (sabrahmakaṃ), its generations with their contemplatives & brahmins (sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiṃ), their rulers & commonfolk (sadevamanussaṃ);

He teaches the Dharma (dhammaṃ deseti) that is good in the beginning (ādikalyāṇaṃ), good in the middle (majjhekalyāṇaṃ), good at the end (pariyosānakalyāṇaṃ), with the meaning and with the expression (sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ), utterly perfect and pure (kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ), he reveals the holy life (brahmacariyaṃ pakāseti).

A householder (gahapati) or a householder's son (gahapatiputto) or one born in some other family, hears that Dharma.

Having heard this Dhamma, he gains faith (saddhaṃ paṭilabhati) in the Tathāgata.

Endowed with such faith (saddhāpaṭilābhena samannāgato), he reflects: ‘The household life is crowded (sambādho gharāvāso), a path of dust (rajāpatho). Going forth is like the open air (abbhokāso pabbajjā).

It is not easy, living the household life (agāraṃ ajjhāvasatā), to live the fully-perfected (ekantaparipuṇṇaṃ) holy life, purified and polished like a conch-shell (ekantaparisuddhaṃ saṅkhalikhitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ carituṃ).

After some time he abandons his accumulation of wealth (bhogakkhandhaṃ pahāya), be it large or small (appaṃ vā mahantaṃ vā); he abandons his circle of relatives (ñātiparivaṭṭaṃ pahāya), be it large or small (appaṃ vā mahantaṃ vā); he shaves off his hair and beard (kesamassuṃ ohāretvā), puts on saffron robes (kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā), and goes forth from home (agārasmā) to homelessness (anagāriyaṃ pabbajati).

20. “If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain due to past actions, then surely, monks, the tathāgata must have done good deeds in the past, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain due to the creative act of a Supreme God, then surely, monks, the tathāgata must have been created by a good Supreme God, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain due to circumstance and nature, then the tathāgata surely must have good luck, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain due to class among the six classes of birth, then the tathāgata surely must belong to a good class, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain are caused by exertion here and now, then the tathāgata surely must strive well here and now, since he now feels such taintless pleasant feelings.

“If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain are caused by what was done in the past, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if not, then the tathāgata is still to be praised. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain are caused by the creative act of a Supreme God, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if not, then the tathāgata is still to be praised. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain are caused by circumstance and nature, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if not, then the tathāgata is still to be praised. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain are caused by class, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if not, then the tathāgata is still to be praised. If, monks, beings feel pleasure and pain are caused by exertion here and now, then the tathāgata is to be praised; if not, then the tathāgata is still to be praised.”

“So the tathāgatas speak, monks. And because the tathāgatas speak thus, there are these ten legitimate grounds for praising them.”

Thus spoke the Lord. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Devadaha Discourse is finished, the first.
