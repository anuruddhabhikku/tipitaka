<p n="160" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">160</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0217"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0142"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘saṅkhārupapattiṃ <note>saṅkhārūpapattiṃ (syā. kaṃ.), saṅkhāruppattiṃ (sī. pī.)</note> vo, bhikkhave, desessāmi, taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

Thus have I heard. Once the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park.

There the Blessed One addressed the monks, saying: "Monks!"

"Venerable sir," those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: "I will teach you about the origination of formations (saṅkhārupapattiṃ). Listen to that, pay close attention, and I will speak."

"Yes, lord," those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said:

<p n="161" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">161</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu saddhāya samannāgato hoti, sīlena samannāgato hoti, sutena samannāgato hoti, cāgena samannāgato hoti, paññāya samannāgato hoti. Tassa evaṃ hoti – ‘aho vatāhaṃ kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā khattiyamahāsālānaṃ <note>khattiyamahāsālānaṃ vā (syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note> sahabyataṃ upapajjeyya’nti. So taṃ cittaṃ dahati, taṃ cittaṃ adhiṭṭhāti, taṃ cittaṃ bhāveti <pb ed="P" n="3.0100"/>. Tassa te saṅkhārā ca vihārā <note>vihāro (sī. pī.)</note> ca evaṃ bhāvitā evaṃ bahulīkatā <pb ed="M" n="3.0141"/> tatrupapattiyā <note>tatrūpapattiyā (syā. kaṃ.), tatruppattiyā (sī. pī.)</note> saṃvattanti. Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, maggo ayaṃ paṭipadā tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati.

“Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is endowed with śraddhā (faith), endowed with śīla (virtue), endowed with śruta (learning), endowed with tyāga (generosity), and endowed with prajñā (wisdom).

It occurs to him: ‘Oh, that on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), I might reappear in the company of wealthy kṣatriyas (khattiyamahāsālānaṃ)!’

He sets his mind on this (cittaṃ dahati), fixes his mind on this (cittaṃ adhiṭṭhāti), and develops this state of mind (cittaṃ bhāveti).

These saṃskāras (mental formations) and abodes of his, cultivated in this way and developed in this way, lead to rebirth there.

This, bhikkhus, is the path (maggo), the way (paṭipadā) that leads to reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati).

It occurs to him: ‘Oh, that on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), I might reappear in the company of well-to-do brāhmaṇas (brāhmaṇamahāsālānaṃ)… or in the company of well-to-do householders (gahapatimahāsālānaṃ)!’

He fixes his mind on that (cittaṃ dahati), establishes it (cittaṃ adhiṭṭhāti), develops it (cittaṃ bhāveti).

These aspirations (saṅkhārā) and this abiding (vihārā) of his, thus developed (bhāvitā) and cultivated (bahulīkatā), lead to his reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattanti).

This, O bhikkhus, is the path (maggo), the way (paṭipadā) that leads to reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati).

165. Furthermore, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu possesses faith (saddhā), possesses virtue (sīla), possesses learning (suta), possesses generosity (cāga), and possesses wisdom (paññā).

He hears that the Brahmā of a Thousand (sahasso brahmā) is long-lived (dīghāyuko), beautiful (vaṇṇavā), and enjoys great happiness (sukhabahulo).

The Brahmā of a Thousand (sahasso brahmā), bhikkhus, abides intent on pervading a world-system of a thousand worlds (sahassilokadhātuṃ).

And he abides intent on pervading the beings that have reappeared there.

Just as, bhikkhus, a man with good sight might take a gallnut in his hand and review it, so the Brahmā of a Thousand (sahasso brahmā) abides intent on pervading a world-system of a thousand worlds (sahassilokadhātuṃ).

And he abides intent on pervading the beings that have reappeared there.

It occurs to him: ‘Oh, that on the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), after death (paraṃ maraṇā), I might reappear in the company of the Brahmā of a Thousand (sahassassa brahmuno sahabyataṃ upapajjeyya)!’

He fixes his mind on that thought (cittaṃ dahati), establishes it (cittaṃ adhiṭṭhāti), and develops that thought (cittaṃ bhāveti).

These saṅkhāras and abidings (vihārā) of his, thus developed (bhāvitā) and cultivated (bahulīkatā), lead to his reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattanti).

This, bhikkhus, is the path (maggo), this the way (paṭipadā) that leads to reappearance there (tatrupapattiyā saṃvattati).
