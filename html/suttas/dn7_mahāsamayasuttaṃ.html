331. Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One (Bhagavān) was dwelling among the Śākyas at Kapilavatthu in the Great Forest, together with a large Saṅgha of approximately five hundred monks, all of them *arhats*. And most of the *devatās* from ten world-systems had gathered in order to see the Blessed One & the Saṅgha of monks.

Then the thought occurred to four *devatās* of the ranks from the Pure Abodes: “The Blessed One (Bhagavān) is dwelling among the Śākyas at Kapilavatthu in the Great Forest, together with a large Saṅgha of about five hundred monks, all of them *arhats*. And most of the *devatās* from ten world-systems have gathered in order to see the Blessed One & the Saṅgha of monks.

Let us also approach the Blessed One (Bhagavān) and, on arrival, let us each speak a verse (gāthā) in his presence.”

332. "bhagavato purato pāturahesuṃ" in the Pāḷi means that they appeared as if descending in the immediate presence of the Blessed One, but this is not the meaning to be understood here.

Those [beings], having reflected on the verses while staying in the Brahma world, one descended at the eastern *cakravāḷa* (world-wheel) gate, one at the southern *cakravāḷa* gate, one at the western *cakravāḷa* gate, and one at the northern *cakravāḷa* gate.

Then, the Brahmā who descended at the eastern *cakravāḷa* gate, having attained blue *kasiṇa* (a device for focusing the mind), emitted blue rays, making it known that he had arrived to the ten-thousand *cakravāḷa* deities as if offering a jeweled garment. Because the path of the Buddha could not be obstructed by anyone, having come to the immediate path of the Buddha, he paid homage to the Blessed One and stood to one side. Standing to one side, he uttered a verse that he had created himself.

The Brahmā who descended at the southern *cakravāḷa* gate, having attained yellow *kasiṇa*, emitted yellow rays, making it known that he had arrived to the ten-thousand *cakravāḷa* deities as if draping a golden cloth. Thus, he stood there.

The Brahmā who descended at the western *cakravāḷa* gate, having attained red *kasiṇa*, emitted red rays, making it known that he had arrived to the ten-thousand *cakravāḷa* deities as if surrounding [them] with a red and excellent blanket. Thus, he stood there.

The Brahmā who descended at the northern *cakravāḷa* gate, having attained white *kasiṇa*, emitted white rays, making it known that he had arrived to the ten-thousand *cakravāḷa* deities as if draping a beautiful cloth. Thus, he stood there.

In the Pāḷi, however, it says, "bhagavato purato pāturahesuṃ. atha kho tā devatā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhaṃsū" (the deities appeared before the Blessed One. Then those deities, having paid homage to the Blessed One, stood to one side). Thus, the appearance in front, the paying of homage, and the standing to one side are spoken of as if [occurring in] a single moment, but it happened in this sequence, and it is shown having been done together. However, the uttering of the verses is spoken of separately, each in its own place, in the Pāḷi.

There, *mahāsamayo* means a great gathering. *pavanaṃ* is said to be a forest grove. With both of these, the Blessed One said, "In this forest grove today is a great gathering, a great assembly."

Then, in order to show what that assembly was, [he said] "*devakāyā samāgatā*," meaning the bodies of deities have assembled. There, *devakāyā* means a multitude of deities. "*āgatamha imaṃ dhammasamaya*," means that having seen the deities assembled in this *dharma*-assembly, we also have come to this *dharma*-gathering.

What is the reason? "*dakkhitāye aparājitasaṅghaṃ*," [means] to see the unconquered *saṃgha* (community). The meaning is that today itself, having crushed the three Māras and won the battle, we have come to see this unconquered *saṃgha*.

That Brahmā, having spoken this verse, paid homage to the Blessed One and stood right there at the eastern *cakravāḷa* gate.

*mahāsamayo pavanaṃ devakāyā samāgatā devakāyā āgatamha imaṃ dhammasamaya dakkhitāye aparājitasaṅghaṃ*

Then, the second one, having come in the manner spoken of, uttered [a verse]. There, "*tatra bhikkhavo*" means the *bhikkhus* (monks) in that place of assembly. "*samādahaṃsū*" means they joined with concentration. "*cittamattano ujukaṃ akaṃsū*" means having removed all the crooked, bent, and dishonest states, they made their own minds straight. "*sārathīva nettāni gahetvā*" Just as a charioteer, having taken all the reins, stands without urging on or restraining the horses that are properly moving along the road, so too, these five hundred *bhikkhus*, endowed with equanimity in six respects, with guarded doors [of the senses], protect their senses, [and are] wise. We have come here to see these Blessed Ones.

He also, having gone, stood right there in his place.

*tatra bhikkhavo samādahaṃsū cittamattano ujukaṃ akaṃsū sārathīva nettāni gahetvā*

Then the third one, having come in the manner spoken of, uttered [a verse]. There, "*chetvā khīla*" means having cut off the stake of passion, hatred, and delusion. "*paligha*" means the very iron bar of passion, hatred, and delusion. "*indakhīla*" also means the very *indakhīla* (pillar of Indra) of passion, hatred, and delusion. "*ūhacca manejā*" means these *bhikkhus*, without desire, having uprooted and completely removed the *indakhīla* due to the absence of the agitation of craving, "*te carantī*" means they wander in all four directions, wandering without obstruction. "*suddhā*" means free from defilements.
*Vimalā* means spotless. This is a synonym for that.

*Cakkhumatā* means with five eyes, endowed with vision.

*Sudantā* means with well-tamed senses, senses that are tamed, from the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body and mind.

*Susunāgā* means young Nāgas.

"They, in this form, have come to see the Blessed One, who is tamed by an unsurpassed teacher of yoga, [taming] young Nāgas."

He went, and stood just where he was before.

Having cut off the stake ( *khīla*), the bar (*paligha*), the post of Indra (*indakhīla*), having drawn out desire (*manejā*), they wander, pure (*śuddhā*), spotless (*vimalā*), endowed with vision (*cakkhumatā*), well-tamed (*sudantā*), young Nāgas (*susunāgā*). Then the fourth one came and spoke in the manner described.

There, *gatāse* means gone by means of going to a refuge free from doubt (*nibbematikasaraṇagamanena gatā*).

He went, and stood just where he was before.

The opposite, however, are called capable (*bhabbā*), and even if they live far away, they go and gather them.

This, moreover, the *Mahāsamayasutta*, by name, is agreeable and pleasing to the deities; therefore, by one speaking auspiciously, in new locations, this very *sutta* should be spoken.

The deities, indeed—"We will listen to this *sutta*,"—with attentive ears, they wander.

At the conclusion of the discourse, moreover, hundreds of thousands of myriads of deities attained *arhatship*; there was no reckoning of those beginning with stream-enterers.

This is a story concerning the agreeable and pleasing nature of it to the deities—in the Koṭipabbata-vihāra, near the Nāgaleṇa-gate, in a *nāgarukkha*, a certain deity-daughter dwells.

A certain young one, inside the cave, recites this *sutta*.

The deity-daughter, having heard [it], at the conclusion of the *sutta*, with a great sound, gave approval.

"Who is that?"

"I, venerable sir, am a deity-daughter."

"Why did you give approval?"

"Venerable sir, having heard this *sutta* on the day it was spoken by the Ten-Powered One, having sat in the great forest, today I heard [it]; without losing even one syllable from what was spoken by the Blessed One, this *dharma* is well-grasped by you."

"Was it heard by you when the Ten-Powered One was speaking?"

"Yes, venerable sir."

"A great assembly of deities, indeed, occurred; where were you standing when you were listening?"

"I, venerable sir, am a deity dwelling in the great forest; when those deities of great power were coming, in Jambudīpa, I did not obtain a place; then, having come to this Tambapaṇṇidīpa, having stood in Jambukolapaṭṭana, I began to listen; there, too, when those deities of great power were coming, successively retreating, in the Rohaṇa-district, from the back part of a great village, in the ocean, having entered water up to my neck, there I stood listening."

"From the place where you were standing, did you see the Teacher from afar, O deity?"

"What are you saying? Venerable sir, the Teacher, teaching the *dharma* in the great forest, constantly regarding only me, thinking, 'She is fearing,' I was hiding in the waves."

"On that day, indeed, hundreds of thousands of myriads of deities attained *arhatship*; did you also, then, attain *arhatship*?"

"No, venerable sir."

"Did you attain the fruit of non-returner ( *anāgāmiphala* ) there, do you think?"

"No, venerable sir."

"Did you attain the fruit of once-returner (*sakadāgāmiphala*) there, do you think?"

"No, venerable sir."

"Three deities who had attained the path, indeed, had passed beyond reckoning; were you one who had arisen as a stream-enterer there, do you think?"

The deity, on that day, having attained the fruit of stream-entry (*sotāpattiphala*), fearing, said—"The venerable one is asking what should not be asked."

Then that monk said to her—"Is it possible, O deity, for you to show us your self-nature (*attabhāva*)?"

"It is not possible, venerable sir, to show my entire body; I will show a portion the size of a finger-joint to the venerable one,"—with a keyhole, he directed his finger toward the cave, it was like the time of the rising of a thousand moons and a thousand suns.

The deity, "Be heedful, venerable sirs," having venerated the young monk, departed.

Thus, this *sutta* is agreeable and pleasing to the deities; the deities cherish it.
