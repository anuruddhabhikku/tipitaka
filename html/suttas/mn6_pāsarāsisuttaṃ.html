272. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pāvisi. Atha kho sambahulā bhikkhū yenāyasmā ānando tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘cirassutā no, āvuso ānanda, bhagavato sammukhā dhammī kathā. Sādhu mayaṃ, āvuso ānanda, labheyyāma bhagavato sammukhā dhammiṃ kathaṃ savanāyā’’ti. ‘‘Tena hāyasmanto yena rammakassa brāhmaṇassa assamo tenupasaṅkamatha; appeva nāma labheyyātha bhagavato sammukhā dhammiṃ kathaṃ savanāyā’’ti. ‘‘Evamāvuso’’ti kho te bhikkhū āyasmato ānandassa paccassosuṃ. Atha kho bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya caritvā pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘āyāmānanda, yena pubbārāmo migāramātupāsādo tenupasaṅkamissāma divāvihārāyā’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato paccassosi. Atha kho bhagavā āyasmatā ānandena saddhiṃ yena pubbārāmo migāramātupāsādo tenupasaṅkami divāvihārāya. Atha kho bhagavā sāyanhasamayaṃ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – ‘‘āyāmānanda, yena pubbakoṭṭhako tenupasaṅkamissāma gattāni parisiñcitu’’nti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato paccassosi.
272. Thus have I heard: At one time, the Blessed One (Bhagavān) was dwelling in Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī), in the Jeta Grove (Jetavana), in Anāthapiṇḍika’s (Anāthapiṇḍadasya) monastery (ārāma).

Then, early in the morning, the Blessed One having dressed, took his bowl and outer robe and went into Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī) for alms (piṇḍāya).

Then a number of monks (bhikkhus) approached the venerable Ānanda; having approached, they said to the venerable Ānanda: “It has been a long time, friend Ānanda, since we have heard a Dhamma talk (dharmī kathā) directly from the Blessed One (bhagavato sammukhā).

It would be good, friend Ānanda, if we might hear a Dhamma talk directly from the Blessed One (bhagavato sammukhā dhammīṃ kathaṃ savanāya)!”

“Then, venerable ones, go to the hermitage (assamo) of the brahmin (brāhmaṇassa) Rammaka; perhaps you might hear a Dhamma talk directly from the Blessed One (bhagavato sammukhā dhammīṃ kathaṃ savanāya)!”

“Yes, friend,” the monks replied to the venerable Ānanda.

Then the Blessed One, having wandered for alms in Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī), after the meal, returning from his alms round, addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Come, Ānanda, let us go to the Eastern Park (pubbārāmo), the Palace of Migāra’s Mother (Migāramātupāsādo), for the day’s abiding (divāvihārāya)!”

“So be it, venerable sir (bhante),” replied the venerable Ānanda to the Blessed One.

Then the Blessed One, together with the venerable Ānanda, went to the Eastern Park (pubbārāmo), the Palace of Migāra’s Mother (Migāramātupāsādo), for the day’s abiding (divāvihārāya).

Then the Blessed One, in the evening, rising from retreat (paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito), addressed the venerable Ānanda: “Come, Ānanda, let us go to the eastern tank (pubbakoṭṭhako) to bathe our limbs (gattāni parisiñcitu)!”

“So be it, venerable sir (bhante),” replied the venerable Ānanda to the Blessed One.
272.Evaṃme sutanti pāsarāsisuttaṃ. Tattha sādhu mayaṃ, āvusoti āyācantā bhaṇanti. Ete kira pañcasatā bhikkhū janapadavāsino ‘‘dasabalaṃ passissāmā’’ti sāvatthiṃ anuppattā. Satthudassanaṃ pana etehi laddhaṃ, dhammiṃ kathaṃ na tāva suṇanti. Te satthugāravena ‘‘amhākaṃ, bhante , dhammakathaṃ kathethā’’ti vattuṃ na sakkonti. Buddhā hi garū honti, ekacāriko sīho migarājā viya, pabhinnakuñjaro viya, phaṇakataāsīviso viya, mahāaggikkhandho viya ca durāsadā vuttampi cetaṃ – ‘‘Āsīviso yathā ghoro, migarājāva kesarī; Nāgova kuñjaro dantī, evaṃ buddhā durāsadā’’ti. Evaṃ durāsadaṃ satthāraṃ te bhikkhū sayaṃ yācituṃ asakkontā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ yācamānā ‘‘sādhu mayaṃ, āvuso’’ti āhaṃsu. Appeva nāmāti api nāma labheyyātha. Kasmā pana thero te bhikkhū ‘‘rammakassa brāhmaṇassa assamaṃ upasaṅkameyyāthā’’ti āha? Pākaṭakiriyatāya. Dasabalassa hi kiriyā therassa pākaṭā hoti; jānāti thero, ‘‘ajja satthā jetavane vasitvā pubbārāme divāvihāraṃ karissati; ajja pubbārāme vasitvā jetavane divāvihāraṃ karissati; ajja ekakova piṇḍāya pavisissati; ajja bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto imasmiṃ kāle janapadacārikaṃ nikkhamissatī’’ti. Kiṃ panassa evaṃ jānanatthaṃ cetopariyañāṇaṃ atthīti? Natthi. Anumānabuddhiyā pana katakiriyāya nayaggāhena jānāti. Yañhi divasaṃ bhagavā jetavane vasitvā pubbārāme divāvihāraṃ kātukāmo hoti, tadā senāsanaparikkhārabhaṇḍānaṃ paṭisāmanākāraṃ dasseti, thero sammajjanisaṅkārachaḍḍanakādīni paṭisāmeti. Pubbārāme vasitvā jetavanaṃ divāvihārāya āgamanakālepi eseva nayo. Appeva nāmā Yadā pana ekako piṇḍāya pavisitukāmo hoti, tadā pātova sarīrapaṭijagganaṃ katvā gandhakuṭiṃ pavisitvā dvāraṃ pidhāya phalasamāpattiṃ appetvā nisīdati. Thero ‘‘ajja bhagavā bodhaneyyabandhavaṃ disvā nisinno’’ti tāya saññāya ñatvā ‘‘ajja, āvuso, bhagavā ekako pavisitukāmo, tumhe bhikkhācārasajjā hothā’’ti bhikkhūnaṃ saññaṃ deti. Yadā pana bhikkhusaṅghaparivāro pavisitukāmo hoti, tadā gandhakuṭidvāraṃ upaḍḍhapidahitaṃ katvā phalasamāpattiṃ appetvā nisīdati, thero tāya saññāya ñatvā pattacīvaraggahaṇatthāya bhikkhūnaṃ saññaṃ deti. Yadā janapadacārikaṃ nikkhamitukāmo hoti, tadā ekaṃ dve ālope atirekaṃ bhuñjati, sabbakālaṃ caṅkamanañcāruyha aparāparaṃ caṅkamati, thero tāya saññāya ñatvā ‘‘bhagavā, āvuso, janapadacārikaṃ caritukāmo, tumhākaṃ kattabbaṃ karothā’’ti bhikkhūnaṃ saññaṃ deti. Bhagavā paṭhamabodhiyaṃ vīsati vassāni anibaddhavāso ahosi, pacchā pañcavīsati vassāni abbokiṇṇaṃ sāvatthiṃyeva upanissāya vasanto ekadivase dve ṭhānāni paribhuñjati. Jetavane rattiṃ vasitvā punadivase bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto dakkhiṇadvārena sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pavisitvā pācīnadvārena nikkhamitvā pubbārāme divāvihāraṃ karoti. Pubbārāme rattiṃ vasitvā punadivase pācīnadvārena sāvatthiṃ piṇḍāya pavisitvā dakkhiṇadvārena nikkhamitvā jetavane divāvihāraṃ karoti. Kasmā? Dvinnaṃ kulānaṃ anukampāya. Manussattabhāve ṭhitena hi anāthapiṇḍikena viya aññena kenaci, mātugāmattabhāve ṭhitāya ca visākhāya viya aññāya itthiyā tathāgataṃ uddissa dhanapariccāgo kato nāma natthi, tasmā bhagavā tesaṃ anukampāya ekadivase imāni dve ṭhānāni paribhuñjati. Tasmiṃ pana divase jetavane vasi, tasmā thero – ‘‘ajja bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ piṇḍāya caritvā sāyanhakāle gattāni parisiñcanatthāya pubbakoṭṭhakaṃ gamissati; athāhaṃ gattāni parisiñcitvā ṭhitaṃ bhagavantaṃ yācitvā rammakassa brāhmaṇassa assamaṃ gahetvā gamissāmi. Evamime bhikkhū bhagavato sammukhā labhissanti dhammakathaṃ savanāyā’’ti cintetvā te bhikkhū evamāha. Migāramātupāsādoti visākhāya pāsādo. Sā hi migārena seṭṭhinā mātuṭṭhāne ṭhapitattā migāramātāti vuccati. Paṭisallānā vuṭṭhitoti tasmiṃ kira pāsāde dvinnaṃ mahāsāvakānaṃ sirigabbhānaṃ majjhe bhagavato sirigabbho ahosi. Thero dvāraṃ vivaritvā antogabbhaṃ sammajjitvā mālākacavaraṃ nīharitvā mañcapīṭhaṃ paññapetvā satthu saññaṃ adāsi. Satthā sirigabbhaṃ pavisitvā dakkhiṇena passena sato sampajāno sīhaseyyaṃ upagamma darathaṃ paṭippassambhetvā uṭṭhāya phalasamāpattiṃ appetvā nisīditvā sāyanhasamaye tato vuṭṭhāsi. Taṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ ‘‘paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito’’ti. Migāramātupāsādo migāramātā Paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito Parisiñcitunti yo hi cuṇṇamattikādīhi gattāni ubbaṭṭento mallakamuṭṭhādīhi vā ghaṃsanto nhāyati, so nhāyatīti vuccati. Yo tathā akatvā pakatiyāva nhāyati, so parisiñcatīti vuccati. Bhagavatopi sarīre tathā haritabbaṃ rajojallaṃ nāma na upalimpati, utuggahaṇatthaṃ pana bhagavā kevalaṃ udakaṃ otarati. Tenāha – ‘‘gattāni parisiñcitu’’nti. Pubbakoṭṭhakoti pācīnakoṭṭhako. Parisiñcitu nhāyatī parisiñcatī Pubbakoṭṭhako Sāvatthiyaṃ kira vihāro kadāci mahā hoti kadāci khuddako. Tathā hi so vipassissa bhagavato kāle yojaniko ahosi, sikhissa tigāvuto, vessabhussa aḍḍhayojaniko, kakusandhassa gāvutappamāṇo, koṇāgamanassa aḍḍhagāvutappamāṇo, kassapassa vīsatiusabhappamāṇo, amhākaṃ bhagavato kāle aṭṭhakarīsappamāṇo jāto. Tampi nagaraṃ tassa vihārassa kadāci pācīnato hoti, kadāci dakkhiṇato, kadāci pacchimato, kadāci uttarato. Jetavane gandhakuṭiyaṃ pana catunnaṃ mañcapādānaṃ patiṭṭhitaṭṭhānaṃ acalameva. Cattāri hi acalacetiyaṭṭhānāni nāma mahābodhipallaṅkaṭṭhānaṃ isipatane dhammacakkappavattanaṭṭhānaṃ saṅkassanagaradvāre devorohaṇakāle sopānassa patiṭṭhaṭṭhānaṃ mañcapādaṭṭhānanti. Ayaṃ pana pubbakoṭṭhako kassapadasabalassa vīsatiusabhavihārakāle pācīnadvāre koṭṭhako ahosi. So idānipi pubbakoṭṭhakotveva paññāyati. Kassapadasabalassa kāle aciravatī nagaraṃ parikkhipitvā sandamānā pubbakoṭṭhakaṃ patvā udakena bhinditvā mahantaṃ udakarahadaṃ māpesi samatitthaṃ anupubbagambhīraṃ. Tattha ekaṃ rañño nhānatitthaṃ, ekaṃ nāgarānaṃ, ekaṃ bhikkhusaṅghassa, ekaṃ buddhānanti evaṃ pāṭiyekkāni nhānatitthāni honti ramaṇīyāni vippakiṇṇarajatapaṭṭasadisavālikāni. Iti bhagavā āyasmatā ānandena saddhiṃ yena ayaṃ evarūpo pubbakoṭṭhako tenupasaṅkami gattāni parisiñcituṃ . Athāyasmā ānando udakasāṭikaṃ upanesi. Bhagavā rattadupaṭṭaṃ apanetvā udakasāṭikaṃ nivāsesi. Thero dupaṭṭena saddhiṃ mahācīvaraṃ attano hatthagatamakāsi. Bhagavā udakaṃ otari. Sahotaraṇenevassa udake macchakacchapā sabbe suvaṇṇavaṇṇā ahesuṃ. Yantanālikāhi suvaṇṇarasadhārānisiñcamānakālo viya suvaṇṇapaṭapasāraṇakālo viya ca ahosi. Atha bhagavato nhānavattaṃ dassetvā nhatvā paccuttiṇṇassa thero rattadupaṭṭaṃ upanesi. Bhagavā taṃ nivāsetvā vijjulatāsadisaṃ kāyabandhanaṃ bandhitvā mahācīvaraṃ antantena saṃharitvā padumagabbhasadisaṃ katvā upanītaṃ dvīsu kaṇṇesu gahetvā aṭṭhāsi. Tena vuttaṃ – ‘‘pubbakoṭṭhake gattāni parisiñcitvā paccuttaritvā ekacīvaro aṭṭhāsī’’ti. acalacetiyaṭṭhānāni Evaṃ ṭhitassa pana bhagavato sarīraṃ vikasitakamaluppalasaraṃ sabbapāliphullaṃ pāricchattakaṃ tārāmarīcivikasitaṃ ca gaganatalaṃ siriyā avahasamānaṃ viya virocittha. Byāmappabhāparikkhepavilāsinī cassa dvattiṃsavaralakkhaṇamālā ganthetvā ṭhapitā dvattiṃsacandamālā viya, dvattiṃsasūriyamālā viya, paṭipāṭiyā ṭhapitā dvattiṃsacakkavatti dvattiṃsadevarājā dvattiṃsamahābrahmāno viya ca ativiya virocittha, vaṇṇabhūmināmesā. Evarūpesu ṭhānesu buddhānaṃ sarīravaṇṇaṃ vā guṇavaṇṇaṃ vā cuṇṇiyapadehi vā gāthāhi vā atthañca upamāyo ca kāraṇāni ca āharitvā paṭibalena dhammakathikena pūretvā kathetuṃ vaṭṭatīti evarūpesu ṭhānesu dhammakathikassa thāmo veditabbo.
272. Thus have I heard: the Pāsarāsi Sūtra.

There, while requesting, they speak, "Excellent, friend!"

These approximately five hundred bhikṣus, residents of the countryside, having arrived at Śrāvastī, [thinking,] "We will see the Ten-Powered One."

But the sight of the Teacher was obtained by them. Why then, do they not hear the Dharma?

They, out of reverence for the Teacher, are not able to say, "Venerable sir, may you speak the Dharma to us."

For Buddhas are venerable, like a solitary lion, king of beasts, like a rutting elephant, like a hooded poisonous snake, like a great mass of fire, they are difficult to approach. This was said:

"Just as a poisonous snake is dreadful, the lion is king of beasts;

Just as an elephant is tusked, so are Buddhas difficult to approach."

Thus, those bhikṣus, not being able to ask the Teacher, who is difficult to approach, themselves, requesting the venerable Ānanda, said, "Excellent, friend!"

Perhaps you may obtain [this].

Why then did the Elder say to those bhikṣus, "Go to the hermitage of the brāhmaṇa Rammaka"? Because of manifest action.

For the action of the Ten-Powered One is manifest to the Elder. The Elder knows, "Today the Teacher, having stayed in Jetavana, will spend the day at Pūrvārāma; today, having stayed at Pūrvārāma, he will spend the day at Jetavana; today he, alone, will enter for alms; today, surrounded by the bhikṣu-saṃgha, at this time, he will set out on a tour of the countryside."

Does he have the knowledge of others' minds in order to know thus? No.

But he knows by inference, by the method of grasping the action that has been done.

For on whichever day the Blessed One, having stayed at Jetavana, intends to spend the day at Pūrvārāma, on that day he shows the preparation for the lodging, the Elder arranges the sweeping, the dust, the throwing away [of rubbish], and so on.

The same method applies also at the time of coming to Jetavana for the day's dwelling, having stayed at Pūrvārāma.

Perhaps, when he intends to enter alone for alms, then, early in the morning, having performed the care of his body, having entered the Perfume Chamber, having closed the door, having emerged from fruition-attainment, he sits down.

The Elder, knowing with that perception, "Today the Blessed One, having seen those who are fit to be awakened, is seated," gives the bhikṣus a sign, "Today, friends, the Blessed One intends to enter alone, you should be prepared for the alms round."

When, however, the bhikṣu-saṃgha entourage intends to enter, then, having made the door of the Perfume Chamber half-closed, having emerged from fruition-attainment, he sits down, the Elder, knowing with that perception, gives the bhikṣus a sign for the purpose of taking the bowl and robes.

When he intends to set out on a tour of the countryside, then he eats one or two extra mouthfuls, having mounted the walkway at all times, he walks back and forth, the Elder, knowing with that perception, gives the bhikṣus a sign, "Friends, the Blessed One intends to go on a tour of the countryside, you should do what is to be done."

The Blessed One, during the first awakening, spent twenty years unattached; afterwards, for twenty-five years, dwelling dependent only on crowded Śrāvastī, on one day, he enjoys two places.

Having spent the night in Jetavana, on the following day, surrounded by the bhikṣu-saṃgha, having entered Śrāvastī for alms by the southern gate, having left by the eastern gate, he spends the day at Pūrvārāma.

Having spent the night in Pūrvārāma, on the following day, having entered Śrāvastī for alms by the eastern gate, having left by the southern gate, he spends the day at Jetavana.

Why? Out of compassion for the two families.

For, while abiding in the state of being a human being, there is no gift of wealth, having been made to the Tathāgata, offered by someone else like Anāthapiṇḍika, and by another woman like Visākhā, while abiding in the state of being a woman, therefore the Blessed One, out of compassion for them, on one day, enjoys these two places.

But on that day he stayed in Jetavana, therefore the Elder [thought]: "Today the Blessed One, having wandered for alms in Śrāvastī, in the evening, will go to the former storehouse to bathe his limbs; then, having bathed my limbs, having asked the Blessed One who is standing [there], I will take [him to] the hermitage of the brāhmaṇa Rammaka.

Thus these bhikṣus will obtain the Dharma talk in the presence of the Blessed One for the sake of hearing," thinking thus, he said this to those bhikṣus.

Migāramātupāsāda means Visākhā's mansion.

For she is called Migāramātā because she was placed in the position of a mother by the merchant Migāra.

"Having arisen from seclusion" means that in that mansion, in the middle of the auspicious chambers of the two great disciples, was the Blessed One's auspicious chamber.
The elder opened the door, swept the inner chamber, removed the faded garlands, set out the chair-stool, and signaled the Teacher.

The Teacher entered the auspicious chamber, mindfully and consciously lay down on his right side in the lion's posture, dispelled any discomfort, arose, emerged from fruition attainment, sat down, and then, in the late afternoon, got up.

Referring to that, it was said, "Having arisen from seclusion."

Migāramātupāsāda, Migāramātā, having arisen from seclusion.

"Parisiñcituṃ" means one who bathes by rubbing their body with powder, earth, and so on, or by scrubbing with a bowl-brush and so on, is called "nhāyati".

One who bathes without doing so, simply by nature, is called "parisiñcati".

Even in the Blessed One's body, dirt and grime to be removed do not adhere; however, for the sake of upholding tradition, the Blessed One simply goes down into the water.

Therefore, he said, "To bathe the limbs."

"Pubbakoṭṭhako" means eastern chamber.

Parisiñcituṃ, nhāyati, parisiñcati, pubbakoṭṭhako. In Sāvatthī, it is said that the monastery is sometimes large and sometimes small.

Thus, in the time of Vipassī Buddha, it was one yojana in extent; in the time of Sikhī, three gāvutas; in the time of Vessabhū, half a yojana; in the time of Kakusandha, the measure of a gāvuta; in the time of Koṇāgamana, half a gāvuta; in the time of Kassapa, twenty usabhas; in the time of our Blessed One, it was eight karīsas in extent.

That city, too, of that monastery, was sometimes to the east, sometimes to the south, sometimes to the west, sometimes to the north.

In Jetavana, however, the established place of the four legs of the Perfumed Chamber's couch was unmoving.

These four are indeed named acalacetiyaṭṭhānāni "unmoving shrine places": the place of the Great Bodhi Tree's Platform, the place in Isipatana where the Wheel of the Dharma was set in motion, the place at the gate of Saṅkassa City where the stairway was established at the time of the gods' descent, and the place of the couch's legs.

This eastern chamber, however, in the time of Kassapa, the One with the Ten Powers, in the era of the twenty-usabha monastery, was the chamber at the eastern gate.

Even now, it appears as the eastern chamber.

In the time of Kassapa, the One with the Ten Powers, Aciravatī, surrounding the city and flowing along, reaching the eastern chamber, breaking through with water, created a great water-lake that was level-banked and gradually deep.

There, one bathing place for the king, one for the townsfolk, one for the bhikkhu Sangha, one for the Buddhas—thus, there were separate bathing places, delightful, with sand like scattered silver plates.

Thus, the Blessed One, together with the venerable Ānanda, approached that eastern chamber in this manner to bathe his limbs.

Then the venerable Ānanda offered a water-cloth.

The Blessed One, removing the red upper robe, put on the water-cloth.

The elder, together with the upper robe, made the great robe reach his own hand.

The Blessed One went down into the water.

As soon as he entered the water, the fish and turtles were all the color of gold.

It was like a time of sprinkling with streams of golden essence from water-spouts, and like a time of spreading out golden cloths.

Then, after showing the bathing custom of the Blessed One and having bathed and come back up, the elder offered the red upper robe.

The Blessed One, having worn it, tied on a body-band like a streak of lightning, gathered up the great robe with its extremities, made it like the inside of a lotus bud, took the offered robe in his two ears, and stood there.

Therefore it was said: "Having bathed the limbs in the eastern chamber, having come back up, he stood there in a single robe."

Acalacetiyaṭṭhānāni.

Thus, the body of the Blessed One, standing, with its blossoming lotus and uppala lake, fully adorned on all sides, resembled the pāricchattaka tree, and the sky, with its expanded starlight rays, was as if mocking glory.

And his thirty-two excellent marks, woven into a garland, shone exceedingly, as if thirty-two moon garlands, as if thirty-two sun garlands, as if thirty-two cakravartins arrayed in order, thirty-two devarājas, thirty-two Great Brahmās. This is the ground of color.

In places like these, when speaking of the color of the Buddhas' bodies or the color of their virtues, a skilled dhamma speaker, bringing forward meanings, similes, and reasons from verses or from summaries, is able to fill [the audience] and speak, and in places like these, the strength of the dhamma speaker should be understood.

"jarādhammo" means having the nature of aging (jīraṇasabhāvo).

"byādhidhammo" means having the nature of sickness (byādhisabhāvo).

"maraṇadhammo" means having the nature of death (maraṇasabhāvo).

"sokadhammo" means having the nature of sorrow (socanakasabhāvo).

"saṃkilesadhammo" means having the nature of defilement (saṃkilissanasabhāvo).

"puttabhariya” means sons and wives (puttā ca bhariyā ca).

This method applies everywhere.

"jātarūparajata" here, "jātarūpa" means gold (suvaṇṇaṃ).

"rajata" means whatever is fit for use, such as metal coins (lohamāsakādi).

"jātidhammā hete, bhikkhave, upadhayo" these are called the five aggregates of clinging pertaining to sensual pleasure; he shows that all of these have the nature of birth.

In the sections on sickness, etc., gold and silver are not included, for head diseases, etc., are not called sicknesses of these; nor is death, reckoned as decease, like that of beings, nor does sorrow arise.

But it is taken in the section on defilements, because one is defiled by defilements beginning with desire (ayādīhi pana saṃkilesehi saṃkilissatīti saṃkilesadhammavāre gahitaṃ).

Likewise, because it arises from the seasons, in the section on birth (tathā utusamuṭṭhānattā jātidhammavāre).

And having taken dirt, because of decay, in the section on aging (malaṃ gahetvā jīraṇato jarādhammavāre ca).

Suppose a forest deer is wandering in the forest wilds: he walks confidently, stands confidently, sits confidently, lies down confidently.

What is the reason? He is out of the hunter's range.

So too, quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion.

This bhikkhu is said to have blindfolded Māra, to have become invisible to the Evil One by depriving Māra’s eye of its opportunity.

Again, with the stilling of applied and sustained thought, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the second jhāna, which has self-confidence and singleness of mind without applied and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of concentration.

This bhikkhu is said to have blindfolded Mara…

Again, with the fading away as well of rapture, a bhikkhu abides in equanimity, and mindful and fully aware, still feeling pleasure with the body, he enters upon and abides in the third jhāna, on account of which noble ones announce: ‘He has a pleasant abiding who has equanimity and is mindful.’

This bhikkhu is said to have blindfolded Mara…

Again, with the abandoning of pleasure and pain, and with the previous disappearance of joy and grief, a bhikkhu enters upon and abides in the fourth jhāna, which has neither-pain-nor-pleasure and purity of mindfulness due to equanimity.

Now, because not only householders seek the five sensual pleasures (pañcakāmaguṇa), but also renunciates, when enjoying the four requisites without reflection, [engage in] ignoble seeking through the five sensual qualities; therefore, to show that, he said, "These are the five sensual qualities, bhikkhus," and so forth.

Therein, in the nine kinds of robes, bowls, and so on, four sensual qualities (kāmaguṇa) are obtained, namely, forms cognizable by eye-consciousness (cakkhuviññeyyā rūpātiādayo).

Here, taste is the taste of enjoyment (paribhogarasa).

In agreeable alms food (piṇḍapāta) and medicine, all five are obtained.

In lodging (senāsana), as with robes, there are four.

But here too, taste is the taste of enjoyment (paribhogarasa).

" Ye hi keci, bhikkhave (Those who, bhikkhus)"—why did he begin? Having shown the five sensual qualities (pañca kāmaguṇe), now, lest some should say, "From the time of renunciation, whence comes ignoble seeking? For renunciates, there is only noble seeking," to prevent that, he began this discourse to show that "Even for renunciates, enjoying the four requisites without reflection is ignoble seeking."

There, gadhitā (attached) means attached with the attachment of craving (taṇhāgedhena gadhitā).

Mucchitā (deluded) means deluded by the delusion of craving (taṇhāmucchāya mucchitā).

Ajjhopannā (bound) means overwhelmed by craving (taṇhāya ajjhogāḷhā).

Anādīnavadassāvino (not seeing the danger) means not seeing the danger (ādīnavaṃ apassantā).

Anissaraṇapaññā (lacking the wisdom of escape) means escape is called reflective knowledge (paccavekkhaṇañāṇaṃ).

They are devoid of that.

Now, showing an analogy to establish that meaning, he said, "Just as, bhikkhus," and so forth.

Here, the connection of the analogy should be understood thus: the wandering deer are like śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas; the four requisites are like the snare set in the forest by the hunter; enjoying the four requisites without reflection is like their time for lying down, having covered the heap of snares of that hunter.

The time when the hunter comes, and the deer cannot go anywhere it pleases, is like the time when Māra can do as he pleases with the śramaṇas and brāhmaṇas, their state of having come under the power of Māra—this is the meaning.

But the time when the un-snared deer lies down covering the heap of snares is like the śramaṇas' and brāhmaṇas' reflective enjoyment of the four requisites; the deer's going anywhere it pleases when the hunter comes is like the śramaṇas' and brāhmaṇas' not coming under the power of Māra.

Vissattho (trustful) means fearless (nibbhayo) and without apprehension (nirāsaṅko).

The rest is clear everywhere.

Just as, bhikkhus, trustful.

The commentary on the Pāsarāsi Sutta is finished.
