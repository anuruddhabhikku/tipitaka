272. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘bhikkhavo’’ti. ‘‘Bhadante’’ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca – ‘‘bhaddekarattassa vo, bhikkhave, uddesañca vibhaṅgañca desessāmi. Taṃ suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī’’ti. ‘‘Evaṃ, bhante’’ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Na hi no saṅgaraṃ tena, mahāsenena maccunā. ‘‘Evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ, ahorattamatanditaṃ; Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate muni’’ munīti (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.). munīti (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī) in the Jetavana (Jetavana) in Anāthapindika’s Park.

There the Fortunate One addressed the monks, saying: "Monks!"

"Venerable sir," those monks replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this: "Monks, I will teach you the summary and exposition of one who has had an auspicious day (bhaddekaratta)."

Listen, pay close attention. I will speak.”

“As you say, lord,” the monks responded to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said:

“One should not yearn for the past,
nor be expectant about the future;

For the past is left behind,
and the future has not been reached.

But whoever sees insightfully the present phenomenon (paccuppannaṃ dhammaṃ)
again and again in each case,

Unmoved (asaṃhīraṃ), unshaken (asaṃkuppaṃ),
knowing that, one should cultivate it.

Today the effort must be made;
Tomorrow Death may come, who knows?

There is no bargaining with Mortality (maccunā) and his mighty horde (mahāsenena).

One who abides thus, ardent (ātāpiṃ),
untiring (atanditaṃ) day and night,

That one truly is auspicious (bhaddekarattoti),
so the sage (muni) declares, serene (santo)."

Atītanti atīte pañcakkhandhe. Nānvāgameyyāti taṇhādiṭṭhīhi nānugaccheyya. Nappaṭikaṅkheti taṇhādiṭṭhīhi na pattheyya. Yadatītanti idamettha kāraṇavacanaṃ. Yasmā yaṃ atītaṃ, taṃ pahīnaṃ niruddhaṃ atthaṅgataṃ, tasmā taṃ puna nānugaccheyya. Yasmā ca yaṃ anāgataṃ, taṃ appattaṃ ajātaṃ anibbattaṃ, tasmā tampi na pattheyya. 272. "Thus I have heard" [is the beginning of] the bhaddekarattasutta.

There, bhaddekarattassa means, due to being endowed with the application of insight (vipassanā), [it refers to] the auspicious single night (bhaddakassa ekarattassa).

Uddesa means outline (mātikaṃ).

Vibhaṅga means [a text that is] fit for detailed exposition (vitthārabhājanīyaṃ).

Atīta means the past five aggregates (pañcakkhandhe).

Nānvāgameyyā means one should not go along with it through craving and wrong views (taṇhādiṭṭhīhi nānugaccheyya).

Nappaṭikaṅkhe means one should not long for it through craving and wrong views (taṇhādiṭṭhīhi na pattheyya).

Yadatīta this is a statement of reason here.

Since what is past is abandoned, ceased, and has passed away, therefore one should not go along with it again.

And since what is future is unobtained, unborn, and unproduced, therefore one should not long for that either.

Atīta nānvāgameyyā nappaṭikaṅkhe yadatīta

Tattha tatthā means even with regard to a present object of consciousness (dhammaṃ) wherever it arises, there itself one sees it with the seven contemplations beginning with the contemplation of impermanence (anicānupassanādīhi sattahi anupassanāhi), or in a forest etc., there itself he sees.

Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ this is said in order to show insight and counter-insight.

For insight is not disturbed or shaken by lust etc., thus [it is] asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ, that one should develop and increase, one should practice counter-insight, this is what is meant.

Or alternatively, nirvāṇa is not disturbed or shaken by lust etc., thus [it is] asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ.

That, having known (vidvā) the wise bhikkhu should develop, repeatedly making that its object, increasing it by attaining that and that fruition attainment (phalasamāpattiṃ), this is the meaning.

Tattha tatthā asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppa vidvā

Now, for the sake of one who is developing it: ajjeva kiccamātappa means the energy (vīriyaṃ) that has obtained the name ātappa through the burning and tormenting of the defilements (kilesānaṃ ātāpanaparitāpanena) should be done today itself.

Ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve means who knows whether tomorrow there will be life or death.

For one should generate energy thinking, "Today itself I will give a gift, or observe morality, or do some other wholesome deed." Thus, [he] shows that one should not produce the thought, "There is still time today, tomorrow or the day after tomorrow I will do it," but rather, "I will do it today itself."

Mahāsenena means the many causes of death such as fire, poison, weapons etc. are his army (senā), through that great army, together with such a great army (mahāsenena) of death (maccunā), there is no agreement saying, "Wait a few days until I perform the necessary acts such as the Buddha-puja".

Thus, there is no agreement consisting of friendly conversation, or consisting of giving bribes saying, "Take this hundred or thousand and wait a few days," or consisting of a mass of strength saying, "I will ward it off with this mass of strength."

Saṅgaro is a name for friendly conversation, giving bribes, and a mass of strength; therefore, this meaning is stated.

Ajjeva kiccamātappa ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve mahāsenenā saṅgaro

Atandita means unwearied, energetic.

Because of being one who has undertaken thus, he is an auspicious single night (bhaddekaratto).

Thus, santo buddhamuni indicates that person who has undertaken thus is "an auspicious single night."

Atandita bhaddekaratto santo buddhamuni

273. ‘‘Kathañca , bhikkhave, atītaṃ anvāgameti? ‘Evaṃrūpo ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, ‘evaṃvedano ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, ‘evaṃsañño ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, ‘evaṃsaṅkhāro ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, ‘evaṃviññāṇo ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti – evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, atītaṃ anvāgameti. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, atītaṃ nānvāgameti? ‘Evaṃrūpo ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, ‘evaṃvedano ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, ‘evaṃsañño ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, ‘evaṃsaṅkhāro ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, ‘evaṃviññāṇo ahosiṃ atītamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti – evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, atītaṃ nānvāgameti.

How, monks, does one revive the past? Thinking, ‘I had such material form (rūpa) in the past (atītamaddhāna)’ one finds delight (nandi) in that. Thinking, ‘I had such feeling (vedanā) in the past’…‘I had such perception (saññā) in the past,’…‘I had such formations (saṅkhāra) in the past,’…‘I had such consciousness (viññāṇa) in the past’ one finds delight in that. That is how, monks, one revives the past (atītaṃ anvāgameti).

How, monks, does one not revive the past? Thinking, ‘I had such material form in the past’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I had such feeling in the past’…‘I had such perception in the past,’…‘I had such formations in the past,’…‘I had such consciousness in the past’ one does not find delight in that. That is how, monks, one does not revive the past (atītaṃ nānvāgameti).

274. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati? ‘Evaṃrūpo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti, evaṃvedano siyaṃ…pe… evaṃsañño siyaṃ… evaṃsaṅkhāro siyaṃ… evaṃviññāṇo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhānanti tattha nandiṃ samanvāneti – evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati? ‘Evaṃrūpo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti, evaṃvedano siyaṃ … evaṃsañño siyaṃ… evaṃsaṅkhāro siyaṃ… ‘evaṃviññāṇo siyaṃ anāgatamaddhāna’nti tattha nandiṃ na samanvāneti – evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati.
274. Monks, how does one build up hope upon the future? Thinking, ‘I may have such rūpa (material form) in the future’ one finds delight in that. Thinking, ‘I may have such vedanā (feeling) in the future,’…‘I may have such saṃjñā (perception) in the future,’…‘I may have such saṃskāra (formations) in the future,’…‘I may have such vijñāna (consciousness) in the future,’ one finds delight in that. That is how, monks, one builds up hope upon the future.

Monks, how does one not build up hope upon the future? Thinking, ‘I may have such rūpa (material form) in the future’ one does not find delight in that. Thinking, ‘I may have such vedanā (feeling) in the future,’…‘I may have such saṃjñā (perception) in the future,’…‘I may have such saṃskāra (formations) in the future,’…‘I may have such vijñāna (consciousness) in the future,’ one does not find delight in that. That is how, monks, one does not build up hope upon the future.

275. ‘‘Kathañca, bhikkhave, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīrati? Idha, bhikkhave, assutavā puthujjano ariyānaṃ adassāvī ariyadhammassa akovido ariyadhamme avinīto sappurisānaṃ adassāvī sappurisadhammassa akovido sappurisadhamme avinīto rūpaṃ attato samanupassati, rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ, attani vā rūpaṃ, rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ; vedanaṃ…pe… saññaṃ… saṅkhāre… viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati, viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ attani vā viññāṇaṃ, viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ – evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīrati. ‘‘Kathañca , bhikkhave, paccuppannesu dhammesu na saṃhīrati? Idha, bhikkhave, sutavā ariyasāvako ariyānaṃ dassāvī ariyadhammassa kovido ariyadhamme suvinīto sappurisānaṃ dassāvī sappurisadhammassa kovido sappurisadhamme suvinīto na rūpaṃ attato samanupassati, na rūpavantaṃ vā attānaṃ, na attani vā rūpaṃ, na rūpasmiṃ vā attānaṃ; na vedanaṃ… na saññaṃ… na saṅkhāre… na viññāṇaṃ attato samanupassati, na viññāṇavantaṃ vā attānaṃ, na attani vā viññāṇaṃ, na viññāṇasmiṃ vā attānaṃ – evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, paccuppannesu dhammesu na saṃhīrati. ‘‘Atītaṃ nānvāgameyya, nappaṭikaṅkhe anāgataṃ; Yadatītaṃ pahīnaṃ taṃ, appattañca anāgataṃ. ‘‘Paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ, tattha tattha vipassati; Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppaṃ, taṃ vidvā manubrūhaye. ‘‘Ajjeva kiccamātappaṃ, ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve; Na hi no saṅgaraṃ tena, mahāsenena maccunā. ‘‘Evaṃ vihāriṃ ātāpiṃ, ahorattamatanditaṃ; Taṃ ve bhaddekarattoti, santo ācikkhate munī’’ti. ‘‘‘Bhaddekarattassa vo, bhikkhave, uddesañca vibhaṅgañca desessāmī’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ idametaṃ paṭicca vutta’’nti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. Bhaddekarattasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ paṭhamaṃ.
275. And how, bhikkhus, is one entangled in present phenomena? Here, bhikkhus, an uninstructed ordinary person, who has no regard for the noble ones, is unskilled and undisciplined in the Dhamma of the noble ones, who has no regard for true men, and is unskilled and undisciplined in the Dhamma of true men, regards material form (rūpa) as self (ātman), or self as possessed of material form, or material form as in self, or self as in material form. He regards feeling (vedanā)…perception (saṃjñā)…volitional formations (saṃskāra)…consciousness (vijñāna) as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness – in this way, bhikkhus, one is entangled in present phenomena.

And how, bhikkhus, is one not entangled in present phenomena? Here, bhikkhus, a well-taught noble disciple, who has regard for the noble ones, is skilled and disciplined in their Dhamma, who has regard for true men, and is skilled and disciplined in their Dhamma, does not regard material form as self, or self as possessed of material form, or material form as in self, or self as in material form. He does not regard feeling…nor perception…nor volitional formations…nor consciousness as self, or self as possessed of consciousness, or consciousness as in self, or self as in consciousness – in this way, bhikkhus, one is not entangled in present phenomena.

"One should not revive the past, nor should one yearn for the future;

What is past is left behind, and the future has not yet been reached.

But whoever sees insightfully here and now each phenomenon,

Unwavering, unshaken— knowing it, let him develop it.

Today is the time to act intently; who knows, tomorrow death may come?

There is no bargain to be struck with Death and his mighty hosts."

"The one who dwells thus earnestly, tirelessly, day and night—

That one, truly, is called 'one of auspicious destiny' by the peaceful sage."

"I will teach you, bhikkhus, both the summary and the detailed explanation of the 'Auspicious Destiny'” – it was with reference to this that this was said."

This is what the Blessed One said.
Gratified, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Bhaddekaratta Sutta, the first, is finished.
275.Kathañca, bhikkhave, paccuppannesu dhammesu saṃhīratīti idaṃ ‘‘paccuppannañca yo dhammaṃ, tattha tattha vipassati. Asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppa’’nti uddesassa niddesatthaṃ vuttaṃ. Kāmañcettha ‘‘kathañca, bhikkhave, paccuppannaṃ dhammaṃ na vipassatī’’tiādi vattabbaṃ siyā, yasmā pana asaṃhīrāti ca asaṃkuppāti ca vipassanā vuttā, tasmā tassā eva abhāvañca bhāvañca dassetuṃ saṃhīratīti mātikaṃ uddharitvā vitthāro vutto. Tattha saṃhīratīti vipassanāya abhāvato taṇhādiṭṭhīhi ākaḍḍhiyati. Na saṃhīratīti vipassanāya bhāvena taṇhādiṭṭhīhi nākaḍḍhiyati. Sesaṃ sabbattha uttānamevāti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Bhaddekarattasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
275. How, monks, does one shrink in present circumstances? That is, “whoever sees the present thing, here and there.” asaṃhīraṃ asaṃkuppa is said to be the meaning of the designation of the subject matter.

If it should be said, “How, monks, does one not see the present thing?”, because asaṃhīrāti ca asaṃkuppāti ca vipassanā vuttā, therefore, to show the presence and absence of that very thing, having lifted up the saṃhīratīti mātikaṃ, the expansion is stated.

There, saṃhīratī means, due to the absence of insight, one is drawn by craving and views.

na saṃhīratī means, with the presence of insight, one is not drawn by craving and views.

The rest is clear throughout.
