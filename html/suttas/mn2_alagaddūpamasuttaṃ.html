Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī (Śrāvastī) in the Jetavana (Jeta Grove) at Anāthapiṇḍika’s (Anathapindika’s) monastery.

At that time, a pernicious view had arisen in a monk named Ariṭṭha (Arishta), formerly of the vulture killers (gaddhabādhipubba), thus: “As I understand the Dharma taught by the Blessed One, those things called obstructions (antarāyikā dharmā) by the Blessed One are not able to obstruct one who engages in them.”

Several monks, having heard about this, [said:] “The monk Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers, has such a pernicious view: ‘As I understand the Dharma taught by the Blessed One, those things called obstructions by the Blessed One are not able to obstruct one who engages in them.’”

Then these monks went to the monk Ariṭṭha; having gone there, they said to Ariṭṭha: “Is it true, friend Ariṭṭha, that such a pernicious view has arisen in you: ‘As I understand the Dharma taught by the Blessed One, those things called obstructions by the Blessed One are not able to obstruct one who engages in them’?”

“As I understand it, friend, the Dharma was taught by the Blessed One thus: ‘Those things called obstructions by the Blessed One are not able to obstruct one who engages in them.’”

Then those monks, desiring to detach Ariṭṭha from that pernicious view, questioned and cross-questioned and remonstrated with him: “Do not say so, friend Ariṭṭha. Do not misrepresent the Blessed One; it is not right to misrepresent the Blessed One, and the Blessed One would not speak thus. In many discourses, friend Ariṭṭha, the Blessed One has stated that obstructive things are truly obstructive, and that they are able to obstruct one who engages in them. The Blessed One has stated that sensual pleasures provide little gratification, much suffering, and much despair, and that the danger in them is more. The Blessed One has stated that sensual pleasures are like a skeleton… like a piece of meat… like a grass torch… like a pit of coals… like a dream… like borrowed goods… like a tree laden with fruit… like a slaughterhouse… like a sword stake… similar to a snake’s head; they are much suffering and much trouble, and the danger in them is greater.”

Even so, the monk Ariṭṭha, being questioned and cross-questioned and remonstrated with by those monks, tenaciously clung to that same pernicious view, insisted upon it, and said: “As I understand it, friend, the Dharma was taught by the Blessed One thus: ‘Those things called obstructions by the Blessed One are not able to obstruct one who engages in them.’”

234. It is like this: "Thus I have heard," namely, the Alagaddūpama-sutta.

There, "those who oppress desires" are called gaddhabādhino; gaddhabādhipubbo [means] the ancestors of the gaddhabādhino; this means "born in a family of vulture-killers."

"Those that make intervention in heaven and liberation" are called antarāyikā (obstructive things).

These are fivefold by way of action, defilement, result, disparagement, command, and transgression.

There, the five deeds bringing immediate retribution are called action-obstructive things (kammantarāyikā).

Likewise, the act of defiling a bhikkhunī (nun) only causes obstruction to liberation (mokkha), not to heaven (sagga).

Determined wrong views are called defilement-obstructive things (kilesantarāyikā).

The rebirth-dharmas of hermaphrodites, animals, and the ambisexual are called result-obstructive things (vipākantarāyikā).

The dharmas of disparaging noble ones (ariyūpavādadhammā) are called disparagement-obstructive things (upavādantarāyikā); however, they [are obstructive] only until they ask forgiveness from the noble ones; not beyond that.

The seven intentional transgressions of the aggregates of offenses (āpattikkhandhā) are called command-transgression-obstructive things (āṇāvītikkamantarāyikā).

Likewise, they [are obstructive] only until he acknowledges the state of being a bhikkhu, or until he departs [from the order], or until he confesses; not beyond that.

There, this bhikkhu, being very learned and a speaker of the Dharma, knows the remaining obstructive things; however, because of his lack of skill in the Vinaya, he does not know the obstructive things related to the transgression of the precepts. Therefore, being alone, he thought thus: "These householders, while enjoying the five sense qualities, are stream-enterers (sotāpanna), once-returners (sakadāgāmin), and non-returners (anāgāmin)."

"Even bhikkhus see forms cognizable by the eye that are pleasing…pe… they touch tangible objects cognizable by the body, they enjoy soft things, such as spreads and blankets, etc.; all this is permissible."

"Why are forms, sounds, smells, tastes, and tactile objects not permissible only for women? These too are permissible," thus [he thinks].

Thus, mixing taste with taste, making enjoyment with desire and passion and enjoyment without desire and passion one, as if bringing a very fine thread together with coarse ropes, as if comparing Mount Sineru with a mustard seed, having produced a wicked view, and opposing the all-knowing wisdom, saying, "Why did the Blessed One prescribe the first expulsion offense (paṭhamapārājikaṃ) with great effort, as if binding the great ocean; there is no fault in this," rejecting the wisdom of self-confidence, throwing sticks and thorns, etc., on the noble path, he gave a blow to the wheel of the command of the Conqueror [by saying] "there is no fault in sexual intercourse."

Therefore, he said: "As I understand the Dharma taught by the Blessed One," etc.

"Just as," namely, like that.

"Questioning," etc., [means] asking, "What is your view, declare your view," those who question are called those who interrogate (samanuyuñjantī).

Those who establish a view are called those who support (samanuggāhanti).

Those who ask for a reason, [saying] "For what reason do you say this?" are called those who explain (samanubhāsanti).

"Like a skeleton," etc. (M. Ni. 2.42-48) "like a skeleton" [is to be understood] in the sense of little satisfaction.

"Like a lump of flesh" [is to be understood] in the sense of being much shared.

"Like a grass torch" [is to be understood] in the sense of not being burned.

"Like a pit of embers" [is to be understood] in the sense of great torment.

"Like a dream" [is to be understood] in the sense of momentary presence.

"Like something borrowed" [is to be understood] in the sense of being temporary.

"Like the fruit of a tree" [is to be understood] in the sense of causing the destruction of all limbs, great and small.

"Like a butcher's knife" [is to be understood] in the sense of excessive cutting.

"Like a spear or trident" [is to be understood] in the sense of piercing through.

"Like the head of a snake" [is to be understood] in the sense of a dangerous and frightening snake.
thāmasā means with tenacity of views (dṛṣṭi-thāmena).

parāmāsā means with adherence to views (dṛṣṭi-parāmāsena).

abhinivissa voharatī means he speaks having determined, or it means he reveals.

"By which, while cross-examining, [they are like] a skeleton (aṭṭhikaṅkalūpamā), a lump of flesh (maṃsapesūpamā), a torch of grass (tiṇukkūpamā), a pit of embers (aṅgārakāsūpamā), a dream (supinakūpamā), a loan (yācitakūpamā), tree fruits (rukkhaphalūpamā), a sword and ploughshare (asisūnūpamā), a stake (sattisūlūpamā), the head of a snake (sappasirūpamā), with tenacity of views (thāmasā), with adherence to views (parāmāsā), he speaks having determined."

Since the bhikkhus were unable to detach the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers (gaddhabādhipubba), from that pernicious view (pāpakā diṭṭhigatā), they went to the Blessed One (bhagavā), and after paying homage to him (bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā), they sat down at one side (ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu).

Seated to one side, the bhikkhus told the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ): “Venerable sir (bhante), such a pernicious view (pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ) has arisen in the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers (gaddhabādhipubbassa): ‘As I understand the Dharma (dhammaṃ) taught by the Blessed One (bhagavatā desitaṃ), those things called obstructions (antarāyikā dhammā) by the Blessed One (bhagavatā) are not able to obstruct one who engages in them (te paṭisevato nālaṃ antarāyāyā)’."

We have heard, venerable sir (bhante): “Such a pernicious view (pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ) has arisen in the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers (gaddhabādhipubbassa): ‘As I understand the Dharma (dhammaṃ) taught by the Blessed One (bhagavatā desitaṃ), those things called obstructions (antarāyikā dhammā) by the Blessed One (bhagavatā) are not able to obstruct one who engages in them (te paṭisevato nālaṃ antarāyāyā)’."

Then, venerable sir (bhante), we went to the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers (gaddhabādhipubbo), and said to the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers (gaddhabādhipubbaṃ): “Is it true, friend Ariṭṭha (āvuso ariṭṭha), that such a pernicious view (pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ) has arisen in you: ‘As I understand the Dharma (dhammaṃ) taught by the Blessed One (bhagavatā desitaṃ), those things called obstructions (antarāyikā dhammā) by the Blessed One (bhagavatā) are not able to obstruct one who engages in them (te paṭisevato nālaṃ antarāyāyā)’?”

When this was said, venerable sir (bhante), the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers (gaddhabādhipubbo), said to us: “As I understand the Dharma (dhammaṃ) taught by the Blessed One (bhagavatā desitaṃ), those things called obstructions (antarāyikā dhammā) by the Blessed One (bhagavatā) are not able to obstruct one who engages in them (te paṭisevato nālaṃ antarāyāyā).”

Then, venerable sir (bhante), wishing to detach the bhikkhu Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers (gaddhabādhipubbaṃ), from this pernicious view (pāpakā diṭṭhigatā), we questioned him, pressed him, and reasoned with him: “Don’t say that, friend Ariṭṭha (āvuso ariṭṭha), don’t misrepresent the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ abbhācikkhi); for it’s not right to misrepresent the Blessed One (bhagavato abbhakkhānaṃ), and the Blessed One (bhagavā) would never say such a thing (evaṃ vadeyya). In many ways, friend Ariṭṭha (anekapariyāyenāvuso ariṭṭha), the obstructive things (antarāyikā dhammā) have been described as obstructive (antarāyikā vuttā bhagavatā) by the Blessed One (bhagavatā), and they are indeed able to obstruct one who engages in them (alañca pana te paṭisevato antarāyāya). The Blessed One (bhagavatā) has said that sensual pleasures (kāmā) provide little gratification (appassādā), much suffering (bahudukkhā), and much despair (bahupāyāsā), and how great is the danger (ādīnavo) in them (ettha bhiyyo). 240. Kullūpama means similar to a raft.

Nittharaṇatthāyā means for the purpose of crossing over the four floods.

Udakaṇṇava means if water is deep but not wide, or wide but not deep, that is not called an ocean.

But if water is both deep and wide, that is called an ocean.

Therefore, mahantaṃ udakaṇṇava means great, wide, and deep water; this is the meaning here.

Sāsaṅkaṃ is a name [for a place] where a dwelling place of thieves is seen.

A place to stand, a place to sit, a place to lie down is seen.

Sappaṭibhayaṃ is a name [for a place] where people are seen killed by thieves, plundered, and assaulted.

Uttarasetū means a bridge built over an ocean of water.

Kullaṃ bandhitvā means kullo is the name of [something] bound together, having made a bundle for the purpose of crossing over.

But having spread out and bound together things like mats and pot-shreds, these are called rafts (uḷumpoti).

Uccāretvā means having placed [it].

Kiccakārī means one who makes bowls, one who is skilled, one who is fit; this is the meaning.

Dhammāpi vo pahātabbāti, here dhammā means samatha (calm abiding) and vipaśyanā (insight).

The Blessed One abandoned desire and passion even in samatha and in vipaśyanā.

Where did he abandon desire and passion in samatha? “Thus, Udayin, I declare the abandoning even of the realm of neither perception nor non-perception. Do you see, Udayin, that fetter, whether subtle or gross, whose abandoning I do not declare?” (Majjhima Nikāya 2.156) Here, he abandoned desire and passion in samatha.

“If you, monks, do not cling to, cherish, treasure, or treat as a possession this view, so purified and so bright..." (Majjhima Nikāya 1.401) Here, he abandoned desire and passion through vipaśyanā.

Here, indeed, the meaning is that in both places, while causing [one] to abandon, he said, "Even dharmas are to be abandoned, how much more so adharmas!"

Therefore, the intention here is: Monks, I declare the abandonment of desire and passion in such tranquil and delightful dharmas. What about this unrighteous, vulgar, outcast, corrupt, and base dharma, where this worthless man Ariṣṭha, who considers himself without fault, says that desire and passion for the five objects of desire are not sufficient for obstruction.

Just as with Ariṣṭha, you should not throw filth or rubbish into my teaching. Thus, the Blessed One, even with this advice, reproves Ariṣṭha.

"There being self, [it is] mine," there being "mine," [it is] proper that there be "I."

"In truth" means in reality; "in that way" means in suchness or in steadfastness.

Now, these five aggregates [are] impermanent (anitya), suffering (duḥkha), not-self (anātman); not grasping in this way by means of the threefold round, this Arishta, as if throwing embryo-waste into my teaching (śāsana), showing [this], he says, "What do you think, monks, is form permanent or not?" etc.

Therein, “Impermanent, venerable sir,” venerable sir, because having been, it is not, therefore [it is] impermanent (anitya).

Because of the state of arising and passing away, because of change, because of temporariness, and because of opposing permanence, it is impermanent (anitya) for these four reasons.

“Suffering (duḥkha), venerable sir,” venerable sir, suffering (duḥkha) in the sense of oppression, suffering (duḥkha) as burning, and suffering (duḥkha) as opposing pleasure that is the basis of non-suffering; it is suffering (duḥkha) for these four reasons.

Not-self (anātman) is not evident.

This is called a pillar (esikā) because of the meaning of being profoundly ingrained.

Orambhāgiyānī means things that partake of what is lower, are causes/conditions for rebirth in the realm of desire (kāmabhava).

"Thus, well-proclaimed (evaṃ svākkhāto)" means that beginning from the threefold revolution (tiparivaṭṭato), it is suitable to bring it up to this point; by reversal, it is suitable to bring it up to this point with love and affection (pemamattakena) from heaven as the final goal (saggaparāyaṇato).

"Tattered rag-wearer (chinnapilotiko)" means a rag (pilotikā) is said to be a torn (chinnaṃ), broken (bhinnaṃ), stitched (sibbitaṃ) here and there, knotted (gaṇṭhikataṃ), old (jiṇṇaṃ) cloth (vatthaṃ), that which he does not have; he is clothed in an eight- or nine-cubit, unwashed (ahata), new cloth (sāṭakaṃ), he is called a chinnapilotiko.

This dharma too is such; here, there is no state of being torn, broken, stitched, and knotted by way of deceitfulness and so on.

Moreover, trash (kacavaro) is said to be a rag (pilotikoti).

Thus, even though there is the state of being torn for the ascetic trash (samaṇakacavarassa chinnattāpi), this dharma is called "tattered rag-wearer (chinnapilotiko nāma)."

(Pu. Pa. 30).
