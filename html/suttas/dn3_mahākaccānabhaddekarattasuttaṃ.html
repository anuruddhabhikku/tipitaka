THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Park of the Hot Springs (tapodārāme).

Then, when it was near dawn, the venerable Samiddhi went to the hot springs (tapoda) to bathe his limbs.

Having bathed his limbs in the hot springs (tapode), he came out, and stood wearing only one robe, drying his limbs.

Then, when the night was well advanced, a certain deity (devatā) of beautiful appearance who illuminated the whole of the Hot Springs (tapoda), approached the venerable Samiddhi. On arrival, having stood to one side, the deity (devatā) said this to the venerable Samiddhi: “Bhikkhu, do you remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta)?” “No, friend (āvuso), I do not remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta).” “Then, friend (āvuso), do you remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta)?” “I, too, bhikkhu, do not remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta).” “Then, bhikkhu, do you remember the verses (gāthā) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratti)?” “No, friend (āvuso), I do not remember the verses (gāthā) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratti).” “Then, friend (āvuso), do you remember the verses (gāthā) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratti)?” “I, too, bhikkhu, do not remember the verses (gāthā) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratti).” “Bhikkhu, learn the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta). Bhikkhu, master the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta). Bhikkhu, remember the summary (uddesa) and exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta). The summary (uddesa) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta), bhikkhu, and the exposition (vibhaṅga), are connected with the meaning (atthasaṃhito) and are the beginning of the holy life (ādibrahmacariyako).” The deity (devatā) said this. Having said this, she vanished right there.

Then, when the night was over, the venerable Samiddhi went to the Blessed One. After paying homage to him, he sat down at one side.

Sitting to one side, the venerable Samiddhi said to the Blessed One:

“Here, venerable sir, late in the night, I got up and went to the Hot Springs to bathe my limbs. After bathing in the Hot Springs, I came up out of the water and stood in one robe drying my limbs. Then, venerable sir, a certain deity of beautiful appearance, in the far extreme of the night, illuminating the entirety of the Hot Springs, approached me. On arrival, she stood to one side. Standing to one side, that deity said to me, ‘Do you, bhikṣu, remember the summary (uddeśa) and analysis (vibhaṅga) of the Fortunate One (bhaddekaratta)?’"

“When this was said, venerable sir, I said to that deity, ‘No, friend (āvusos), I do not remember the summary and analysis of the Fortunate One. But do you, friend, remember the summary and analysis of the Fortunate One?’ ‘Bhikṣu, I also do not remember the summary and analysis of the Fortunate One. But do you, friend, remember the verses of the Fortunate One?’ ‘Bhikṣu, I also do not remember the verses of the Fortunate One. Bhikṣu, learn the summary and analysis of the Fortunate One; bhikṣu, master the summary and analysis of the Fortunate One; bhikṣu, remember the summary and analysis of the Fortunate One. Bhikṣu, the summary and analysis of the Fortunate One is beneficial and belongs to the fundamentals of the holy life.’ That is what the deity said, venerable sir; having said this, she vanished right there. It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would teach me the summary and analysis of the Fortunate One.”

“Then, bhikṣu, listen and pay close attention; I will speak.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Samiddhi replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One said this:

“One should not revive the past, nor long for the future;

What is past is left behind, and the future is unattained.

But one sees clearly the present phenomenon

as it arises in each case. The wise one should cultivate that,

invincible, unshakeable.

Today is the time to act with zeal; who knows, death may come tomorrow?

There is no bargaining with death and its mighty host.

The wise ones call such a person,

who lives thus, ardent and unwearied day and night, ‘truly fortunate.’” (bhaddekaratta).

The Blessed One said this. Having said this, the Sugata got up from his seat and entered the dwelling.

What is past is left behind, and the future is unattained.

invincible, unshakeable.

Today is the time to act with zeal; who knows, death may come tomorrow?

There is no bargaining with death and its mighty host.

The wise ones call such a person,

who lives thus, ardent and unwearied day and night, ‘truly fortunate.’”

282. “But how, friend, does one revive the past? Thinking, ‘My eye was thus in the past and forms were thus,’ one’s consciousness becomes bound up with desire and lust (chanda-rāga) for that. Because one’s consciousness is bound up with desire and lust, one delights in that. When one delights in that, one revives the past. Thinking, ‘My ear was thus in the past and sounds were thus…My nose and odours…My tongue and flavours…My body and tangibles…My mind was thus in the past and mind-objects were thus,’ one’s consciousness becomes bound up with desire and lust for that. Because one’s consciousness is bound up with desire and lust, one delights in that. When one delights in that, one revives the past. That is how one revives the past.

“And how, friend, does one not revive the past? Thinking, ‘My eye was thus in the past and forms were thus,’ one’s consciousness does not become bound up with desire and lust. Because one’s consciousness is not bound up with desire and lust, one does not delight in that. When one does not delight in that, one does not revive the past. Thinking, ‘My ear was thus in the past and sounds were thus…My nose and odours…My tongue and flavours…My body and tangibles…My mind was thus in the past and mind-objects were thus,’ one’s consciousness is not bound up with desire and lust. Because one’s consciousness is not bound up with desire and lust, one does not delight in that. When one does not delight in that, one does not revive the past. That is how one does not revive the past.

283. How, friend, does one build up hope upon the future (anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati)? Thinking, ‘My eye may be thus in the future and forms may be thus,’ one sets one’s heart on obtaining what has not yet been obtained (appaṭiladdhassa paṭilābhāya cittaṃ paṇidahati). Because one sets one’s heart thus, one delights in that (cetaso paṇidhānapaccayā tadabhinandati). When one delights in that, one builds up hope upon the future (anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati).

That is how one builds up hope upon the future (evaṃ kho, āvuso, anāgataṃ paṭikaṅkhati).

How, friend, does one not build up hope upon the future (anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati)? Thinking, ‘My eye may be thus in the future and forms may be thus,’ one does not set one’s heart on obtaining what has not yet been obtained (appaṭiladdhassa paṭilābhāya cittaṃ nappaṇidahati). Because one does not set one’s heart thus, one does not delight in that (cetaso appaṇidhānapaccayā na tadabhinandati). “Thinking, ‘My ear may be thus in the future and sounds may be thus…My nose and odours…My tongue and flavours…My body and tangibles…My mind may be thus in the future and mind-objects may be thus,’ one does not set one’s heart on obtaining what has not yet been obtained (appaṭiladdhassa paṭilābhāya cittaṃ nappaṇidahati). When one does not delight in that, one does not build up hope upon the future (anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati). That is how one does not build up hope upon the future (evaṃ kho, āvuso, anāgataṃ nappaṭikaṅkhati).

284. Now, how is one vanquished with respect to presently arisen states? Friends, the eye and forms… both are what is present.

When one’s consciousness is bound up with desire and lust (chandarāga) for that which is presently arisen, because one’s consciousness is bound up with desire and lust, one delights in that. When one delights in that, one is vanquished in regard to presently arisen states.

Friends, the ear and sounds… the nose and odours… the tongue and flavours… the body and tangibles… the mind and mind-objects that are presently arisen,

When one’s consciousness is bound up with desire and lust for that which is presently arisen. Because one’s consciousness is bound up with desire and lust, one delights in that. When one delights in that, one is vanquished in regard to presently arisen states. That is how one is vanquished in regard to presently arisen states.

Now, friends, how is one not vanquished with respect to presently arisen states? Friends, the eye (cakṣu) and forms (rūpa)… both are what is present (paccuppannaṃ).

When one’s consciousness (vijñāṇaṃ) is not bound up with desire and lust (chandarāga) for that which is presently arisen, because one’s consciousness is not bound up with desire and lust, one does not delight in that. When one does not delight in that, one is invincible in regard to presently arisen states.

Friends, the ear (sotaṃ) and sounds (saddā)… the nose (ghānaṃ) and odours (gandhā)… the tongue (jivhā) and flavours (rasā)… the body (kāyo) and tangibles (phoṭṭhabbā)… the mind (mano) and mind-objects (dhammā) that are presently arisen,

When one’s consciousness is not bound up with desire and lust for that which is presently arisen. Because one’s consciousness is not bound up with desire and lust, one does not delight in that. When one does not delight in that, one is invincible in regard to presently arisen states. That is how one is invincible in regard to presently arisen states.

285. Then the Blessed One, having briefly stated the teaching without explaining it in detail, rose from his seat and entered the monastery.

"He does not review the past…pe…

That is what the sage calls one who lives for a single auspicious night."

"I understand the detailed meaning of this summary given in brief by the Blessed One without expounding the detailed meaning to be as follows.

Now, friends, if you wish, go to the Blessed One and ask him about the meaning of this. As the Blessed One explains it to you, so you should remember it."

Then the monks, having delighted and rejoiced in the venerable Mahā Kaccāna’s words, rose from their seats and went to the Blessed One. After paying homage to him, they sat down at one side.

As they were sitting there, these monks spoke thus to the Lord: “The Blessed One, having briefly stated the teaching without explaining it in detail, rose from his seat and entered the monastery—

"He does not review the past…pe…"

That is what the sage calls one who lives for a single auspicious night."

“Venerable sir, after the Blessed One had recently departed, this occurred to us: ‘The Blessed One, having briefly stated the teaching without explaining it in detail, rose from his seat and entered the monastery—

“He does not review the past, nor does he long for the future;

That which is past is left behind, and the future is unattained.

But whoever sees the Dharma in the present moment, in each and every instance,

Unwavering and unperturbed—knowing this, let him cultivate it.

Today is the time for exertion; who knows if death will come tomorrow?

Indeed, there is no bargain to be struck with Death and his mighty host.

He who dwells thus, zealous, untiring day and night—

That is what the sage calls one who lives for a single auspicious night.”’

“Who, indeed, might explain in detail the meaning of this brief statement of the Blessed One, who without analyzing the meaning in detail, rose from his seat and entered the monastery?” Then, venerable sir, we thought: “The venerable Mahā Kaccāna is praised by the Teacher and esteemed by his wise companions in the holy life.

The venerable Mahā Kaccāna is capable of expounding the detailed meaning of this summary given in brief by the Blessed One without expounding the detailed meaning.

Suppose we went to him and asked him the meaning of this.”

Then, venerable sir, we went to the venerable Mahā Kaccāna and asked him about the meaning.

The venerable Mahā Kaccāna expounded the meaning to us with these terms, statements, and phrases.”

“Monks, Mahā Kaccāna is wise; monks, Mahā Kaccāna has great wisdom.

If you had asked me the meaning of this, I would have explained it to you in the same way that Mahā Kaccāna has explained it.

Such is its meaning.

and so you should remember it.”

The Blessed One spoke thus.

Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Mahākaccānabhaddekaratta Sutta is finished, the third.
