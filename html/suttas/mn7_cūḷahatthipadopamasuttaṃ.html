288. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tena kho pana samayena jāṇussoṇi brāhmaṇo sabbasetena vaḷavābhirathena vaḷabhīrathena (sī. pī.) sāvatthiyā niyyāti divādivassa. Addasā kho jāṇussoṇi brāhmaṇo pilotikaṃ paribbājakaṃ dūratova āgacchantaṃ. Disvāna pilotikaṃ paribbājakaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘Handa, kuto nu bhavaṃ vacchāyano āgacchati divādivassā’’ti? ‘‘Ito hi kho ahaṃ , bho, āgacchāmi samaṇassa gotamassa santikā’’ti. ‘‘Taṃ kiṃ maññati, bhavaṃ vacchāyano, samaṇassa gotamassa paññāveyyattiyaṃ? ‘‘Paṇḍito maññe’’ti. ‘‘Ko cāhaṃ, bho, ko ca samaṇassa gotamassa paññāveyyattiyaṃ jānissāmi! Sopi nūnassa tādisova yo samaṇassa gotamassa paññāveyyattiyaṃ jāneyyā’’ti. ‘‘Uḷārāya khalu bhavaṃ vacchāyano samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ pasaṃsāya pasaṃsatī’’ti. ‘‘Ko cāhaṃ, bho, ko ca samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ pasaṃsissāmi? ‘‘Pasatthapasatthova so bhavaṃ gotamo seṭṭho devamanussāna’’nti. ‘‘Kaṃ pana bhavaṃ vacchāyano atthavasaṃ sampassamāno samaṇe gotame evaṃ abhippasanno’’ti abhippasanno hotīti (syā.)? abhippasanno hotīti (syā.) ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bho, kusalo nāgavaniko nāgavanaṃ paviseyya. So passeyya nāgavane mahantaṃ hatthipadaṃ, dīghato ca āyataṃ, tiriyañca vitthataṃ. So niṭṭhaṃ gaccheyya – ‘mahā vata, bho, nāgo’ti. Evameva kho ahaṃ, bho, yato addasaṃ samaṇe gotame cattāri padāni athāhaṃ niṭṭhamagamaṃ – ‘sammāsambuddho bhagavā, svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo, suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho’ti.
288. Thus have I heard: On one occasion the Blessed One (Bhagavā) was living near Śrāvastī (Sāvatthiyaṃ) in Jeta's Grove (Jetavana), Anāthapindika's Park (Anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme).

Now on that occasion, the brahmin Jāṇussoṇi was driving out of Śrāvastī in the middle of the day in an all-white chariot (sabbasetena vaḷavābhirathena).

The brahmin Jāṇussoṇi saw the wanderer Pilotika coming in the distance. Seeing the wanderer Pilotika, he said this:

“Friend Vacchāyana, from where are you coming in the middle of the day?”

“Sir (bho), I am coming from the presence of the ascetic Gotama (samaṇassa gotamassa santikā).”

“What does Master Vacchāyana think of the recluse Gotama’s lucidity of wisdom (paññāveyyattiyaṃ)?”

“I think [he is] wise (paṇḍito maññe).”

“Who am I, sir (bho), and who is [able to understand] the recluse Gotama’s lucidity of wisdom (paññāveyyattiyaṃ jānissāmi)! That sort of person would be needed to understand the recluse Gotama’s lucidity of wisdom (paññāveyyattiyaṃ jāneyyā)!”

“Master Vacchāyana praises the ascetic Gotama with lofty praise (uḷārāya khalu bhavaṃ vacchāyano samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ pasaṃsāya pasaṃsatī)!”

“Who am I, sir (bho), to praise the ascetic Gotama (samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ pasaṃsissāmi)?"

“Indeed, the esteemed Gotama (bhavaṃ gotamo) is the best (seṭṭho) among gods and humans (devamanussāna)!”

“But, Master Vacchāyana, seeing what advantage are you so convinced about the ascetic Gotama (samaṇe gotame evaṃ abhippasanno)?”

“Just as, sir (bho), a skilled elephant tracker (kusalo nāgavaniko) might enter an elephant wood (nāgavanaṃ paviseyya), and might see in the elephant wood (nāgavane) a big elephant’s footprint (mahantaṃ hatthipadaṃ), long in extent (dīghato ca āyataṃ) and broad across (tiriyañca vitthataṃ). He would come to the conclusion: ‘Indeed (vata), this is a big bull elephant (mahā nāgo).’

“In the same way (evameva kho ahaṃ), sir (bho), when I saw four footprints (cattāri padāni) of the ascetic Gotama (samaṇe gotame), then I came to the conclusion (niṭṭhamagamaṃ): ‘The Blessed One (bhagavā) is a Fully Enlightened One (sammāsambuddho), the Dharma (dhammo) is well proclaimed by the Blessed One (svākkhāto bhagavatā), the Saṅgha (saṅgho) of the Blessed One’s disciples is practising the good way (suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho).’”
288.Evaṃme sutanti cūḷahatthipadopamasuttaṃ. Tattha sabbasetena vaḷavābhirathenāti, ‘‘setā sudaṃ assā yuttā honti setālaṅkārā. Seto ratho setālaṅkāro setaparivāro, setā rasmiyo, setā patodalaṭṭhi, setaṃ chattaṃ, setaṃ uṇhīsaṃ, setāni vatthāni, setā upāhanā, setāya sudaṃ vālabījaniyā bījiyatī’’ti (saṃ. ni. 5.4) evaṃ vuttena sakalasetena catūhi vaḷavāhi yuttarathena. Ratho ca nāmeso duvidho hoti – yodharatho, alaṅkārarathoti. Tattha yodharatho caturassasaṇṭhāno hoti nātimahā, dvinnaṃ tiṇṇaṃ vā janānaṃ gahaṇasamattho. Alaṅkāraratho mahā hoti, dīghato dīgho, puthulato puthulo. Tattha chattaggāhako vālabījaniggāhako tālavaṇṭaggāhakoti evaṃ aṭṭha vā dasa vā sukhena ṭhātuṃ vā nisīdituṃ vā nipajjituṃ vā sakkonti, ayampi alaṅkārarathoyeva. So sabbo sacakkapañjarakubbaro rajataparikkhitto ahosi. Vaḷavā pakatiyā setavaṇṇāva. Pasādhanampi tādisaṃ rajatamayaṃ ahosi. Rasmiyopi rajatapanāḷi suparikkhittā. Patodalaṭṭhipi rajataparikkhittā. Brāhmaṇopi setavatthaṃ nivāsetvā setaṃyeva uttarāsaṅgamakāsi, setavilepanaṃ vilimpi, setamālaṃ pilandhi, dasasu aṅgulīsu aṅgulimuddikā, kaṇṇesu kuṇḍalānīti evamādialaṅkāropissa rajatamayova ahosi. Parivārabrāhmaṇāpissa dasasahassamattā tatheva setavatthavilepanamālālaṅkārā ahesuṃ. Tena vuttaṃ ‘‘sabbasetena vaḷavābhirathenā’’ti. Sāvatthiyā niyyātīti so kira channaṃ channaṃ māsānaṃ ekavāraṃ nagaraṃ padakkhiṇaṃ karoti. Ito ettakehi divasehi nagaraṃ padakkhiṇaṃ karissatīti puretarameva ghosanā karīyati; taṃ sutvā ye nagarato na pakkantā, te na pakkamanti. Ye pakkantā, tepi, ‘‘puññavato sirisampattiṃ passissāmā’’ti āgacchanti. Yaṃ divasaṃ brāhmaṇo nagaraṃ anuvicarati, tadā pātova nagaravīthiyo sammajjitvā vālikaṃ okiritvā lājapañcamehi pupphehi abhippakiritvā puṇṇaghaṭe ṭhapetvā kadaliyo ca dhaje ca ussāpetvā sakalanagaraṃ dhūpitavāsitaṃ karonti. Brāhmaṇo pātova sīsaṃ nhāyitvā purebhattaṃ bhuñjitvā vuttanayeneva setavatthādīhi attānaṃ alaṅkaritvā pāsādā oruyha rathaṃ abhiruhati. Atha naṃ te brāhmaṇā sabbasetavatthavilepanamālālaṅkārā setacchattāni gahetvā parivārenti; tato mahājanassa sannipātanatthaṃ paṭhamaṃyeva taruṇadārakānaṃ phalāphalāni vikiritvā tadanantaraṃ māsakarūpāni; tadanantaraṃ kahāpaṇe vikiranti; mahājanā sannipatanti. Ukkuṭṭhiyo ceva celukkhepā ca pavattanti. Atha brāhmaṇo maṅgalikasovatthikādīsu maṅgalāni ceva suvatthiyo ca karontesu mahāsampattiyā nagaraṃ anuvicarati. Puññavantā manussā ekabhūmakādipāsāde āruyha sukapattasadisāni vātapānakavāṭāni vivaritvā olokenti. Brāhmaṇopi attano yasasirisampattiyā nagaraṃ ajjhottharanto viya dakkhiṇadvārābhimukho hoti. Tena vuttaṃ ‘‘sāvatthiyā niyyātī’’ti. Sāvatthiyā niyyātī Divā divassāti divasassa divā, majjhanhakāleti attho. Pilotikaṃ paribbājakanti pilotikāti evaṃ itthiliṅgavohāravasena laddhanāmaṃ paribbājakaṃ. So kira paribbājako daharo paṭhamavaye ṭhito suvaṇṇavaṇṇo buddhupaṭṭhāko, pātova tathāgatassa ceva mahātherānañca upaṭṭhānaṃ katvā tidaṇḍakuṇḍikādiparikkhāraṃ ādāya jetavanā nikkhamitvā nagarābhimukho pāyāsi. Taṃ esa dūratova āgacchantaṃ addasa. Etadavocāti anukkamena santikaṃ āgataṃ sañjānitvā etaṃ, ‘‘handa kuto nu bhavaṃ vacchāyano āgacchatī’’ti gottaṃ kittento vacanaṃ avoca. Paṇḍito maññeti bhavaṃ vacchāyano samaṇaṃ gotamaṃ paṇḍitoti maññati, udāhu noti ayamettha attho. Sineruṃ vā himavantaṃ vā pathaviṃ vā ākāsaṃ vā pametukāmena tappamāṇova daṇḍo vā rajju vā laddhuṃ vaṭṭati. 288. Thus, "me suta" is the Cūḷahatthipadopamasuttaṃ.

There, "sabbasetena vaḷavābhirathenā" means, "Indeed, white horses are yoked [to the chariot], they have white ornaments. The chariot is white, its ornaments are white, its retinue is white, the reins are white, the whip is white, the parasol is white, the turban is white, the clothes are white, the shoes are white, and indeed, it is fanned by a white fan." Thus it was said, [the chariot was] equipped with four white mares, entirely white.

The chariot is twofold: a war chariot (yodharatho) and an ornamented chariot (alaṅkārarathoti). There, the war chariot is square-shaped and not very large, capable of holding two or three people. The ornamented chariot is large, long, and wide. There, umbrella holders, fan holders, and palm-leaf fan holders, eight or ten people, can easily stand, sit, or lie down. This too is an ornamented chariot. It is all silver-plated, including the wheels, the frame, and the yoke. The mares are naturally white. The decorations are also silver. The reins are also well-covered with silver tubes. The whip is also covered with silver. The brāhmaṇa also dressed in white clothes, put on a white upper garment, smeared himself with white unguent, wore a white garland, finger rings on ten fingers, earrings on his ears, so his ornaments were all silver. The retinue of brāhmaṇas, about ten thousand, were likewise adorned with white clothes, unguents, garlands, and ornaments. Thus it was said, "sabbasetena vaḷavābhirathenā".

"sāvatthiyā niyyātī" means that he circles the city once every six months. The announcement is made well in advance that "the city will be circled in so many days"; those who have not left the city, having heard that, do not leave. Those who have left come back, thinking, "We will see the prosperity of the meritorious one." On the day the brāhmaṇa goes around the city, early in the morning, they sweep the city streets, scatter sand, scatter flowers with fried grains, spread [them] around, place filled water pots, raise banana trees and flags, and perfume the entire city with incense. The brāhmaṇa, early in the morning, takes a bath, eats a pre-noon meal, dresses himself in white clothes and so on, descends from the palace, and mounts the chariot. Then those brāhmaṇas, adorned with all white clothes, unguents, garlands, take white parasols and surround him; then, for the purpose of gathering the multitude, first they scatter fruits and unripe fruits for the young children, then they scatter māsakas; then they scatter kahāpaṇas; the masses gather. Shouts and waving of clothes arise. Then, the brāhmaṇa goes around the city with great prosperity, with auspicious [things] such as maṅgalikas and sovatthikas. The meritorious people, having ascended to palaces with one or more stories, open the windows that are like parrot wings and look. The brāhmaṇa, as if covering the city with his fame, glory, and prosperity, faces the southern gate. Thus it was said, "sāvatthiyā niyyātī".

How so? By first putting on and wearing the three robes, he protects and nourishes his body during his wanderings; therefore, [it] is for protecting the body (kāyaparihārikaṃ). By straining water with a piece of his robe and drinking it, and when accepting allowable fruits while eating, he protects and nourishes his belly; therefore, [it] is for sustaining the belly (kucchiparihārikaṃ).

Moreover, with the alms bowl, he draws water at bathing time and during hut repair time; therefore, [it] is for protecting the body. By taking food and eating at mealtime, [it] is for sustaining the belly.

The knife is for protecting the body if one uses it to cut toothwood or to smoothen the legs and surface of one’s bed and chair or the poles of a tent; for sustaining the belly if one uses it to slice sugarcane or to pare coconuts, and so on.

The needle is for protecting the body when sewing robes, for sustaining the belly when piercing and eating cake or fruit.

The girdle is for protecting the body when tying it and wandering about, for sustaining the belly when tying sugarcane and so on and accepting [offerings].

The water strainer is for protecting the body by straining water and bathing, and during monastery-furniture repair time; it is for sustaining the belly when one uses it to strain drinking water or to accept sesame seeds, rice grains, or honey- tree fruits while eating.

This is the limit of requisites for one using eight requisites.

As for protecting the body (kāyaparihārikaṃ) and sustaining the belly (kucchiparihārikaṃ): For one using nine requisites, when he stays in a bedroom, a bedspread situated there or a key is allowed.

For one using ten requisites, a sitting couch or a piece of leather is allowed.

For one using eleven requisites, a walking stick or an oil tube is allowed.

For one using twelve requisites, an umbrella or a pair of sandals is allowed.

It should not be said that, of these bhikkhus, only the one who uses eight requisites is content, and that the others are not content, have many desires, and are difficult to support.

These bhikkhus are also content, have few desires, are easy to support, and live a simple life.

However, the Exalted One did not explain this discourse by way of those bhikkhus; he explained it by way of the one who uses eight requisites.

For this one wraps up his small knife and needle in his water strainer, puts the strainer inside his almsbowl, hangs the bowl over his shoulder, clothes himself in his three robes and waistband, and sets out happily wherever he wishes.

There is nothing at all he has to turn back for and take.

Thus, showing the simple life of this bhikkhu, the Exalted One said, “He is content with a robe that protects his body,” and so on.

There, “protecting the body (kāyaparihārikena)” means merely protecting the body.

“Sustaining the belly (kucchiparihārikena)” means merely sustaining the belly.

“Having undertaken, he goes forth (samādāyeva pakkamati)” means that he takes all those eight requisites, fastens them to his body, and then goes forth. There is no attachment or bond, thinking, “This is my monastery, my enclosure, my attendant.” Like an arrow freed from the bowstring, like a drunken elephant separated from the herd, enjoying whatever lodging, forest thicket, tree root, or mountain cave he wishes, he lives alone, sits alone, and in all his deportment is solitary, without a second.

Protecting the body! Sustaining the belly! Having undertaken, he goes forth!

There, “a bird, a fowl (pakkhī sakuṇo)” means a fowl that has wings.

“Flies (ḍetī)” means flies up.
This, in brief, is the meaning: Having learned that in such and such a region there is a tree with ripened fruits (paripakkaphalo), birds (sakuṇā) come from various directions and eat those fruits, picking and tearing at them with their claws (nakhapakkha), wings, and beaks (tuṇḍādīhi).

It never occurs to them, “This is for today’s meal (ajjatanāya), that is for tomorrow’s meal (svātanāya).”

When the fruits (phale) are finished they do not set up a guard (ārakkhaṃ) around that tree (rukkha), nor do they set up a feather (pattaṃ), claw mark (nakhaṃ), or beak mark (tuṇḍaṃ) there; but being independent (anapekkho) of that tree (rukkhe), whichever direction (disābhāgaṃ) one desires (icchati), he flies up (uppatitvā) and goes in that direction as freely as if bearing his own wings (sapattabhārova).

296. So what does this show by "with this"? It shows the attainment of suitable conditions for dwelling in the wilderness (araṇya-vāsa).

For whomever these four conditions are not present, for them, dwelling in the wilderness is not successful. They are liable to the state of having to talk with wild animals or forest wanderers. The deities dwelling in the wilderness make a terrifying noise, saying, "What use is dwelling in the wilderness for such an evil monk?" They strike their heads with their hands and act as if fleeing.

A bad reputation would also spread about concerning him: "Such and such a bhikkhu, having entered the forest, did this and that evil deed.”

But for whomever these four conditions are present, for them dwelling in the wilderness is successful. Since, reflecting on his own virtue (śīla), he does not see any black spot or blemish, generating joy, he then crosses over the noble ground, properly discerning that [joy's] decay and disappearance. The deities dwelling in the wilderness, being delighted, speak praise. His fame becomes widespread, like a drop of oil sprinkled on water.

There, "vivitta" means empty, without noise, without din; this is the meaning.

Indeed, referring to this, it is said in the Vibhaṅga: “'Vivitta' means even if a dwelling (senāsana) is nearby, it is not crowded with householders and monks; therefore it is vivitta” (Vibha. 526).

Because one lies down and sits in it, it is called a dwelling (senāsana); this is a designation for beds, chairs, and so forth.

Therefore, it is said: “'Senāsana' means a bed is also a dwelling, a chair is also [a dwelling], a cushion, a pillow, a vihāra, a building with a sloping roof, a rectangular building with a gabled roof, a flat-roofed building, a natural cave, a multi-storeyed mansion, a circular house, a rock cave, a bamboo thicket, a foot of a tree, a pavilion—these are also dwellings. Or any place to which bhikkhus return, all this is a dwelling.”

Moreover, "a vihāra, a building with a sloping roof, a rectangular building with a gabled roof, a flat-roofed building, a natural cave"—this is called a vihāra-dwelling.
