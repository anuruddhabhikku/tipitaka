<p n="144" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">144</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0190"/><pb ed="V" n="3.0124"/> me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde sambahulehi abhiññātehi abhiññātehi therehi sāvakehi saddhiṃ – āyasmatā ca sāriputtena āyasmatā ca mahāmoggallānena <note>mahāmoggalānena (ka.)</note> āyasmatā ca mahākassapena āyasmatā ca mahākaccāyanena āyasmatā ca mahākoṭṭhikena āyasmatā ca mahākappinena āyasmatā ca mahācundena āyasmatā ca anuruddhena <pb ed="P" n="3.0079"/> āyasmatā ca revatena āyasmatā ca ānandena, aññehi ca abhiññātehi abhiññātehi therehi sāvakehi saddhiṃ.

<p rend="bodytext">Tena kho pana samayena therā bhikkhū nave bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti. Appekacce therā bhikkhū dasapi bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti, appekacce therā bhikkhū vīsampi bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti, appekacce therā bhikkhū tiṃsampi bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti, appekacce therā bhikkhū cattārīsampi bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti. Te ca navā bhikkhū therehi bhikkhūhi ovadiyamānā anusāsiyamānā uḷāraṃ pubbenāparaṃ visesaṃ jānanti <note>pajānanti (syā. kaṃ.), sañjānanti (ka.)</note>.

<note>pajānanti (syā. kaṃ.), sañjānanti (ka.)</note>
Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Sāvatthī in the Eastern Park, in the Palace of Migāra's Mother, together with many very well-known elder disciples - the venerable Sāriputta, the venerable Mahā Moggallāna, the venerable Mahā Kassapa, the venerable Mahā Kaccāyana, the venerable Mahā Kotthita, the venerable Mahā Kappina, the venerable Mahā Cunda, the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Revata, the venerable Ānanda, and other very well-known elder disciples.

Now on that occasion elder bhikkhus were teaching and instructing new bhikkhus; some elder bhikkhus were teaching and instructing ten new bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus were teaching and instructing twenty new bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus were teaching and instructing thirty new bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus were teaching and instructing forty new bhikkhus. And the new bhikkhus, being taught and instructed by the elder bhikkhus, achieved successive stages of high distinction.

<p n="145" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">145</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā tadahuposathe pannarase pavāraṇāya puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto abbhokāse nisinno hoti. Atha kho bhagavā tuṇhībhūtaṃ tuṇhībhūtaṃ bhikkhusaṅghaṃ anuviloketvā <pb ed="T" n="3.0191"/> bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘āraddhosmi, bhikkhave, imāya paṭipadāya; āraddhacittosmi, bhikkhave, imāya paṭipadāya. Tasmātiha, bhikkhave, bhiyyosomattāya vīriyaṃ ārabhatha appattassa pattiyā, anadhigatassa adhigamāya <pb ed="M" n="3.0123"/>, asacchikatassa sacchikiriyāya. Idhevāhaṃ sāvatthiyaṃ komudiṃ cātumāsiniṃ āgamessāmī’’ti. Assosuṃ kho jānapadā bhikkhū – ‘‘bhagavā kira tattheva sāvatthiyaṃ komudiṃ cātumāsiniṃ āgamessatī’’ti. Te jānapadā bhikkhū sāvatthiṃ <note>sāvatthiyaṃ (syā. kaṃ. pī. ka.)</note> osaranti bhagavantaṃ dassanāya. Te ca kho therā bhikkhū bhiyyosomattāya nave bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti. Appekacce therā bhikkhū dasapi bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti, appekacce therā bhikkhū vīsampi bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti <pb ed="V" n="3.0125"/>, appekacce therā bhikkhū tiṃsampi bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti, appekacce therā bhikkhū cattārīsampi bhikkhū ovadanti anusāsanti. Te ca navā bhikkhū therehi bhikkhūhi ovadiyamānā anusāsiyamānā uḷāraṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0080"/> pubbenāparaṃ visesaṃ jānanti.

On that occasion—on the Uposatha day of the fifteenth, the full-moon night of the Pavarana ceremony—the Blessed One was seated in the open, surrounded by the saṅgha of bhikkhus.

Then, surveying the silent saṅgha of bhikkhus, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: "Oh bhikkhus, I am content with this practice; oh bhikkhus, my mind is content with this practice. Therefore, strive all the more for the attainment of the unattained (appattassa pattiyā), for the accomplishment of the unaccomplished (anadhigatassa adhigamāya), for the realization of the unrealized (asacchikatassa sacchikiriyāya). I will remain right here in Śrāvastī until the Kaumudī full moon of the fourth month."

The bhikkhus in the countryside heard, "The Blessed One will remain there in Śrāvastī until the Kaumudī full moon of the fourth month."

Those bhikkhus from the countryside then went to Śrāvastī to see the Blessed One.

And those elder bhikkhus taught and instructed the new bhikkhus all the more intensively. Some elder bhikkhus were advising and instructing ten bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus were advising and instructing twenty bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus were advising and instructing thirty bhikkhus, some elder bhikkhus were advising and instructing forty bhikkhus. And those new bhikkhus, being advised and instructed by the elder bhikkhus, came to know a superior and distinct progress from before.

<p n="146" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">146</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā tadahuposathe pannarase komudiyā cātumāsiniyā puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā bhikkhusaṅghaparivuto abbhokāse nisinno hoti. Atha kho bhagavā tuṇhībhūtaṃ tuṇhībhūtaṃ bhikkhusaṅghaṃ anuviloketvā bhikkhū āmantesi – ‘‘apalāpāyaṃ, bhikkhave, parisā; nippalāpāyaṃ, bhikkhave, parisā; suddhā sāre <note>suddhasāre patiṭṭhitā (syā. kaṃ. pī.)</note> patiṭṭhitā. Tathārūpo ayaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhusaṅgho; tathārūpā ayaṃ, bhikkhave, parisā yathārūpā <pb ed="T" n="3.0192"/> parisā āhuneyyā pāhuneyyā dakkhiṇeyyā añjalikaraṇīyā anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassa. Tathārūpo ayaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhusaṅgho; tathārūpā ayaṃ, bhikkhave, parisā yathārūpāya parisāya appaṃ dinnaṃ bahu hoti, bahu dinnaṃ bahutaraṃ. Tathārūpo ayaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhusaṅgho; tathārūpā ayaṃ, bhikkhave, parisā yathārūpā parisā dullabhā dassanāya lokassa. Tathārūpo ayaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhusaṅgho; tathārūpā ayaṃ, bhikkhave, parisā yathārūpaṃ parisaṃ alaṃ yojanagaṇanāni dassanāya gantuṃ puṭosenāpi’’ <note>puṭosenāpi, tathārūpo ayaṃ bhikkhave bhikkhusaṃgho, tathārūpā ayaṃ parisā (sī. pī. ka.)</note>.

146.
On that occasion, the Blessed One, surrounded by the bhikṣu-saṅgha, was seated in the open on the fifteenth, the full-moon night of the Komudi full moon of the fourth month (cātumāsiniyā puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā).

Then, surveying the silent bhikṣu-saṅgha, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus thus: "This assembly, bhikkhus, is free from prattle; this assembly, bhikkhus, is free from prattle; it is pure, established in the essence (śuddha sāre patiṭṭhitā)."

Such, bhikkhus, is this bhikkhu-saṅgha; such, bhikkhus, is this assembly (parisā). Such an assembly as is worthy of gifts (āhuneyyā), worthy of hospitality (pāhuneyyā), worthy of offerings (dakkhiṇeyyā), worthy of reverential salutation (añjalikaraṇīyā), an unsurpassed field of merit (anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ) for the world (lokassa).

Such, bhikkhus, is this bhikkhu-saṅgha; such, bhikkhus, is this assembly. Such an assembly that a small gift given to it becomes great (bahu), and a great gift greater (bahutaraṃ).

Such, bhikkhus, is this bhikkhu-saṅgha; such, bhikkhus, is this assembly. Such an assembly as is rare for the world to see (dullabhā dassanāya lokassa).

Such, bhikkhus, is this bhikkhu-saṅgha; such, bhikkhus, is this assembly. Such an assembly as would be worth journeying many leagues with a travel-bag (puṭosenāpi) to see.

<p n="147" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">147</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Santi, bhikkhave, bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe arahanto khīṇāsavā vusitavanto katakaraṇīyā ohitabhārā anuppattasadatthā parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanā sammadaññāvimuttā – evarūpāpi, bhikkhave, santi bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe <pb ed="M" n="3.0124"/>. Santi, bhikkhave, bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātikā tattha parinibbāyino anāvattidhammā tasmā lokā – evarūpāpi, bhikkhave, santi bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe. Santi, bhikkhave, bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṃ tanuttā sakadāgāmino sakideva <note>sakiṃ deva (ka.)</note> imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvā dukkhassantaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0081"/> karissanti – evarūpāpi, bhikkhave, santi bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe <pb ed="T" n="3.0193"/>. <p rend="bodytext">‘‘Santi, bhikkhave, bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe catunnaṃ satipaṭṭhānānaṃ bhāvanānuyogamanuyuttā viharanti <pb ed="V" n="3.0126"/> – evarūpāpi, bhikkhave, santi bhikkhū imasmiṃ bhikkhusaṅghe. Satta bojjhaṅgā bhāvitā bahulīkatā vijjāvimuttiṃ paripūrenti.

“Monks, in this Saṅgha of monks, there are arahants whose corruptions are destroyed, who have lived the spiritual life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, attained their own goal, destroyed the fetters of becoming, and are liberated through perfect knowledge. Such monks are in this Saṅgha of monks.

Monks, in this Saṅgha of monks, there are those who, through the destruction of the five lower fetters, will be reborn spontaneously [in the Pure Abodes], and there attain final Nibbāna, never again to return from that world. Such monks are in this Saṅgha of monks.

Monks, in this Saṅgha of monks, there are those who, through the destruction of the three fetters, and weakening of passion, hatred, and delusion, are once-returners, who will return only once to this world and then put an end to suffering. Such monks are in this Saṅgha of monks.

Monks, in this Saṅgha of monks, there are those who, through the destruction of the three fetters, are stream-enterers, no longer subject to downfall, assured, destined for enlightenment. Such monks are in this Saṅgha of monks.

Monks, in this Saṅgha of monks, there are monks who live devoted to the practice of the four foundations of mindfulness. Such monks are in this Saṅgha of monks.

Monks, in this Saṅgha of monks, there are monks who live devoted to the practice of the four right exertions… the four bases of power… the five faculties… the five powers… the seven factors of enlightenment… the Noble Eightfold Path. Such monks are in this Saṅgha of monks.

Monks, in this Saṅgha of monks, there are monks who live devoted to the practice of loving-kindness… compassion… sympathetic joy… equanimity… contemplation of repulsiveness… contemplation of impermanence. Such monks are in this Saṅgha of monks.

Monks, in this Saṅgha of monks, there are monks who live devoted to the practice of mindfulness of breathing ānāpānasmṛti.

Monks, mindfulness of breathing, when developed and cultivated, is of great fruit and great benefit.

Monks, mindfulness of breathing, when developed and cultivated, perfects the four foundations of mindfulness.

The four foundations of mindfulness, when developed and cultivated, perfect the seven factors of enlightenment (satta bojjhaṅga).

The seven factors of enlightenment (satta bojjhaṅga), when developed and cultivated, perfect true knowledge and liberation (vijjāvimutti).

148. “And how, bhikkhus, is mindfulness of breathing developed and cultivated, so that it is of great fruit and great benefit? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu, gone to the forest, or to the foot of a tree, or to an empty house, sits down, folding his legs crosswise, setting his body straight, and established mindfulness at the front. Simply mindful, he breathes in; mindful, he breathes out.

“Breathing in long, he understands: ‘I breathe in long’; or breathing out long, he understands: ‘I breathe out long.’ Breathing in short, he understands: ‘I breathe in short’; or breathing out short, he understands: ‘I breathe out short.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the whole body of breath’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the whole body of breath.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in tranquillising the bodily formation ( kāyasaṅkhāra )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out tranquillising the bodily formation.’

“He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing rapture ( pīti )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing rapture.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing pleasure ( sukha )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing pleasure.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the mental formation ( cittasaṅkhāra )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the mental formation.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in tranquillising the mental formation’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out tranquillising the mental formation.’

“He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the mind ( citta )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the mind.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in gladdening the mind’; life trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out gladdening the mind.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in concentrating the mind’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out concentrating the mind.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in liberating the mind’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out liberating the mind.’

“He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating impermanence ( aniccānupassī )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating impermanence.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating dispassion ( virāgānupassī )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating dispassion.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating cessation ( nirodhānupassī )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating cessation.’ He trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating relinquishment ( paṭinissaggānupassī )’; he trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating relinquishment.’

“Bhikkhus, that is how mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānassati) is developed and cultivated, so that it is of great fruit and great benefit.

149. Bhikkhus, how is mindfulness of breathing developed and how is it cultivated so as to fulfill the four foundations of mindfulness ( cattāro satipaṭṭhāne )? Bhikkhus, on whatever occasion a bhikkhu, breathing in long, understands: ‘I breathe in long,’ or breathing out long, understands: ‘I breathe out long’; breathing in short, understands: ‘I breathe in short,’ or breathing out short, understands: ‘I breathe out short’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the whole body of breath’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the whole body of breath’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in tranquillising the bodily formation (kāyasaṅkhāraṃ)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out tranquillising the bodily formation (kāyasaṅkhāraṃ)’; on that occasion, bhikkhus, the bhikkhu dwells contemplating the body in the body (kāye kāyānupassī), ardent (ātāpī), clearly comprehending (sampajāno), mindful (satimā), having removed covetousness (abhijjhā) and displeasure (domanassaṃ) in regard to the world (loke).

I say, monks, that this is a certain kind of body amongst the bodies, namely: breathing in (assāsa), breathing out (passāsā).

That is why on that occasion a bhikkhu abides contemplating the body as a body (kāye kāyānupassī), ardent (ātāpī), fully aware (sampajāno), and mindful (satimā), having put away covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassaṃ) for the world (loke).

“Bhikkhus, on whatever occasion a bhikkhu trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing rapture (pīti)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing rapture (pīti)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing pleasure (sukha)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing pleasure (sukha)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the mental formation (cittasaṅkhāra)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the mental formation (cittasaṅkhāra)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in tranquillising the mental formation (cittasaṅkhāraṃ)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out tranquillising the mental formation (cittasaṅkhāraṃ)’; on that occasion, bhikkhus, the bhikkhu dwells contemplating feelings (vedanāsu) as feelings, ardent (ātāpī), clearly comprehending (sampajāno), mindful (satimā), having put away covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassaṃ) for the world (loke).

I say that this is a certain feeling among the feelings, namely, giving close attention to in-breathing and out-breathing (assāsapassāsānaṃ sādhukaṃ manasikāraṃ).

That is why on that occasion a bhikkhu abides contemplating feelings (vedanāsu) as feelings, ardent (ātāpī), fully aware (sampajāno), and mindful (satimā), having put away covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassaṃ) for the world (loke).

“Bhikkhus, on whatever occasion a bhikkhu trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in experiencing the mind (citta)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out experiencing the mind (citta)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in gladdening (abhippamodayaṃ) the mind (cittaṃ)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out gladdening (abhippamodayaṃ) the mind (cittaṃ)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in concentrating (samādahaṃ) the mind (cittaṃ)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out concentrating (samādahaṃ) the mind (cittaṃ)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in liberating (vimocayaṃ) the mind (cittaṃ)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out liberating (vimocayaṃ) the mind (cittaṃ)’ - on that occasion, bhikkhus, the bhikkhu dwells contemplating mind in mind (citte cittānupassī), ardent (ātāpī), clearly comprehending (sampajāno), mindful (satimā), having removed covetousness (abhijjhā) and displeasure (domanassaṃ) in regard to the world (loke).

I do not say that there is the development of mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānassatiṃ) for one who is forgetful (muṭṭhassatissa), who is not fully aware (asampajānassa).

That is why on that occasion a bhikkhu abides contemplating mind as mind (citte cittānupassī), ardent (ātāpī), fully aware (sampajāno), and mindful (satimā), having put away covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassaṃ) for the world (loke).

“Bhikkhus, on whatever occasion a bhikkhu trams thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating impermanence (aniccānupassī)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating impermanence (aniccānupassī)’; trams thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating fading away (virāgānupassī)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating fading away (virāgānupassī)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating cessation (nirodhānupassī)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating cessation (nirodhānupassī)’; trams thus: ‘I shall breathe in contemplating relinquishment (paṭinissaggānupassī)’; trains thus: ‘I shall breathe out contemplating relinquishment (paṭinissaggānupassī)’; on that occasion, bhikkhus, the bhikkhu dwells contemplating mind-objects as mind-objects (dhammesu dhammānupassī), ardent (ātāpī), clearly comprehending (sampajāno), mindful (satimā), having removed covetousness (abhijjhā) and displeasure (domanassaṃ) in regard to the world (loke).

Having seen with wisdom (paññāya disvā) the abandoning (pahānaṃ) of covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassānaṃ), he closely looks on with equanimity (sādhukaṃ ajjhupekkhitā hoti).

That is why on that occasion a bhikkhu abides contemplating mind-objects as mind-objects (dhammesu dhammānupassī), ardent (ātāpī), fully aware (sampajāno), and mindful (satimā), having put away covetousness (abhijjhā) and grief (domanassaṃ) for the world (loke).

“Bhikkhus, that is how mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānassati), developed (bhāvitā) and cultivated (bahulīkatā), fulfils the four foundations of mindfulness (cattāro satipaṭṭhāne paripūreti).

150. "And how, monks, are the four establishments of mindfulness developed and how are they cultivated so as to bring the seven factors of enlightenment to fulfillment?

Monks, on whatever occasion a monk abides contemplating the body in the body, ardent, fully knowing, mindful, having put away covetousness and grief for the world, on that occasion unremitting mindfulness is established in him. On whatever occasion, monks, unremitting mindfulness has been established in a monk, on that occasion the mindfulness enlightenment factor (satisambojjhaṅga) is aroused by the monk. On that occasion the monk develops the mindfulness enlightenment factor, on that occasion the mindfulness enlightenment factor goes to fulfilment by development in the monk.

"Abiding thus mindful, he investigates and examines that state with wisdom and embarks upon a full inquiry into it. On whatever occasion, monks, abiding thus mindful, a monk investigates and examines that state with wisdom and embarks upon a full inquiry into it, on that occasion the investigation-of-states enlightenment factor (dhammavicayasambojjhaṅga) is aroused by the monk, and he develops the investigation-of-states enlightenment factor, and the investigation-of-states enlightenment factor comes to fulfilment by development in the monk.

On whatever occasion, monks, tireless energy is aroused in a monk who investigates and examines that state with wisdom and embarks upon a full inquiry into it, on that occasion the energy enlightenment factor (vīriyasambojjhaṅga) is aroused by the monk, and he develops the energy enlightenment factor, and the energy enlightenment factor comes to fulfilment by development in the monk.

"In one whose energy is aroused, unworldly rapture (pīti nirāmisā) arises. On whatever occasion, monks, unworldly rapture arises in a monk whose energy is aroused, on that occasion the rapture enlightenment factor (pītisambojjhaṅga) is aroused by the monk, and he develops the rapture enlightenment factor, and the rapture enlightenment factor comes to fulfilment by development in the monk.

"In one who is uplifted by rapture, both body and mind become tranquil. On whatever occasion, monks, both body and mind become tranquil in a monk who is uplifted by rapture, on that occasion the tranquility enlightenment factor (passaddhisambojjhaṅga) is aroused by the monk, and he develops the tranquility enlightenment factor, and the tranquility enlightenment factor goes to fulfilment by development in the monk.

"For one whose body is tranquil and who is happy the mind becomes concentrated. Evaṃ bhāvitā kho, bhikkhave, satta bojjhaṅgā evaṃ bahulīkatā vijjāvimuttiṃ paripūrentī’’ti.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0202"/> <p rend="centre">Ānāpānassatisuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.

152. How, bhikkhus, are the seven factors of enlightenment developed and how are they cultivated so as to fulfill true knowledge and deliverance? Here, a bhikkhu develops the enlightenment factor of mindfulness ( satisambojjhaṅgaṃ), which is based upon seclusion (vivekanissitaṃ), dispassion (virāganissitaṃ), and cessation (nirodhanissitaṃ), maturing in release (vossaggapariṇāmiṃ). He develops the enlightenment factor of discrimination of phenomena (dhammavicayasambojjhaṅgaṃ)... the enlightenment factor of energy (vīriyasambojjhaṅgaṃ)... the enlightenment factor of rapture (pītisambojjhaṅgaṃ)... the enlightenment factor of tranquility (passaddhisambojjhaṅgaṃ)... the enlightenment factor of concentration (samādhisambojjhaṅgaṃ)... the enlightenment factor of equanimity (upekkhāsambojjhaṅgaṃ), which is based upon seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, maturing in release.

Thus, bhikkhus, the seven factors of enlightenment, developed and cultivated, fulfill true knowledge and deliverance.

This the Blessed One said. Elated, those bhikkhus delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Ānāpānassatisuttaṃ, the eighth, is finished.
