Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling near Icchānaṅgala in the Icchānaṅgala jungle thicket.

Now on that occasion a number of well-known, well-to-do brāhmaṇas were staying at Icchānaṅgala, that is, the brāhmaṇa Caṅki, the brāhmaṇa Tārukkha, the brāhmaṇa Pokkharasāti, the brāhmaṇa Jāṇussoṇi, the brāhmaṇa Todeyya, and other well-known, well-to-do brāhmaṇas.

Then, while the students Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja were walking and wandering for exercise, this discussion arose between them: "How, sir, does one become a brāhmaṇa?" The student Bhāradvāja said: "When one is well-born on both sides, of pure maternal and paternal descent seven generations back, unassailable and impeccable in respect of birth, then, sir, one is a brāhmaṇa."

The student Vāseṭṭha said: "When one is virtuous and fulfills the observances, then, sir, one is a brāhmaṇa."

But the brāhmaṇa student Bhāradvāja could not convince the brāhmaṇa student Vāseṭṭha, nor could the brāhmaṇa student Vāseṭṭha convince the brāhmaṇa student Bhāradvāja.

Then the student Vāseṭṭha addressed the student Bhāradvāja: “Sir, the śramaṇa Gotama, the son of the Śākyans, who went forth from a Śākyan clan, is living at Icchānaṅgala, in the wood near Icchānaṅgala.

Now a good report of Master Gotama has been spread to this effect: ‘That Blessed One is an Arhat, a fully-enlightened Buddha (sammāsambuddha), perfected in knowledge (vijjā) and conduct (caraṇa), a Well-Farer (sugata), Knower of the worlds (lokavidū), unequalled Trainer of men to be tamed (purisadammasārathi), Teacher (satthā) of gods (deva) and humans (manussa), a Buddha, a Blessed Lord (bhagavā)’.”

Come, Bhāradvāja, let us go to the śramaṇa Gotama and ask him about this matter.

As the śramaṇa Gotama will explain it to us, so we will remember it."

"Just so, dear friend," the student Bhāradvāja replied to the student Vāseṭṭha.

454. Thus have I heard (evaṃ me suta), namely, the Vāseṭṭha Sutta.

There, "in the Icchānaṅgala grove" (icchānaṅgalavanasaṇḍe) means in a grove not far from the village of Icchānaṅgala.

Caṅkī and the others, even five people, were the family priests (purohita) of King Pasenadi Kosala.

"Other well-known (brāhmins)" (aññe ca abhiññātā) means many other well-known brāhmaṇas (brāhmaṇā).

It seems every six months (chaṭṭhe chaṭṭhe māse) they would assemble in two places (dvīsu ṭhānesu sannipatanti).

When they want to purify their lineage (jātiṃ sodhetukāmā honti), then they assemble near Pokkharasāti for the purpose of purifying the lineage (jātisodhanatthaṃ) at Ukkaṭṭhā.

When they want to purify the mantras (mante sodhetukāmā honti), then they assemble in Icchānaṅgala (icchānaṅgale sannipatanti).

At this time (imasmiṃ kāle) they had assembled for the purpose of completely purifying the mantras (mantasodhanatthaṃ sannipatiṃsu).

"This intervening talk" (ayamantarā kathā) means the talk they were going on with, relating it to their friendly relationship; in between that talk (kathāya antarā) there arose another talk (ayam aññā kathā udapādi).

"Virtuous" (śīlavā) means possessed of good qualities (guṇavā).

"Accomplished in conduct" (vattasampanno) means accomplished in behavior (ācārasampanno).

Then Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja, the students, went to the Blessed One; having gone, they exchanged greetings with the Blessed One. After exchanging courteous greetings and an amiable talk, they sat down on one side.

While seated on one side, the student Vāseṭṭha addressed the Blessed One in verses:

“We are both known as acknowledged experts in the three Vedas ( tevijja );

I am [a student] of Pokkharasāti, and this student is of Tārukkha."

“Among those skilled in the three Vedas, we are known as the foremost;

versed in the [Vedic] texts, skilled in grammar, disciples like our teachers;

We have a dispute, Gotama, regarding the doctrine of caste (jāti).

Bhāradvāja declares that a brāhmaṇa is [such] by birth (jāti),

while I say [he is such] by deeds (kamma). Know this, O clear-sighted one.

We are unable to convince each other, both of us;

We have come to ask you, who are known as the enlightened one ( sambuddha ).

Just as people, after [the moon] has passed beyond the clouds, with joined palms,

worship and pay homage to Gotama in the world,

We ask you, Gotama, the eye arisen in the world:

Is a brāhmaṇa [such] by birth (jāti), or does he become [such] by deeds (kamma)?

Explain it to us who do not know, so that we may understand a brāhmaṇa.”

“Then I will explain to you, Vāseṭṭha,” said the Blessed One,

“in due order, as it really is;

the classification of beings according to their different births (jāti).

Know that even in the case of grasses and trees, [though] they are not aware,

there is a mark of their species (jāti): their species are different.

Then, among insects and moths, as far as ants,

there is a mark of their species (jāti): their species are different.

Know that even among quadrupeds, both small and large,

there is a mark of their species (jāti): their species are different.

Know that even among those that move on their chests, snakes with long backs,

there is a mark of their species (jāti): their species are different."
Then know that fish, too, in water have their dwelling;
Their birth is their distinctive mark; For many are the kinds of birth.

Then know that birds, too, moving in the sky, have their flight;
Their birth is their distinctive mark; For many are the kinds of birth.

Just as among these births, their birth is their distinctive mark;
Even so among humans, there is no distinctive mark based on birth.

Not in the hairs, nor in the head, nor in the ears, nor in the eyes;
Nor in the mouth, nor in the nose, nor in the lips, nor the brows.

Nor in the shoulders or the neck Nor in the belly or the back;
Nor in the buttocks nor in the breast, nor in private parts nor in sexual intercourse.

Nor with the hands, nor with the feet, nor with fingers, nor with nails;
Nor with the shanks, nor with the thighs, nor with colour, nor with voice;
Their birth is not their distinctive mark, as it is in other births.

Moreover, in bodies, this is not found among humans;
And vocations among humans are designated by convention.

Whoever among humans makes a living by cattle-breeding;
Thus, Vāseṭṭha, know that he is a farmer, not a brāhmaṇa.

Whoever among humans lives by various crafts;
Thus, Vāseṭṭha, know that he is a craftsman, not a brāhmaṇa.

Whoever among humans makes a living by trade;

455. Anuññātapaṭiññātā means: "Trained by you, having been thus permitted by the teachers, and having self-acknowledged, 'Yes, I am trained, teacher.'" Asmā means: "We are." Ahaṃ pokkharasātissa, tārukkhassāyaṃ māṇavo means: "I am Pokkharasāti's senior pupil and foremost student; this is Tārukkha," thus he indicates.

Tevijjāna means: "Of the three Vedas, of the Brahmins." Yadakkhāta means: "Whatever even one word has been declared, in meaning and in expression." Tatra kevalinosmase means: "In knowing that entire [Veda], we are consummate in it," is the meaning. Now, revealing that consummation, he says beginning with padakasmā. There, jappe ācariyasādisā means: "In speaking, we are just like the teachers."

Kammunā means: "According to deeds," according to the ten wholesome courses of action (dasa-kusala-kammapatha). For earlier, referring to the seven kinds of bodily and verbal action, he said, "Because, good sir, he is virtuous," and referring to the three kinds of mental action, "accomplished in observances." For, being endowed with that, he is accomplished in conduct (ācārasampanno). Cakkhumā means: He addresses the Blessed One with the quality of being endowed with the five eyes (pañcacakkhū).

Khayātīta means: "Having transcended the state of deficiency, it is full," is the meaning. Peccā means: "Having approached." Namassantī means: "They do homage" (namo karonti).

Cakkhuṃ loke samuppanna means: "The eye (cakṣus) arose in the world of the darkness of ignorance (avidyā), having dispelled that darkness, having become the eye (cakṣus) for the world by showing the benefit of visible Dharma etc."

461. Evaṃ vutte, vāseṭṭhabhāradvājā māṇavā bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama, abhikkantaṃ, bho gotama! Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya – cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantīti – evamevaṃ bhotā gotamena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Ete mayaṃ bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāma dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsake no bhavaṃ gotamo dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gate’’ti. Vāseṭṭhasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ aṭṭhamaṃ.
461. Thus, when this was said, the youths Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja said this to the Blessed One: “Excellent, venerable Gotama, excellent! Just as, venerable Gotama, one might set upright what has been overturned, or uncover what is concealed, or show the way to one who is lost, or hold up a lamp in the darkness so that those with eyes may see forms, in the same way the Dhamma has been made clear in many ways by venerable Gotama.

We go to venerable Gotama for refuge, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of monks.

From today, may venerable Gotama remember us as lay followers who have gone to him for refuge for life.”

The Vāseṭṭha Sutta, the eighth, is finished.
