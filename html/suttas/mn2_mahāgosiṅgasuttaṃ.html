Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living in the Park of the Gosinga Sāla-tree Wood together with a number of very well-known elder disciples—the venerable Śāriputra, the venerable Mahā Moggallāna, the venerable Mahā Kāśyapa, the venerable Anuruddha, the venerable Revata, the venerable Ānanda, and other very well known elder disciples.

Then, when it was evening, the venerable Mahā Moggallāna rose from meditation, went to the venerable Mahā Kāśyapa, and said to him: “Friend Kāśyapa, let us go to the venerable Śāriputra to listen to the dharma (dhamma).”

—“Yes, friend,” the venerable Mahā Kāśyapa replied to the venerable Mahā Moggallāna.

Then the venerable Mahā Moggallāna, the venerable Mahā Kāśyapa, and the venerable Anuruddha went to the venerable Śāriputra to listen to the dharma (dhamma).

The venerable Ānanda saw the venerable Mahā Moggallāna, the venerable Mahā Kāśyapa, and the venerable Anuruddha going to the venerable Śāriputra to listen to the dharma (dhamma).

Thereupon he went to the venerable Revata and said to him: “Friend Revata, those true men are going to the venerable Śāriputra to listen to the dharma (dhamma). Let us also go to the venerable Śāriputra to listen to the dharma (dhamma).”

—“Yes, friend,” the venerable Revata replied to the venerable Ānanda.

Then the venerable Revata and the venerable Ānanda went to the venerable Śāriputra to listen to the dharma (dhamma).

332. Thus have I heard: the Mahāgosiṅga Sūtra.

There, "in the Gosiṅga Sāla-tree-grove," this is said to show the dwelling place.

In other sūtras, "He dwells at Śrāvastī, in Jetavana, in Anāthapiṇḍada's park," thus, first showing the pasture village, afterwards he shows the dwelling place.

In this Mahāgosiṅga Sūtra, however, the Blessed One's pasture village is unfixed; some pasture village or other will exist.

Therefore, the dwelling place itself is thoroughly illuminated.

"It is a forest-based" -- this is the name of the sūtra.

"With many" means with numerous ones.

"Well-known, well-known" means everywhere famous, manifest.

"With elders, with disciples" means with elders who are endowed with dharmas that are firm-making, such as the prātimokṣa-saṃvara, and with disciples, because they were born at the end of the savana, together in one place.

Now, showing the real form of those elders, he says, "with the venerable Sāriputta," and so on.

There, the venerable Sāriputta is well-known in the Buddha's Dispensation through his qualities, beginning with ethical conduct (śīla).

To those with eyes, the sun standing in the midst of the sky is like the moon, and to those standing on the seashore, the ocean is known as manifest.

Not only must his greatness be understood through the power of the qualities that have come in this sutra, but also, from this, the dhammadāyāda-sūtra, the anaṅgaṇa-sūtra, the sammādiṭṭhi-sūtra, the sīhanāda-sūtra, the rathavinīta, the mahāhatthipadopama, the mahāvedalla, the cātuma-sūtra, the dīghanakha, the anupada-sūtra, the sevitabbāsevitabba-sūtra, the saccavibhaṅga-sūtra, the piṇḍapātapārisuddhi, the sampasādanīyaṃ, the saṅgīti-sūtra, the dasuttarasutta, the pavāraṇā-sūtra (Saṃ.

Mahākassapa:

The venerable Anuruddha was also known and famous as a great elder through qualities such as morality and the qualities that come in this sutta. Moreover, his greatness should be understood by way of the Cūḷagosiṅga Sutta, the Naḷakapāna Sutta, the Anuttariya Sutta, the Upakkilesa Sutta, the Anuruddha Saṃyutta, the Mahāpurisavitakka Sutta, the elder's renunciation, and the foremost (etadagga). For in the foremost, it is said, “This, monks, is the foremost of my disciples who are monks with the divine eye, namely, Anuruddha” (A. Ni. 1.192).

The venerable Anuruddha:

And by the venerable Revata: Here, however, there are two Revatas: Khadiravaniyarevata and Kaṅkhārevata. Of these, Khadiravaniyarevata was the younger brother of the Dhammasenāpati elder, and he is not intended here. But the elder who is full of doubt, “Akappa is guḷa, akappiya is muggā” (Mahāva. 272), is intended here as Revata. He too was known and famous as a great elder through qualities such as morality and the qualities that come in this sutta. Moreover, his greatness should be understood by his renunciation and by the foremost (etadagga). For in the foremost, it is said, “This, monks, is the foremost of my disciples who are monks who meditate, namely, Kaṅkhārevata” (A. Ni. 1.204).

And by the venerable Revata:

The venerable Ānanda was also known and famous as a great elder through qualities such as morality and the qualities that come in this sutta. Moreover, his greatness should be understood by way of the Sekkha Sutta, the Bāhitika Sutta, the Āneñjasappāya, the Gopakamoggallāna, the Bahudhātuka, the Cūḷasuññata, the Mahāsuññata, the Acchariyabbhuta Sutta, the Bhaddekaratta, the Mahānidāna, the Mahāparinibbāna, the Subha Sutta, the Cūḷaniyalokadhātusutta, renunciation, and the foremost (etadagga). For in the foremost, it is said, “This, monks, is the foremost of my disciples who are monks who are much learned, namely, Ānanda” (A. Ni. 1.219-223).

The venerable Ānanda:

And by others who were known and recognized: Not only by these, but the Blessed One dwelt in the Gosiṅgasālavanadāya together with other great elders and disciples who were famous for their great qualities. For the venerable Sāriputta himself, being of great wisdom, took many other monks of great wisdom and then dwelt surrounding the ten-powered one. The venerable Mahāmoggallāna himself was powerful, the venerable Mahākassapa himself was an ascetic, the venerable Anuruddha himself had the divine eye, the venerable Revata himself delighted in jhāna, the venerable Ānanda himself was much learned, and he took many other much learned monks and then dwelt surrounding the ten-powered one. Thus, at that time, these and other known great elders, about thirty thousand monks, should be understood as dwelling surrounding the ten-powered one.

And by others who were known and recognized:

Having arisen from seclusion: Having arisen from the seclusion of the fruition attainment.

He approached the venerable Mahākassapa: It seems that the elder, having arisen from seclusion, looking at the western world-element, saw the sun-circle, fully fifty yojanas, setting like an earring falling from the ear of a drunkard playing in the forest, like a red blanket being gathered up and put into a box, and like a golden bowl worth a hundred thousand [coins] falling from a jewel-snake's tooth. Then, looking at the eastern world-element, he saw the moon-circle, forty-nine yojanas, decorated with a hare-mark, rising from the ocean's belly of cloud color and on the summit of the eastern cakkavāḷa mountain, like a silver wheel being turned around after being grasped at the rim, like a stream of milk coming out of a silver mountain, and like a white swan flapping its wings and leaping into the sky. Then he looked at the sāla forest.
At that time, the sāla trees, from their roots to their tips, were entirely covered with flowers, shining as if draped in fine cloth (dukūla), as if adorned with pearl necklaces.

The surface of the earth was adorned as if for a flower-scattering offering (puṣpa-saṃthara-pūjā), and it was as if sprinkled with lac-dye by the pollen falling there and there.

Swarms of bees and madhukara were intoxicated by the pollen of the flowers, wandering in the groves as if singing.

And then it was the day of the uposatha.

Then the elder thought, "How shall I spend this night?" For noble disciples are known to delight in hearing the Dharma.

Then it occurred to him, "Today, I will go to my elder brother, the dharma-senāpati-tthera, and spend the night delighting in the Dharma."

As he was going, instead of going alone, he thought, "I will go and take my dear friend, the Mahākassapa-tthera." Rising from his seat, he clapped his outer cloak, approached the venerable Mahākassapa.

Having arisen from seclusion, he approached the venerable Mahākassapa.

"Thus, friend," The elder Mahākassapa, because he was a noble disciple who loved to hear the Dharma, having heard his words, without making any excuse such as, "Go, friend, my head aches or my back hurts," but with a delighted heart, said, "Even so, friend," and so on.

This is the method everywhere.

"Just as Ānanda would explain it," just as Ānanda would explain it correctly, thus Ānanda explained it in accordance with his own capacity and in accordance with his own inclination. This is the meaning.

Ānanda Thera was indeed very learned himself, and his inclination was thus: "Oh, that the spiritual practitioners in the teaching might be very learned!"

Why? Because for the very learned, what is permissible and impermissible, what is blameworthy and blameless, what is weighty and light, what has a remedy and what is without remedy is clear.

Having learned the Buddha's teachings, the very learned one, reflecting, knows, "In this place, morality is spoken of; in this place, concentration; in this place, insight; in this place, the path, the fruit, and nirvāṇa." Having fulfilled morality in the place where morality is appropriate, having fulfilled concentration in the place where concentration is appropriate, having taken up the essence of insight in the place where insight is appropriate, one develops the path and realizes the fruit.

Therefore, the Thera has this inclination: "Oh, that spiritual practitioners, having learned one, two, three, four, or five collections (of teachings), reflecting, fulfilling morality and so on in the places where morality and so on are appropriate, might realize the path, the fruit, and nirvāṇa in due order!"

Kathaṃrūpena, āvuso sāriputta, bhikkhunā gosiṅgasālavanaṃ sobheyyā’ti? Evaṃ vutte, bhante, āyasmā sāriputto maṃ etadavoca – ‘idhāvuso, moggallāna, bhikkhu cittaṃ vasaṃ vatteti no ca bhikkhu cittassa vasena vattati. So yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati pubbaṇhasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ viharati; yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati majjhanhikasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā majjhanhikasamayaṃ viharati; yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati sāyanhasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā sāyanhasamayaṃ viharati. Seyyathāpi, āvuso moggallāna, rañño vā rājamahāmattassa vā nānārattānaṃ dussānaṃ dussakaraṇḍako pūro assa. So yaññadeva dussayugaṃ ākaṅkheyya pubbaṇhasamayaṃ pārupituṃ , taṃ tadeva dussayugaṃ pubbaṇhasamayaṃ pārupeyya; yaññadeva dussayugaṃ ākaṅkheyya majjhanhikasamayaṃ pārupituṃ, taṃ tadeva dussayugaṃ majjhanhikasamayaṃ pārupeyya; yaññadeva dussayugaṃ ākaṅkheyya sāyanhasamayaṃ pārupituṃ, taṃ tadeva dussayugaṃ sāyanhasamayaṃ pārupeyya. Evameva kho, āvuso moggallāna, bhikkhu cittaṃ vasaṃ vatteti, no ca bhikkhu cittassa vasena vattati. So yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati pubbaṇhasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ viharati; yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati majjhanhikasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā majjhanhikasamayaṃ viharati; yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati sāyanhasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā sāyanhasamayaṃ viharati. Evarūpena kho, āvuso moggallāna, bhikkhunā gosiṅgasālavanaṃ sobheyyā’’’ti. ‘‘Sādhu sādhu, moggallāna! Yathā taṃ sāriputtova sammā byākaramāno byākareyya. Sāriputto hi, moggallāna, cittaṃ vasaṃ vatteti no ca sāriputto cittassa vasena vattati. So yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati pubbaṇhasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā pubbaṇhasamayaṃ viharati; yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati majjhanhikasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā majjhanhikasamayaṃ viharati; yāya vihārasamāpattiyā ākaṅkhati sāyanhasamayaṃ viharituṃ, tāya vihārasamāpattiyā sāyanhasamayaṃ viharatī’’ti.
344. When that was said, the venerable Mahā Moggallāna told the Blessed One: “Then, venerable sir, I addressed the venerable Sāriputta thus: ‘Friend Sāriputta, we have all declared our own inspiration.’

Now, venerable sir, we ask the venerable Sāriputta: ‘Friend Sāriputta, the Gosinga Sāla-tree Wood is delightful, the night is moonlit, the sāla trees are all in blossom, and heavenly scents seem to be floating in the air. What kind of bhikkhu, friend Sāriputta, could illuminate this Gosinga Sāla-tree Wood?’” When that was said, the venerable Sāriputta told me: “Here, friend Moggallāna, a bhikkhu wields mastery over his mind, and the bhikkhu does not let the mind wield mastery over him.

In the morning he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in during the morning; at midday he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in at midday; in the evening he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in during the evening.

Suppose a king or a king’s minister had a chest full of variously coloured garments.

In the morning he could put on whatever pair of garments he wanted to put on in the morning; at midday he could put on whatever pair of garments he wanted to put on at midday; in the evening he could put on whatever pair of garments he wanted to put on in the evening.

So too, friend Moggallāna, a bhikkhu wields mastery over his mind, he does not let the mind wield mastery over him.

In the morning he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in during the morning; at midday he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in at midday; in the evening he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in during the evening.

That kind of bhikkhu could illuminate this Gosinga Sāla-tree Wood.’ ”

“Good, good, Moggallāna! Sāriputta, speaking rightly, should speak just as he did.

For Sāriputta wields mastery over his mind, he does not let the mind wield mastery over him.

In the morning he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in during the morning; at midday he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in at midday; in the evening he abides in whatever abiding or attainment he wants to abide in during the evening.”
344. Āyasmā sāriputto paññāpāramiyā matthakaṃ patto, paññavāyeva ca cittaṃ attano vase vattetuṃ sakkoti, na duppañño. Duppañño hi uppannassa cittassa vase vattetvā ito cito ca vipphanditvāpi katipāheneva gihibhāvaṃ patvā anayabyasanaṃ pāpuṇāti. Tasmā therassa evaṃ hoti – ‘‘aho vata sabrahmacārī acittavasikā hutvā cittaṃ attano vase vattetvā sabbānassa visevitavipphanditāni bhañjitvā īsakampi bahi nikkhamituṃ adentā vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhetvā lokuttaradhammaṃ sacchikareyyu’’nti. Tasmā evaṃ byākāsi.
Venerable Śāriputra has attained the summit of wisdom (prajñāpāramitā), and only the wise are able to control their minds, not the unwise.

Indeed, the unwise, being controlled by the arising mind, wandering here and there, even after a few days, attain the state of a householder and encounter misfortune and disaster.

Therefore, the Elder thought thus: "Oh, that fellow practitioners, being without control over the mind, controlling the mind, having broken all the diverse wanderings experienced by the mind, not allowing it to exit even a little, developing insight (vipaśyanā), might realize the supramundane dharma (lokuttaradharma)!"

Therefore, he explained thus.

345. Evaṃ vutte, āyasmā sāriputto bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘kassa nu kho, bhante, subhāsita’’nti? ‘‘Sabbesaṃ vo, sāriputta, subhāsitaṃ pariyāyena. Api ca mamapi suṇātha yathārūpena bhikkhunā gosiṅgasālavanaṃ sobheyya. Idha, sāriputta, bhikkhu pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkanto nisīdati pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā ujuṃ kāyaṃ paṇidhāya parimukhaṃ satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā – ‘na tāvāhaṃ imaṃ pallaṅkaṃ bhindissāmi yāva me nānupādāya āsavehi cittaṃ vimuccissatī’ti. Evarūpena kho, sāriputta, bhikkhunā gosiṅgasālavanaṃ sobheyyā’’ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te āyasmanto te bhikkhū (ka.) bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti. te bhikkhū (ka.) Mahāgosiṅgasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ dutiyaṃ.
345. Thus spoken, the venerable Śāriputra said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, which of us has spoken well?” “In every way, Śāriputta, what has been spoken is well-said.

Hear also from me what kind of bhikkhu could illuminate this Gosinga Sāla-tree Wood.

Here, Sāriputta, when a bhikkhu has returned from his almsround, after his meal, he sits down, folds his legs crosswise, sets his body erect, and establishing mindfulness in front of him, resolves: ‘I shall not break this sitting position until through not clinging my mind is liberated from the taints.’

That kind of bhikkhu could illuminate this Gosinga Sāla-tree Wood.”

The Blessed One said this.

Elated, those venerable monks delighted in the Blessed One’s statement.

The Mahāgosingasutta, the second, is complete.
345.Sabbesaṃ vo, sāriputta, subhāsitaṃ pariyāyenāti sāriputta, yasmā saṅghārāmassa nāma bahussutabhikkhūhipi sobhanakāraṇaṃ atthi, jhānābhiratehipi, dibbacakkhukehipi, dhutavādehipi, ābhidhammikehipi, acittavasikehipi sobhanakāraṇaṃ atthi. Tasmā sabbesaṃ vo subhāsitaṃ pariyāyena, tena tena kāraṇena subhāsitameva, no dubbhāsitaṃ. Apica mamapi suṇāthāti apica mamapi vacanaṃ suṇātha. Na tāvāhaṃ imaṃ pallaṅkaṃ bhindissāmīti na tāva ahaṃ imaṃ caturaṅgavīriyaṃ adhiṭṭhāya ābhujitaṃ pallaṅkaṃ bhindissāmi, na mocessāmīti attho. Idaṃ kira bhagavā paripākagate ñāṇe rajjasiriṃ pahāya katābhinikkhamano anupubbena bodhimaṇḍaṃ āruyha caturaṅgavīriyaṃ adhiṭṭhāya aparājitapallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā daḷhasamādāno hutvā nisinno tiṇṇaṃ mārānaṃ matthakaṃ bhinditvā paccūsasamaye dasasahassilokadhātuṃ unnādento sabbaññutaññāṇaṃ paṭivijjhi, taṃ attano mahābodhipallaṅkaṃ sandhāya evamāha. Apica pacchimaṃ janataṃ anukampamānopi paṭipattisāraṃ puthujjanakalyāṇakaṃ dassento evamāha. Passati hi bhagavā – ‘‘anāgate evaṃ ajjhāsayā kulaputtā iti paṭisañcikkhissanti, ‘bhagavā mahāgosiṅgasuttaṃ kathento idha, sāriputta, bhikkhu pacchābhattaṃ…pe… evarūpena kho, sāriputta, bhikkhunā gosiṅgasālavanaṃ sobheyyāti āha, mayaṃ bhagavato ajjhāsayaṃ gaṇhissāmā’ti pacchābhattaṃ piṇḍapātapaṭikkantā caturaṅgavīriyaṃ adhiṭṭhāya daḷhasamādānā hutvā ‘arahattaṃ appatvā imaṃ pallaṅkaṃ na bhindissāmā’ti samaṇadhammaṃ kātabbaṃ maññissanti, te evaṃ paṭipannā katipāheneva jātijarāmaraṇassa antaṃ karissantī’’ti, imaṃ pacchimaṃ janataṃ anukampamāno paṭipattisāraṃ puthujjanakalyāṇakaṃ dassento evamāha. Evarūpena kho, sāriputta, bhikkhunā gosiṅgasālavanaṃ sobheyyāti, sāriputta, evarūpena bhikkhunā nippariyāyeneva gosiṅgasālavanaṃ sobheyyāti yathānusandhināva desanaṃ niṭṭhapesīti. Papañcasūdaniyā majjhimanikāyaṭṭhakathāya Mahāgosiṅgasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
345. "O Sāriputta, all of you have spoken well in different ways." Sāriputta, because in a saṃghārāma, there is a cause for excellence even with monks who are very learned, with those delighting in jhāna, with those having the divine eye, with those who are proponents of ascetic practices (dhutavāda), with those versed in the Abhidhamma, and with those who dwell on the absence of thought (acittavasika), there is a cause for excellence.

Therefore, all of you have spoken well in different ways; for that reason, it is well-spoken, not ill-spoken.

"Moreover, listen to me as well": moreover, listen to my words as well.

"I will not break this pallaṅka": I will not break this crossed-legged posture, having undertaken the fourfold exertion, and I will not release it. That is the meaning.

It is said that the Blessed One, having abandoned the glory of kingship when his knowledge had reached maturity, having gone forth, and having ascended the bodhimaṇḍa in due course, having undertaken the fourfold exertion, having sat down cross-legged in the unconquered posture, having become firm in resolve, having broken the head of the three Māras, and resounding the ten thousand world-systems at dawn, realized the knowledge of omniscience (sabbaññutaññāṇa). With reference to that great bodhipallaṅka of his own, he said thus.

Moreover, showing compassion to the later generation, displaying the essence of practice, which is auspicious for ordinary people, he said thus.

The Blessed One sees that – "In the future, kulaputras with such intentions will reflect thus: 'The Blessed One, while teaching the Mahāgosiṅgasutta here, said, "O Sāriputta, a monk after the meal…pe… in this way, O Sāriputta, a monk adorns the Gosiṅga Sāla Forest." We will grasp the Blessed One’s intention.' Having returned from the alms round after the meal, having undertaken the fourfold exertion, having become firm in resolve, and thinking, 'Without attaining arhatship, we will not break this pallaṅka,’ they will consider the ascetic life should be lived." For those who are practicing thus, within a few days, they will make an end to birth, aging, and death." Showing compassion to this later generation, displaying the essence of practice, which is auspicious for ordinary people, he said thus.

"In this way, O Sāriputta, a monk adorns the Gosiṅga Sāla Forest." Sāriputta, in this way, a monk adorns the Gosiṅga Sāla Forest without any other means. Thus, in accordance with the connection, he brought the teaching to a conclusion.

The Mahāgosiṅgasuttavaṇṇanā is finished in the Papañcasūdanī, a commentary on the Majjhimanikāya.
