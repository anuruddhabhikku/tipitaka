273. Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā nātike nādike (sī. syā. pī.) viharati giñjakāvasathe. Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā parito parito janapadesu paricārake abbhatīte kālaṅkate upapattīsu byākaroti kāsikosalesu vajjimallesu cetivaṃsesu cetiyavaṃsesu (ka.) kurupañcālesu majjhasūrasenesu macchasurasenesu (syā.), macchasūrasenesu (sī. pī.) – ‘‘asu amutra upapanno, asu amutra upapanno upapannoti (ka.). Paropaññāsa nātikiyā paricārakā abbhatītā kālaṅkatā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātikā tattha parinibbāyino anāvattidhammā tasmā lokā. Sādhikā navuti nātikiyā paricārakā abbhatītā kālaṅkatā tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṃ tanuttā sakadāgāmino, sakideva sakiṃdeva (ka.) imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvā dukkhassantaṃ karissanti. Sātirekāni pañcasatāni nātikiyā paricārakā abbhatītā kālaṅkatā tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā sotāpannā avinipātadhammā niyatā sambodhiparāyaṇā’’ti.
273. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Nātika in the Brick House (Giñjakāvasatha). Then on that occasion, the Blessed One was describing the rebirths of the devotees who had passed away in various districts all around – in Kāsi, Kosala, the Vajjis, the Mallas, the Cetis, the Vaṃsas, the Kurus, the Pañcālas, and the Middle Sūrasenas – [saying]: “So-and-so has been reborn there, so-and-so has been reborn there.”

More than fifty Nātika devotees, with the destruction of the five lower fetters (orambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni), were reborn spontaneously (opapātikā), and would attain parinirvāṇa there, not liable to return from that world.

Rather more than ninety Nātika devotees, with the destruction of the three fetters and weakening of greed, hatred, and delusion (rāgadosamohānaṃ), were once-returners (sakadāgāmino), and after coming to this world only once, would make an end of suffering (duḥkha).

More than five hundred Nātika devotees, with the destruction of the three fetters, were stream-enterers (sotāpannā), not liable to the downward course, assured, destined for enlightenment (sambodhi).

And then the Nātika attendants heard: “The Blessed One is declaring, up and down the country, regarding devotees who have died and passed away, their rebirths in Kāsi, Kosala, the Vajjis, the Mallas, the Cetis, the Vaṃsas, the Kurus, the Pañcālas, and the Middle Sūrasenas: ‘So-and-so has been reborn there, so-and-so has been reborn there.’

More than fifty Nātika attendants, through the destruction of the five lower fetters ( orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā), have been reborn spontaneously ( opapātikā) there, and will attain final emancipation ( parinibbāyino) there, without returning from that world ( anāvattidhammā tasmā lokā).

Rather more than ninety Nātika attendants, through the destruction of three fetters, and the lessening of lust ( rāga), hatred ( dosa), and delusion ( moha), are once-returners ( sakadāgāmino), who, on returning only once more to this world, will put an end to suffering ( dukkhassantaṃ karissanti).

More than five hundred Nātika attendants, through the destruction of three fetters, are stream-enterers ( sotāpannā), no longer subject to falling away ( avinipātadhammā), assured, destined for enlightenment ( sambodhiparāyaṇā).’

And with that, the Nātika attendants were delighted, joyful, filled with elation and happiness, having heard the Blessed One's exposition of questions ( pañhaveyyākaraṇaṃ) .

Ayaṃ kho panāpi ahosi rājā māgadho seniyo bimbisāro dhammiko dhammarājā hito brāhmaṇagahapatikānaṃ negamānañceva jānapadānañca. Apissudaṃ manussā evamāhaṃsu – ‘yāva maraṇakālāpi rājā māgadho seniyo bimbisāro bhagavantaṃ kittayamānarūpo kālaṅkato’ti. So abbhatīto kālaṅkato bhagavatā abyākato. Tassapissa sādhu veyyākaraṇaṃ bahujano pasīdeyya, tato gaccheyya sugatiṃ. Bhagavato kho pana sambodhi magadhesu. Yattha kho pana bhagavato sambodhi magadhesu, kathaṃ tatra bhagavā māgadhake paricārake abbhatīte kālaṅkate upapattīsu na byākareyya. Bhagavā ce kho pana māgadhake paricārake abbhatīte kālaṅkate upapattīsu na byākareyya, dīnamanā ninnamanā (syā.), dīnamānā (sī. pī.) tenassu māgadhakā paricārakā; yena kho panassu dīnamanā māgadhakā paricārakā kathaṃ te bhagavā na byākareyyā’’ti?
And he thought: ‘There were also Magadhan attendants of long standing who have died and passed away.

I think Aṅgamagadha is empty of Aṅgamāgadhas.

They were devoted to the Buddha, devoted to the Dharma, devoted to the Saṃgha, and perfectly fulfilled in morality.

They have died and passed away and the Blessed One has not declared [their destiny]; it would be good to have a declaration about this: it would make the multitude have faith and so attain a good rebirth.

Now King Śreṇiya Bimbisāra of Magadha was a righteous and lawful king, a friend of Brahmins, householders, townsfolk and country-dwellers.

People living there rejoiced, saying: ‘Thus, having made [us] happy, that righteous and lawful king has passed away, so we lived comfortably in the realm of that righteous and lawful king.’

And he was devoted to the Buddha, devoted to the Dharma, devoted to the Saṃgha, and perfectly fulfilled in morality.

People said: “King Śreṇiya Bimbisāra of Magadha, who praised the Blessed One until the moment of his death, is dead!”

He has passed away, he has died and the Blessed One has not declared [his destiny].

It would be good to have a declaration about this; it would make the multitude have faith and so attain a good rebirth.

Besides, the Blessed One gained complete awakening in Magadha.

Since the Blessed One gained complete awakening in Magadha, why does the Blessed One not declare the future destiny of the Magadhan attendants who have died and passed away?

If the Blessed One does not declare the future destiny of the Magadhan attendants who have died and passed away, the Magadhan attendants would be unhappy; since the Magadhan attendants would be unhappy, why would the Blessed One not declare it?”

277. Then the venerable Ānanda, reflecting in solitude concerning the Magadhan devotees, rose at dawn, approached the Blessed One, saluted him, and sat down to one side.

Sitting to one side, the venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: “Lord, I have heard that the Blessed One is declaring the destiny of departed devotees in various regions—in Kāśi, Kosala, the Vajjis, the Mallas, the Cetis, the Vaṃsas, the Kurus, the Pañcālas, and the Middle Śūrasenas—[saying,] ‘So-and-so has been reborn there, so-and-so has been reborn there.’

More than fifty Nādika devotees, having abandoned the five lower fetters, have been reborn spontaneously and will attain Nirvāṇa without returning to this world.

More than ninety Nādika devotees, having abandoned the three fetters, and with the attenuation of greed, hatred, and delusion, are once-returners, who, returning only once more to this world, will make an end of suffering.

More than five hundred Nādika devotees, having abandoned the three fetters, are stream-enterers, no longer subject to downfall, assured, destined for enlightenment.

Through this, the Nādika devotees were delighted, joyful, filled with rapture and gladness, having heard the Blessed One's explanation of the questions.

These too, Lord, were Magadhan devotees, many and long-standing, who have passed away.

I think Aṅga and Magadha are empty of Magadhan devotees who have passed away.

Those too, Lord, were devoted to the Buddha, devoted to the Dharma, devoted to the Saṃgha, and perfect in their morality. Those have passed away, but have not been declared by the Blessed One.

For them too, there is a good explanation, many people would be pleased, and then they would go to a good destination.

This too, Lord, was King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, a righteous king, a king of Dharma, a benefactor to Brāhmaṇas and householders, as well as to townsfolk and countryfolk.

People are living recounting: ‘Thus that righteous king of ours, that king of Dharma, having made us happy, has passed away.

Thus we lived easily in the domain of that righteous king of Dharma.’

He too, Lord, was devoted to the Buddha, devoted to the Dharma, devoted to the Saṃgha, and perfect in his morality.

People say: ‘Even until the time of his death, King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, praising the Blessed One, has passed away.’

He has passed away, but has not been declared by the Blessed One; for him too, there is a good explanation, many people would be pleased, and then they would go to a good destination.

Moreover, Lord, the Blessed One’s enlightenment was in Magadha.

Since, Lord, the Blessed One’s enlightenment was in Magadha, how is it that the Blessed One does not declare the destiny of the Magadhan devotees who have passed away? If the Blessed One did not declare the destiny of the Magadhan devotees who have passed away, the Magadhan devotees would be saddened; since the Magadhan devotees would be saddened, how could the Blessed One not declare it?”

Then the venerable Ānanda, speaking thus to the Blessed One about the Magadhan devotees, rose from his seat, saluted the Blessed One, circumambulated him, and departed.

287. "As well proclaimed by that Blessed One": As well proclaimed, as well spoken by that Blessed One, my Teacher.

"Bases of spiritual power ( iddhipāda)": Here, it should be understood that iddhi is in the sense of succeeding, and pāda in the sense of foundation.

"Abounding in spiritual power": Because of abounding in spiritual power.

"Skill in spiritual power": For the state of the arising of spiritual power, it is said to be the state of being skilled through familiarity cultivated by repeated practice.

"The state of transforming by means of spiritual power": For the state of transforming by means of spiritual power, for the sake of showing [things] having made [them] of various kinds.

"Endowed with the will-concentration-effort formation": Among these and others, concentration that has will as its cause or will as its excess is will-concentration (chandasamādhi); this is a designation for the concentration that has been attained by making the will to do ( kattukamyatāchandaṃ) the ruler.

Formations that are effort (padhāna) are effort-formations (padhānasaṅkhārā). This is a term for the energy of right endeavour accomplishing its fourfold function.

"Endowed" (samannāgata): Endowed with will-concentration (chandasamādhi) and with effort-formation (padhānasaṅkhāra).

"Base of spiritual power" (iddhipāda): The meaning is, the total of consciousness and its remaining concomitants except the concentration and the will, which are, in the sense of resolve, the road to the concentration due to zeal and will to strive associated with the direct-knowledge consciousness, which latter are themselves termed “power” either by treatment as “production” or in the sense of “succeeding” or by treatment in this way, “beings succeed by its means, thus they are successful; they are enriched, promoted" (iddhipāda).

And it was said, "The aggregate of feeling, the aggregate of perception, the aggregate of formations, the aggregate of consciousness of such a one is the base of spiritual power" (Vibha. 434).

The meaning should be understood in the remaining [cases] too in this way.

Just as will, having made will the ruler, the concentration that has been attained is called will-concentration (chandasamādhi), so is it said that energy, mind, and investigation, having made [them] the ruler, the concentration that has been attained is investigation-concentration (vīmaṃsāsamādhi).

Moreover, access concentration is a base (pāda), the first jhāna is spiritual power (iddhi). Access to the first jhāna is a base (pāda), the second jhāna is spiritual power (iddhi); thus, in the former part is the base (pāda), in the latter part is spiritual power (iddhi); thus the meaning should be understood here.

The extended discussion of the bases of spiritual power (iddhipādakathā) has been spoken in the Visuddhimagga and in the Vibhaṅgaṭṭhakathā.

But some say, "Spiritual power (iddhi) is accomplished. The base of spiritual power (iddhipāda) is unaccomplished." In order to crush their view, in the Abhidhamma the section called Uttaracūḷikavāra was brought: "There are four bases of spiritual power (iddhipāda): the base of spiritual power of will (chandiddhipāda), the base of spiritual power of energy (vīriyiddhipāda), the base of spiritual power of mind (cittiddhipāda), the base of spiritual power of investigation (vīmaṃsiddhipāda). Therein, what is the base of spiritual power of will (chandiddhipāda)? Here, a bhikkhu, on the occasion when he develops the supramundane jhāna that is an outlet and leads to dispersal, having abandoned the field of false views with the reaching of the first grade, secluded from sense desires he enters upon and dwells in the first jhāna, which is of painful practice and slow in direct knowledge. The will, the wishing to do, the desire to do, the skillful will for the Dhamma that exists at that time is called the base of spiritual power of will (chandiddhipāda); the remaining dharmas are associated with the base of spiritual power of will" (Vibha. 458). But these have come only in the sense of the supramundane. There, the Elder Raṭṭhapāla, having made will the burden, produced the supramundane Dhamma; the Elder Soṇa, having made energy the burden; the Elder Sambhūta, having made mind the burden; the venerable Mogharāja, having made investigation the burden.

Uttaracūḷikavāra There, just as among four sons of ministers who, desiring a higher position, lived depending on the king, one, will being born in service, knowing both the king's inclination and his liking, pleasing the king by attending [to him] by day and by night, attained a higher position; just as that one, so should the producer of supramundane dhamma by means of the burden of will be understood.

But another one, "Who can attend [to him] day by day? When an affair has arisen, I will please [him] by means of prowess," having been sent by the king to a frontier region that was in revolt, having crushed the enemy by means of prowess, attained a higher position; just as that one, so should the producer of supramundane dhamma by means of the burden of energy be understood.

Another one, "Attending [to him] day by day, even though covered with armor and a hundred arrows, is indeed a burden; I will please [him] by means of the power of a mantra," having made familiarity in the science of the ruling class, pleasing the king by means of the arrangement of a mantra, attains a higher position; just as that one, so should the producer of supramundane dhamma by means of the burden of mind be understood.
Another [example]: "What [is the use] of these services and the like? Kings give a higher position to one endowed with good birth. Giving to such a one, will he give to me?" Relying only on good birth, he attained a higher position. Just as he [did], so should one know that, relying on perfectly pure investigation, the one who bears the burden of investigation brings about supramundane Dharma.

288. sukhassādhigamāyā means for the attainment of the happiness of absorption, the happiness of the path, and the happiness of the fruit.

saṃsaṭṭho means with associated mind.

ariyadhammanti means the Dhamma taught by the noble Lord Buddha.

suṇātīti means he hears [the Dhamma] being taught in the presence of the Teacher or by monks, nuns, etc.

yoniso manasikarotīti means he applies his mind in a methodical way, with reason, with cause, in the manner of 'impermanent' etc.

“‘yoniso manasikāro nāma upāyamanasikāro pathamanasikāro, anicce aniccanti dukkhe dukkhanti anattani anattāti asubhe asubhanti saccānulomikena vā cittassa āvaṭṭanā anvāvaṭṭanā ābhogo samannāhāro manasikāro, ayaṃ vuccati yonisomanasikāro’ti.*” means wise consideration is right thinking and attention according to reality. When a thing is impermanent, one considers it as impermanent; this turning of the mind to the actuality of things, the repeated dwelling thereon and the thinking in keeping therewith, - this is called the ‘wise consideration.’

When yoniso manasikāra is spoken in this way, it means one undertakes the action.

asaṃsaṭṭhoti means he dwells not associated with sense pleasures or defilements.

uppajjati sukhanti means the happiness of the first absorption arises.

sukhā bhiyyo somanassanti means when one arises from that attainment, joy arises again and again due to the happiness of absorption.

pamudāti means weak joy due to satisfaction.

pāmojjanti means stronger joy.

paṭhamo okāsādhigamoti means the first absorption stands having suppressed the five hindrances and having taken its opportunity, therefore it is said “the first attainment of opportunity”.

oḷārikāti Here, the bodily and verbal processes are coarse, how are mental processes coarse? Because they are not abandoned. Bodily processes are abandoned by the fourth absorption, verbal processes by the second absorption, mental processes by the cessation attainment. Thus, even when bodily and verbal processes are abandoned, they still remain, because they are not abandoned based on what has been abandoned, they are called coarse.

sukhanti means the happiness of the fruit attainment of the fourth absorption that arises for one arising from cessation.

sukhā bhiyyo somanassati means joy again and again for one arising from the fruit attainment.

dutiyo okāsādhigamoti means the fourth absorption stands having suppressed happiness and suffering and having taken its opportunity, therefore it is said “the second attainment of opportunity.”

But the second and third absorptions are already taken when the fourth is taken, therefore they are not mentioned separately.

idaṃ kusalantiādīsu kusalaṃ nāma dasakusalakammapathā means In this wholesome, wholesome means the ten paths of wholesome action.

akusalanti means the ten paths of unwholesome action.

sāvajjadukādayopi etesaṃ vaseneva veditabbā means the pairs of blameworthy things should be understood in the same way.

sabbañceva panetaṃ kaṇhañca sukkañca sappaṭibhāgañcāti kaṇhasukkasappaṭibhāgaṃ. means all of this is black and white and has a counterpart.

nibbānameva hetaṃ appaṭibhāgaṃ means only nirvāṇa does not have a counterpart.

avijjā pahīyatīti means ignorance that covers the round [of rebirth] is abandoned.

vijjā uppajjatīti means the knowledge of the path of arahantship arises.

sukhanti means both the happiness of the path of arahantship and the happiness of the fruit.

sukhā bhiyyo somanassanti means joy again and again for one arising from the fruit attainment.

tatiyo okāsādhigamoti means the path of arahantship stands having suppressed all the defilements and having taken its opportunity, therefore it is said “the third attainment of opportunity.”

But the remaining paths are already included when that is taken, therefore they are not mentioned separately.

But these three attainments of opportunity should be spoken of extensively in terms of the thirty-eight objects.
How? Having discriminated all objects in the Visuddhimagga in the way that has been described, by way of approach and by way of absorption, in twenty-four places, the first jhāna should be spoken of as "the first attainment of opportunity" (okāsādhigama).

In thirteen places, having brought the second and third jhānas, and in fifteen places, having brought the fourth jhāna and the cessation attainment (nirodhasamāpatti) to completion, it should be spoken of as "the second attainment of opportunity."

However, the ten approach jhānas, being the foundation of the path, partake of the third attainment of opportunity.

Moreover, of the three trainings (śikṣā), the training in higher ethics (adhiśīlaśikṣā) partakes of the first attainment of opportunity, the training in higher mind (adhicittaśikṣā) the second, the training in higher wisdom (adhiprajñāśikṣā) the third; thus, it should be spoken of in terms of the training.

Also, in the fruits of religious life (sāmaññaphala), from the Lesser Precepts (cūḷasīla) up to the first jhāna is the first attainment of opportunity.

From the second jhāna up to the peak of existence (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana) is the second.

From insight (vipaśyanā) up to arhatship is the third attainment of opportunity; thus, it should be spoken of even in terms of the Sāmaññaphala Sutta.

Moreover, of the three baskets (piṭaka), the Vinaya Piṭaka partakes of the first attainment of opportunity, the Sūtra Piṭaka the second, the Abhidharma Piṭaka the third; thus, it should be spoken of even in terms of the piṭaka.




Previously, it seems, the great elders established just this sūtra for the rains-residence ceremony (vassūpanāyikā).

Why? [Thinking,] "We will be able to divide and speak of the three piṭakas."

Indeed, it is not possible to say that the speech of one who, having brought it into accord with the three piṭakas, is badly spoken.

Having caused [others] to practice the three piṭakas, just that sūtra is well-spoken.

Sammāsaṅkappo pahotī means right thought (sammāsaṅkappo) proceeds (pavattati).

The same method applies to all the remaining cases.

But this meaning should be understood in terms of the path (magga) and also in terms of the fruit (phala).

How? For one established in right view of the path (magga-sammādiṭṭhiyaṃ ṭhitassa) right thought of the path (magga-sammāsaṅkappo) proceeds…pe… for one established in knowledge of the path (maggañāṇe ṭhitassa) liberation of the path (maggavimutti) proceeds.

Likewise, for one established in right view of the fruit (phala-sammādiṭṭhiyaṃ ṭhitassa) right thought of the fruit (phala-sammāsaṅkappo) proceeds…pe… for one established in knowledge of the fruit (phala-sammāñāṇe ṭhitassa) liberation of the fruit (phalavimutti) proceeds.

Svākkhāto etc. are described in the Visuddhimagga.

Apārutā means open (vivaṭā).

Amatassā means of Nibbāna (nirvāṇa).

Dvārā means paths of entry (pavesanamaggā).

Aveccappasādenā means with unwavering faith (acalappasādena).

Dhammavinītā means led out by the right guidance (sammāniyyānena niyyātā).

Svākkhāto, Visuddhimagge, apārutā, amatassā, dvārā, aveccappasādenā, dhammavinītā

Atthāyaṃ itarā pajā he says with reference to the non-returner (anāgāmino sandhāyāha), it is said that non-returners exist (anāgāmino ca atthīti vuttaṃ hoti).

Puññabhāgā means produced by a meritorious portion (puññakoṭṭhāsena nibbattā).

Ottappa means a measure of reverence (ottappamāno).

Therefore, "never may I be false" means he reveals that I am not able to be reckoned because of fear of false speech (musāvādabhayena saṅkhātuṃ na sakkomi), but not that I do not have the power to be reckoned (na pana mama saṅkhātuṃ balaṃ natthīti dīpeti).

291. ‘Imamatthaṃ, bhante, brahmā sanaṅkumāro bhāsittha, imamatthaṃ, bhante, brahmuno sanaṅkumārassa bhāsato vessavaṇassa mahārājassa evaṃ cetaso parivitakko udapādi – ‘‘acchariyaṃ vata bho, abbhutaṃ vata bho, evarūpopi nāma uḷāro satthā bhavissati, evarūpaṃ uḷāraṃ dhammakkhānaṃ, evarūpā uḷārā visesādhigamā paññāyissantī’’ti. Atha, bhante, brahmā sanaṅkumāro vessavaṇassa mahārājassa cetasā cetoparivitakkamaññāya vessavaṇaṃ mahārājānaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘taṃ kiṃ maññati bhavaṃ vessavaṇo mahārājā atītampi addhānaṃ evarūpo uḷāro satthā ahosi, evarūpaṃ uḷāraṃ dhammakkhānaṃ, evarūpā uḷārā visesādhigamā paññāyiṃsu. Anāgatampi addhānaṃ evarūpo uḷāro satthā bhavissati, evarūpaṃ uḷāraṃ dhammakkhānaṃ, evarūpā uḷārā visesādhigamā paññāyissantī’’’ti.
“Venerable sir, this was the meaning spoken by Brahmā Sanankumāra. As Brahmā Sanankumāra was speaking this meaning, such a thought arose in the mind of the Great King Vessavaṇa: ‘It is amazing, it is wonderful, that such a glorious ( uḷāra) Teacher (satthā) should arise, that there should be such a glorious ( uḷāra) proclamation of the Dharma (dhammakkhānaṃ), and that such glorious ( uḷārā) paths to the sublime (visesādhigamā) should be made known (paññāyissantī)!’"

Then, venerable sir, Brahmā Sanankumāra, knowing with his mind the thought in the mind of the Great King Vessavaṇa, said this to the Great King Vessavaṇa: "What do you think, Great King Vessavaṇa? In the past ( atītampi addhānaṃ) there has been such a glorious (uḷāro) Teacher (satthā), such a glorious (uḷāraṃ) proclamation of the Dharma (dhammakkhānaṃ), and such glorious (uḷārā) paths to the sublime (visesādhigamā) were made known (paññāyiṃsu).

And there will be again in the future (anāgatampi addhānaṃ) such a glorious (uḷāro) Teacher (satthā), such a glorious (uḷāraṃ) proclamation of the Dharma (dhammakkhānaṃ), and such glorious (uḷārā) paths to the sublime (visesādhigamā) will be made known (paññāyissantī).'"
291.Taṃ kiṃ maññati bhavanti iminā kevalaṃ vessavaṇaṃ pucchati, na panassa evarūpo satthā nāhosīti vā na bhavissatīti vā laddhi atthi. Sabbabuddhānañhi abhisamaye viseso natthi.
What does he think that is, O Bhava? With this, he only asks Vessavaṇa. But the view that a teacher of such a form did not exist or will not exist does not exist for him. Indeed, there is no difference in the clear understanding of all Buddhas.

292. ‘‘‘Imamatthaṃ, bhante, brahmā sanaṅkumāro devānaṃ tāvatiṃsānaṃ abhāsi, imamatthaṃ vessavaṇo mahārājā brahmuno sanaṅkumārassa devānaṃ tāvatiṃsānaṃ bhāsato sammukhā sutaṃ sutvā (sī. pī.) sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ sayaṃ parisāyaṃ ārocesi’’. Imamatthaṃ janavasabho yakkho vessavaṇassa mahārājassa sayaṃ parisāyaṃ bhāsato sammukhā sutaṃ sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ paṭiggahetvā (sī. pī.) bhagavato ārocesi. Imamatthaṃ bhagavā janavasabhassa yakkhassa sammukhā sutvā sammukhā paṭiggahetvā sāmañca abhiññāya āyasmato ānandassa ārocesi, imamatthamāyasmā ānando bhagavato sammukhā sutvā sammukhā paṭiggahetvā ārocesi bhikkhūnaṃ bhikkhunīnaṃ upāsakānaṃ upāsikānaṃ. Tayidaṃ brahmacariyaṃ iddhañceva phītañca vitthārikaṃ bāhujaññaṃ puthubhūtaṃ yāva devamanussehi suppakāsitanti. paṭiggahetvā (sī. pī.) Janavasabhasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ pañcamaṃ.
“Venerable sir, Brahmā Sanankumāra spoke about this matter to the gods of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven. The great king Vessavaṇa heard this matter directly from Brahmā Sanankumāra as he spoke to the gods of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, accepted it directly, and announced it himself in the assembly.”

Janavasabha the Yaksha heard this matter directly from the great king Vessavaṇa as he spoke in his own assembly, accepted it directly, and reported it to the Blessed One.

Having heard this matter directly from Janavasabha the Yaksha and having accepted it directly, the Blessed One, through his own understanding, related it to the venerable Ānanda. The venerable Ānanda, having heard this matter directly from the Blessed One and having accepted it directly, related it to the monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen.

And so this holy life has become successful, prosperous, extended, popular, widespread, and well proclaimed among gods and humans.

The Janavasabha Sutta, the fifth, is finished.
292.Sayaṃparisāyanti attano parisāyaṃ. Tayidaṃ brahmacariyanti taṃ idaṃ sakalaṃ sikkhattayabrahmacariyaṃ. Sesaṃ uttānameva. Imāni pana padāni dhammasaṅgāhakattherehi ṭhapitānīti. Iti sumaṅgalavilāsiniyā dīghanikāyaṭṭhakathāyaṃ Janavasabhasuttavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitā.
292. Sayaṃparisāya means in his own assembly.

Tayidaṃ brahmacariya means that entire three-fold training and holy life.

The rest is evident.

These words, however, were established by the elders who compiled the Dhamma.

Thus ends the commentary on the Janavasabha Sutta in the Sumaṅgalavilāsinī, commentary to the Dīgha Nikāya.
