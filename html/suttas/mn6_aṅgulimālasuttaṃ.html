347. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī in the Jeta Grove, Anāthapindika’s monastery.

And at that time, in King Pasenadi of Kosala's realm, there was a bandit named Aṅgulimāla, who was brutal, bloody-handed, devoted to killing and slaying, and showed no mercy to living beings.

He had turned villages into non-villages, towns into non-towns, settled countrysides into unsettled countrysides.

Having repeatedly killed human beings, he wore a garland of fingers (aṅgulīmāla).

Then, in the morning, having dressed, the Blessed One took his bowl and robe and entered Sāvatthī for alms.

Having wandered for alms in Sāvatthī, and returning from his alms round after his meal, he set his lodging in order. Carrying his bowl and robe, he went along the road to where the bandit Aṅgulimāla was staying.

Cowherds, shepherds, farmers, and travelers saw the Blessed One going along the road to where the bandit Aṅgulimāla was staying.

Having seen the Blessed One, they said this to him: "Don't go along that road, samaṇa (ascetic).

On that road, samaṇa, is the bandit Aṅgulimāla, who is brutal, bloody-handed, devoted to killing and slaying, and shows no mercy to living beings.

He has turned villages into non-villages, towns into non-towns, and settled countrysides into unsettled countrysides.

Having repeatedly killed human beings, he wears a garland of fingers.

Indeed, samaṇa, groups of ten, twenty, thirty, forty, even fifty men have gone along this road,

and even they have fallen into the hands of the bandit Aṅgulimāla."

When this was said, the Blessed One kept going in silence.

A second time... A third time, cowherds, shepherds, farmers, and travelers said this to the Blessed One: "Don't go along that road, samaṇa. On that road, samaṇa, is the bandit Aṅgulimāla, who is brutal, bloody-handed, devoted to killing and slaying, and shows no mercy to living beings. 347. Thus have I heard.
This is the Aṅgulimāla Sūtra.
Therein, regarding "he wears a garland of fingers," why does he wear [it]? Because of his teacher's instruction.
This is the sequential story:

It is said that he took rebirth in the womb of a Brāhmaṇī woman named Mantāṇī, the chief queen of King Kosala.
In the night, the Brāhmaṇī experienced a stirring in her womb.
At the time of his birth from his mother's womb, weapons blazed in the entire city, and the king's auspicious bird, which had been placed on the royal bed, also blazed.
The Brahmin, having gone out, while looking at the constellation, [saw that he] was born under a robber constellation. So he went to the king and asked about [his] happiness and rest.

The king [asked], "Where is happiness, teacher? My auspicious weapons are blazing; I think there will be an obstacle to the kingdom or to life."
[The Brāhmin replied,] "Do not fear, great king; a boy has been born in my house. By the power of his merit, not only in your palace, but also in the entire city weapons are blazing."
"What will he become, teacher?" "He will become a thief, great king."
"Will he be a lone thief, or a thief who harms the kingdom?" "He will be a lone thief, deva."
Having spoken thus, wishing to grasp the king's mind, he said, "Kill him, deva!"
"Being a lone thief, what will he do? He will be like a single rice stalk in a field of a thousand karīsas. Guard him!"
While they were taking his name, the auspicious sword-stick that had been placed on the bed, the arrows that had been placed on the roof, and the razor for making tālavaṇṭas that had been placed in the cotton [all] blazed. These, while blazing, did no harm. Therefore they gave him the name Ahiṃsaka (Non-Harmer).
At the time for receiving instruction in the arts, they sent him to Takkasīla.

Ahiṃsaka, having become a resident of the Dharma, began his training in the arts.
He was accomplished in his vows, quick to respond to what needed to be done, pleasant in his conduct, and sweet in his speech.
The other residents became hostile.
They [thought], "From the time Ahiṃsaka-youth arrived, we are not known [or, visible]. How can we break him?" Sitting down, they planned, "Having made a separation between the teacher, we will break him." Becoming three groups, first some approached the teacher, bowed, and stood [before him].
"What is it, tāta?" "In this house a story is heard."
"What is it, tāta?" "We think that Ahiṃsaka-youth is ruining [you] among yourselves."
The teacher, scolding, said, "Go away, vasalas! Do not destroy my son among yourselves!" And he spat [at them].
Then the others, and then those three groups also, having come and having spoken in the same way, said, "Not believing us, examine and know [for yourselves]!"

The teacher, having seen them speaking with affection, thinking "There must be attachment," considered, "Should I kill him or not?"
Then he thought, "If I kill him, [people will say] 'The teacher, the leader of the region, having created an offense, deprives of life the youth who has come to his presence to receive instruction in the arts.'"
Again, no one will come to receive instruction in the arts; thus my gain will be diminished." Then, [thinking] "He is no longer of use for the arts," I will say, "Kill a thousand men!"
Surely one will rise up here and kill him.

“We are born in a family that does not harm living beings, it is not possible, teacher.”

“A craft ( śippa , skill) without proper respect does not give fruit, dear father.”

He took the five weapons, paid homage to his teacher, and entered the forest.

Even at the place of entering the forest, even in the middle of the forest, even at the place of exiting the forest, having stood, he would kill people.

He would not take clothing or wrapping.

Counting only “one, two,” he goes, he does not even learn counting.

Even by nature, he is wise, but his mind does not settle because he is a slayer of living beings. Therefore, unsystematically, he does not even consider counting, having cut off each finger, he sets it aside.

The fingers rot in the place where they are set aside; therefore, having pierced [them], having made a garland of fingers, he wore it. Because of that, the designation "Aṅgulimāla" (garland of fingers) arose in him.

He made the entire forest devoid of movement; there was no one by name who was able to go to the forest for the sake of wood and so on.

In the night part even having come to a village, having struck the door with his foot, he would knock it down.

Then, having killed [people] even in their sleep, taking “one, one,” he goes.

The village moved away [to another place], it stayed in a market town; the market town [moved] to a city.

People, starting from three yojanas away, abandoning their houses, taking their children in their hands, having come, surrounding Sāvatthī, having built a camp, having gathered in the royal courtyard, said: "A thief, Aṅgulimāla by name, is in your majesty's conquered territory," and so on, speaking, they wept.

Bhaggava, knowing that "He will be my son," said to the brāhmaṇī: “Lady, a thief named Aṅgulimāla has arisen; he is none other than your son, Ahimsakakumāra (the harmless youth)."

Now the king will go out to seize him. What should be done?" "Go, husband, take my son and come here."

"I am not able, noble lady, for indeed, there is no trust in four types of people: a thief is untrustworthy because he is my old friend; a branch (of a tree) is untrustworthy because it is my old resting place; a king is untrustworthy because he reveres me; a woman is untrustworthy because she has come under my control."

But a mother’s heart is tender.

Therefore, having decided, "I myself will go and bring my son," she departed.

On that day, the Blessed One, at dawn, looking at the world, seeing Aṅgulimāla, [thought:] "If I go, there will be well-being for him.

Having stood in a forest without a village, having heard a four-lined verse, having gone forth in my presence, he will realize the six abhiññās (supernormal knowledges)."

"If I do not go, having offended his mother, he will be irredeemable; I will make an act of grace towards him," having dressed in the early morning, having entered [the village] for alms, having finished his meal, desiring to favor him, he departed from the vihāra.

To show this meaning, "Then, indeed, the Blessed One," and so on, was said.

348. Then the Blessed One, remaining silent, went on.

The thief Aṅgulimāla saw the Blessed One coming in the distance.

Seeing him, he thought: “It is wonderful, truly amazing! Groups of ten, twenty, thirty, forty, and fifty men go along this road, struggling along.

And even they fall into my hands. But now this ascetic comes attacking, as it were, alone and without a companion.

Why don’t I rob this ascetic of his life?”

Then the thief Aṅgulimāla, taking up his sword and shield, buckling on his bow and quiver, followed right behind the Blessed One.

Then the Blessed One worked such a feat of psychic power that Aṅgulimāla, though running with all his might, could not catch up with the Blessed One walking at normal pace.

Then this thought occurred to the thief Aṅgulimāla: “It is wonderful, truly amazing! In the past I could catch even a running elephant, a running horse, a running chariot, a running deer. But now, though running with all my might, I cannot catch up with this ascetic walking at normal pace!” He stopped and said this to the Blessed One: “Stop, stop, ascetic!”

“I have stopped, Aṅgulimāla. You stop.”

Then this thought occurred to the thief Aṅgulimāla: “These ascetics, sons of the Śākyans, are speakers of the truth, asserters of the truths. But this ascetic, even while walking, says, ‘I have stopped, Aṅgulimāla. You stop.’ Why don’t I question this ascetic?”

For karma, when being done, fills up three sections.

In the seven consciousnesses, whether wholesome or unwholesome, the first impulsive consciousness is called karma experienced as pleasant in the immediate present ( dṛṣṭadharmavedanīyakarma).

It gives its result in this same selfhood.

Likewise, if it is unable to do so, it is called lapsed karma ( ahosikamma): “There has been karma, there has been no karma-result, there will be no karma-result, there is no karma-result,” according to this triad.

The seventh impulsive consciousness that accomplishes the aim is called karma ripening in the next existence (upapajjavedanīyakamma).

It gives its result in the next selfhood.

Likewise, if it is unable to do so, it is called lapsed karma ( ahosikamma) in the way already described.

The five impulsive consciousnesses in between are called karma ripening in subsequent existences (aparāpariyavedanīyakamma).

It gives its result in the future when it gets the opportunity.

As long as the round of rebirths continues, it never becomes lapsed karma ( ahosikamma).

But for the Elder, these two karmas—karma ripening in the next existence and karma ripening in subsequent existences—have been uprooted by the path of arahantship, which destroys karma; karma experienced as pleasant in the immediate present still exists.

"Association with sensual pleasure" (kāmaratisanthava): In both kinds of sensual pleasures, do not pursue or engage in attachment and delight in craving.

"This was not a bad decision of mine" (nayidaṃ dummantitaṃ mamā): The thought I had, seeing the Perfectly Awakened Buddha, that I would go forth, that thought of mine was not a bad decision.

"Amongst the divided qualities" (saṃvibhattesu dhammesū): Among the qualities that have arisen in the world, saying "I am the teacher" (ahaṃ satthāti), that which is the best, nirvāṇa (nibbāna), that itself I approached, attained, and reached. Therefore, this coming of mine is named "welcome" (svāgataṃ), gone (gatanti).

"Three knowledges" (tisso vijjā): Knowledge of former abodes (pubbenivāsa), divine eye (dibbacakkhu), and knowledge of the destruction of the outflows (āsavakkhayapaññā).

"The Buddha's teaching has been done" (kataṃ buddhassa sāsana): Whatever duties should be done in the Buddha's teaching, all that has been done by me.

Through the three knowledges (tīhi vijjāhi) and the nine supramundane qualities (lokuttaradhammehi), the teaching has been brought to its highest point.

Association with sensual pleasure (kāmaratisanthava), this was not a bad decision of mine (nayidaṃ dummantitaṃ mamā), amongst the divided qualities (saṃvibhattesu dhammesū), three knowledges (tisso vijjā), the Buddha's teaching has been done (kataṃ buddhassa sāsana).

The Aṅgulimālasuttavaṇṇanā, commentary to the Aṅgulimāla Sutta in the Papañcasūdanī, commentary to the Majjhima Nikāya, is finished.
