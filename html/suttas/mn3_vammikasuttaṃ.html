Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Anāthapindika’s monastery, in the Jeta Grove, near Sāvatthi.

Now on that occasion the venerable Kumārakassapa was living in the Blind Men’s Grove (andhavana).

Then, when the night was well advanced, a certain deity (devatā) of beautiful appearance who illuminated the whole of the Blind Men’s Grove approached the venerable Kumārakassapa and stood at one side.

Standing at one side, that deity said this to the venerable Kumārakassapa:

“Monk, this anthill (vammiko), [namely:] the anthill (vammīko) is in some way corresponding to Sanskrit, smokes by night, and blazes by day.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Dig, wise Sumedha (sumedha), taking the knife (sattham)’.

Digging, wise Sumedha, taking the knife, saw a bar (laṅgiṃ): ‘A bar (laṅgī), venerable sir (bhadante)’.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Remove the bar (laṅgiṃ); dig, wise Sumedha, taking the knife’.

Digging, wise Sumedha, taking the knife, saw a toad (uddhumāyikaṃ).

‘A toad (uddhumāyikā), venerable sir’.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Remove the toad (uddhumāyikaṃ); dig, wise Sumedha, taking the knife’.

Digging, wise Sumedha, taking the knife, saw a fork (dvidhāpathaṃ).

‘A fork (dvidhāpatho), venerable sir’.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Remove the fork (dvidhāpathaṃ); dig, wise Sumedha, taking the knife’.

Digging, wise Sumedha, taking the knife, saw a sieve (caṅgavāraṃ).

‘A sieve (caṅgavāro), venerable sir’.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Remove the sieve (caṅgavāraṃ); dig, wise Sumedha, taking the knife’.

Digging, wise Sumedha, taking the knife, saw a tortoise (kummaṃ).

‘A tortoise (kummo), venerable sir’.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Remove the tortoise (kummaṃ); dig, wise Sumedha, taking the knife’.

Digging, wise Sumedha, taking the knife, saw an axe and block (asisūnaṃ).

‘An axe and block (asisūnā), venerable sir’.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Remove the axe and block (asisūnaṃ); dig, wise Sumedha, taking the knife’.

Digging, wise Sumedha, taking the knife, saw a piece of meat (maṃsapesiṃ).

‘A piece of meat (maṃsapesi), venerable sir’.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Remove the piece of meat (maṃsapesiṃ); dig, wise Sumedha, taking the knife’.

Digging, wise Sumedha, taking the knife, saw a Nāga serpent (nāgaṃ).

‘A Nāga serpent (nāgo), venerable sir’.

The brāhmana said thus: ‘Let the Nāga serpent stay; do not harm the Nāga serpent; honor the Nāga serpent’."

249.Evaṃme sutanti vammikasuttaṃ. Tattha āyasmāti piyavacanametaṃ. Kumārakassapoti tassa nāmaṃ. Kumārakāle pabbajitattā pana bhagavatā, ‘‘kassapaṃ pakkosatha, idaṃ phalaṃ vā khādanīyaṃ vā kassapassa dethā’’ti vutte, katarassa kassapassāti kumārakassapassāti evaṃ gahitanāmattā tato paṭṭhāya vuḍḍhakālepi ‘‘kumārakassapo’’ tveva vuccati. Apica raññā posāvanikaputtattāpi taṃ ‘‘kumārakassapo’’ti sañjāniṃsu. Ayaṃ panassa pubbayogato paṭṭhāya āvibhāvakathā – Thero kira padumuttarassa bhagavato kāle seṭṭhiputto ahosi. Athekadivasaṃ bhagavantaṃ citrakathiṃ ekaṃ attano sāvakaṃ ṭhānantare ṭhapentaṃ disvā bhagavato sattāhaṃ dānaṃ datvā, ‘‘ahampi bhagavā anāgate ekassa buddhassa ayaṃ thero viya citrakathī sāvako bhaveyya’’nti patthanaṃ katvā puññāni karonto kassapassa bhagavato sāsane pabbajitvā visesaṃ nibbattetuṃ nāsakkhi. Tadā kira parinibbutassa bhagavato sāsane osakkante pañca bhikkhū nisseṇiṃ bandhitvā pabbataṃ abhiruyha samaṇadhammaṃ akaṃsu. Saṅghatthero tatiyadivase arahattaṃ patto. Anuthero catutthadivase anāgāmī ahosi. Itare tayo visesaṃ nibbattetuṃ asakkontā devaloke nibbattiṃsu. Tesaṃ ekaṃ buddhantaraṃ devesu ca manussesu ca sampattiṃ anubhontānaṃ eko takkasilāyaṃ rājakule nibbattitvā pukkusāti nāma rājā hutvā bhagavantaṃ uddissa pabbajitvā rājagahaṃ gacchanto kumbhakārasālāyaṃ bhagavato dhammadesanaṃ sutvā anāgāmiphalaṃ patto. Eko ekasmiṃ samuddapaṭṭane kulaghare nibbattitvā nāvaṃ āruyha bhinnanāvo dārucīrāni nivāsetvā lābhasampattiṃ patto, ‘‘ahaṃ arahā’’ti cittaṃ uppādetvā, ‘‘na tvaṃ arahā, gaccha satthāraṃ pañhaṃ pucchā’’ti atthakāmāya devatāya codito tathā katvā arahattaphalaṃ patto. Eko rājagahe ekissā kuladārikāya kucchimhi uppanno. Sā ca paṭhamaṃ mātāpitaro yācitvā pabbajjaṃ alabhamānā kulagharaṃ gatā gabbhasaṇṭhitampi ajānantī sāmikaṃ ārādhetvā tena anuññātā bhikkhunīsu pabbajitā. Tassā gabbhininimittaṃ disvā bhikkhuniyo devadattaṃ pucchiṃsu, so ‘‘assamaṇī’’ti āha. Dasabalaṃ pucchiṃsu, satthā upālittheraṃ paṭicchāpesi. Thero sāvatthinagaravāsīni kulāni visākhañca upāsikaṃ pakkosāpetvā sodhento, – ‘‘pure laddho gabbho, pabbajjā arogā’’ti āha. Satthā ‘‘suvinicchitaṃ adhikaraṇa’’nti therassa sādhukāraṃ adāsi. Sā bhikkhunī suvaṇṇabimbasadisaṃ puttaṃ vijāyi, taṃ gahetvā rājā pasenadi kosalo posāpesi. ‘‘Kassapo’’ti cassa nāmaṃ katvā aparabhāge alaṅkaritvā satthu santikaṃ netvā pabbājesi. Iti rañño posāvanikaputtattāpi taṃ ‘‘kumārakassapo’’ti sañjāniṃsūti. Andhavaneti evaṃnāmake vane. Taṃ kira vanaṃ dvinnaṃ buddhānaṃ kāle avijahitanāmaṃ andhavanaṃtveva paññāyati. Tatrāyaṃ paññattivibhāvanā – appāyukabuddhānañhi sarīradhātu na ekagghanā hoti. Adhiṭṭhānānubhāvena vippakiriyati. Teneva amhākampi bhagavā, – ‘‘ahaṃ na ciraṭṭhitiko, appakehi sattehi ahaṃ diṭṭho, yehi na diṭṭho, teva bahutarā, te me dhātuyo ādāya tattha tattha pūjentā saggaparāyaṇā bhavissantī’’ti parinibbānakāle, ‘‘attano sarīraṃ vippakiriyatū’’ti adhiṭṭhāsi. Dīghāyukabuddhānaṃ pana suvaṇṇakkhandho viya ekagghanaṃ dhātusarīraṃ tiṭṭhati. Andhavane Kassapassāpi bhagavato tatheva aṭṭhāsi. Tato mahājanā sannipatitvā, ‘‘dhātuyo ekagghanā na sakkā viyojetuṃ, kiṃ karissāmā’’ti sammantayitvā ekagghanameva cetiyaṃ karissāma, kittakaṃ pana hotu tanti āhaṃsu. Eke sattayojaniyanti āhaṃsu. Etaṃ atimahantaṃ, anāgate jaggituṃ na sakkā, chayojanaṃ hotu, pañcayojanaṃ… catuyojanaṃ… tiyojanaṃ… dviyojanaṃ… ekayojanaṃ hotūti sanniṭṭhānaṃ katvā iṭṭhakā kīdisā hontūti bāhirante iṭṭhakā rattasuvaṇṇamayā ekagghanā satasahassagghanikā hontu, abbhantarimante paññāsasahassagghanikā. Haritālamanosilāhi mattikākiccaṃ kayiratu, telena udakakiccanti niṭṭhaṃ gantvā cattāri mukhāni catudhā vibhajiṃsu. Ācariyassa paṇṇato pana cetiyaṭṭhānato paṇṇaṃ paṭhamataraṃ ācariyassa hatthaṃ agamāsi. So paṇṇaṃ vācetvā cetiyaṃ vandissāmīti ekakova nikkhami. Antarāmagge aṭaviyaṃ pañca corasatāni uṭṭhahiṃsu. Tatrekacce taṃ disvā iminā sakalajambudīpato hiraññasuvaṇṇaṃ sampiṇḍitaṃ, nidhikumbhī no pavaṭṭamānā āgatāti avasesānaṃ ārocetvā taṃ aggahesuṃ. Kasmā tātā, maṃ gaṇhathāti? 249. Thus have I heard (me suta) from the Vammikasutta. There, the venerable (āyasmā) is a term of affection. “Kumārakassapa” was his name. Because he went forth in his youth (kumārakāle pabbajitattā), the Blessed One (bhagavatā) said, "Call Kassapa, give this fruit or food to Kassapa," and because his name was taken up as "Kumārakassapa," therefore even in old age, he was called "Kumārakassapa." This is what is meant. Moreover, they recognized him as "Kumārakassapa" also because the king raised him as his adopted son (posāvanikaputtattāpi). This is the story of his manifestation from his prior practice:

The elder (thero) was a wealthy man's son (seṭṭhiputto) in the time of the Blessed One Padumuttara. Then, one day, having seen the Blessed One placing one of his disciples, Citrakathi, in a certain position, and having given alms (dānaṃ) to the Blessed One for a week (sattāhaṃ), he made the aspiration (patthanaṃ), "May I also, in the future, be a disciple of a Buddha, a Citrakathi, like this elder (thero viya citrakathī sāvako bhaveyya)," and while performing meritorious deeds (puññāni karonto), he was unable to attain distinction (visesaṃ nibbattetuṃ nāsakkhi) after going forth (pabbajitvā) in the dispensation (sāsane) of the Blessed One Kassapa.

Then, when the dispensation of the Blessed One, who had attained final nirvāṇa (parinibbutassa bhagavato), declined (osakkante), five monks (bhikkhū) built a ladder (nisseṇiṃ bandhitvā), climbed a mountain (pabbataṃ abhiruyha), and practiced the monastic life (samaṇadhammaṃ akaṃsu). The Sangha elder (saṅghatthero) attained arahantship (arahattaṃ patto) on the third day (tatiyadivase). The sub-elder (anuthero) became a non-returner (anāgāmī ahosi) on the fourth day (catutthadivase). The other three, unable to achieve distinction (visesaṃ nibbattetuṃ asakkontā), were reborn in the heaven world (devaloke nibbattiṃsu). Of those (tesaṃ), one (eko), experiencing prosperity (sampattiṃ anubhontānaṃ) among gods (devesu ca) and humans (manussesu ca) for an aeon (buddhantaraṃ), was reborn in a royal family (rājakule nibbattitvā) in Takkasilā, became a king named Pukkusāti (pukkusāti nāma rājā hutvā), went forth (pabbajitvā) having renounced the Blessed One (bhagavantaṃ uddissa), and while going to Rājagaha (rājagahaṃ gacchanto), having heard the Blessed One's (bhagavato) teaching of the Dharma (dhammadesanaṃ sutvā) in a potter's shed (kumbhakārasālāyaṃ), attained the fruit of non-returning (anāgāmiphalaṃ patto). One (eko), having been reborn (nibbattitvā) in a family (kulaghare) in a certain seaport town (samuddapaṭṭane), having boarded a ship (nāvaṃ āruyha), having settled the timber dues of the broken ships (bhinnanāvo dārucīrāni nivāsetvā), attained wealth and prosperity (lābhasampattiṃ patto), and having generated the thought, "I am an arahant (arahā)," was urged by a deity (devatāya codito) who desired his welfare (atthakāmāya), "You are not an arahant, go ask the teacher (satthāraṃ pañhaṃ pucchā)," having done so (tathā katvā), he attained the fruit of arahantship (arahattaphalaṃ patto).

One (eko) was born (uppanno) in Rājagaha in the womb (kucchimhi) of a certain maiden (kuladārikāya). She (sā ca), first (paṭhamaṃ) having asked her parents (mātāpitaro yācitvā) and not obtaining ordination (pabbajjaṃ alabhamānā), having gone to a family home (kulagharaṃ gatā), not even knowing that she was pregnant (gabbhasaṇṭhitampi ajānantī), having pleased her husband (sāmikaṃ ārādhetvā) and being permitted by him (tena anuññātā), went forth among the nuns (bhikkhunīsu pabbajitā). The nuns (bhikkhuniyo), having seen the signs of her pregnancy (gabbhininimittaṃ disvā), asked Devadatta (devadattaṃ pucchiṃsu), and he (so) said, "She is not a samaṇa (assamaṇī)." They asked the One with Ten Powers (dasabalaṃ pucchiṃsu), and the Teacher (satthā) had Upāli, the elder (upālittheraṃ), examine her (paṭicchāpesi). The elder (thero), having had families (kulāni) living in Sāvatthi (sāvatthinagaravāsīni) and Visākha, the female lay follower (upāsikaṃ pakkosāpetvā), summoned and purified her (sodhento), said, "The pregnancy was conceived before (pure laddho gabbho), the going forth is healthy (pabbajjā arogā)." The Teacher (satthā) gave his approval (sādhukāraṃ adāsi) to the elder (therassa), "The case (adhikaraṇa) is well decided (suvinicchitaṃ)." That nun (sā bhikkhunī) gave birth (vijāyi) to a son (puttaṃ) like a golden image (suvaṇṇabimbasadisaṃ), and having taken him (taṃ gahetvā), King (rājā) Pasenadi Kosala raised him (posāpesi). Having named him Kassapa (kassapo’’ti nāmaṃ katvā) and decorating him (alaṅkaritvā) at a later time (aparabhāge), he led him (netvā) to the Teacher's (satthu) presence (santikaṃ) and had him ordained (pabbājesi). Andhavana (andhavane) means in such and such a forest (evaṃnāmake vane). That forest (taṃ kira vanaṃ), in the time of two Buddhas (dvinnaṃ buddhānaṃ kāle), is known as Andhavana (andhavanaṃtveva paññāyati), having not lost its name (avijahitanāmaṃ). There (tatra), this is the exposition of the designation (paññattivibhāvanā) - indeed, the bodily relics (sarīradhātu) of Buddhas with short lifespans (appāyukabuddhānañhi) are not of one mass (na ekagghanā hoti). By the power of their resolution (adhiṭṭhānānubhāvena), they are scattered (vippakiriyati). Just so (teneva), our Blessed One (amhākampi bhagavā) – “I am not long-lasting (na ciraṭṭhitiko), I am seen by few beings (appakehi sattehi ahaṃ diṭṭho), those by whom I am not seen (yehi na diṭṭho), they are more numerous (teva bahutarā), taking my relics (dhātuyo ādāya), worshiping them here and there (tattha tattha pūjentā), they will be destined for heaven (saggaparāyaṇā bhavissantī)” – at the time of final Nirvāṇa (parinibbānakāle), resolved (adhiṭṭhāsi), “May my body (attano sarīraṃ) be scattered (vippakiriyatū)!” However (pana), the bodily relics (dhātusarīraṃ) of Buddhas with long lifespans (dīghāyukabuddhānaṃ) remain (tiṭṭhati) as one mass (ekagghanaṃ), like a golden trunk (suvaṇṇakkhandho viya).

In Andhavana (andhavane), it was the same (tatheva aṭṭhāsi) for the Blessed One Kassapa (kassapassāpi bhagavato).
Then, the great multitude gathered together and, having conferred, [said:] "The relics are solid and cannot be separated; what shall we do?" Having agreed, they [said:] "We will build a solid cetiya. How large should it be?"

251. Cātummahābhūtikassā means consisting of the four great elements ( catumahābhūtamayassa).

Kāyassetaṃ adhivacana means a name for the body ( sarīrassa nāmaṃ).

Just as there is an external anthill ( vammiko), it is called vammiko because it vomits (vamati), it is a vomiter ( vantako), it is a refuge for vomit ( vantussayo), and it is connected with the stickiness of vomit (vantasinehasambandho). [It is called vammiko] for these four reasons.

It is vammiko because it vomits various kinds of living beings such as snakes, ahimaṅgus, saundūras, house-geckos, and goḷikās.

Vantakoti vammiko means vammiko because it vomits sores (upacikāhi).

Upacikāhi vamitvā mukhatuṇḍakena ukkhittapaṃsucuṇṇena kaṭippamāṇenapi porisappamāṇenapi ussitoti vammiko means it is called vammiko because, having vomited sores, it is raised with the powder of dust thrown up by the mouth like the size of the hip or the size of a person.

Upacikāhi [page 2.0129] vantakheḷasinehena ābaddhatāya sattasattāhaṃ deve vassantepi na vippakiriyati, nidāghepi tato paṃsumuṭṭhiṃ gahetvā tasmiṃ muṭṭhinā pīḷiyamāne sineho nikkhamati, evaṃ vantasinehena sambaddhoti vammiko means because it is bound by the stickiness of vomit and saliva, even if the god rains for seven weeks, it is not scattered. Even in the hot season, if a handful of dust is taken from it and the stickiness comes out when it is pressed with that fist, it is vammiko because it is connected with the stickiness of vomit in this way.

This body is also like that. "From the eye [page 2.0035] is eye-excrement," and in this way, it is vammiko because it vomits various kinds of impure dirt.

Buddhapaccekabuddhakhīṇāsavā imasmiṃ attabhāve nikantipariyādānena attabhāvaṃ chaḍḍetvā gatāti ariyehi vantakotipi vammiko means it is also vammiko because buddhas, paccekabuddhas, and khīṇāsavas, having abandoned existence in this existence by means of the complete abandoning of craving, have gone, [and] it is vomited by the noble ones (ariyehi vantakotipi vammiko).

What are the seven? Lust is eliminated, hatred… delusion… pride… belief in a permanent self (sakkāyadiṭṭhi)… doubt (vicikicchā)… clinging to rules and vows (sīlabbataparāmāsa) is eliminated.
It is because of the elimination of these seven qualities, monk, that [one is called] a brāhmaṇa." (Cūḷani. Mettagūmāṇavapucchāniddesa 28).

"Wise" (sumedho) means beautiful wisdom (sundarapañño).
"Sekkhassā," here, because he is training, [he is called] a trainee (sekkho).
As he said, "Because he trains, monk, therefore he is called a trainee (sekkho).
And what does he train in? He trains in higher virtue (adhisīla), he trains in higher mind (adhicitta), and he trains in higher wisdom (adhipañña)." (A. Ni. 3.86).

This is an appellation of the Thus-Gone One, a brāhmaṇa, wise (sumedho), a trainee (sekkhassa).

"Appellation of wisdom" (paññāya adhivacana) means this is an appellation of mundane and supramundane wisdom, not of a weapon or sword.
"Of energetic effort" (vīriyārambhassa) [means] of bodily and mental energy (vīriya).
That is just the course of wisdom (paññāgatikameva).
Mundane wisdom is mundane, supramundane wisdom is supramundane.
Here, however, this is an explanation of the meaning: of wisdom (paññāya).
The designation vīriyārambhassā:

Once upon a time, a learned brāhmaṇa from the countryside, leaving his village early in the morning with his students, would spend the day in the forest reciting mantras, returning to the village in the evening.

On the road between, there was a termite mound.

At night it smoked, and during the day it blazed.

The brāhmaṇa said to his pupil, the wise youth Sumedha, "Dear Sumedha, this termite mound smokes at night and blazes during the day. We will observe its changes. Split it into four parts and throw them away."

"As you wish," said Sumedha, and taking a spade, standing firmly on the ground with even feet, he did as he was told.

There, the Blessed One was like the ācārya brāhmaṇa.

The śaikṣa bhikṣu was like the wise youth Sumedha.

The body is like the termite mound.

"Dear Sumedha, this termite mound smokes at night and blazes during the day. We will observe its changes. Split it into four parts and throw them away." Just as this was the time the brāhmaṇa spoke, so the Blessed One spoke, "Monk, divide this body, composed of the four great elements, into four parts and examine it."

Just as the wise youth Sumedha took a spade, saying "As you wish," and acted accordingly, so the śaikṣa bhikṣu: "That which is solidity in the twenty parts is the earth element (pṛthivī-dhātu). That which is cohesion in the twelve parts is the water element (āpo-dhātu). That which is maturing in the four parts is the fire element (tejo-dhātu). That which is support in the six parts is the air element (vāyo-dhātu)." Thus, the consideration of the body in terms of the four elements should be understood.

laṅgīti kho, bhikkhūti why did the Blessed One show ignorance to be a cripple? Just as, when the gate of a city is shut and the bar is applied, the going forth of the people is obstructed, and those who are inside the city remain inside.

Those who are outside remain outside.

Just so, when the cripple of ignorance falls on the face of wisdom, the going forth of wisdom that leads to nirvāṇa is obstructed. Therefore, ignorance is shown to be a cripple.

pajaha avijjanti Here, the abandonment of ignorance is discussed in terms of taking up and questioning the object of meditation (karmaṭṭhāna).

laṅgīti kho, bhikkhū pajaha avijja uddhumāyikāti

kho, bhikkhūti Here, uddhumāyikamaṇḍūko is the name of a frog that is not large, but the size of a fingernail, and lives inside old leaves, trees, or vines.

When it is struck with the tip of a stick, the tip of a vine, or with powdered dust, it swells up and becomes large and round, the size of a beluva fruit. Then, making its four feet point towards the sky, it ceases to move, and goes into the power of its enemy, becoming food for crows and the like.

Just so, when this anger arises, at first it is only a slight disturbance of the mind.

If it is not checked at that moment, it grows and causes distortion of the face.

Then, if it is not checked, it causes trembling of the jaws.

Then, if it is not checked, it causes the uttering of harsh, unwanted speech.

Then, if it is not checked, it causes wild glances in all directions.

Then, if it is not checked, it causes pulling and tugging.

Then, if it is not checked, it causes touching a clod of dirt, a stick, or a weapon with the hand.

Then, if it is not checked, it causes striking with a stick or a weapon.

Then, if it is not checked, it causes harming others and harming oneself.

So it was said, "Because this anger, having killed others, kills oneself, to that extent this anger has reached the highest point and attained the greatest extent."

There, just as when the four feet of the uddhumāyika frog are in the sky, its movement ceases, and the uddhumāyika, going into the power of its enemy, becomes food for crows and the like, just so, a person possessed by anger cannot take up an object of meditation (karmaṭṭhāna) and develop it. He goes into the power of his enemy and becomes subject to the will of all the māras.

Therefore, the Blessed One said, "uddhumāyikāti kho, bhikkhu, this is a designation for the affliction of anger."

There, the affliction of anger is simply anger that has reached strength.

pajaha kodhūpāyāsanti Here, the abandonment of affliction is discussed through reflective abandonment.
“Good, bhikkhus! The bloated frog…”

“Give up anger and vexation! Concerning ‘the two-way path’ (dvidhāpatha), it is like a man with resources and possessions, traveling along a difficult road through the wilderness, who comes to a fork in the road (dvedhāpatha). Unable to decide, ‘Should I go this way? Or should I go that way?’ he just stands there. Then thieves rise up, inflict misfortune and disaster on him. Just so, a bhikkhu, having taken up a basic meditation subject (mūlakammaṭṭhāna) and sat down, if doubt (kaṅkhā) arises concerning the Buddha, etc., is unable to develop that meditation subject. Then all the māras, beginning with the kilesa-māra, inflict misfortune and disaster on him. Thus, uncertainty (vicikicchā) is like a two-way path.
Therefore, the Blessed One said: ‘Dvidhāpatha, bhikkhu, is a designation (adhivacana) for uncertainty (vicikicchā).’
‘Give up uncertainty (vicikicchā)’ means that abandoning uncertainty is discussed in terms of undertaking and questioning about a meditation subject.

The two-way path…

Give up uncertainty! Concerning ‘the drain’ (caṅgavāra), it is like when a launderer pours water into a filter for lye, one water-pot, or two, or ten, or twenty, or even a hundred pots, just keeps flowing out. Not even a drop of water remains. Just so, a puggala endowed with the hindrances (nīvaraṇa) has no wholesome qualities (kusala-dhamma) remaining within.
Therefore, the Blessed One said: ‘Caṅgavāra, bhikkhu, is a designation (adhivacana) for the five hindrances (pañcannaṃ nīvaraṇānaṃ)’
‘Give up the five hindrances (pañcanīvaraṇe)’ means that abandoning the hindrances is discussed in terms of overcoming them by means of strength and temporary suppression.

The drain, give up the five hindrances! Concerning ‘the turtle’ (kummo), it is like a turtle whose four feet and head are drawn in; there are just five limbs (aṅgāni). Just so, all conditioned (saṅkhata) things, when grasped, become just five aggregates (khandha).
Therefore, the Blessed One said: ‘The turtle, bhikkhu, is a designation (adhivacana) for the five aggregates of clinging (pañcannaṃ upādānakkhandhānaṃ)’
‘Give up the five aggregates of clinging (pañcupādānakkhandhe)’ means that abandoning desire and lust (chandarāgappahānaṃ) for the five aggregates is discussed.

The turtle, give up the five aggregates of clinging! Concerning ‘the butcher’s block’ (asisūnā), here, just as after putting meat on a butcher’s block (sūnā), they chop it with a sword (asinā), so these beings (sattā), being killed by the kilesa-desires (kilesakāmehi) for the sake of sense-objects (vatthukāmatthāya), having made the sense-objects supreme, are cut and chopped by the kilesa-desires.
Therefore, the Blessed One said: ‘The butcher’s block, bhikkhu, is a designation (adhivacana) for the five strands of sensual desire (pañcannaṃ kāmaguṇānaṃ)’
‘Give up the five strands of sensual desire (pañca kāmaguṇe)’ means that abandoning desire and lust (chandarāgappahānaṃ) for the five strands of sensual desire is discussed.

The butcher’s block…

Give up the five strands of sensual desire! Concerning ‘the lump of flesh’ (maṃsapesī), here, this ‘lump of flesh’ (maṃsapesī) is something desired by many people; even khattiyas and other humans desire it, and crows and other animals do too.
These beings, intoxicated by ignorance (avijjāya sammattā), approaching delight and lust (nandirāgaṃ upagamma), develop the round (vaṭṭaṃ vaḍḍhenti).
Or, just as a lump of flesh sticks wherever it is put, so these beings, bound by delight and lust (nandirāgabaddhā), stick in the round (vaṭṭe lagganti); even having attained suffering (dukkhaṃ patvāpi) they do not shrink back.
Thus, delight and lust is like a lump of flesh.
Therefore, the Blessed One said: ‘The lump of flesh, bhikkhu, is a designation (adhivacana) for delight and lust (nandirāgassa)’
‘Give up delight and lust (nandīrāga)’ means that abandoning delight and lust by means of the fourth path is discussed.

The lump of flesh…

Give up delight and lust!
