269. Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the Squirrels’ Sanctuary ( kalandakanivāpe).

Now on that occasion the wanderer Sakuludāyin was staying in the Peacocks’ Sanctuary, the wanderers’ park (moranivāpe paribbājakārāme), with a large assembly of wanderers.

Then, when it was morning, the Blessed One dressed, and taking his bowl and outer robe (pattacīvaramādāya), went into Rajagaha for alms.

Then the thought occurred to the Blessed One: “It is still too early to wander for alms in Rājagaha. Suppose I went to the wanderer Sakuludāyin in the Peacocks’ Sanctuary, the wanderers’ park (moranivāpo paribbājakārāmo).”

Then the Blessed One went to the Peacocks’ Sanctuary, the wanderers’ park (moranivāpo paribbājakārāmo).

At that time, the wanderer Sakuludāyin was sitting with a large company of wanderers, making a great noise and commotion, engaged in various kinds of irrelevant talk, such as talk about kings, robbers, and ministers of state; armies, dangers, and wars; food, drink, garments, and lodgings; garlands and scents; relatives, vehicles, villages, towns, cities, and countries; women and heroes; street talk; talk about those who have passed away; miscellaneous talk; stories about the world and the sea; and talk about existence and non-existence.

Then the wanderer Sakuludāyin saw the Blessed One coming in the distance. Seeing him, he quieted his own assembly thus: “Sirs, be quiet; sirs, make no noise. Here comes the śramaṇa Gotama; that venerable one likes quiet and praises quiet. Maybe, if he perceives our group as quiet, he will consider it worth his while to come our way.”

Then those wanderers were silent.

271. “Taṃyevettha paṭibhātūti”: If you are desirous of hearing the Dharma, a single question and a single cause should arise for you here.

“Yathā maṃ paṭibhāseyyāti”: The explanation shows that he teaches the Dharma happily, so that a teaching of the Dharma might arise for me through whatever cause, by that cause, with the arising of speech.

“Tassa mayhaṃ, bhanteti”: It seems that he, having seen that, recalling only the one with the ten powers, thought, "If the Blessed One were here, he would have made this meaning of the spoken word clear to me today by illuminating it like a thousand lamps."

Therefore, he said, "Tassa mayhaṃ, bhanteti," and so forth.

There, "Aho nūnāti" is a pair of particles in the sense of recollection.

By that, to him recollecting the Blessed One, it occurred, "Oh, surely the Blessed One, oh, surely the Well-gone One!"

"Yo imesanti": That is, of these dharmas.

"Sukusaloti": Very skillful, expert, clever.

That Blessed One, oh, surely he would speak; that Well-gone One, oh, surely he would speak. For the Blessed One’s gnosis of former abodes, many hundreds of thousands of eons, are manifestly single-grounded. This is the meaning here.

"Tassa vāhaṃ pubbantaṃ ārabbhāti": For indeed, one who is a gainer respectfully listens, knowing that he was a kṣatriya in the past, that he was a brāhmaṇa.

But one who is not a gainer only shows a mere shaking of the head, saying, "It will be thus, it will be thus."

Therefore, he said thus: "Having begun with the former end for him, may I please his mind with an explanation of the question."

Tassa vāhaṃ pubbantaṃ ārabbhā.

"So vā maṃ aparantanti": For indeed, to one possessing the divine eye, knowledge of the future succeeds; therefore, he said thus.

The other is just as was said before.

So vā maṃ aparanta dhammaṃ te desessāmīti: It seems that this one will not understand even when taught in the past, nor will he understand when taught in the future.

Then the Blessed One, wishing to teach him the causal mode that is gentle and subtle, said thus.

What then will he understand? He will not understand this even today, but the Blessed One, having seen that in the future a cause for his disposition will come to be, said thus.

Dhammaṃ te desessāmī paṃsupisācakanti: A goblin born in an impure place.

For he, having taken a root, has an invisible body.

There, this is the story: A certain yakṣiṇī, having made two children sit at the gate of the thūpārāma, went into the city to seek food.

The children, having seen a certain piṇḍapātika tthera, said, "Venerable sir, our mother has entered into the city. You should say to her, 'Having taken whatever gain there is, go quickly, for the children are not able to endure their hunger.'"

"How will I see her?" "Venerable sir, take this!" They gave him a piece of root.

Thousands of yakṣas appeared to the tthera. He saw that yakṣiṇī with the sign given by the children, deformed, repulsive, desiring only the filth of the womb in the street.

Having seen that meaning, he told it.

When she said, "How do you see me?", he showed her the piece of root. She snatched it away and took it.

Thus, the paṃsupisācakas, having taken a root, have invisible bodies.

With reference to that, he said, "I do not see even a paṃsupisācaka."

Na pakkhāyatīti: He is not seen, he does not appear.

Seeing in the future also the condition and state, the Blessed One, remaining, did not establish even one bhikkhu as foremost in loving-kindness meditation (mettāvihārimhi etadagge).

Indeed, the Blessed One sees: "In the future, this one, having gone forth in my teaching (sāsana), will be the foremost of those practicing loving-kindness meditation (mettāvihārīnaṃ aggo bhavissati)."

So, when the Blessed One had attained final emancipation (parinibbute), in the time of the righteous King Aśoka, having been reborn in Pāṭaliputra, having gone forth and attained arhatta, he became the Elder Assagutta, foremost of those practicing loving-kindness meditation (mettāvihārīnaṃ aggo ahosi).

By the Elder's power of loving-kindness (mettānubhāvena), even animals (tiracchānagatāpi) regained a mind of loving-kindness (mettacittaṃ paṭilabhiṃsu). The Elder, having become the instructor in the precepts (ovādācariyo) for the community of bhikkhus in all of Jambudīpa, dwelt in a hermitage suitable for his conduct (vattanisenāsane āvasi). A forest of thirty yojanas became a single meditation-house (padhānagharaṃ ahosi).

The Elder, having spread out a piece of leather in the sky, sat there and taught meditation (kammaṭṭhānaṃ kathesi).

As time went on, without even going for alms (bhikkhācārampi agantvā), sitting in the monastery, he taught meditation (kammaṭṭhānaṃ kathesi). People, going to the monastery itself, gave offerings (dānamadaṃsu).

King Dharmāśoka, having heard of the Elder's virtue (guṇaṃ sutvā), desiring to see him, sent [messengers] three times.

The Elder thought, "I will give counsel to the community of bhikkhus," but he did not go even once.

Assagutta Thera.

The Culassakuludayisuttavaṇṇanā is finished.
