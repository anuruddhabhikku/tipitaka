THUS HAVE I HEARD. On one occasion the Blessed One was wandering in the Magadhan country and eventually arrived at Rajagaha. There he went to the potter Bhaggava and said to him: “If it is not inconvenient for you, Bhaggava, I will stay one night in your workshop.”

“It is not inconvenient for me, venerable sir. There is a homeless one already staying there. If he consents, venerable sir, stay as long as you like.”

Then, by that occasion, a clansman named Pukkusāti, having faith in the Blessed One, had gone forth from the house life into homelessness. He was the first to stay in that potter's workshop. Then the Blessed One went to the venerable Pukkusāti and said to him: “If it is not inconvenient for you, bhikkhu, I will stay one night in the workshop.”

“It is spacious, friend, the potter's workshop. Let the venerable one stay as long as he likes.”
Then the Blessed One entered the potter’s workshop and, after preparing a spread of grass at one end, sat down, folding his legs crosswise, setting his body erect, and establishing mindfulness in front of him. Then the Blessed One spent much of the night seated in meditation. The venerable Pukkusāti also spent much of the night seated in meditation.

Then the Blessed One thought: “This clansman conducts himself in a way that inspires confidence. Suppose I were to question him.” Then the Blessed One said to the venerable Pukkusāti: “For whom, bhikkhu, have you gone forth? Who is your teacher? Whose Dhamma do you approve of?” “There is, friend, the renunciate Gotama, a son of the Sakyans, who went forth from the Sakyan clan. Now a good report of that Blessed Gotama has spread: ‘Indeed, that Blessed One is an Arhat, a fully enlightened Buddha, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, sublime, knower of the world, incomparable leader of persons to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans, enlightened, blessed.’ I have gone forth on account of that Blessed One, and that Blessed One is my teacher. I approve of the Dhamma of that Blessed One.” “But where, bhikkhu, does that Blessed One, the Arhat, the fully enlightened Buddha, now reside?” “There is, friend, a city in the northern districts named Sāvatthī. Would you recognise him if you saw him?” “No, friend, I have never seen that Blessed One before, nor would I recognise him if I saw him.”

343.
"This, bhikṣu, is a person consisting of six elements (chadhātu), six sense bases (chaphassāyatana), eighteen mental considerations (aṭṭhārasamanopavicāra), and four foundations (caturādhiṭṭhāna); where, standing, the currents of conceit (maññassava) do not proceed; and when the currents of conceit do not proceed, one is called a sage (muni) at peace (santa)."

One should not neglect wisdom (paññāṃ), should preserve truth (saccaṃ), should cultivate relinquishment (cāgaṃ), and should train for peace (santi). This is the summary (uddeso) of the exposition (vibhaṅgassa) of the elements (dhātu).

"And what, O bhikṣu, is the earth element (pathavīdhātu)? The earth element can be internal (ajjhattikā) or external (bāhirā).

And what, O bhikṣu, is the internal earth element (ajjhattikā pathavīdhātu)? So anabhisaṅkharonto anabhisañcetayanto bhavāya vā vibhavāya vā na kiñci <pb ed="T" n="3.0444"/> loke upādiyati, anupādiyaṃ na paritassati, aparitassaṃ paccattaṃyeva parinibbāyati. ‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ, nāparaṃ itthattāyā’ti pajānāti.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0444"/>
362. “He understands thus: ‘If I were to direct this equanimity, so purified and bright, to the base of infinite space (ākāsānañcāyatana) and to develop my mind accordingly, this would be conditioned (saṅkhataṃ etaṃ).

If I were to direct this equanimity, so purified and bright, to the base of infinite consciousness (viññāṇañcāyatana)… this would be conditioned.

If I were to direct this equanimity, so purified and bright, to the base of nothingness (ākiñcaññāyatana)… this would be conditioned.

If I were to direct this equanimity, so purified and bright, to the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (nevasaññānāsaññāyatana) and to develop my mind accordingly, this would be conditioned.’

Since he does not form any condition (abhisaṅkharoti) or generate any volition (abhisañcetayati) tending towards either being (bhavāya) or non-being (vibhavāya), he does not cling to anything (upādiyati) in this world (loke). Not clinging (anupādiyaṃ), he is not agitated (na paritassati). Being unagitated (aparitassaṃ), he personally attains Nirvana (parinibbāyati).

He understands: ‘Destroyed is birth (khīṇā jāti), the holy life has been lived (vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ), what had to be done has been done (kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ), there is no more coming to any state of being (nāparaṃ itthattāyā).’
363
<p n="363" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">363</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘So sukhañce vedanaṃ vedeti, ‘sā aniccā’ti pajānāti, ‘anajjhositā’ti pajānāti, ‘anabhinanditā’ti pajānāti. Dukkhañce vedanaṃ vedeti <pb ed="M" n="3.0288"/>, ‘sā aniccā’ti pajānāti, <hi rend="bold">‘anajjhositā’ti <pb ed="V" n="3.0294"/> pajānāti, ‘anabhinanditā’ti pajānāti. Adukkhamasukhañce vedanaṃ vedeti, ‘sā aniccā’ti pajānāti, ‘anajjhositā’ti pajānāti, ‘anabhinanditā’ti pajānāti.</hi>

<pb ed="V" n="3.0294"/>
If he feels a pleasant feeling (sukhaṃ vedanaṃ), he understands: ‘It is impermanent (aniccā)’; he understands: ‘It is not held to (anajjhositā)’; he understands: ‘It is not delighted in (anabhinanditā)’.

If he feels a painful feeling (dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ), he understands: ‘It is impermanent (aniccā)’; he understands: ‘It is not held to (anajjhositā)’; he understands: ‘It is not delighted in (anabhinanditā)’.

If he feels a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhaṃ vedanaṃ), he understands: ‘It is impermanent (aniccā)’; he understands: ‘It is not held to (anajjhositā)’; he understands: ‘It is not delighted in (anabhinanditā)’.
364
<p n="364" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">364</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘So sukhañce vedanaṃ vedeti, visaṃyutto naṃ vedeti; dukkhañce vedanaṃ vedeti, visaṃyutto naṃ vedeti; adukkhamasukhañce vedanaṃ vedeti, visaṃyutto naṃ vedeti. So kāyapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayamāno ‘kāyapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayāmī’ti pajānāti, jīvitapariyantikaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0245"/> vedanaṃ vedayamāno ‘jīvitapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayāmī’ti pajānāti, ‘kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā uddhaṃ jīvitapariyādānā idheva sabbavedayitāni anabhinanditāni sītībhavissantī’ti pajānāti.

364. “If he feels a pleasant feeling (sukhaṃ vedanaṃ), he feels it detached (visaṃyutto naṃ vedeti); if he feels a painful feeling (dukkhaṃ vedanaṃ), he feels it detached (visaṃyutto naṃ vedeti); if he feels a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling (adukkhamasukhaṃ vedanaṃ), he feels it detached (visaṃyutto naṃ vedeti).

When he feels a feeling terminating with the body (kāyapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ), he understands: ‘I feel a feeling terminating with the body (kāyapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayāmī’ti pajānāti.’ When he feels a feeling terminating with life (jīvitapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ), he understands: ‘I feel a feeling terminating with life (jīvitapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayāmī’ti pajānāti.’ He understands: ‘On the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), with the ending of life (jīvitapariyādānā), all that is felt (sabbavedayitāni), not being delighted in (anabhinanditāni), will become cool (sītībhavissantī) right here (idheva).’
365
<p n="365" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">365</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Seyyathāpi, bhikkhu, telañca paṭicca vaṭṭiñca paṭicca telappadīpo jhāyati; tasseva telassa ca vaṭṭiyā ca pariyādānā aññassa ca anupahārā <note>anupāhārā (sī. pī.), anupādānā (ka.)</note> anāhāro nibbāyati; evameva kho, bhikkhu, kāyapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayamāno ‘kāyapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayāmī’ti pajānāti, jīvitapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayamāno ‘jīvitapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayāmī’ti pajānāti, ‘kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā uddhaṃ jīvitapariyādānā idheva sabbavedayitāni <pb ed="T" n="3.0445"/> anabhinanditāni sītībhavissantī’ti pajānāti. Tasmā evaṃ samannāgato bhikkhu iminā paramena paññādhiṭṭhānena samannāgato hoti. Esā hi, bhikkhu, paramā ariyā paññā yadidaṃ – sabbadukkhakkhaye ñāṇaṃ.

365. Bhikkhu, just as an oil lamp burns in dependence on oil and a wick, and when the oil and wick are used up, and no more fuel is added (anupāhārā, anupādānā), it is extinguished from lack of fuel. In the same way, when a bhikkhu feels a feeling terminating with the body (kāyapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ), he understands: ‘I feel a feeling terminating with the body (kāyapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayāmī).’ When he feels a feeling terminating with life (jīvitapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ), he understands: ‘I feel a feeling terminating with life (jīvitapariyantikaṃ vedanaṃ vedayāmī).’ He understands: ‘On the dissolution of the body (kāyassa bhedā), with the ending of life (jīvitapariyādānā), all that is felt, not being delighted in (anabhinanditāni), will become cool right here (sītībhavissantī).’

Therefore, a bhikkhu possessing this is endowed with the supreme foundation of wisdom (prajñā).

For this, bhikkhu, is the supreme noble wisdom (paramā ariyā paññā), namely, the knowledge (jñānaṃ) of the destruction of all suffering (sabbadukkhakkhaye).
366
<p n="366" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">366</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Tassa sā vimutti sacce ṭhitā akuppā hoti. Tañhi, bhikkhu, musā yaṃ mosadhammaṃ, taṃ saccaṃ yaṃ amosadhammaṃ nibbānaṃ. Tasmā evaṃ samannāgato bhikkhu iminā paramena saccādhiṭṭhānena samannāgato hoti. Etañhi, bhikkhu, paramaṃ ariyasaccaṃ yadidaṃ – amosadhammaṃ nibbānaṃ.

‘‘His deliverance, being founded upon truth (sacce), is unshakeable (akuppā) hoti.

For that is false (musā), bhikkhu, which has a deceptive nature (mosadhammaṃ), and that is true (saccaṃ) which has an undeceptive nature (amosadhammaṃ) – Nibbana (nibbānaṃ).

Therefore, a bhikkhu possessing this truth (saccādhiṭṭhānena) possesses the supreme foundation of truth (saccādhiṭṭhānena).

For this, bhikkhu, is the supreme noble truth (paramaṃ ariyasaccaṃ), namely, Nibbana (nibbānaṃ), which has an undeceptive nature (amosadhammaṃ).
367
<p n="367" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">367</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Tasseva kho pana pubbe aviddasuno upadhī honti samattā samādinnā. Tyāssa pahīnā honti ucchinnamūlā tālāvatthukatā anabhāvaṃkatā āyatiṃ anuppādadhammā. Tasmā evaṃ samannāgato bhikkhu <pb ed="M" n="3.0289"/> iminā paramena cāgādhiṭṭhānena samannāgato hoti. Eso hi, bhikkhu, paramo ariyo cāgo yadidaṃ – sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo.

“Formerly, when he was ignorant (avidvasuno), he acquired and developed attachments (upadhī honti samattā samādinnā); now he has abandoned them (pahīnā), cut them off at the root (ucchinnamūlā), made them like a palm stump (tālāvatthukatā), obliterated them (anabhāvaṃkatā) so that they are no more subject to future arising (āyatiṃ anuppādadhammā). Therefore a bhikṣu possessing this relinquishment (cāga) possesses the supreme foundation of relinquishment (cāgādhiṭṭhānena). For this, bhikṣu, is the supreme noble relinquishment (paramo ariyo cāgo), namely, the relinquishing of all attachments (sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo).
368
<p n="368" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">368</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘Tasseva kho pana pubbe aviddasuno abhijjhā hoti chando sārāgo. Svāssa <pb ed="V" n="3.0295"/> pahīno hoti ucchinnamūlo tālāvatthukato anabhāvaṃkato āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo. Tasseva kho pana pubbe aviddasuno āghāto hoti byāpādo sampadoso. Svāssa pahīno hoti ucchinnamūlo <pb ed="P" n="3.0246"/> tālāvatthukato anabhāvaṃkato āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo. Tasseva kho pana pubbe aviddasuno avijjā hoti sammoho. Svāssa pahīno hoti ucchinnamūlo tālāvatthukato anabhāvaṃkato āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo. Tasmā evaṃ <pb ed="T" n="3.0446"/> samannāgato bhikkhu iminā paramena upasamādhiṭṭhānena samannāgato hoti. Eso hi, bhikkhu, paramo ariyo upasamo yadidaṃ – rāgadosamohānaṃ upasamo. ‘Paññaṃ nappamajjeyya, saccamanurakkheyya, cāgamanubrūheyya, santimeva so sikkheyyā’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ.

368. “Formerly, when he was ignorant, he experienced covetousness (abhijjhā), desire (chando), and lust (sārāgo).

<pb ed="v" n="3. 0295"/> Now, these are abandoned in him, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump (tālāvatthukato), obliterated (anabhāvaṃkato) so that they are no longer subject to future arising (āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo).

Formerly, when he was ignorant, he experienced anger (āghāto), ill will (byāpādo), and hate (sampadoso).

Now, these are abandoned in him, cut off at the root, <pb ed="p" n="3. 0246"/> made like a palm stump (tālāvatthukato), obliterated (anabhāvaṃkato) so that they are no longer subject to future arising (āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo).

Formerly, when he was ignorant, he experienced ignorance (avijjā) and delusion (sammoho).

Now, these are abandoned in him, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump (tālāvatthukato), obliterated (anabhāvaṃkato) so that they are no longer subject to future arising (āyatiṃ anuppādadhammo).

Therefore, <pb ed="t" n="3. 0446"/> a bhikkhu possessing this is one who possesses the supreme foundation of peace (upasamādhiṭṭhānena).

For this, bhikkhu, is the supreme noble peace (paramo ariyo upasamo), namely, the pacification (upasamo) of lust (rāga), hate (dosa), and delusion (mohānam).

‘One should not neglect wisdom (paññaṃ), should preserve truth (saccaṃ), should cultivate relinquishment (cāgaṃ), and should train for peace (santi)’— so it was with reference to this that it was said, this was said in relation to this.
369
<p n="369" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">369</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> ‘‘‘Yattha ṭhitaṃ maññassavā nappavattanti, maññassave kho pana nappavattamāne muni santoti vuccatī’ti – iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ. Kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? ‘Asmī’ti, bhikkhu, maññitametaṃ, ‘ayamahamasmī’ti maññitametaṃ, ‘bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘na bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘rūpī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘arūpī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘saññī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘asaññī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ, ‘nevasaññīnāsaññī bhavissa’nti maññitametaṃ. Maññitaṃ, bhikkhu, rogo maññitaṃ gaṇḍo maññitaṃ sallaṃ. Sabbamaññitānaṃ tveva, bhikkhu, samatikkamā muni santoti vuccati. Muni kho pana, bhikkhu, santo na jāyati, na jīyati, na mīyati, na kuppati, na piheti. Tañhissa, bhikkhu, natthi yena jāyetha, ajāyamāno kiṃ jīyissati, ajīyamāno kiṃ mīyissati, amīyamāno kiṃ kuppissati, akuppamāno kissa <note>kiṃ (ka.)</note> pihessati? ‘Yattha ṭhitaṃ maññassavā nappavattanti, maññassave kho pana nappavattamāne muni santoti vuccatī’ti – iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ. Imaṃ kho me tvaṃ, bhikkhu, saṃkhittena chadhātuvibhaṅgaṃ dhārehī’’ti.

369. ‘Where, when established, the tides of conceiving do not sweep, and when the tides of conceiving no longer sweep over him, he is called a sage at peace’— so it was said.

But with reference to what was this said? ‘I am’ (asmīti) is conceiving, O Bhikkhu; ‘I am this’ (ayamahamasmīti) is conceiving; ‘I will be’ (bhavissaṃti) is conceiving; ‘I will not be’ (na bhavissaṃti) is conceiving; ‘I will be with form’ (rūpī bhavissaṃti) is conceiving; ‘I will be without form’ (arūpī bhavissaṃti) is conceiving; ‘I will be with perception’ (saññī bhavissaṃti) is conceiving; ‘I will be without perception’ (asaññī bhavissaṃti) is conceiving; ‘I will be neither with nor without perception’ (nevasaññīnāsaññī bhavissaṃti) is conceiving.

Conceiving (maññitaṃ), O Bhikkhu, is a disease; conceiving is a tumour; conceiving is a dart.

By overcoming all conceivings (sabbamaññitānaṃ samatikkamā), O Bhikkhu, one is called a sage at peace (muni santo).

And the sage at peace (muni kho pana, bhikkhu, santo) is not born (na jāyati), does not age (na jīyati), does not die (na mīyati), is not shaken (na kuppati), and is not agitated (na piheti).

For there is nothing present in him by which he might be born (tañhissa natthi yena jāyetha). Not being born, how could he age (ajāyamāno kiṃ jīyissati)? Not ageing, how could he die (ajīyamāno kiṃ mīyissati)? Not dying, how could he be shaken (amīyamāno kiṃ kuppissati)? Not being shaken, why should he be agitated (akuppamāno kissa pihessati)? ‘Where, when established, the tides of conceiving do not sweep, and when the tides of conceiving no longer sweep over him, he is called a sage at peace’— so it was with reference to this that it was said (iti yaṃ taṃ vuttaṃ, idametaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ).

Bhikkhu, bear in mind this brief exposition of the six elements (imaṃ kho me tvaṃ, bhikkhu, saṃkhittena chadhātuvibhaṅgaṃ dhārehi).
370
<p n="370" rend="bodytext"><hi rend="paranum">370</hi><hi rend="dot">.</hi> Atha <pb ed="T" n="3.0447"/><pb ed="M" n="3.0290"/> kho āyasmā pukkusāti – ‘‘satthā kira me anuppatto, sugato kira me anuppatto sammāsambuddho kira me anuppatto’’ti uṭṭhāyāsanā ekaṃsaṃ cīvaraṃ katvā bhagavato pādesu sirasā nipatitvā bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – ‘‘accayo maṃ, bhante, accagamā yathābālaṃ yathāmūḷhaṃ yathāakusalaṃ, yohaṃ bhagavantaṃ āvusovādena samudācaritabbaṃ amaññissaṃ. Tassa me, bhante, bhagavā accayaṃ accayato paṭiggaṇhātu āyatiṃ saṃvarāyā’’ti. ‘‘Taggha tvaṃ, bhikkhu, accayo accagamā yathābālaṃ yathāmūḷhaṃ yathāakusalaṃ <pb ed="P" n="3.0247"/>, yaṃ maṃ tvaṃ āvusovādena <pb ed="V" n="3.0296"/> samudācaritabbaṃ amaññittha. Yato ca kho tvaṃ, bhikkhu, accayaṃ accayato disvā yathādhammaṃ paṭikarosi, taṃ te mayaṃ paṭiggaṇhāma. Vuddhihesā, bhikkhu, ariyassa vinaye yo accayaṃ accayato disvā yathādhammaṃ paṭikaroti, āyatiṃ saṃvaraṃ āpajjatī’’ti. ‘‘Labheyyāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavato santike upasampada’’nti. ‘‘Paripuṇṇaṃ pana te, bhikkhu, pattacīvara’’nti? ‘‘Na kho me, bhante, paripuṇṇaṃ pattacīvara’’nti. ‘‘Na kho, bhikkhu, tathāgatā aparipuṇṇapattacīvaraṃ upasampādentī’’ti.

<p rend="bodytext">Atha kho āyasmā pukkusāti bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinanditvā anumoditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā pattacīvarapariyesanaṃ pakkāmi. Atha kho āyasmantaṃ pukkusātiṃ pattacīvarapariyesanaṃ carantaṃ vibbhantā gāvī <note>bhantagāvī (sī. pī.), gāvī (syā. kaṃ.)</note> jīvitā voropesi. Atha kho sambahulā <pb ed="T" n="3.0448"/> bhikkhū yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ – ‘‘yo so, bhante, pukkusāti nāma kulaputto bhagavatā saṃkhittena ovādena ovadito so kālaṅkato. Tassa kā gati, ko abhisamparāyo’’ti? ‘‘Paṇḍito, bhikkhave, pukkusāti kulaputto paccapādi dhammassānudhammaṃ, na ca maṃ dhammādhikaraṇaṃ vihesesi <note>viheṭhesi (sī. syā. kaṃ.) viheseti (ka.)</note>. Pukkusāti, bhikkhave, kulaputto pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā’’ti.

<note>bhantagāvī (sī. pī.), gāvī (syā. kaṃ.)</note> <pb ed="T" n="3.0448"/> <note>viheṭhesi (sī. syā. kaṃ.) viheseti (ka.)</note> <p rend="bodytext">Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti.

<p rend="centre">Dhātuvibhaṅgasuttaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ dasamaṃ.

370. Then, venerable Pukkusāti thought: "Indeed, the Teacher has come to me! Indeed, the Sublime One has come to me! Indeed, the Fully Enlightened One has come to me!" Rising from his seat, he arranged his upper robe over one shoulder, prostrated with his head at the Blessed One's feet, and said: "Venerable sir, a transgression (accayo) overcame me, in that like a fool, confused and unskilled, I presumed to address the Blessed One as 'friend'. May the Blessed One accept my transgression (accayaṃ) as a transgression (accayato) so that I may restrain myself in the future (āyatiṃ saṃvarāya)."

"Truly, monk (bhikkhu), a transgression (accayo) overcame you, in that like a fool, confused and unskilled, you presumed to address me as 'friend.' But since you, monk (bhikkhu), seeing your transgression (accayaṃ) as such, make amends in accordance with the Dhamma (yathādhammaṃ paṭikarosi), we forgive (paṭiggaṇhāma) you. This is growth (vuddhihesā), monk (bhikkhu), in the discipline (vinaye) of the Noble One (ariyassa): that seeing a transgression (accayaṃ) as such, one makes amends in accordance with the Dhamma (yathādhammaṃ paṭikaroti) and undertakes restraint (saṃvaraṃ āpajjati) in the future (āyatiṃ)."

"May I receive ordination (upasampada) in the Blessed One's presence, venerable sir (bhante)!"

"Are your bowl (patta) and robes (cīvara) complete, monk (bhikkhu)?" "No, venerable sir (bhante), my bowl (patta) and robes (cīvara) are not complete." "Monk (bhikkhu), the Tathāgatas do not grant ordination (upasampādentī) to one whose bowl (patta) and robes (cīvara) are incomplete."

Then, venerable Pukkusāti, delighting and rejoicing in the Blessed One's words, rose from his seat, paid homage to the Blessed One, circumambulated him, and departed to seek a bowl (patta) and robes (cīvara).

Then, while venerable Pukkusāti was wandering in search of a bowl (patta) and robes (cīvara), a stray cow (gāvī) deprived him of life (jīvitā voropesi).

Then a number of monks (bhikkhū) approached the Blessed One; having approached, they paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, those monks (bhikkhū) said to the Blessed One: "Venerable sir (bhante), the clansman Pukkusāti, who was advised by the Blessed One with a brief instruction (saṃkhittena ovādena ovadito), has passed away (kālaṅkato). What was his destination (kā gati)? What is his future state (abhisamparāyo)?"

"Monks (bhikkhave), the wise Pukkusāti has practiced according to the Dhamma, and he did not trouble me about the Dhamma. Pukkusāti, monks (bhikkhave), has, with the destruction (parikkhayā) of the five lower fetters (pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ), reappeared spontaneously (opapātiko) and will attain final Nirvāṇa (parinibbāyī) there, not liable to return (anāvattidhammo) from that world (tasmā lokā)."

This is what the Blessed One said. Elated, those monks (bhikkhū) delighted in the Blessed One's statement.

The tenth Dhātuvibhaṅgasutta is concluded.
