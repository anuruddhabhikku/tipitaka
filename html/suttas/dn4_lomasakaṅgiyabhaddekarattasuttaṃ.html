Thus have I heard:

Once the Blessed One (bhagavā) was dwelling at Sāvatthi (Śrāvastī) in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapindika’s monastery (ārāma).

Now on that occasion, the venerable Lomasakangiya was living in the Sakyan country at Kapilavatthu in Nigrodha’s Park.

Then Candana, a young deva (devaputto) of beautiful appearance, when the night was far advanced, illuminating the whole of Nigrodha’s Park, approached the venerable Lomasakangiya; having approached, he stood to one side.

Standing to one side, Candana the deva’s son said this to the venerable Lomasakangiya: “Do you, bhikkhu (monk), remember the summary (uddesa) and the exposition (vibhaṅga) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ (bhaddekaratta)?”

“No, friend (āvuso), I do not remember the summary and the exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’”

“Then, friend, do you remember the summary and the exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’?”

“I, too, bhikkhu, do not remember the summary and the exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’”

“Then, bhikkhu, do you remember the verses (gāthā) of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’?”

“No, friend, I do not remember the verses of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’”

“Then, friend, do you remember the verses of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’?”

“Yes, bhikkhu, I remember the verses of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’”

“How then, friend, do you remember the verses of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’?”

“Once, bhikkhu, the Blessed One was dwelling among the devas in Tāvatiṃsa, at the foot of the pāricchattaka tree, on the paṇḍukambala stone.

There the Blessed One spoke to the Tāvatiṃsa devas the summary and the exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’:

“One should not hark back to the past, nor long for the future;

That which is past is left behind, and the future is not yet reached.

“But whoever sees insightfully each present state,

Unshaken, unperturbed— knowing that, let him cultivate it.

“Today itself should be done what ought to be done; who knows if death will come tomorrow?

For there is no bargain to be made by us with Death and his great army.

“The one who dwells thus ardently, tireless, day and night,

That one, truly, is ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment,’ so the sage (muni), at peace, declares.”

“Thus, bhikkhu, I remember the verses of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’

Learn, bhikkhu, the summary and the exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’; master, bhikkhu, the summary and the exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’; remember, bhikkhu, the summary and the exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment.’

The summary and the exposition of ‘One Who Has One Fortunate Attachment’ is beneficial, bhikkhu; it belongs to the fundamentals of the holy life (ādibrahmacariyaka).”

That is what was said by Candana the young deva.

Having said this, he thereupon vanished at once.

<pb ed="T" n="3.0372"/> <pb ed="M" n="3.0242"/>
Thereupon, the venerable Lomasakaṅgiya, after that night had passed, arranged his lodging, took his bowl and robe, and left on a walking tour for Sāvatthī.

Wandering by stages, he arrived at Sāvatthī, Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. He went to the Blessed One and, on arrival, having bowed down to him, sat to one side.

As he was sitting there, the venerable Lomasakaṅgiya said this to the Blessed One:

“Once, lord, I was staying in the Sakyan country at Kapilavatthu in Nigrodha’s Park.

Then, lord, a certain young deity, in the far extreme of the night, his extreme radiance lighting up the entirety of Nigrodha’s Park, came to me and, on arrival, stood to one side.

Standing to one side, lord, that young deity said this to me: ‘Do you, bhikkhu, remember the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta?’ When this was said, I said this to that young deity: ‘No, friend, I do not remember the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta.

But do you, friend, remember the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta?’ ‘I, too, bhikkhu, do not remember the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta.

But do you, bhikkhu, remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?’ ‘No, friend, I do not remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.

But do you, friend, remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?’ ‘Yes, I do, bhikkhu, I remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.’

‘But how, friend, do you remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta?’ Once, bhikkhu, the Blessed One was staying among the gods of the Tāvatiṃsa heaven at the foot of the Pāricchattaka tree, on the Paṇḍukambala stone.

There the Blessed One recited the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta to the gods of the Tāvatiṃsa heaven:

“One should not chase after the past…pe…

‘He is truly called fortunate, a monk of the present,’ thus says the peaceful sage.”

“In this way, bhikkhu, I remember the verses of the bhaddekaratta.

Learn, bhikkhu, the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta; master, bhikkhu, the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta; remember, bhikkhu, the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta.

The summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta is beneficial, bhikkhu; it belongs to the fundamentals of the holy life.”

That is what the young deity said, lord; and after saying that, he vanished right there.

It would be good, lord, if the Blessed One would teach me the summary and exposition of the bhaddekaratta."

The Bhagavān asked, “Monk, do you know that young deity (devaputta)?” “Sir, I do not know that young deity (devaputta),” the monk replied.

“Bhikkhu, that young deity (devaputta) is named Candana.

Bhikkhu, that young deity (devaputta) heeds the Dhamma, gives it attention, engages it with all his mind, hears it with eager ears.

So, bhikkhu, listen and attend closely to what I shall say.”

“Yes, venerable sir,” the venerable Lomasakangiya replied to the Blessed One.

The Blessed One then said this:

“Let not a person revive the past
Or on the future build his hopes;

For the past is left behind,
And the future has not been attained.

But whoever sees insightfully the present phenomenon
Again and again, in each case,

Is unshaken, unperturbed:
Knowing thus, let him develop it.

Ardently doing what should be done today,
for — who knows? — tomorrow death.

There is no bargaining with Mortality
& his mighty horde.

One who dwells thus ardently,
tirelessly, day and night

That one, indeed, is called ‘noble solitary,’
so says the sage.”

“And how, bhikkhu, does one revive the past?... “That is how, bhikkhu, one revives the past.

“And how, bhikkhu, does one not revive the past?... “That is how, bhikkhu, one does not revive the past.

“And how, bhikkhu, does one build hopes on the future?... “That is how, bhikkhu, one builds hopes on the future.

“And how, bhikkhu, does one not build hopes on the future?... “That is how, bhikkhu, one does not build hopes on the future.

“And how, bhikkhu, does one get drawn together in present phenomena?... “That is how, bhikkhu, one gets drawn together in present phenomena.

“And how, bhikkhu, does one not get drawn together in present phenomena?... “That is how, bhikkhu, one does not get drawn together in present phenomena.

“Let not a person revive the past
Or on the future build his hopes;

For the past is left behind,
And the future has not been attained.

But whoever sees insightfully the present phenomenon
Again and again, in each case,

Is unshaken, unperturbed:
Knowing thus, let him develop it.

Ardently doing what should be done today,
for — who knows? — tomorrow death.
There is no bargaining with Mortality (maccunā) & his mighty horde (mahāsenena).

"Living thus, ardent (ātāpiṃ), tirelessly (atanditaṃ) day and night;
That one indeed, the Fortunate One of a Single Night (bhaddekaratta), the wise ones (munī) declare (ācikkhate)."

The Blessed One (bhagavā) spoke thus (idamavoca).
The venerable (āyasmā) Lomasakangiya, with a joyful mind (attamanā), rejoiced (abhinandi) in the Blessed One's (bhagavato) words (bhāsitaṃ).

The Lomasakangiyabhaddekarattasutta, the fourth (catutthaṃ), is concluded (niṭṭhitaṃ).
